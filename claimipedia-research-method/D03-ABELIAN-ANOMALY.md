# D-03 Abelian-Higgs anomaly strike

## Result

The trace anomaly can open the metric–Higgs spectral ray, but the leading decoupled contribution still does not produce a full three-coordinate family.

Write the trace schematically as

`O_g = a O_H + c_beta O_F + ...`,

where `O_H=H^dagger H`, `O_F=F_mu_nu F^mu_nu`, and `c_beta` is proportional to `beta_e/(2e)`. At the first spectral order, `O_H` excites scalar pairs and `O_F` excites gauge pairs. They are orthogonal sectors of the free asymptotic Hilbert space.

Consequently,

`rho = rho_H (a,1)(a,1)^T + c_beta^2 rho_F (1,0)(1,0)^T`,

and

`det rho = c_beta^2 rho_H rho_F`,

`Delta = det(rho)/rho_HH = c_beta^2 rho_F`.

The anomaly therefore gives strictly positive spectral innovation wherever the scalar and gauge continua overlap.

## The new obstruction

Every leading matrix nevertheless belongs to the fixed plane

`span{(a,1)(a,1)^T, E_gg}`.

It may be positive definite at each energy while the family spans only two of the three coordinates of `H_2(R)`. Fixed-point matrix rank and family dimension are different discriminants.

The third direction requires a genuinely mixed amplitude whose source vector is not confined to either the original Higgs ray or the metric-only anomaly axis. In perturbation theory this means calculating interaction-induced common intermediate states and checking whether their orientation changes with energy, momentum, temperature or background.

## Consequence

The emerging ladder is now:

1. free improved Higgs: one spectral ray;
2. leading gauge anomaly: a rank-two matrix in a fixed plane;
3. mixed interacting spectrum: possible full `H_2(R)` family;
4. transformation closure: only then test `Sp(4,R)` response covariance.

This is a stronger and more falsifiable programme than inferring the group from positivity alone.
