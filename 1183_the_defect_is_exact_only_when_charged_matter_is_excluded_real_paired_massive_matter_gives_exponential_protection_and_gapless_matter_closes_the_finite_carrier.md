## 1. Case A: topological-sector-only fold

Suppose the \(S^1/\mathbb Z_2\) construction is a fold of the emergent discrete gauge sector only. Standard-Model matter either obeys boundary conditions excluding it from the fixed planes or remains in a separate bulk sector with no charged zero modes on the handle.

Then:

- no charged \(2+1\)-dimensional fermion determinant is present;
- no parity anomaly induces a self Chern–Simons term;
- the orbifold bulk projection sets the retained twist to \(p=0\);
- the nine-dimensional torus Hilbert space is exact within the TQFT.

This is a consistent effective boundary condition. It is not derived from the current Furey/Jordan action.

## 2. Case B: massive charged defect matter

Let charged excitations of mass \(m>0\) propagate on the defect. Integrating them out can shift the parity-odd Chern–Simons level according to their charges and signed masses.

Two conditions are then needed:

1. the total induced level must vanish modulo the allowed \(\mathbb Z_3\) twist class;
2. virtual charged worldlines winding around the handle must remain suppressed.

If the spectrum is paired by the Real/reflection structure with masses \(+m\) and \(-m\) in matching charge sectors, parity-odd contributions cancel. The topological degeneracy is nevertheless only asymptotic at finite size:

\[
\Delta E\sim e^{-mL},
\]

where \(L\) is the shortest noncontractible cycle.

This is the standard meaning of a protected topological sector in a microscopic system: exact in the infrared/TQFT limit, exponentially split at finite volume.

## 3. Case C: gapless charged matter

If a residual-\(\mathbb Z_3\)-charged field is gapless on the defect, its worldline can wind without exponential suppression. The BF zero modes interact with a continuum of arbitrarily low-energy states.

Then:

- the full Hilbert space is not nine-dimensional;
- the topological factor need not separate;
- modular transformations are not operations on an isolated ground-state multiplet;
- the finite \(Sp(4,\mathbb F_3)\) carrier is not an exact physical subsystem.

The abstract BF operator algebra still exists as a response sector, but the strong ground-space interpretation closes.

## 4. Are Standard-Model modes necessarily gapless?

Gauge bosons of the unbroken Standard Model are neutral under the family \(U(1)_X\) direction, although global quotient data link colour centre to the discrete lift. Neutral gapless modes do not terminate the electric \(\mathbb Z_3\) Wilson line.

Quarks and leptons carry the relevant charges. In the broken phase they may be massive, confined or excluded from the defect, but the light neutrino sector makes the quantitative protection scale potentially small. No numerical claim is made here.

The crucial distinction is:

- massive dynamical charges break an exact one-form symmetry but do not automatically destroy topological order;
- they turn exact line conservation into an emergent infrared rule and produce exponentially small finite-size splitting;
- gapless charges destroy the isolated topological ground-space factor.

## 5. Parity anomaly

A \(2+1\)-dimensional charged fermion generally contributes a half-integral parity-odd level before the full gauge-invariant regulator and spectrum are included. Only the total regulated spectrum is meaningful.

The required audit needs:

- every fixed-plane fermion and its residual charge;
- its orbifold parity;
- the sign of each real mass eigenvalue;
- conjugate/Real pairings;
- allowed local counterterms;
- the relation to the colour-centre quotient.

None is fixed by the BF parity assignment alone.

Four-dimensional anomaly cancellation removes a mandatory inflow obstruction but does not compute this signed three-dimensional level.

## 6. Exactness hierarchy

The programme should use three distinct claims:

### Algebraically exact

The quotient BF theory has a nine-state torus Hilbert space and two-qutrit Wilson algebra.

### Effective-topologically exact

Only the first is currently proved. The second is a consistent conditional construction. The third is neither expected generically nor required for ordinary topological protection.
