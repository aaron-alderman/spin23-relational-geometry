# The four target moments are exactly a double Jordan frame, and the missing CP1 supplies the last two coordinates

## Main result

The four coefficients targeted by the degree-sixteen potential are not four unrelated phenomenological numbers. They are an invertible moment-coordinate system for the overlap of two ordered Jordan frames.

Let the nondegenerate Hermitian family tensors have spectral resolutions

\[
A=\sum_{i=1}^3 a_iE_i,
\qquad
B=\sum_{j=1}^3 b_jF_j,
\]

where \((E_1,E_2,E_3)\) and \((F_1,F_2,F_3)\) are ordered rank-one Jordan frames. Define their overlap matrix

\[
P_{ij}=\operatorname{Tr}(E_iF_j)=|U_{ij}|^2.
\]

Then

\[
y_{pq}=\operatorname{Tr}(A^pB^q)
=\sum_{ij}a_i^p b_j^qP_{ij},
\qquad p,q\in\{1,2\}.
\]

For distinct \(a_i\) and \(b_j\), the four numbers

\[
(y_{11},y_{21},y_{12},y_{22})
\]

determine the doubly stochastic overlap matrix \(P\) uniquely, and conversely. Thus

\[
\boxed{
\text{four target moments}
\longleftrightarrow
\text{overlap of two ordered Jordan frames modulo CP sign}.
}
\]

The earlier Peirce analysis now fits exactly. One rank-one projector supplies only two physical overlap coordinates. Choosing a line in its rank-two complement—the \(\mathbb{CP}^1\) direction of the charge-three cross-Peirce link—supplies the remaining two. The link therefore has precisely the right geometric size to complete the mixing frame.

This is a kinematic closure, not yet a dynamical derivation.

## 1. The moment map is invertible

The overlap matrix obeys

\[
\sum_jP_{ij}=1,
\qquad
\sum_iP_{ij}=1.
\]

These conditions leave four independent real coordinates. Let

\[
V_a=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&1&1\\
a_1&a_2&a_3\\
a_1^2&a_2^2&a_3^2
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
V_b=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&1&1\\
b_1&b_2&b_3\\
b_1^2&b_2^2&b_3^2
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Their determinants are Vandermonde products:

\[
\det V_a=\prod_{i<k}(a_k-a_i),
\qquad
\det V_b=\prod_{j<\ell}(b_\ell-b_j).
\]

For nondegenerate spectra both are nonzero. The full moment table

\[
M_{pq}=\sum_{ij}a_i^pP_{ij}b_j^q,
\qquad p,q\in\{0,1,2\},
\]

satisfies

\[
M=V_aPV_b^T.
\]

The row and column sums fix every entry of \(M\) with \(p=0\) or \(q=0\). The only unfixed entries are exactly

\[
M_{11},M_{21},M_{12},M_{22},

\]

which are the four mixed moments. Hence

\[
\boxed{
P=V_a^{-1}M(V_b^{-1})^T.
}
\]

This is a global affine inversion on the doubly stochastic affine space, not merely a local Jacobian statement.

## 2. Why one projector gives only two physical coordinates

A primitive projector \(F_1\in\mathbb{CP}^2\) has four real parameters. But once the nondegenerate frame \((E_i)\) is fixed, its diagonal stabilizer acts by independent row rephasings. Modulo the effective two-torus, the invariant data are only

\[
p_i=\operatorname{Tr}(E_iF_1)=|U_{i1}|^2,
\qquad
\sum_i p_i=1.
\]

That is two real numbers.

Equivalently, all invariants formed from \(A\) and one projector are generated by

\[
\operatorname{Tr}(AF_1),
\qquad
\operatorname{Tr}(A^2F_1),
\]

after using \(F_1^2=F_1\), \(\operatorname{Tr}F_1=1\), and the cubic Cayley–Hamilton relation for \(A\).

Therefore a single Peirce split cannot carry the full four-parameter PMNS frame. It fixes one probability column and leaves an internal completion unresolved.

## 3. The completion fiber is exactly CP1

Fix \(F_1\). Its orthogonal complement is a complex two-plane. An ordered completion requires choosing a second line

\[
F_2\subset I-F_1.
\]

The space of such lines is

\[
\mathbb{CP}^1,
\]

with two real dimensions. Then

\[
F_3=I-F_1-F_2

\]

is forced.

Thus the continuous dimension count is

\[
\boxed{
2\ \text{from the first projector overlaps}
+2\ \text{from the completion }\mathbb{CP}^1
=4\ \text{physical mixing coordinates}.
}
\]

This is precisely the geometry found in document 1195. A nonzero charge-three cross-Peirce link \(S\in\operatorname{Hom}(L,Q)\otimes R^3\) selects its image line in \(Q\), hence selects \(F_2\) and completes the frame.

## 4. What is and is not encoded by the overlaps

The matrix \(P_{ij}=|U_{ij}|^2\) determines the three mixing angles and \(|J|\) at a generic unistochastic point. It does not distinguish

\[
U\quad\text{from}\quad U^*.
\]

The two possibilities are the two orientations of the same overlap geometry. The full complex Jordan frame retains that orientation, but the four trace moments forget its sign.

Therefore:

- the degree-sixteen CP-even potential selects an overlap geometry;
- its minima occur as a CP-conjugate pair of full frames;
- the degree-twelve pseudoscalar can distinguish the two only if an oriented CP datum is admitted.

## 5. Why this still does not derive flavour

The equivalence

\[
y^*\longleftrightarrow P^*\longleftrightarrow
\text{relative Jordan-frame overlap}

\]

changes the language of the missing datum but does not remove it.

A norm-only cross-Peirce potential leaves the completion \(\mathbb{CP}^1\) degenerate. A response operator with a simple spectrum can select the full frame, but if that response is independently supplied then the orientation has merely moved into the response tensor.

The reflexive closure problem is now exact:

\[
\boxed{
\text{derive a nondegenerate response whose spectral Jordan frame reproduces the four overlap moments that generated it.}
}
\]

That is the frame-valued version of the earlier projector fixed-point equation.
