# The quartic extension embeds in the binary Heisenberg group, and its phase is a Clifford shear

## Status

**Exact finite-algebra result, conditional only on the already selected field-labelled binary basis.**

The missing dual-character sector can now be adjoined explicitly. The order-eight extension generated by (s-r) and (t-r) embeds in the order-64 binary Heisenberg group, and the common square (2r) maps to the central operator (-I). The quartic phase table is realized by the diagonal Clifford operator

\[
U_q=S^\dagger\otimes S^\dagger.
\]

This is a genuine (X/Z) completion. It also proves that the recurring order-24 stabilizer does **not** act as the twice-punctured code group on this selected phase subgroup: it fixes the subgroup pointwise.

## 1. Finite carrier from the integral (D_2) phase space

Let (L\cong\mathbb F_2^2) be the intrinsic binary reduction of the two-puncture (D_2) lattice in the selected labelled basis, and let (L^*) be its dual-character space. The finite phase space is

\[
V=L\oplus L^*,
\qquad
\omega((x,z),(y,w))=z\cdot y+w\cdot x.
\]

This is the binary reduction of the integral symplectic completion (D_2\oplus D_2^*). The distinction between (L) and the puncture-coordinate image remains important: the determinant-two change of basis found earlier is not invertible modulo two. The present construction uses the intrinsic labelled (D_2) basis, not the naive two-coordinate reduction.

## 2. The order-64 Heisenberg group

On (\mathcal H=\mathbb C[L]\cong\mathbb C^4), define

\[
X^x|v\rangle=|v+x\rangle,
\qquad
Z^z|v\rangle=(-1)^{z\cdot v}|v\rangle.
\]

Write

\[
P(k,x,z)=i^kX^xZ^z,
\qquad
k\in\mathbb Z_4,quad x\in L,quad z\in L^*.
\]

Then

\[
P(k,x,z)P(\ell,y,w)
=P(k+\ell+2z\cdot y,,x+y,,z+w).
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{|H(L)|=4\cdot4\cdot4=64},
\qquad
Z(H(L))=\{i^kI:k\in\mathbb Z_4\}\cong\mu_4.
\]

The commutator is

\[
[P(k,x,z),P(\ell,y,w)]
=(-1)^{z\cdot y+w\cdot x}I,
\]

so the required noncommutativity appears only after both (L) and (L^*) are present.

## 3. Exact embedding of the quartic extension

The binary quadratic phase is

\[
q(x_1,x_2)=3x_1+3x_2\pmod4,
\]

with polarization

\[
q(x+y)-q(x)-q(y)=2x\cdot y\pmod4.
\]

Recall

\[
A=\langle s-r,t-r\rangle
\cong\mathbb Z_4\oplus\mathbb Z_2,
\]

whose generators have common square (2r). Define

\[
\Phi(\sigma(x)+\epsilon,2r)
=(-1)^\epsilon i^{q(x)}X^x,
\qquad
\epsilon\in\mathbb F_2.
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
\Phi(s-r)=-iX_1,
\qquad
\Phi(t-r)=-iX_2,
\qquad
\Phi(2r)=-I.}
\]

Both lifted translations have order four, commute, and square to (-I). Moreover,

\[
\Phi(\sigma(x)+\epsilon2r)
\Phi(\sigma(y)+\eta2r)
=\Phi\bigl(\sigma(x+y)+(\epsilon+\eta+x\cdot y)2r\bigr).
\]

Thus the section cocycle (2r(x\cdot y)) is represented exactly by the central sign in the Heisenberg group. The earlier abelian extension was not incorrect; it was the lifted (X)-Lagrangian inside the full noncommutative group.

## 4. The quartic phase is a Clifford operator

Define

\[
U_q|v\rangle=i^{q(v)}|v\rangle.
\]

In the ordered basis (00,10,01,11),

\[
U_q=\operatorname{diag}(1,-i,-i,-1)
=S^\dagger\otimes S^\dagger.
\]

It normalizes the Heisenberg group by

\[
\boxed{
U_qP(k,x,z)U_q^{-1}
=P(k+q(x),,x,,z+x).}
\]

After quotienting out the central phase, the induced symplectic action is

\[
(x,z)\longmapsto(x,z+x).
\]

This element has order two in (Sp(4,2)), while its Clifford lift has order four because

\[
\boxed{U_q^2=Z^{(1,1)}.}
\]

This is the finite-metaplectic feature expected here: the symplectic involution lifts to an operator whose square remains a Pauli element before passing to the projective quotient.

## 5. What the construction joins

The same function (q) now occurs in four exact roles:

1. the negative-(D_2) discriminant phase;
2. the twice-punctured binary code phase;
3. the pullback of (\chi_r:M/I\to\mathbb Z_4) along the nonsplit section;
4. the diagonal entries of a Clifford normalizer of the finite Heisenberg group.

The quartic source direction and the (X/Z) phase space are therefore compatible in a concrete representation, not merely through equal phase distributions.

## 6. The order-24 coincidence is now pinned

The ordered-section stabilizer inside

\[
\operatorname{Aut}(M/I,\chi_r)\cong AGL(3,2)
\]

has order 24. But it fixes both selected tails (s,t), and hence fixes (s-r) and (t-r) individually. Its induced action on

\[
\langle-iX_1,-iX_2\rangle
\]

is pointwise trivial.

The twice-punctured code automorphism group also has order 24, but acts nontrivially on the retained projective column configuration. Consequently,

\[
\boxed{|G_1|=|G_2|=24\quad\text{does not produce an action intertwiner}.}
\]

Any future identification must use additional degrees of freedom—most naturally the complementary affine tail, the full Heisenberg normalizer, or the retained code columns—not the selected order-eight phase subgroup alone.

## 7. What is proved and what remains open

### Proved

- the full binary Heisenberg group has order 64 and centre (mu_4);
- the quartic extension embeds injectively as an abelian order-eight lifted (X)-subgroup;
- (2r) maps to the central sign (-I);
- the phase table is implemented by (S^\dagger\otimes S^\dagger);
- its Clifford action covers the shear ((x,z)\mapsto(x,z+x));
- the order-24 ordered-section stabilizer acts trivially on the selected phase subgroup.

### Still open

- deriving the labelled (D_2) basis directly from Furey/Jordan data;
- lifting the integral puncture and root involutions separately into this Clifford representation;
- finding a nontrivial representation of the code's order-24 group in the full normalizer;
- identifying the primitive-wedge (Spin(2,3)) action of the same Clifford element;
- constructing the odd-prime/qutrit analogue from the integral carrier.

## 8. Stop-everything and falsification gates

### Stop-everything

1. The code's order-24 group embeds in the Clifford normalizer and acts on the same labelled (D_2\oplus D_2^*) carrier.
2. The integral shear's primitive wedge produces the selected index-six (Spin(2,3)) fiber without a fitted conjugation.
3. Furey's field operators derive (X_1,X_2) and their dual characters as the two faces of one Jordan-triple carrier.

### Falsifiers and boundaries

- the phase extension alone remains abelian;
- the Heisenberg commutator comes from (X/Z) duality, not from the symmetric quadratic cocycle;
- the order-24 stabilizer coincidence is not an identification;
- the construction depends on the selected labelled binary basis and does not repair the previously falsified puncture-exchange equivariance.
