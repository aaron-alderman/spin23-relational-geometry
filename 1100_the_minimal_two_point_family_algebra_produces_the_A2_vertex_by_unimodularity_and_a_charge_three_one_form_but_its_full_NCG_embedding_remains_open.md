## Executive result

The smallest unital finite algebra that distinguishes one charged-lepton
spectral line from the other two without distinguishing the pair is

\[
\boxed{\mathcal A_{\rm fam}=\mathbb C\oplus\mathbb C.}
\]

Given a chosen rank-one projector \(P_\alpha\) and

\[
Q_\alpha=I-P_\alpha,
\]

represent it on family space by

\[
\pi_\alpha(z,w)=zP_\alpha+wQ_\alpha.
\]

Its unitary group acts with phase \(a\) on the exceptional line and phase \(b\)
on the two-dimensional complement. The determinant-one condition is

\[
a+2b=0.
\]

With primitive normalization \(b=1\), this gives

\[
\boxed{
X_{\rm fam}=-2P_\alpha+Q_\alpha
=I-3P_\alpha
=\Delta_\alpha.
}
\]

Thus the \(A_2\) family vertex is not inserted as a mysterious charge vector:
it is the unimodular generator of the minimal \(1\oplus2\) family algebra.

If the finite Dirac operator contains a nonzero edge

\[
K=P_\alpha D_FQ_\alpha,
\]

generated by \(K\). Under \(X_{\rm fam}\), it has charge

\[
(-2)-(+1)=-3,
\]

while its adjoint has charge \(+3\). This is exactly the charge required of the
flavon that reconnects the minimal lepton texture.

The result closes the abstract family-factor construction. It does **not**
prove that tensoring or adjoining this algebra to the actual finite geometry

## 1. Minimality of the algebra

A one-dimensional unital complex \(*\)-algebra acts by scalar multiples of
the identity and cannot distinguish a rank-one line from its rank-two
complement.

The algebra generated by the two orthogonal central idempotents

\[
P_\alpha,\qquad Q_\alpha=I-P_\alpha
\]

is

\[
\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb C}\{P_\alpha,Q_\alpha\}
\cong\mathbb C\oplus\mathbb C.
\]

It is therefore the minimal unital commutative \(*\)-algebra implementing the
desired \(1+2\) split.

Using \(\mathbb C^3\) would distinguish all three families separately and
introduce a larger \(U(1)^2\) relative gauge sector. Nothing in the current
branch requires that enlargement.

## 2. Unimodularity produces the \(A_2\) weight

Write a family-algebra unitary as

\[
u(a,b)=e^{ia}P_\alpha+e^{ib}Q_\alpha.
\]

On the three-dimensional family space,

\[
\det u=e^{i(a+2b)}.
\]

The connected determinant-one subgroup therefore obeys

\[
a+2b=0.
\]

Its primitive Lie-algebra generator can be chosen as

\[
X_{\rm fam}
=-2P_\alpha+Q_\alpha
=I-3P_\alpha.
\]

In the mass basis with \(P_e=\operatorname{diag}(1,0,0)\), for example,

\[
X_{\rm fam}
=\operatorname{diag}(-2,1,1).
\]

These are precisely the traceless family augmentations found in the
continuous anomaly solution. Cycling the chosen rank-one projector gives the
three \(A_2\) vertices.

The factor three is forced by the multiplicities \(1+2\):

\[
q_{\rm exceptional}-q_{\rm pair}=-2-1=-3.
\]

## 3. Represented one-forms

Decompose a finite Dirac operator relative to
\(F=P_\alpha F\oplus Q_\alpha F\):

\[
D_F=
\begin{pmatrix}
D_{PP}&K\\
K^\dagger&D_{QQ}
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

For \(b=(c,d)\in\mathbb C\oplus\mathbb C\),

\[
[D_F,\pi(b)]
=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&(d-c)K\\
(c-d)K^\dagger&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Thus:

- if \(K=0\), the family algebra produces no off-block scalar one-form;
- if \(K\ne0\), \(\Omega^1_{D_F}(\mathcal A_{\rm fam})\) contains the
  \(P\leftrightarrow Q\) edge;
- the two orientations transform with opposite relative charges.

For the unimodular generator,

\[
[X_{\rm fam},K]
=(-2-1)K
=-3K,
\]

and

\[
[X_{\rm fam},K^\dagger]
=+3K^\dagger.
\]

The charge-three flavon is therefore the natural inner fluctuation of the
minimal family-splitting edge.
