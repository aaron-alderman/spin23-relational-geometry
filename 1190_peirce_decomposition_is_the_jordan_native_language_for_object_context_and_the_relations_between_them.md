# Peirce decomposition is the Jordan-native language for the selected object, its context, and the relations between them

## Executive answer

Peirce decomposition is not a new physical hypothesis introduced late in the programme. It is the standard Jordan-algebra decomposition determined by an idempotent projector \(P\). The programme had already been using it under other names:

\[
\text{selected family}
\;\oplus\;
\text{remaining pair}
\;\oplus\;
\text{off-diagonal relations}.
\]

In Jordan-native language these are the Peirce spaces

\[
J_1(P),
\qquad
J_0(P),
\qquad
J_{1/2}(P).
\]

For \(J=\operatorname{Herm}_3(\mathbb C)\), with \(Q=I-P\), they are

\[
\begin{aligned}
J_1(P)&=PJP,\\
J_0(P)&=QJQ,\\
J_{1/2}(P)&=PJQ\oplus QJP
\quad\text{as a real Hermitian relation space}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The angle is that Peirce decomposition separates **things** from the **relations that connect them**. The rank split supplies two diagonal faces, while the half-Peirce sector is the relational carrier between them.

## 1. Why the eigenvalues are \(1,\frac12,0\)

The Jordan product on Hermitian matrices is

\[
A\circ B=\frac12(AB+BA).
\]

Fix an idempotent

\[
P\circ P=P,
\qquad P^2=P.
\]

Consider left Jordan multiplication

\[
L_P(A)=P\circ A.
\]

Relative to \(I=P+Q\), write a Hermitian matrix as

\[
A=
\begin{pmatrix}
a&v^\dagger\\
v&B
\end{pmatrix},
\]

where \(a\in\mathbb R\), \(B\in\operatorname{Herm}_2(\mathbb C)\), and \(v\in\mathbb C^2\). Then

\[
P\circ A
=
\begin{pmatrix}
a&\frac12v^\dagger\\
\frac12v&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Therefore:

- \(PAP\) has eigenvalue \(1\);
- \(QAQ\) has eigenvalue \(0\);
- the off-diagonal relation has eigenvalue \(1/2\).

Thus

\[
J=J_1(P)\oplus J_{1/2}(P)\oplus J_0(P).
\]

The label \(1/2\) is an eigenvalue of Jordan multiplication by \(P\). It is **not** a physical half-charge.

## 2. What the three spaces mean here

### Peirce-one: the selected line

\[
J_1(P)=PJP.
\]

For rank-one \(P\), this is one real diagonal degree of freedom. In the family reading, it corresponds to the singled-out family direction.

### Peirce-zero: the complementary plane

\[
J_0(P)=QJQ.
\]

This is the Jordan algebra living entirely inside the remaining rank-two sector. It contains both the collective identity of that pair and distinctions internal to it.

### Peirce-half: relations across the split

\[
J_{1/2}(P)=PJQ\oplus QJP.
\]

This sector is neither “the one” nor “the two.” It consists of maps connecting them. In matrix language it is off diagonal; in relational language it is the possible mixing, transition, or transport between the selected direction and its complement.

The essential interpretation is therefore

\[
\boxed{
J_1=\text{selected object},
\quad
J_0=\text{context/complement},
\quad
J_{1/2}=\text{relation between them}.
}
\]

## 3. The Peirce multiplication rules

The Jordan product respects the decomposition through the Peirce rules:

\[
\begin{aligned}
J_1\circ J_1&\subseteq J_1,\\
J_0\circ J_0&\subseteq J_0,\\
J_1\circ J_0&=0,\\
J_1\circ J_{1/2}&\subseteq J_{1/2},\\
J_0\circ J_{1/2}&\subseteq J_{1/2},\\
J_{1/2}\circ J_{1/2}&\subseteq J_1\oplus J_0.
\end{aligned}
\]

The final rule is the most important conceptually:

\[
\boxed{
\text{two cross-relations compose into endpoint observables.}
}
\]

A relation from \(P\) to \(Q\), composed with its reverse, produces information internal to \(P\) or \(Q\). This is the Jordan-algebra version of the idea that observable properties arise from closed relational paths.

It also explains why unrestricted associative multiplication is unnecessary. Jordan closure remembers the physically relevant symmetrized return paths without declaring every ordered product an independent observable.

## 4. Peirce-half versus charge \(\pm3\)

Two different operators act on the same relational sector.

Jordan multiplication by \(P\) gives

\[
L_P(A)=P\circ A=\frac12A,
\qquad A\in J_{1/2}(P).
\]

But the commutator derivation generated by

\[
X=I-3P
\]

distinguishes the two orientations:

\[
\begin{aligned}
i[X,A_{PQ}]&\sim-3iA_{PQ},\\
i[X,A_{QP}]&\sim+3iA_{QP}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\text{Jordan/Peirce view:}&\quad\text{one unoriented real relation space }J_{1/2},\\
\text{Lie/module view:}&\quad\text{two oriented complex relation spaces of charges }\pm3.
\end{aligned}
}
\]

This distinction explains several previous results:

- the Jordan algebra sees that a relation exists;
- the commutator sees its direction;
- the fundamental module retains its central phase;
- the bare Jordan automorphism quotient loses the residual \(\mathbb Z_3\);
- an additional orientation/lift is required for the physical carrier.

Peirce decomposition is therefore where the Jordan and gauge descriptions meet without becoming identical.

## 5. Why it appeared late

Earlier stages treated \(P\) mainly as a fixed matrix selecting one family. In that setting, ordinary block notation was sufficient:

\[
\begin{pmatrix}
1&0\\
0&2
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Peirce language became necessary only after three developments:

1. the programme committed to a genuinely Jordan, rather than merely matrix, interpretation;
2. the off-diagonal blocks became candidate physical relational carriers rather than disposable mixing terms;
3. the projector was allowed to move, \(P=P(x)\).

Once \(P\) moves,

\[
D_{\mathcal A}P
\in
J_{1/2}(P)\otimes\Omega^1(M).
\]

The half-Peirce space is then literally the tangent space to the orbit of possible projectors:

\[
T_P\mathbb{CP}^2
\cong
\operatorname{Hom}(P\mathbb C^3,Q\mathbb C^3).
\]

At that point “off-diagonal block” understates the geometry. Peirce decomposition identifies it as the infinitesimal space of possible changes of selection.

## 7. What it may physically correspond to

Several readings remain open and must not be collapsed prematurely.

### Family mixing

The most direct reading identifies the half-Peirce sector with operators connecting the exceptional family to the other two. Yukawa entries across the \(1\oplus2\) split live in this location and carry the relative charge three.

### A charge-three link or flavon

A field transforming in \(\operatorname{Hom}(L,Q)\) has relative weight three. Such a field can serve as the link whose expectation value breaks the continuous rank-split symmetry and populates cross-family mass terms.

This does not mean every Peirce-half element is the physical flavon. It identifies the representation in which such a link naturally lives.

### Goldstone or coset motion

If the projector is a dynamical order parameter with vacuum orbit \(\mathbb{CP}^2\), the Peirce-half directions are tangent to that orbit and therefore look like Goldstone directions before explicit breaking or gauging.

This creates a phenomenological hazard: an unconstrained dynamical projector produces unwanted light modes.

### Second fundamental form

For a connection on the ambient rank-three bundle, \(DP\) measures the failure of the selected line-plus-plane splitting to be covariantly constant. It is the bundle analogue of a second fundamental form. Quadratic combinations

\[
P\,DP\wedge DP
\]

contribute to the induced curvature of the selected line. This is the route by which Peirce relations generated the exact factor three in the preceding calculation.

### Relational carrier

At the most abstract level, the half-Peirce sector is the candidate carrier of “between-ness”: it records how two otherwise orthogonal faces can influence one another without being identified.

## 8. What Peirce does not yet solve

Peirce decomposition is canonical once \(P\) is chosen, but it does not explain:

- why that \(P\) is selected;
- whether \(P\) is fundamental, composite, or emergent;
- why the associated relative phase becomes locally gauged;
- how unwanted projector Goldstones become massive;
- why the central \(\mathbb Z_3\) remains faithful on matter;
- whether the Berry connection has the independent flat sectors required by BF theory;
- which orientation of the \(\pm3\) relation is physically realized.

It organizes the gap. It does not close it.

## 9. Why it matters to the “one carrier, two faces, three relationships” intuition

Peirce decomposition gives a precise version of the intuition:

\[
\underbrace{J_1}_{\text{one face}}
\quad\oplus\quad
\underbrace{J_0}_{\text{other face}}
\quad\oplus\quad
\underbrace{J_{1/2}}_{\text{relation}}.
\]

But “three” here should not be understood merely as three boxes. The richer ternary structure comes from the multiplication rule

\[
J_{1/2}\circ J_{1/2}
\subseteq
J_1\oplus J_0.
\]

A relation leaves one face, traverses the middle, and returns as an observable on a face. The endpoints and the passage form a closed compositional unit.

The two oriented passages carry \(\pm3\), while their Jordan symmetrization is the unoriented Peirce-half observable. This is the exact place where:

- two faces;
- directed relation;
- ternary/Jordan composition;
- charge three;
- and curvature from closed relational paths

meet.

## 10. Shortest formulation

Peirce decomposition answers:

> Once an idempotent has selected “this” from “the rest,” what kinds of observables and relations become possible?

Its answer is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{this},
\qquad
\text{the rest},
\qquad
\text{the relations crossing between them}.
}
\]

In this programme:

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
P&=\text{selection},\\
J_1\oplus J_0&=\text{the two faces created by selection},\\
J_{1/2}&=\text{the relational carrier},\\
\pm3&=\text{the two orientations of that carrier},\\
DP&=\text{its motion through spacetime},\\
PDP\wedge DP&=\text{curvature generated by relational motion}.
\end{aligned}
}
\]

Peirce arrived late as terminology, but the structure has been present from the moment the programme wrote \(I=P+Q\).
