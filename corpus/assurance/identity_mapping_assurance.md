# Identity mapping assurance

Status: an engineering assurance method and executable website report. This is not a scientific claim or an aesthetic certification.

The [identity assurance page](https://aaronalderman.com/assurance/identities/) exposes the gap between the homepage's intended identity relationships and its implemented connections. It is the first worked example in the [assurance topic index](https://aaronalderman.com/assurance/), alongside independent accessibility and internationalisation sagas described in the [portfolio record](assurance_portfolio.md). Its [machine-readable report](https://aaronalderman.com/assurance/identity-mappings.json) is generated by the same process algebra as the publication.

The declared scope is the Sourcerer, Author, Dad and Christian homepage identities. The surface inventory covers the opening, navigation, Questions entrance, Threads, Atlas/Map, application catalogue, look and feel, interaction, assurance and footer. Destination article, thread and map internals need separate audits.

## The distinction being preserved

An unchanged destination is an observation. It does not establish that the relationship should be invariant. The initial counterexample is Threads: its artwork changes with identity, while its destination remains shared. The report treats the expected identity-to-thread-context connection as missing.

| Status | Contract |
| --- | --- |
| Intentionally invariant | Explicit scoped policy and reason; not inferred from equal outputs. |
| Identity mapped | Each scoped world has a value, and the runtime projection and matching cross writer are present. |
| Missing | An expected world value or connection is absent, or an invariant policy conflicts with a cross writer. |
| Undecided | The intended relationship is not declared; neither equality nor an attractive image resolves it. |

An identity mapping can legitimately resolve to the same value for multiple identities. Conversely, four different images do not establish that the destination content carries those identities.

## The eight reviewed relationships

| Relationship | Decision | Scope and reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shared tools and perspective changes | Intentionally invariant | Workshop and full-screen reader destinations and explicit exit wording are shared. Local journey returns remain identity-mapped, and role choices enter explicit identity URLs. |
| Sharing metadata | Identity-mapped | Canonical identity URLs describe their own title, description and artwork. The cross updates selected-world metadata and a native link to that world's shareable address. |
| Structural labels and directory | Intentionally invariant | Stable section labels and complete-directory wording orient the reader. Curated headings, introductions, membership and order remain identity-mapped. |
| Purpose colours and braid interaction | Intentionally invariant | Purpose selects the pigment. A shared fine braid and finite hover/focus glisten compose with identity surfaces, with reduced-motion and forced-colour behaviour. |
| Display typography | Identity-mapped | Each world declares a heading font stack. Prose and brand typography remain shared; installed fallback fonts are outside this structural guarantee. |
| Semantic brief, palette and artwork assignment | Identity-mapped | The brief and visual assignments belong to each identity. Their presence and bindings can be verified; semantic or visual fitness still requires human judgment. |
| Common footer | Intentionally invariant | Signature, personal statement and external/stewardship destinations are shared within each page family. Compact Map layout and identity-specific return URLs are separately scoped. |
| Questions article | Identity-mapped | The leading question, article, illustration, daylight palette and reflection destinations belong to the selected identity. |

These are scoped editorial decisions, not deductions from equal outputs. The algebra reads generated HTML and scoped CSS and retains missing fragments. Declared intent, structural verification and human semantic review remain distinct fields in the report. A declaration without complete evidence for all four identities stays missing.

The Author Questions mismatch was a real counterexample: its active Questions palette and illustration were lavender while its larger world was evergreen and parchment gold. The active REL palette now uses sage daylight surfaces and forest-green actions, and the hero selects the existing evergreen manuscript illustration. Questions and catalogue surfaces consume the same identity palette; the stylesheet version follows the common publication token. Daylight eyebrow text consumes the darker Questions accent rather than the pale accent intended for dark pages. Original image-generation receipts remain historical provenance, while active colours and artwork assignments are owned by REL facts and checked separately.

The decision evidence is published as `assurance/identity-decisions.json`. Negative checks reintroduce the lavender paper token or predecessor illustration, replace a contextual Questions canonical URL with its legacy alias, remove a world's evidence and contradict the declared decision type. These must produce failed verification rather than a green status. Resolving the semantic mapping does not discharge the report's human-review obligation.

## Threads and Map journeys

Each identity has explicit, shareable routes under `/worlds/{identity}/`: a homepage, three authored reading threads, a Map with identity-specific framing and highlighted recommendations, a Questions destination, and contextual versions of all forty readings. The perspective travels in ordinary links, without relying on browser storage. The cross updates both entrance and header navigation.

The four Maps deliberately share the original forty-place terrain and coordinates. Each has its own introductory artwork, copy and recommended places. Reading text, sources and underlying visited-place identities remain shared; identity adds a reading prompt and return navigation. Marked links offer an explicit exit to the shared corpus or shared reading.

The algebra-owned `identity.journey.rebase` query resolves internal URLs through the existing generic URL mechanism and preserves query strings, fragments and external destinations. `identity.journey.check` checks generated destination bytes. `assurance/identity-journeys.json` records content, artwork, links, homepage/Questions returns and all forty contextual readings for each identity. The Threads and Map assurance rows require that downstream evidence as well as their source fields and cross writers. A wired entrance alone cannot close these two rows.

## Discovery beyond the declared inventory

The algebra-owned `identity.discovery.scan`, `identity.discovery.reconcile` and `identity.discovery.build` queries scan the entire generated `index.html` and every `IdentityTheme.homeRoute`. The input documents are derived from publication facts, not an element inventory. Head metadata, header, nested catalogue output, text, images, links, attributes and footer are observed. Every nonempty text fragment and every attribute receives a document-relative token ordinal, element label, property and rendered value in `assurance/identity-discovery.json`. Duplicate attributes remain separate observations. IDs on otherwise empty elements are observed too; text following a void element cannot borrow its declaration. Whitespace, comments and doctype are excluded.

Reconciliation only associates an exact element selector and property with an existing declaration. `declared-reference` means that reference exists; it is not an additional proof of the relationship's correctness. A declaration for a container's HTML does not classify its descendants. Repeated or equal values stay undecided without a precise reference. The report groups undeclared observations into text, navigation, media, appearance, other attributes and opaque dependencies, preserving every observation in the downloadable evidence. A group is a review unit, not one independent confirmation per member.

This discovers new relationships without adding manual inventory rows first. It also exposes a real residual gap: broad policies for catalogue, footer or appearance do not supply precise declarations for all their output. Adding discovery therefore reopens undecided work rather than preserving a cosmetic zero count. Review each group, discern intended variation, add scoped declarations and verification, and rebuild. Do not clear a group through a blanket ancestor or subtree exemption.

The integrated discovery release records 4,281 observations over five complete homepage documents: 179 exact declaration references and 4,102 undeclared observations in six review groups. The existing inventory remains 13 intentionally invariant and 62 identity-mapped relationships; the six discovered groups reopen the undecided status. The complete suite passes 194 tests and presentation checks. Build execution is 19,312,157 of the unchanged 20,000,000-step limit. These are same-run engineering receipts, not independent confirmations. [The release receipt](../../applications/aaronalderman-hierarchy/verification/identity-discovery-release.json) binds source and generated-artifact hashes, counts and verification boundaries.

Concurrent household work supplied a concrete counterexample: the new “Households over time” link appeared automatically as href, class and text observations in each of the five homepage documents. No inventory row was added to discover it. Those fifteen observations represent one source change, not fifteen independent confirmations. The catalogue's original declared serialization path is preserved. Other publication renderers share a guarded escape specialization of the common HTML grammar, with punctuation, unsafe-attribute and unsafe-URL equivalence checks; structured stylesheet fallback laws retain identical output hashes. These optimizations stay in the algebra and do not raise budgets or change native mechanisms.

The scanner reads generated HTML bytes, not a browser DOM or arbitrary HTML language. Token ordinals are evidence addresses for that rendering, not persistent DOM paths. The five homepage documents are scanned completely; linked corpus destinations, external CSS or script internals, runtime-only states, frames and shadow DOM are outside this discovery pass. Inline script/style bodies are opaque, and their presence remains visible. Existing destination and decision checks retain their narrower contracts. Semantic and visual fitness still requires human review.

## Authority and reproduction

Canonical declarations are `IdentityAssuranceMapping` facts in [source/hierarchy.rel](../../applications/aaronalderman-hierarchy/source/hierarchy.rel). The `identity.assurance.classify` and `identity.assurance.build` queries in [algebra/publication.process.json](../../applications/aaronalderman-hierarchy/algebra/publication.process.json) own classification and report composition. The application catalogue is [source/hierarchy.json](../../applications/aaronalderman-hierarchy/source/hierarchy.json).

From `applications/aaronalderman-hierarchy`:

```text
node bootstrap/run.cjs . dist bootstrap/site.json
node --test hierarchy.test.mjs
```

The build writes `dist/assurance/index.html` and `dist/assurance/identity-mappings.json`. The page links from the shared footer and appears in the sitemap. No browser application logic or native classification mechanism is added.

The classifier checks authored values across the scoped identities, runtime field projections and matching top-level browser writers in `identity.change`. CSS rows check the declared identity variable values and body identity writer. New unregistered cross writers become undecided rows. Every catalogue entry automatically receives a relevance row, resolved from its curated memberships and identity-specific card notes. Unselected projects remain accessible through the shared full directory; an omitted curated project is not inferred to be irrelevant in general.

Collection rows cover each identity's headings, introductions, artwork, selection and ordering. Their evidence includes `IdentityCollection` facts, the `identity.catalogue.view` and HTML serialization calls, the runtime theme record, and the cross's content writer. This is bounded pipeline evidence rather than a proof that each fact reaches every intended descendant. The assurance work preserved the concurrently published personalized collections and name-mark changes rather than reporting against an older homepage.

Negative checks remove world values, worlds, data bindings and browser writers; substitute a wrong field; duplicate a world; introduce an undeclared writer or conflicting second writer; and conflict an invariant policy with an identity writer. Equal-image fixtures must remain identity mappings rather than being promoted to invariants.

## Assurance boundary and change condition

The inventory is declared, supplemented by automatic lexical discovery over complete generated homepage documents. Neither layer is an exhaustive discovery of all DOM states or transitive dependencies. A matching source writer or discovered declaration reference is bounded structural evidence, not proof of browser execution, reachable destination content, visual correctness or semantic coherence. Invariant rows record policy with reasons; they are not mathematical proofs over descendants. Human review remains necessary for semantic aesthetics.

To close a missing row, supply the intended world values and the required connections, then rebuild and verify. To resolve an undecided row, first declare whether variation is intended and why. To strengthen this assurance, extend source discovery, downstream checks and independent browser or semantic evidence without silently changing the meanings of the four statuses.

The ordinary result is a reproducible coverage and open-work report for a multi-perspective website. No conclusion about the physics corpus follows from it.
