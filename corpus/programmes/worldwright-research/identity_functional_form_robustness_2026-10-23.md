# Worldwright identity functional-form robustness

The earlier identity-retention surface established that moving authored identity salience from zero to one changes 87 of 640 intervention classifications. That result did not establish that the model's linear response to intermediate salience was special.

This experiment repeated the matched 640-cell grid under five normalized monotone response laws—linear, quadratic, square-root, normalized logistic and a half-threshold—plus a null control. Each normalized law maps zero to zero and one to one; the null control removes the identity response.

All five normalized forms preserved the 87 endpoint classification changes and produced zero relational or aggregate monotonicity violations. The null control produced zero changes. The intermediate response was highly form-dependent: at authored salience 0.25, the threshold, quadratic, logistic, linear and square-root forms changed respectively 0, 2, 13, 26 and 45 classifications.

The supported conclusion is therefore narrower and stronger. Identity is a retained decision coordinate inside the authored model under a broad family of monotone response laws. The linear coefficient and intermediate dose-response curve are not identified. External observable proxies and held-out comparison remain necessary before the coordinate can claim construct validity.
