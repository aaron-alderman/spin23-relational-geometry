## 1. Unit of analysis

Do not begin with “What personality is this person?” Begin with an observable episode:

- a paragraph;
- a decision;
- a research session;
- a story scene;
- a conflict;
- a response to uncertainty;
- a document or experiment;
- a retelling of an inherited narrative.

Each episode receives:

1. a mode vector;
2. one or more functional archetype assignments;
3. an uncertainty score;
4. evidence for the assignment;
5. its incoming and outgoing transitions;
6. a practical next action.

This avoids turning a context-dependent model into an essence claim.

## 2. The mode vector

Use seven coordinates on ([0,1]):

\[
v=(E,C,F,A,B,T,R).
\]

| Axis | Meaning | Observable indicators |
|---|---|---|
| (E) exploration | opens possibilities | proposes branches, analogies, unfamiliar connections |
| (C) compression | finds concise structure | names invariants, motifs, quotients or common interfaces |
| (F) falsification | tests resistance | asks for no-go results, counterexamples, discriminators |
| (A) application | changes or builds something | creates protocols, experiments, tools, implementations |
| (B) embodiment | attends to lived stakes | invokes practice, qualia, risk, bodily or situated knowledge |
| (T) transmission | preserves knowledge across agents/time | writes syntheses, teaches, publishes, creates reusable memory |
| (R) reflexivity | models its own limits | tracks assumptions, losses, bias, provenance and uncertainty |

These axes are not claimed to be universal psychological factors. They are task-specific coordinates for this programme. Their validity is empirical: they are useful only if they improve prediction and intervention.

## 3. Functional archetypes

Archetypes are defined as prototype regions in the mode space. The labels describe what an episode is doing.

| Archetype | Dominant coordinates | Function | Failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Scout** | high (E) | finds unexplored possibilities | endless branching, analogy inflation |
| **Cartographer** | high (C,R) | organizes the territory and names interfaces | maps without contact or action |
| **Auditor** | high (F,R) | locates false bridges and hidden assumptions | sterile negation, premature closure |
| **Builder** | high (A), moderate (F) | constructs models, tests and interventions | builds arbitrary machinery without provenance |
| **Witness** | high (B,R) | preserves lived or tacit evidence | treats felt force as public proof |
| **Steward** | high (T,R) | maintains memory, provenance and intergenerational continuity | canon formation and frozen doctrine |
| **Translator** | high (C,T) | moves structure between languages and communities | erases differences while smoothing them |
| **Integrator** | balanced (C,A,T) | closes a cycle into a usable synthesis | forces premature unity |

An episode may be a mixture. The point is not to force every move into one box; it is to make the mixture and its consequences inspectable.

## 6. The observed story

The sequence is not merely a list of topics. It is a transformation:

\[
\begin{array}{c}
\text{felt attractor}\\
\downarrow\\
\text{compressive motif}\\
\downarrow\\
\text{typed mathematical conjecture}\\
\downarrow\\
\text{parallel falsification and construction}\\
\downarrow\\
\text{epistemology of the remaining intuition}\\
\downarrow\\
\text{operational mapping}.
\end{array}
\]

This story has a recognizable archetypal structure:

1. **Call:** an unexplained attraction toward (Spin(2,3)) and relational triples;
2. **Differentiation:** multiple branches and mathematical languages appear;
3. **Trial:** exact calculations reject several attractive but untyped connections;
4. **Descent:** the missing bridge cannot be derived internally;
5. **Recovery:** the failure becomes a precise missing object rather than a collapse of the programme;
6. **Return:** the work asks how intuition itself can be retained and operationalized;
7. **Application:** the framework begins governing research decisions.

The intergenerational payload is not “(Spin(2,3)) is the centre.” It is a method:

> Preserve the attraction, multiply its interpretations, demand typed bridges, record no-go results, and transmit both the invariant and the losses.

## 7. Basin map

The programme currently exhibits four recurring basins.

### Basin 1 — Universal interface

**Attractor:** apparently different structures converge on a small carrier such as (H_2(\mathbb R)), a symplectic four-space or (Spin(2,3)).

**Stabilizers:** exact group isomorphisms, Jordan/conformal relationships, phase-space geometry.

**Escape condition:** demonstrate that a proposed connection is only dimensional or analogical and lacks a shared action.

### Basin 2 — One/two/three relational compression

**Attractor:** one carrier differentiates into two faces linked by three relations or a ternary product.

**Stabilizers:** Peirce decomposition, ternary composition, narrative economy and binary contrast with mediation.

**Escape condition:** find a domain in which the motif predicts no invariant or admits incompatible realizations equally well.

### Basin 3 — Missing intertwiner

**Attractor:** separate structures resemble one another but lack a canonical typed map.

**Stabilizers:** repeated commutant and orbit calculations; the unresolved (\mathbb{CP}^2) orientation.

**Escape condition:** construct a provenance-bearing target action and protected non-scalar Gram, or prove every admissible Gram is scalar.

### Basin 4 — From intuition to audit to preservation

**Attractor:** intuition opens a branch, formal work narrows it, and the surviving residue is preserved rather than erased.

**Stabilizers:** the programme's explicit epistemic labels—established, conditional, no-go, indeterminate and speculative.

**Escape condition:** demonstrate that the process either suppresses genuine novelty or repeatedly retains unfalsifiable ideas.

## 8. Practical application: a research-state dashboard

For every new claim or intuition, record:

| Field | Required content |
|---|---|
| episode | smallest meaningful research move |
| evidence | exact text, calculation, observation or experience |
| mode vector | ((E,C,F,A,B,T,R)) with confidence intervals |
| archetypal function | primary and secondary functions |
| current basin | which recurring research attractor is active |
| transition | previous and proposed next function |
| legible content | what is already explicit and testable |
| illegible residue | what remains felt, embodied or unformalized |
| compression loss | what the current representation discards |
| falsifier | cheapest observation that would change the map |
| action | next concrete calculation, experiment or elicitation |
| disposition | retain, test, pin, close or transmit |

This converts the framework into a decision instrument.

## 9. Intervention rules

The map matters only if it changes action.

### Scout saturation

If (E) remains high for several episodes while (F) and (A) remain low:

**Intervention:** freeze branching temporarily; select one typed bridge and one cheap falsifier.

### Cartographer saturation

If (C) is high but no observable or implementation follows:

**Intervention:** require an input, transformation, output and failure criterion.

### Auditor saturation

If (F) dominates and all branches are being closed:

**Intervention:** preserve the first missing datum and ask for the smallest constructive model that supplies it.

### Builder without provenance

If (A) is high but (R) is low:

**Intervention:** audit which coefficients, labels and orientations were inserted by hand.

### Witness without test

If (B) is high but no contrasting case exists:

**Intervention:** collect phenomenologically similar cases that evoke different judgments.

### Steward saturation

If (T) is high but the corpus no longer changes decisions:

**Intervention:** compress the archive into live questions, stop conditions and executable tests.

## 10. Prediction and validation

The pilot makes prospective predictions.

### Prediction 1

When the programme enters a Scout/Cartographer loop, the next productive move will require an Auditor or Builder intervention rather than another synthesis.

### Prediction 2

The “missing intertwiner” basin will recur until a target action is supplied; renaming carriers will not remove it.

### Prediction 3

Documents that combine high (F,A,R) will produce the most durable frontier advances because they test, construct and preserve assumptions simultaneously.

### Prediction 4

The one/two/three motif will continue to generate hypotheses across domains, but only a subset will survive the shared-action test.

### Prediction 5

An effective intergenerational account of the programme will preserve the transition method more reliably than the full sequence of technical details.

Test these against the next 20 substantial research episodes. Score vectors before outcomes are known. Then measure:

- agreement between independent annotators;
- whether archetype assignment predicts the next successful move;
- whether interventions shorten unproductive loops;
- whether the basin labels remain stable under paraphrase;
- and whether simpler models predict equally well.

If the map does not improve prospective decisions, discard or revise it.

## 11. LLM implementation

An LLM can support the dashboard, but it must not be its sole authority.

### LLM responsibilities

- segment the stream into candidate episodes;
- propose vectors with textual evidence;
- offer two alternative mappings;
- detect repeated basins and stalled transitions;
- generate falsifiers and next actions;
- preserve minority interpretations;
- compare prospective predictions with later outcomes.

### Human responsibilities

- correct what the model could not feel or observe;
- identify stakes and context absent from the record;
- reject psychologically invasive or essence-like labels;
- choose which intervention is acceptable;
- determine whether the map is useful.

### Required output discipline

Every model-generated assignment should include:

\[
(\text{vector},\text{confidence},\text{evidence},
\text{alternative},\text{falsifier}).
\]

A naked personality label is not an acceptable output.
