## Executive result

There is no single attention system, will centre, ignition point or consciousness
region.  The useful unit is a typed transition implemented by a distributed,
recurrent network.

McGilchrist's five conventional attention labels can be made more precise, but
they should not be treated as five isolated modules:

1. **alertness** -- reaching or maintaining readiness to respond;
2. **sustained attention** -- preserving task-relevant processing over time;
3. **vigilance** -- monitoring for infrequent or unpredictable relevant events;
4. **focused attention** -- preferentially processing one target or stream while
   suppressing competitors;
5. **divided attention** -- coordinating more than one task or stream under a
   finite-capacity bottleneck.

McGilchrist explicitly presents these as distinctions made by conventional
neuropsychology rather than as five categories invented by him; his own
compression emphasizes the contrast between narrow committed focus and broad
uncommitted vigilance
([McGilchrist interview, 2012](https://www.wpr.org/shows/to-the-best-of-our-knowledge/iain-mcgilchrist-on-the-master-and-his-emissary)).

The first three primarily vary the **intensity and temporal continuity** of
attention.  The last two primarily vary its **selectivity and allocation**.
McGilchrist's more important distinction between broad/open/uncommitted and
narrow/targeted/manipulative attention is therefore not simply a sixth item.  It
describes a higher-order **attentional stance** assembled from arousal,
selection, spatial scope, task set, expectation and readiness to reorient.

The defensible hemispheric result is graded rather than absolute.  Right-sided
frontoparietal systems make particularly important contributions to alerting,
target detection and stimulus-driven reorienting, while bilateral dorsal
frontoparietal systems support goal-directed orienting.  Focus, division and
executive control recruit bilateral networks, even where a task or component is
lateralized.  “The right hemisphere is broad and the left is narrow” is a useful
phenomenological compression, not a sufficient anatomical model.

## 1. Rules of the atlas

Every entry separates seven things:

\[
\boxed{
\text{experience}
\neq
\text{operation}
\neq
\text{network}
\neq
\text{region}
\neq
\text{measurement}
\neq
\text{cause}
\neq
\text{interpretation}
}
\]

A region appearing in an fMRI contrast does not mean that the region uniquely
performs the named function.  A stimulation effect does not mean that normal
function is localized to the stimulated tissue: stimulation recruits fibres and
connected networks.  A lesion supplies causal evidence but may disrupt remote
systems.  Anatomical labels also vary: territory called **dACC** in one
literature substantially overlaps territory called **aMCC** in another.

Evidence is graded as:

- **E0 -- interpretive:** phenomenology, analogy or untested localization;
- **E1 -- correlational:** task activation, connectivity or individual
  differences;
- **E2 -- convergent:** replicated modalities, lesion association or
  cross-species convergence;
- **E3 -- causal:** focal lesion, stimulation or controlled perturbation with a
  specific behavioural effect;
- **E4 -- discriminating:** preregistered, independently replicated causal or
  adversarial evidence that defeats named alternatives.

No present atlas entry licenses an E4 claim that one network is *the* substrate
of will, attention or consciousness.

## 2. McGilchrist's five, made operational

| Label | Operational question | Candidate systems and nodes | Main discriminator | Present precision |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| alertness | Is the organism ready to process and respond *now*? | ascending brainstem arousal systems; locus coeruleus--noradrenaline; thalamus; basal forebrain; right frontal and parietal cortex | tonic readiness versus cue-triggered phasic gain | reasonably separable from orienting, but entangled with arousal and motor readiness |
| sustained attention | Can a selected task set and performance level be maintained across time? | cingulo-opercular network; aMCC/dACC--preSMA; anterior insula/frontal operculum; dorsal attention network; LC | time-on-task stability and lapse structure | distributed control state, not a single vigilance centre |
| vigilance | Can an unexpected, infrequent but relevant event be detected? | right-dominant ventral attention network; TPJ; inferior/middle frontal cortex; LC; thalamus; interaction with dorsal attention network | rare-target detection after controlling general arousal | overlaps sustained attention but adds uncertainty and reorienting |
| focused/selective attention | Which representation receives enhanced processing and which competitors are suppressed? | bilateral dorsal attention network: frontal eye fields and intraparietal/superior parietal cortex; pulvinar; sensory cortex; frontoparietal control | target enhancement and distractor suppression at matched arousal | modality- and feature-specific implementations sit beneath a common selection grammar |
| divided attention | How are multiple task sets or streams scheduled under limited capacity? | bilateral multiple-demand/frontoparietal network; dlPFC; IPS; preSMA/aMCC; basal ganglia; cerebellum | dual-task cost after controlling switching and working memory | often rapid scheduling or multiplexing, not lossless parallel attention |

The classical alerting--orienting--executive framework is a complementary
factorization.  Alerting asks *when* resources are available; orienting asks
*where or to what* they are directed; executive control asks *which rule wins
under conflict*.  Petersen and Posner's updated synthesis identifies brainstem
and right-hemisphere contributions to alerting, parietal systems for orienting,
and medial frontal/anterior cingulate systems for executive control
([Petersen and Posner, 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3413263/)).

Corbetta and colleagues sharpen the orienting distinction: bilateral frontal
eye field--intraparietal systems bias selection, while a right-dominant ventral
frontoparietal network interrupts and resets the current state when an
unexpected relevant event appears
([Corbetta, Patel and Shulman, 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18466742/)).
Direct left--right comparison confirms that the strongest right dominance is
outside the core bilateral dorsal network
([Shulman et al., 2010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20219998/)).

|---|---|---|---|

| salience | estimate that an internal or external change merits resource reallocation | anterior insula, aMCC/dACC, amygdala, striatum, neuromodulatory systems | attention or subjective importance as a whole |
| reorienting | interrupt the current allocation and redirect processing | right TPJ/inferior frontal ventral attention network interacting with bilateral dorsal attention network | sustained task maintenance |
| task-set maintenance | keep rules and goals active across an extended episode | cingulo-opercular network, lateral PFC, preSMA/aMCC, basal ganglia | selection of each individual percept |
| executive allocation | estimate whether, where and how much control is worth deploying | aMCC/dACC, anterior insula, multiple-demand network, striatum and valuation systems | an unconstrained inner commander |
| conscious access / ignition | nonlinear amplification and widespread availability of selected information | recurrent posterior processing plus candidate frontoparietal and thalamocortical broadcast mechanisms | settled proof that prefrontal ignition is necessary for experience |
| metacognition | estimate the reliability of a perception, memory, decision or policy | domain-specific combinations of anterior PFC, dlPFC, medial PFC/pgACC, anterior insula and evidence-generating systems | one global confidence meter |
| motivational commitment / “will” | keep expected value operative despite effort, uncertainty or negative affect | aMCC-centred network with anterior insula, vmPFC/OFC, striatum, preSMA/SMA, hypothalamic and brainstem effectors | a unitary willpower substance |

| self-model and autobiographical continuity | relate present state to body, person, others and remembered narrative | medial PFC, PCC/precuneus, hippocampal memory systems, TPJ and distributed default-network subsystems | a single anatomical self-location |
| retention / transformation | change future policy, valuation or model after an outcome | hippocampal and cortical consolidation, basal-ganglia reinforcement learning, cerebellar adaptation, neuromodulation and sleep-dependent plasticity | temporary control deployment or insight |

## 4. The aMCC join: “movement of will”

The aMCC is best placed between evaluation and embodied control, not above the
rest of the brain as a sovereign executive.

Direct aMCC stimulation in two patients produced autonomic mobilization,
expectation of a challenge and a determination to overcome it
([Parvizi et al., 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24316296/)).  In a much
larger retrospective map of 1,789 cingulate stimulation sites in 329 epilepsy
patients, aMCC stimulation evoked integrated goal-oriented behaviours.  The
responses had an “actotopic” organization: body-directed actions, actions in
nearby space, and whole-body actions toward extrapersonal space occupied
different zones
([Caruana et al., 2018](https://academic.oup.com/brain/article/141/10/3035/5072015)).

Human midcingulate patterns also encode the identity and progress of extended
goal-directed sequences
([Holroyd et al., 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29866834/)), while
human medial-frontal single neurons encode errors and error history associated
with later behavioural adjustment
([Fu et al., 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30528064/)).

The candidate operation is therefore:

\[
\Gamma_t=
f(\text{goal value},\text{effort},\text{error},\text{threat/pain},
\text{body state},\text{affordance},\text{task progress}),
\]

\[
\Gamma_t\longrightarrow
(\text{control gain},\text{autonomic mobilization},
\text{action switch},\text{persistence or withdrawal}).
\]

This makes “movement of will” unusually literal: a goal becomes effective only
when it can recruit bodily state, action geometry and an executable policy.  The
aMCC is a candidate coupling node in that transition.  It does not by itself
supply the goal, determine its moral worth, generate all conscious experience,
or retain the resulting transformation.

## 5. Ignition is a typed hypothesis, not a synonym for consciousness

Global Neuronal Workspace models use **ignition** for nonlinear recurrent
amplification that makes selected information widely available.  Local sensory
recurrence, attentional selection, report preparation and conscious access must
nevertheless remain separately measurable.

The 2025 preregistered adversarial comparison of GNWT and IIT found conscious
content information in posterior and prefrontal regions, stronger orientation
information posteriorly, and challenges to key temporal predictions of both
theories.  It did not establish one anatomical ignition switch
([Cogitate Consortium et al., 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-08888-1)).

Arousal is also distinct from access.  Central-lateral thalamic stimulation can
restore wake-like cortical dynamics in anaesthetized macaques, supporting a
causal thalamocortical contribution to conscious *capacity*, not a complete
account of conscious *content*
([Redinbaugh et al., 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32053769/)).

## 6. Corpus compilation

The corpus already uses

\[
\begin{aligned}
y_t&=\mathcal O(x_t),\\
m_{t+1}&=G(m_t,y_t,u_t),\\
s_{t+1}&=H(s_t,m_t,y_t,\varepsilon_t),\\
u_t&=\pi(m_t,s_t),\\
x_{t+1}&=F(x_t,u_t).
\end{aligned}
\]

The atlas refines this without assigning one variable to one structure:

\[
\begin{aligned}
a_t&=\text{arousal/readiness},\\
q_t&=\text{selection and attentional scope},\\
i_t&=\text{access/availability state},\\
c_t&=\text{control-allocation gain},\\
u_t&=\text{selected embodied action},\\
g_t&=\text{agency attribution},\\
r_t&=\text{retained update}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The resulting recurrent process is

\[
(x,m,s,a,q,i,c,u,g,r)_t
\longrightarrow
(x,m,s,a,q,i,c,u,g,r)_{t+1}.
\]

It should not be read as a serial pipeline.  The arrows are recurrent and
partly parallel.  Its purpose is experimental separation: a subject may be
awake without access to one stimulus, attend without later agency attribution,
recognize error without allocating control, act without retaining change, or
retain a habit without conscious access to its update rule.

This sharpens three existing corpus results:

1. [recognition is not transformation](../orientation/orientation_architecture.md#11-recognition-is-not-transformation);
2. [selective interruptibility](../orientation/orientation_architecture.md#12-interruptibility-and-recursive-agency) requires reorienting plus control and action access, not unlimited reopening;
3. the [reflexive model](../../claims/1100-1199/1144_consciousness_is_a_live_organizational_spike_but_not_yet_the_missing_physical_coupling.md) requires a model, a model of reliability/control, a policy and retained update, none of which should be identified with consciousness merely because conscious humans exhibit them together.

## 7. Evidence discriminators without new subjects

These are **analysis designs**, not proposals to recruit or intervene on people.
They can be pursued through published results, ethically released open data,
existing clinical or lesion evidence, computational models and cross-study
triangulation.  Any later use of human data remains governed by the consent,
privacy and reuse conditions under which those data already exist.

### A. Broad versus narrow attention

Search existing studies and open task data for independent variation in spatial
scope and target predictability at comparable arousal, sensory and motor load.
Compare dorsal/ventral attention-network coupling, pupil-linked arousal,
unexpected-event detection and target precision.  A hemisphere-wide story
fails if a specific right ventral reorienting network interacting with
bilateral dorsal selection explains the evidence better.

### B. Recognition-to-enactment gate

Join existing error-monitoring, effort, autonomic, lesion and stimulation
records that separately identify error detection, policy report, control
allocation, bodily mobilization, action initiation and later retention.  The
proposed aMCC join predicts the strongest convergent effect on allocation and
mobilization, not on the existence of the represented correction or memory
consolidation itself.

### C. Attention versus ignition

Reanalyse or meta-analyse datasets that already cross stimulus strength, task
relevance, report requirement and attentional allocation.  Preserve posterior
content decoding, frontoparietal availability, thalamocortical state and delayed
report as separate records.  No late signal counts as consciousness-specific
if task, report or memory demand explains it.

### D. Will versus agency

Use existing paradigms in which intention formation, motor execution, outcome
congruence and explicit authorship judgment vary independently.  The atlas
predicts partially separable intentionality and agency networks; a single
“free-will signal” should not survive a joined analysis.

## 8. Stop conditions

- Stop any claim that a complex function belongs exclusively to one region or
  hemisphere when only correlational activation supports it.
- Stop using **attention**, **awareness**, **access**, **report**, **working
  memory**, **will**, **action** and **agency** as interchangeable labels.
- Stop if an aMCC-labelled variable merely renames effort, pain, conflict or
  movement without predicting their dissociation.
- Stop if “ignition” is inferred solely from a late report-related component.
- Stop if a phenomenological distinction cannot be translated into at least one
  controlled behavioural or perturbational contrast.
- Preserve negative results: failure of one neural implementation does not erase
  the experienced distinction, but it prevents anatomical promotion.

## Current verdict

The most illuminating provisional architecture is not a list of brain centres.
It is a set of typed, recurrent transformations:

\[
\boxed{
\text{capacity}
\leftrightarrow
\text{selection}
\leftrightarrow
\text{access}
\leftrightarrow
\text{evaluation}
\leftrightarrow
\text{control allocation}
\leftrightarrow
\text{embodied action}
\leftrightarrow
\text{agency}
\leftrightarrow
\text{retention}
}
\]

McGilchrist's broad and narrow modes are plausible recurrent regimes across
this architecture.  The aMCC is a particularly interesting candidate for the
control-allocation/enactment join.  Neither result yet identifies the physical
or phenomenal basis of consciousness as a whole.

## 9. MECE completion contract

The preceding feature list is useful but not MECE.  It mixes primitive
operations (selection), composite capacities (will), inferred products (the
self-model), dynamical signatures (ignition), output channels (report) and
phenomenology (experienced content).  A complete map must type those things
before associating them with anatomy.

Strict MECE anatomy is impossible and undesirable: the same insula, thalamic,
cingulate or frontoparietal tissue can participate in several operations.  The
MECE claim therefore applies only at a declared **functional grain**:

> A primitive operator is classified by the new output type it produces from
> its immediate inputs.  A named capacity is assigned to exactly one primary
> operator, may depend on other operators, and may carry any number of declared
> overlays.

At this grain, the atlas is complete when every proposed mental or behavioural
feature can be represented as one of:

1. one primitive operator;
2. a composition of primitive operators;
3. an overlay specifying target, timescale, modality, stance or output channel;
4. an unresolved phenomenal or ontological residue; or
5. an implementation detail below the selected grain.

“Complete” consequently means **closed under description at this operational
grain**, not “the brain is solved.”  Any feature that fits none of these five
classes is a witnessed hole and forces a new atlas version.

## 10. Closed operational basis

The minimal present basis contains fifteen typed operators.  They are not a
serial staircase: they form a recurrent control loop, and several may run in
parallel.

| ID | Primitive operation | Typed output | Candidate implementation | Ground measurement |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| O01 | regulate capacity and viability | viable readiness state | brainstem arousal systems, hypothalamus, basal forebrain, central thalamus, autonomic and endocrine effectors | sleep/wake state, responsiveness, EEG, pupil, ECG, respiration, perturbational complexity |
| O02 | transduce external and internal change | modality-specific evidence | sensory receptors and pathways, spinal/vagal interoception, thalamic relays, primary sensory cortex | thresholds, evoked responses, sensory lesions and masking |
| O03 | estimate hidden state | posterior belief about world, body or agent | sensory and association hierarchies, hippocampal and cerebellar models, insula, default-network subsystems | psychometric functions, decoding, prediction error, illusion and generalization |
| O04 | select and set precision/scope | routing weights over evidence or representations | dorsal attention network, pulvinar, sensory gain systems, basal-ganglia and prefrontal gating | cueing benefit, distractor cost, gaze, pupil and modality-specific gain |
| O05 | make selected content cross-system available | availability token or broadcast state | recurrent posterior processing with candidate frontoparietal and thalamocortical coordination | cross-task generalization, no-report contrasts, delayed use and causal masking/perturbation |
| O06 | actively maintain a state without current input | maintained task or memory state | frontoparietal and sensory working-memory systems, thalamus, basal ganglia | delay accuracy, interference, perturb-and-recover and state decoding |
| O07 | compute relevance and interrupt priority | priority or reset signal | anterior insula/aMCC salience systems, right ventral attention network, amygdala and neuromodulators | surprise-by-relevance factorial, reorienting latency, autonomic response |
| O08 | evaluate state and possible outcome | multidimensional value/cost vector | vmPFC/OFC, striatum, amygdala, insula, hypothalamus and distributed affective systems | revealed preference, effort/risk/delay discounting, affect and physiological cost |
| O09 | form or select target conditions | ranked goal/constraint stack | hippocampal--prefrontal prospective systems, vmPFC/OFC, striatum and hypothalamic need systems | goal choice, devaluation, prospective planning and preference reversal |
| O10 | allocate control | gain, strategy and interruption budget | aMCC/dACC, anterior insula, lateral PFC, multiple-demand network and striatum | demand avoidance, control discounting, conflict adaptation and effort perturbation |
| O11 | select a policy | probability distribution over interventions | basal ganglia, prefrontal and premotor systems, hippocampal planning and cerebellum | choice and reaction-time distributions, exploration, stopping and policy switch |
| O12 | actuate an intervention | motor, autonomic, cognitive or symbolic output | motor/premotor cortex, brainstem/spinal effectors, autonomic systems and language motor networks | EMG, force, kinematics, speech, gaze and visceral response |
| O13 | compare predicted and observed consequence | signed error, progress and credit signal | cerebellum, aMCC/dACC, striatum, sensory comparator systems and neuromodulators | ERN, reward-prediction error, sensory attenuation and post-error adjustment |
| O14 | update online parameters or state | changed model, value or policy parameter | hippocampus, striatum, cerebellum, cortex and neuromodulatory plasticity | trial-wise learning rate, reversal, transfer and causal feedback manipulation |
| O15 | consolidate, reorganize or down-select offline | stabilized or restructured long-term model | hippocampal--neocortical replay, sleep oscillations and systems/synaptic consolidation | next-day retention, transfer, sleep staging and targeted memory reactivation |

The bodily end is essential.  Interoception provides feedback for allostatic
control rather than merely adding “feelings” after cognition
([Sennesh et al., 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34942287/)).  The memory
end is also active: sleep-related replay and oscillatory coupling can stabilize
and reorganize learning rather than simply store a finished result
([Kim and Park, 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40962324/)).

This compilation also prevents two common double counts.  Working memory and
attention overlap anatomically but differ operationally: maintenance preserves
information in the absence of input, while selection changes which information
is preferentially processed
([Oberauer, 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31517246/)).  Metacognition
does not require a new homunculus; it is state estimation redirected toward the
reliability of a first-order process.

Four overlay axes may be attached to any operator without inventing a new
faculty:

- **target:** world, body, current task, self, other person, group or operator;
- **timescale:** milliseconds, episode, day, development or institution;
- **mode:** automatic/deliberate, broad/narrow, tonic/phasic, online/offline;
- **expression:** covert state, motor action, autonomic action or public symbol.

Hemispheric weighting, network membership, oscillation and neurotransmitter are
implementation annotations, not additional operations.  Phenomenal character
is recorded as an unresolved overlay until a discriminating bridge is found.

## 12. Closed-loop model

Let (b_t) be bodily state, (x_t) external state, (h_t) viability/readiness,
(y_t) transduced evidence, (m_t) estimated state, (q_t) routing weights,
(i_t) cross-system availability, (w_t) maintained task state, (p_t)
interrupt priority, (v_t) valuation, (g_t) targets, (c_t) control gain,
π_t policy, (u_t) intervention, ε_t outcome error and θ_t learned
parameters.  A grounded closure is:

\[
\begin{aligned}
h_{t+1},b_{t+1} &= R(h_t,b_t,x_t,u_t), &&\text{O01}\\
y_t &= T(x_t,b_t), &&\text{O02}\\
m_t &= I(y_t,m_{t-1};q_t,\theta_t), &&\text{O03}\\
q_t &= Q(m_t,g_t,p_t,c_t), &&\text{O04}\\
i_t &= A(m_t,q_t,h_t), &&\text{O05}\\
w_{t+1} &= M(w_t,i_t,g_t), &&\text{O06}\\
p_t &= S(m_t,w_t,g_t), &&\text{O07}\\
v_t &= V(m_t,b_t,g_t;\theta_t), &&\text{O08}\\
g_{t+1} &= G(g_t,v_t,m_t,b_t), &&\text{O09}\\
c_t &= C(v_t,g_t,p_t,w_t), &&\text{O10}\\
\pi_t &= P(m_t,w_t,g_t,c_t;\theta_t), &&\text{O11}\\
u_t &\sim \pi_t, &&\text{O12}\\
\varepsilon_t &= E(y_{t+1},m_t,u_t,v_t,g_t), &&\text{O13}\\
\theta_{t+1} &= L(\theta_t,\varepsilon_t,m_t,u_t), &&\text{O14}\\
\bar\theta_{d+1} &= K(\theta_{1:d},\text{offline state}), &&\text{O15}.
\end{aligned}
\]

This is a **model class**, not a claim that each line has one neural home or
that the brain literally evaluates the equations sequentially.  Its value is
identifiability: deleting or merging an operator must change a named output and
therefore a possible experiment.

## 13. Touching ground without experimenting on people

The first ground model should not begin with new subject recruitment or with
fMRI localization.  It should begin by recovering already-existing traces and
asking whether the proposed interfaces can reproduce them.

The admissible evidence ladder is:

1. **published observations and effect estimates**, including null results;
2. **ethically released open datasets**, under their original consent, privacy
   and reuse constraints;
3. **existing lesion, stimulation and clinical records** as literature
   evidence, without creating new interventions;
4. **comparative and naturalistic observations** already collected;
5. **computational reconstruction and ablation**, including synthetic agents;
6. **cross-dataset prediction**, with immutable provenance and no refitting of
   the claimed discriminator.

The central object is not a person-level score.  It is a candidate **transfer
law** between two typed operators.  For each join, recover:

\[
\boxed{
(\text{source output},\text{candidate carrier},\text{translation law},
\text{target input},\text{observable trace},\text{failure mode})
}
\]

A hierarchical state-space model may be fit to already-available records, with
dataset and person effects treated as nuisance structure rather than targets of
classification.  The full operator graph competes against nested models that
delete or merge joins.  A middle is retained only if it improves held-out
prediction, survives dataset shift and preserves the named dissociation.

The relevant ground records may include timestamped task events, accuracy,
reaction time, gaze, pupil, EEG, autonomic measures, movement, confidence,
authorship judgement, sleep and later retention—but only where those measures
already exist under an admissible reuse basis.  Missing modalities remain
explicitly missing; they are not imputed into apparent evidence.

## 14. Coverage and reopening rule

The machine atlas maps every v1 construct and every named feature above to one
primary operator.  Dependencies are many-to-many; primary ownership is not.
The atlas must reopen when:

- a feature cannot compile into primitives plus overlays;
- two primitives remain observationally indistinguishable across all proposed
  interventions;
- one primitive produces two independently perturbable output types;
- a declared ground measure cannot distinguish the target from a dependency;
- anatomy is being used to repair a functional ambiguity; or
- phenomenal status is silently inferred from access, report or self-model.

This is the point at which the atlas touches ground: it is complete enough to
fail, and every failure has a typed place to enter.

## 15. The missing-middle programme

The fifteen operators are endpoints.  The programme's principal unknowns are
the lawful joins between them.  The canonical loop contains fifteen middles:

| Join | Missing middle |
|---|---|
| O01→O02 | how capacity, arousal and bodily state alter the gain and fidelity of incoming evidence |
| O02→O03 | how modality-specific signals become a calibrated estimate of a hidden cause |
| O03→O04 | how a belief, need or uncertainty becomes a precision/scope allocation |
| O04→O05 | what converts preferential processing into cross-system availability |
| O05→O06 | what makes an available content actively maintainable after input disappears |
| O03/O06/O09→O07 | how novelty becomes task- and organism-relevant enough to interrupt |
| O03/O07→O08 | how inferred significance becomes embodied value, cost or valence |
| O01/O03/O08→O09 | how needs and values become a ranked target rather than a feeling |
| O06/O07/O08/O09→O10 | how a target earns a particular amount and kind of control |
| O03/O06/O09/O10→O11 | how beliefs, maintained constraints and control become one policy distribution |
| O11→O12 | how an abstract policy recruits motor, autonomic, cognitive or symbolic effectors |
| O02/O03/O11/O12→O13 | how predicted and observed consequences are aligned and assigned credit |
| O13→O14 | how error and credit change the correct model, value or policy parameter |
| O14→O15 | how labile online change is selected, reorganized, weakened or stabilized offline |
| O15/O02→O03 | how retained structure is reconstructed under a present cue without becoming literal playback |

Four cross-cutting middles deserve separate attention:

- **recognition→enactment:** why an accurately represented correction sometimes
  cannot acquire control over policy and action;
- **selection→access→phenomenality:** which joins are computationally testable
  and where phenomenal status remains underdetermined;
- **intention/efference/outcome→agency:** how causal evidence becomes
  self-attribution rather than merely successful control;
- **broad↔narrow switching:** how scope, precision, commitment and interrupt
  threshold change together without being reduced to hemisphere labels.

Each middle is tracked independently in the
[missing-middle register](brain_function_missing_middle_register_v1.json).
An endpoint association does not close a middle.  Closure requires an explicit
carrier, translation law, observable trace, competing bridge and a failure
condition.

## 16. Relationship-first atlas

The [brain relationship algebra](brain_relationship_algebra_v1.md) and its
[machine-readable atlas](brain_relationship_atlas_v1.json) now make the joins,
not the inventories, the primary model.  They import the operators, familiar
features, candidate systems, measures and middles above as typed nodes, then
record compilation, implementation, grounding and transformation separately.

This prevents several silent collapses.  A candidate brain system is not the
exclusive owner of an operation; a measure is not the construct; report does
not imply access; access does not imply phenomenal status; and an unrecorded
edge is not treated as a negative result.  Genuine joint dependencies remain
hyperedges.  Candidate anatomical systems and measures remain separate
alternatives unless evidence earns a conjunction.

The executable query surface makes each path inspectable and preserves its
source identities.  Projected path searches are explicitly labelled as
exploratory because traversing one source of a hyperedge does not satisfy its
other prerequisites.
