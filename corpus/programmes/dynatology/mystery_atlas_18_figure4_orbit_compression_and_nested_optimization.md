## Result

A high-resolution inspection of Lu and Steinhardt's Figure 4 sharpens the collared choice system without requiring a full reconstruction of the Darb-i Imam panel.

The figure contains three canonical girih-to-Penrose mapping panels:

\[
H\leftrightarrow\text{Penrose patch},
\qquad
B\leftrightarrow\text{Penrose patch},
\qquad
D\leftrightarrow\text{Penrose patch},
\]

for the elongated hexagon, bowtie and decagon. The Penrose patches carry red and blue ribbons whose continuity across Penrose edges is the matching detector. The source attributes the 2, 2 and 10 mapping choices to the breaking of the corresponding girih-tile symmetries.

Consequently, the fourteen choices should not be transcribed as fourteen unrelated drawings. The source-licensed test is whether they are the symmetry orbits of the three displayed canonical mappings.

## 1. Orbit compression

Let the canonical **coarse-side signature tuples** have lengths

\[
|u_H|=6,
\qquad |u_B|=6,
\qquad |u_D|=10.
\]

Each entry of \(u_T\) is itself an ordered micro-word along one parent girih side. The enlarged figure shows Penrose subdivision lines and ribbon crossings meeting coarse sides, so one parent side must not be treated as one atomic edge symbol. The micro-word lengths remain to be measured.

Without equivariance, independently recording every **coarse-side record** of every choice would require

\[
2\cdot6+2\cdot6+10\cdot10=124
\]

coarse-side records. If each choice is generated by the source-declared tile symmetry from one canonical panel, only

\[
6+6+10=22
\]

canonical coarse-side records are required, together with cyclic-shift actions.

This is a conditional compression theorem:

\[
\boxed{
\text{three canonical side-tuples + verified symmetry action}
\Longrightarrow
\text{all fourteen choice side-tuples}.
}
\]

It reduces the number of independently traced parent-side records by 102, or approximately 82.3 percent. This is not yet an 82.3-percent reduction in individual micro-symbols because their multiplicities are uncounted. The condition matters: the rotation-generated tuples must be checked against the figure and edge-sewing behavior rather than assumed from the choice counts alone.

## 2. The alphabet is not yet identified

The figure clearly distinguishes red and blue Penrose ribbons, but colour alone is not necessarily a complete micro-edge label. A coarse-side state is expected to be an ordered word and may also require:

- entry position along the girih edge;
- tangent direction or handedness;
- which Penrose subtile supplies the ribbon segment;
- ordered red/blue phase; and
- orientation under edge reversal.

The original girih strapwork cannot substitute for this alphabet. The primary paper states that every girih edge already carries two decorating lines meeting its midpoint at \(72^\circ\) and \(108^\circ\), ensuring ordinary girih continuity automatically. Figure 4's defect detector instead belongs to the superposed Penrose ribbons.

Thus the next act is symbol discovery from the canonical panels, not assignment of a preconceived binary label.

## 3. The repair exposes a second optimization level

Figure 4F shows one local point-defect patch. The yellow dotted marks identify Penrose edge mismatches. The repair exchanges the positions of a bowtie and a hexagon, producing a defect-free Penrose patch.

This is not merely a new choice \(c_v\) on a fixed girih adjacency graph. It changes local placement. Let

- \(\pi\) denote a count-preserving girih placement or adjacency state;
- \(C(\pi)\) denote its allowed 10/2/2 Penrose mapping assignments; and
- \(E(\pi,c)\) count ribbon mismatches.

The source procedure has the nested form

\[
\boxed{
\min_{\pi\in\mathcal R(n_D,n_H,n_B)}
\;\min_{c\in C(\pi)} E(\pi,c).
}
\]

The inner problem optimizes symmetry-breaking Penrose phases on one fixed girih plan. The outer problem moves through a count-preserving repair fibre when a residual point defect remains.

The bowtie--hexagon exchange leaves the global count vector invariant. Therefore it also leaves every count-only invariant from Mystery Atlases 15 and 16 unchanged while altering the boundary detector. This independently strengthens the factorization no-go.

The girih branch now has the tower

The common statement is not an identification of girih and muqarnas. It is a reusable compiler principle:

## 5. Source-resolution boundary

The primary PDF supports the following visual facts:

- three canonical girih-to-Penrose panels are present;
- red and blue ribbon systems are distinguished;
- one local mismatch patch and its bowtie--hexagon repair are shown; and
- the repair preserves the two tile populations involved.

It does not provide a printed edge-symbol table, labels for all rotated choices or a machine-readable adjacency list for the 3,700-tile panel. Those remain transcription work. The present result deliberately stops before assigning edge symbols from line colour alone.

## Decision

The full historical panel still does not need replication. Figure 4 compresses the immediate problem to three canonical words and one local repair witness. It also shows that admissibility has two discrete layers: phase choice within a fixed plan and count-preserving movement between plans. The boundary alphabet remains unfilled, but its required information and decisive acceptance test are now explicit.
