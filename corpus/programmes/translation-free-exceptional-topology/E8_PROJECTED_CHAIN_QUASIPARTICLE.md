The width-six plateau is carried by a dressed, mobile defect rather than an unexplained collective eigenvalue. In the local eigenbasis, the periodic ground state acquires a small extensive virtual-excitation background. Subtracting that background from the first inversion-odd, reflection-even state leaves 0.995748, 0.995754, 0.995755 and 0.995755 excess local excitation units at widths three through six. The last increment is only (3.7\times10^{-9}). The carrier is therefore almost exactly one added local quantum above the same dressed vacuum.

Projecting onto all seven one-local-excitation flavours gives a Bloch Hamiltonian whose sampled continuum minimum is at (k=0), with energy 0.1816600162 above the product vacuum. The exact projected many-body width-six gap is 0.1826155983, a 0.523% difference. Restricting further to the lowest local flavour changes the band estimate by only (7.2\times10^{-5}). The raw one-excitation-sector weight declines from 98.48% to 96.58% with width because vacuum dressing grows with volume; the background-subtracted excitation count is the size-stable diagnostic.

## What this changes

The flat finite-size gap now has a concrete effective-theory interpretation: an odd one-quantum quasiparticle with a (k=0) band minimum and a small multi-particle dressing cloud. The next useful object is a quasi-local dressed creation operator, not another brute-force width by itself. Its leakage into two-and-higher-excitation sectors supplies the remainder that a controlled Schrieffer-Wolff, Feshbach or finite-size lower-bound argument must bound.

## Claim boundary

The one-particle calculation is variational relative to the product vacuum, while the many-body gap is measured above the dressed vacuum. Their agreement identifies the carrier but is not a variational lower bound on the gap. No thermodynamic gap, full-charge-chain result or rigorous dressing remainder is claimed.
