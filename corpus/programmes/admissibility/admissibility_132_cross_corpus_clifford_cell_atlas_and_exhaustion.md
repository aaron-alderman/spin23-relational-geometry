## The recurring cell

The corpus repeatedly contains the same small real algebraic grammar:

\[
C^2=-1,
\qquad R^2=+1,
\qquad CR=-RC.
\]

It follows that (S=CR) also squares to (+1), and

\[
\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}\{1,C,R,S\}
\cong Cl(1,1)\cong M_2(\mathbb R).
\]

The null combinations of (C) and (R) give two directed square-zero maps.
This cell is therefore the common local alphabet behind rotation, reflection
or boost, and directed shear. What repeats is the algebraic grammar—not yet a
single object travelling unchanged through every branch.

## What the recurrence implies

The repeated cell suggests a reusable compiler:

\[
\text{typed distinction }G
+\text{exchange }J
\longrightarrow
\text{complex direction }I=GJ
\longrightarrow
\text{two null transfers }N_\pm.
\]

This reverses the earlier intuition that the quarter-turn must always be
found first. In several branches, the primitive data are two involutions: a
grading that distinguishes faces and an exchange that relates them. The
quarter-turn is their product, and the dual directions are their null
mixtures.

That is a useful signpost for new corpus work. Search first for:

1. an authored binary grading;
2. an independently authored exchange reversing that grading;
3. a response object showing whether their product stays within one channel
   or exchanges two channels;
4. a detector retaining the direction of the two nilpotent mixtures.

## What it does not imply

The recurrence does not establish a universal aether, a common time
coordinate, gravity, quantum mechanics, consciousness dynamics or a shared
microscopic substrate. (M_2(\mathbb R)) is the universal endomorphism
algebra of a real two-state fibre, so it should recur whenever a typed binary
distinction and its exchange coexist. Physical content begins with the way
that cell is coupled to nodes, fields, boundaries, spectra and records.

Nor does the rank-one cell equal the corpus's full
(Sp(4,\mathbb R)\cong Spin_0(2,3)) structure. It supplies a local
(Spin_0(2,1)) grammar. Reaching the rank-two geometry requires a second
coupled direction, a preserved nondegenerate form, the correct cross-root
relations and a source-selected action.

## Literature boundary

The compact/para-complex anticommuting grammar belongs to established
split-quaternionic and para-hypercomplex geometry; for a geometric treatment
of structures related to split quaternions, see Davidov, Grantcharov and
Mushkarov, [arXiv:1205.2580](https://arxiv.org/abs/1205.2580). This literature
supports the mathematical classification, not the identification of the four
corpus carriers or any physical interpretation of their common local algebra.
