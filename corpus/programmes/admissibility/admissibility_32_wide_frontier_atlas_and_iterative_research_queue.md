## Purpose

The admissibility programme must not become a single-branch search for confirming analogies. Its central object is an evolving zone, so the research process must repeatedly:

1. inventory filled, bounded, excluded and uncharted cells;
2. regroup open gates by measurable object rather than historical vocabulary;
3. rank them by experimental reach, falsifiability and usefulness under a null;
4. execute the highest-value packet;
5. return to the whole map whenever a path becomes underidentified, repetitive or dependent on missing external data.

This document is the first corpus-wide queue built under that rule.

## Ranking rule

Each lane receives integer scores from zero to five:

- **corpus leverage**: how much existing exact work it can test;
- **experimental reach**: proximity to an executable apparatus or computation;
- **falsifiability**: sharpness of a pass/fail discriminator;
- **null utility**: value retained if the distinctive hypothesis fails;
- **bridge risk**: danger that the physical identification is only analogy.

The initial priority score is

\[
Q=L+E+F+U-R.
\]

It is a steering device, not a probability of truth.

## Frontier atlas

### Lane 1 — open-system contractive selection

**Object:** a projector or state return map with a measured fixed point and tangent spectral radius.  
**Corpus anchors:** Lindblad selectors, two-datum no-go repair, AZ-026--028.  
**Reach:** integrated photonics, microwave resonators, trapped atoms and classical coupled-mode networks.  
**Gate:** compare projector-only neutral memory with an independently calibrated second datum and certify \(\rho(D\mathcal R)<1\) from multiple initial states.  
**Null utility:** system identification, robust control and tomography.  
**Principal risk:** engineering a contraction by hand and mistaking it for a derived selector.

### Lane 2 — inverse-problem and detector equivalence classes

**Object:** the quotient of candidate models by the complete detector map.  
**Corpus anchors:** response learning, stress inverse problems, tomography and nuisance-projected power.  
**Reach:** every experimental packet.  
**Gate:** construct adversarially distinct source models that produce identical nominal observables, then add the cheapest channel that separates them.  
**Null utility:** improved calibration and experimental design.  
**Principal risk:** treating parameter precision inside a misspecified model as identifiability.

### Lane 3 — same-carrier thermoelectric--Casimir closure

**Object:** one causal response tensor predicting Hall, Seebeck, Nernst and boundary force or torque.  
**Corpus anchors:** AZ-029--031 and the two-current cone.  
**Reach:** Co2MnGa films are the present lead.  
**Gate:** matched-film broadband tensor closure followed by a preregistered parallel-minus-antiparallel Casimir observable.  
**Null utility:** material spectroscopy, Casimir metrology and noncontact actuation.  
**Principal risk:** unmeasured optical tails and ordinary magnetic backgrounds.

### Lane 4 — two-current positive response and Planckian transport

**Object:** the particle/energy-current spectral matrix \(C_{ab}(\omega)\), its determinant and rank.  
**Corpus anchors:** the exact \(H_2(\mathbb R)_+\) response cone and the audited scalar \(t\)-\(J\) conductivity code.  
**Reach:** a source-code derivation of the physical lattice energy current, followed by matrix FTLM.  
**Gate:** test positivity and the normalized determinant \(\det C/(\operatorname{tr}C)^2\) across temperature, doping, size and frequency.  
**Null utility:** a genuine thermoelectric extension of Planckian-transport numerics.  
**Principal risk:** proxy energy currents can produce artificial rank-one response.

### Lane 5 — phonon, magnon and hybrid-mode laboratories

**Object:** coupled bosonic mode projectors, avoided crossings, damping matrices, Berry curvature and thermal/spin conversion.  
**Corpus anchors:** the phonon conversion library, dynamic-response map and sparse structured-media references.  
**Reach:** acoustic metamaterials, cavity magnomechanics, magnon polarons and spin-Seebeck spectroscopy.  
**Gate:** identify a three- or four-mode carrier in which splitting, damping, holonomy and detector phase can all be independently tuned.  
**Null utility:** sensing, transduction and mode-conversion engineering.  
**Principal risk:** this is the least developed corpus lane and could merely reproduce standard coupled-mode theory.

### Lane 6 — non-Hermitian and Floquet geometry

**Object:** biorthogonal projectors, exceptional degeneracies, driven holonomy and loss-conditioned return.  
**Corpus anchors:** loss Cayley deformation, sideband transport, modulation and AZ-027 polar decomposition.  
**Reach:** photonic, acoustic and microwave networks.  
**Gate:** separately reconstruct coherent holonomy, singular-value contraction and exceptional-point encircling under reversal.  
**Null utility:** robust non-Hermitian control and sensing.  
**Principal risk:** conflating amplification or differential loss with geometric selection.

### Lane 7 — defects, domain walls and index-carrying boundaries

**Object:** a Fredholm or bulk--boundary index with localized modes and a physical selector.  
**Corpus anchors:** index-three shift, quantum Hall witness, Z3 walls, point-defect localizers and boundary junctions.  
**Reach:** topological circuits, photonics, mechanical lattices and magnetic domain walls.  
**Gate:** realize a protected index while independently measuring the basis freedom or selector that the index does not fix.  
**Null utility:** protected channels and defect-state devices.  
**Principal risk:** using a topological count to claim a family basis, orientation or interaction that it cannot select.

### Lane 8 — hydrodynamic stress and multichannel response

**Object:** frequency- and wavevector-resolved stress, diffusion, sound and thermoelectric spectral matrices constrained by Ward identities.  
**Corpus anchors:** local rank-three hydrodynamic map, KMS/Herglotz sewing and stress inverse-problem no-go.  
**Reach:** numerical many-body models, cold atoms and electronic hydrodynamics.  
**Gate:** determine whether a multichannel response explores the interior of the positive cone or remains on a low-rank ray after physical conservation laws are imposed.  
**Null utility:** transport-coefficient inference and hydrodynamic model discrimination.  
**Principal risk:** Ward identities constrain but do not identify the hidden spectrum.

### Lane 9 — quantum-code and synthetic gauge emulators

**Object:** qutrit/two-qutrit phase space, logical holonomy, finite symplectic shadows and source-derived gates.  
**Corpus anchors:** the large finite-code, Clifford and \(Sp(4)\) claim family.  
**Reach:** superconducting qutrits, trapped ions, photonic synthetic dimensions and programmable qudits.  
**Gate:** implement only provenance-bearing local controls and determine which part of the predicted symplectic closure they generate.  
**Null utility:** qudit control and logical-gate compilation.  
**Principal risk:** arbitrary universal control can synthesize the target group without explaining its physical origin.

### Lane 10 — scattering, causality and positivity bounds

**Object:** allowed regions of amplitudes or response functions before a unique microscopic model is chosen.  
**Corpus anchors:** Herglotz/passivity cones, S-matrix caveats, bootstrap and EFT consistency crosswalk.  
**Reach:** analytic bounds and public scattering/response data.  
**Gate:** derive one inequality from contextual admissibility that is stronger than ordinary unitarity, analyticity, causality and positivity.  
**Null utility:** rigorous exclusion of inconsistent models.  
**Principal risk:** renaming standard positivity as a new foundational principle.

### Lane 11 — flavour, neutrino and CP projector geometry

**Object:** basis-invariant masses, projector quadrilaterals, commutator cubics and texture-zero likelihoods.  
**Corpus anchors:** one of the largest exact branches in the ledger.  
**Reach:** global oscillation fits, flavour data and source-derived model building.  
**Gate:** derive a physical path or response connection whose holonomy predicts a measured CP invariant or texture relation without inserting Yukawa matrices.  
**Null utility:** sharper flavour-model exclusions.  
**Principal risk:** high bridge risk and limited experimental intervention.

### Lane 12 — precision gravity and torsion

**Object:** metric, spin connection, torsion modes and rotating-source response.  
**Corpus anchors:** healthy critical pseudoscalar sector, Lense--Thirring nuisance ratio and hadronic/torsion matching closure.  
**Reach:** existing precision-gravity and spin-dependent-force data, but no derived distinctive signal.  
**Gate:** specify a radiatively controlled action, solve a source configuration and predict one cross-ratio or species dependence not absorbed by calibration.  
**Null utility:** constraints on torsion and modified gravity.  
**Principal risk:** enormous nuisance-to-signal ratios and unprotected coefficients.

### Lane 13 — cosmology, defects and vacuum selection

**Object:** domain-wall abundance, baryogenesis, vacuum energy and cosmological evolution.  
**Corpus anchors:** Z3 wall gate, leptogenesis checklist, H0 lane and vacuum-energy obstruction.  
**Reach:** indirect population-level observations.  
**Gate:** construct a covariant model that reaches CMB, lensing, structure and abundance likelihoods with common parameters.  
**Null utility:** model exclusion.  
**Principal risk:** severe underidentification and scale separation.

### Lane 14 — constructive continuum and mass-gap control

**Object:** regulator-, volume-, time- and operator-uniform spectral exclusion near zero.  
**Corpus anchors:** the exhaustive mass-gap/action programme and its five irreducible gates.  
**Reach:** mathematical and numerical rather than near-term experimental.  
**Gate:** a genuinely new infinite-dimensional estimate or constructive continuum mechanism.  
**Null utility:** certified finite-lattice methods and clearer no-go boundaries.  
**Principal risk:** repeating finite scans that cannot cross the uniform-limit frontier.

## Iterative steering rule

After each packet, re-score every lane and force a zoom-out if any of the following occurs:

- two consecutive steps add formal structure without a new observable;
- the next step depends on an unmeasured external tensor or unavailable dataset;
- the proposed signature can be reproduced by an unconstrained nuisance parameter;
- the physical carrier is chosen only because it resembles the algebra;
- the null result would teach nothing beyond the calibration;
- or the branch repeats a known theorem without tightening a corpus frontier.

Conversely, promote a lane when it gains:

- a source-derived carrier;
- an independently calibrated control parameter;
- a dimensionless discriminator or no-refit prediction;
- a detector model with covariance;
- or an experimentally accessible reversal, scaling or topology test.

## Frontier-level success condition

The atlas succeeds if it repeatedly converts one of the corpus's broad analogies into one of four harder outcomes:

\[
\boxed{
\text{executable measurement}
\quad\text{or}\quad
\text{quantitative bound}
\quad\text{or}\quad
\text{clean exclusion}
\quad\text{or}\quad
\text{replicable anomaly}.
}
\]

The aim is not to force every lane toward new physics. It is to arrange the work so that ordinary physics produces useful devices, methods or bounds, while a surviving residual has nowhere conventional to hide.

## First iteration and re-ranking

The first loop executed four of the five intended directions:

- the hybrid-bosonic survey separated controlled acoustic emulation from adversarial material inference and promoted phonon/magnon work;
- the physical energy-current packet replaced an abstract blocker with a tagged-term construction, an exact continuity oracle and a finite periodic-boundary implementation gate;
- the adversarial detector atlas turned identifiability into a common quantitative service layer for every experiment;
- the gap census added thermodynamic cost, measurement backaction, scale stability, rare events, complexity, causal speed and disorder as cross-cutting process frontiers.

This is the revised five-packet queue:

1. add trajectory thermodynamics and tunable monitoring to the selector emulator;
2. build generative simulators and test global detector equivalence, not only local Fisher separation;
3. implement the physical energy current with a validated periodic convention in the Planckian-(tJ) code;
4. close the Co2MnGa causal tensor and nuisance floor before proposing a force run;
5. test the YIG three-mode and four-mode effective matrices for a resolvable transported doublet.

The re-ranking is substantive. Detector design is no longer one competing experiment; it is infrastructure. Thermodynamic cost and measurement backaction enter develop-now because they attach directly to the selector carrier. The remaining original packets stay active because each has a concrete missing object rather than an invitation to add more analogy.

## Second iteration: selector guardrails

Two exact no-go packets then tested the new thermodynamic and measurement lanes. Detailed-balance relaxation showed that strict contraction and a unique fixed point do not imply stationary dissipation or a universal cost-speed relation. Weak measurement showed that individual records can purify toward pointer states while the unconditional population map retains a whole fixed manifold. In each case a second physical datum—driving, feedback or a reservoir orientation—must be named and costed.

That result activates a stop rule: do not add another abstract selector toy before choosing a platform with a measurable trajectory and resource ledger. The next selector step must combine holonomy/return tomography, monitoring records, conditioned and unconditional maps, dynamical activity and a heat/work proxy in one apparatus.
