## Finding

The first tile atlas contains six locally established realizations:

1. finite contextual admission;
2. the family-to-placement module;
3. the Furey signed \(K_3\) selector;
4. the partial-sewing environment;
5. the controlled small-circle \(SU(3)\) root tile;
6. the passive three-setting conductance fixture.

Five exact algebraic edges connect these nodes through operator identities, representation equivalence or controlled synthetic realization. The algebraic graph is connected.

After imposing physical admission, however, the graph has zero cross-carrier edges. Every candidate edge currently lacks at least one of:

- a source-native label map;
- a derived orientation action;
- compatible endpoint variance or support;
- a common physical action;
- regime transport;
- or a provenance-bearing observable dictionary.

Thus

\[
\boxed{
\text{algebraic atlas connected},
\qquad
\text{physical atlas disconnected}.
}
\]

## Why this is progress

The failure is no longer an amorphous “missing bridge.” Each edge now has a typed blocker:

| Edge | Exact content | First blocker |
|---|---|---|
| contextual → family/placement | same \(S_3/A_2\) module | six unselected label maps |
| contextual → Furey | \(M_F=2I+C\) | sign frame and response law are not source-derived |
| contextual → partial sewing | same \(\mathbf1\oplus A_2\) decomposition | environment/site ontology is unselected |
| contextual → \(SU(3)\) | identical affine-root Laplacian | domain, scale and regime transport absent |
| contextual → A11 fixture | exact \((a,b,c)\) realization | settings-to-branches table is not an endomorphism |

This confirms the corpus's earlier end-to-end audit in a smaller and more explicit normal form: recurrence at endpoints is real, but recurrence is not transport.

## Consequence for global closure

There is presently no licensed global automorphism group of the atlas. In particular, neither \(Spin(2,3)\) nor \(E_8\) can be obtained by taking the union of node labels. A global closure calculation must begin only after at least one physical edge exists; otherwise the result measures the researcher's chosen identifications rather than the system's admitted transformations.

The first decisive advance would therefore be modest but fundamental: one edge, between two independently sourced nodes, whose label permutation and orientation action are fixed by a common action or measurement protocol. That single edge would turn the atlas from a collection of isomorphic charts into the beginning of a geometry.
