## First rule

An invariant is incomplete until its transformation is named. For a group or admissible transformation family \(G\) acting on a space \(X\), a scalar invariant satisfies

\[
I(g\cdot x)=I(x)
\qquad
\text{for all }g\in G.
\]

This definition immediately separates four ideas that are often conflated:

| Idea | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Invariant | unchanged under a declared transformation |
| Conserved quantity | unchanged along a declared dynamics or flow |
| Covariant object | changes by a known representation rule |
| Universal quantity | has the same form or value across a declared class of systems |

A quantity may be one without being the others. Proper time is coordinate invariant but not generally a globally conserved charge. Energy is conserved when time-translation symmetry holds but is observer dependent in relativity. Force is covariant rather than invariant. A material susceptibility can be basis independent after taking its eigenvalues, yet it is neither universal nor conserved.

## Time

### Proper time

For a timelike worldline \(\gamma\) in a Lorentzian spacetime,

\[
\tau[\gamma]
=\frac1c\int_\gamma
\sqrt{-g_{\mu\nu}\,dx^\mu dx^\nu}.
\]

This is invariant under coordinate changes and local Lorentz frame changes once the spacetime geometry and path are fixed. Different paths between events can accumulate different proper times, so proper time is a path functional rather than a universal clock reading.

This makes proper time an excellent admissibility object: a proposed emergent spacetime must supply enough structure for the integral to exist, remain real on admitted timelike paths, and agree with clock comparisons.

### Time-translation invariant

If the action has a continuous time-translation symmetry, Noether's theorem supplies a conserved energy. The conservation belongs to the symmetry and equations of motion together. It need not survive explicit driving, an expanding nonstationary background or an open-system reduction.

### Time reversal

Time reversal provides parity and antiunitary structure, not automatically an arrow of time. A Hamiltonian can be time-reversal symmetric while a particular state, boundary condition or rotating solution is not. Entropy production and persistent records require additional statistical and coarse-graining structure.

The corpus therefore correctly treats these as different questions:

\[
\text{proper duration}
\ne
\text{time-translation charge}
\ne
\text{T symmetry}
\ne
\text{thermodynamic arrow}.
\]

## Force and stress

### Force is normally covariant

In relativistic mechanics,

\[
f^\mu=\frac{dp^\mu}{d\tau}
\]

is a four-vector. Its components vary between frames. With a declared metric, the contraction

\[
f^2=g_{\mu\nu}f^\mu f^\nu
\]

is a scalar. For fixed rest mass the four-force is orthogonal to the four-velocity, but this fact depends on the standard mass-shell dynamics.

### Force from an invariant energy

For a quasistatic configuration described by a scalar free energy \(\mathcal F(d,T,\ldots)\), a generalized force is

\[
F_d=-\frac{\partial\mathcal F}{\partial d}.
\]

The free energy can be invariant under coordinate relabelling and basis changes while the force transforms as the covector dual to the chosen displacement. In the dispersion-force map, the invariant content is more naturally the scattering/free-energy functional than one coordinate force component.

### Stress and flux

A stress tensor is covariant. Its contractions, eigenvalues in a physically selected frame, and integrated fluxes can be invariant. If

\[
\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu}=0,
\]

then integration over suitable hypersurfaces yields conserved charges subject to boundary conditions. Subsystem stress tensors need not be separately conserved when interactions exchange energy and momentum.

Force therefore connects to admissibility through a descent:

\[
\text{action/free energy}
\to T^{\mu\nu}
\to\text{surface flux}
\to\text{momentum transfer or generalized force}.
\]

## Area

### Metric area

For a two-surface \(\Sigma\) with induced metric \(h_{ab}\),

\[
A[\Sigma]=\int_\Sigma d^2\sigma\,\sqrt{\det h}.
\]

This is invariant under reparameterization of the same surface. It is not invariant under a physical deformation that changes the induced metric.

### Oriented flux area

Given a two-form \(F\), the integral

\[
\int_\Sigma F

\]

is coordinate independent and can become topological or quantized when \(F\) is a curvature with the required bundle and normalization. The value can still depend on the homology class and boundary.

### Symplectic area

Phase space carries a two-form

\[
\omega=dp_i\wedge dq^i.
\]

Canonical transformations preserve \(\omega\), making symplectic area an invariant unavailable to arbitrary coordinate maps. Closed action integrals

\[
\oint p_i\,dq^i

\]

are natural semiclassical and adiabatic invariants under the appropriate hypotheses.

This is especially relevant to the corpus because \(Sp(4,\mathbb R)\) preserves a symplectic form. The preserved object is symplectic area in phase space, not ordinary spatial area and not automatically a physical action quantum.

## Volume

### Proper spatial and spacetime volume

On a chosen spatial hypersurface with induced metric \(h\),

\[
V[\Sigma]=\int_\Sigma d^3x\,\sqrt{\det h}.
\]

The hypersurface choice matters. The spacetime measure

\[
dV_4=\sqrt{|g|}\,d^4x

\]

is diffeomorphism invariant as an integration density, but the volume of a region still depends on which geometric region is selected.

### Liouville volume

Hamiltonian flow preserves phase-space volume:

\[
\mathcal L_{X_H}\omega=0,
\qquad
\mathcal L_{X_H}\omega^n=0.
\]

This is a dynamical invariant of symplectic Hamiltonian evolution. It blocks ordinary finite Hamiltonian trajectories from becoming dissipative attractors without coarse-graining, openness or modified dynamics.

### Algebraic volume

Determinants, Pfaffians and cubic norms can behave as algebraic volume forms under specified group actions. The Jordan determinant in the corpus is an example of an invariant polynomial, but calling it physical volume requires a map to a metric measure, phase-space measure or observable density.

## Material

There is no single universal “material invariant.” A material is characterized by a state-dependent response family rather than one immutable number. Useful invariants arise only after the comparison is declared.

For a response tensor \(\chi(\omega,\mathbf k;T,\ldots)\), basis changes can preserve:

\[
\operatorname{tr}\chi,
\qquad
\det\chi,
\qquad
\operatorname{spec}\chi.
\]

Causality and passivity constrain its analytic and spectral structure. Dispersion relations and additional ultraviolet/infrared information can yield sum rules such as integrated spectral weights. Topological materials may carry quantized Chern numbers or related response coefficients while ordinary dielectric functions remain frequency dependent.

The material object most useful to the dispersion-force programme is not a constant \(\varepsilon\), but the causal response on the imaginary axis:

\[
\varepsilon(i\xi),\quad
\mu(i\xi),\quad
\alpha(i\xi),
\]

or the corresponding reflection/scattering operator. Under a change of channel basis, expressions such as

\[
\log\det\!\left(I-\mathcal R_1\mathcal U_{12}
\mathcal R_2\mathcal U_{21}\right)
\]

provide a basis-invariant spectral object when the operators and domains are well defined. Its value remains material-, geometry-, temperature- and separation-dependent.

## Rotation

For ordinary spatial rotations generated by \(J_i\),

\[
J^2=J_x^2+J_y^2+J_z^2

\]

is the quadratic \(SO(3)\) or \(SU(2)\) Casimir. In an irreducible quantum sector,

\[
J^2=\hbar^2j(j+1)I.
\]

The components \(J_i\) depend on the axis; \(J^2\) does not. If the dynamics is rotationally invariant, total angular momentum is also conserved. If the environment or boundary selects an axis, only a subgroup and its corresponding labels may remain protected.

In relativistic physics, spin belongs to the Poincaré classification. The Pauli--Lubanski invariant

\[
W^2=-m^2s(s+1)

\]

in the corpus convention joins rotation to mass and spacetime symmetry. This is stronger than treating a three-dimensional rotation label in isolation.

## Dimensions are not themselves invariants

Time, force, area and volume also name dimensions:

\[
[t]=T,
\qquad
[F]=MLT^{-2},
\qquad
[A]=L^2,
\qquad
[V]=L^3.
\]

Dimensional consistency is an admission gate, but a dimensional quantity's numerical value changes with units. Unit-independent statements are built from dimensionless combinations or ratios. Examples include

\[
\frac{mc^2}{\hbar\omega},
\qquad
\frac{k_BT d}{\hbar c},
\qquad
\frac{F d}{E_0},
\qquad
\frac{A}{\ell^2},
\qquad
\frac{V}{\ell^3}.
\]

The reference scale \(E_0\) or \(\ell\) must itself be derived or operationally fixed. Constructing a dimensionless ratio does not make it universal.

Several useful dimension-changing products are structural rather than invariant by themselves:

\[
F\,d=\text{energy},
\qquad
F\,dt=\text{momentum impulse},
\qquad
E\,dt=\text{action},
\qquad
P\,dV=\text{energy/work}.
\]

Their integrals can be path independent, conserved or quantized only under further conditions.

## The admissibility hierarchy of invariants

The atlas suggests a generative order:

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\text{admissible transformations}\\
&\quad\to\text{preserved forms and measures}\\
&\quad\to\text{scalar invariants and orbit labels}\\
&\quad\to\text{positive representations and conserved flows}\\
&\quad\to\text{dimensionless observables and empirical comparisons}.
\end{aligned}
\]

This order matters. Declaring a metric immediately supplies proper time, area and volume, but does not derive the metric. Declaring a symplectic form supplies canonical area and Liouville volume, but does not derive a Hamiltonian. Declaring a rotation group supplies \(J^2\), but does not select a material or particle sector. Declaring a causal susceptibility supplies response invariants, but does not determine its spectral weights.

The foundational task is therefore not to collect invariants after choosing every structure. It is to ask which minimal admissibility relations force which preserved structures.

## A compact transformation-first register

Every proposed invariant should be recorded with seven fields:

1. **Object:** what carries the data?
2. **Transformation:** what is allowed to change?
3. **Invariant:** what remains fixed?
4. **Domain:** where is the expression defined and finite?
5. **Conservation law:** is it also constant under dynamics, and why?
6. **Measurement:** what operational comparison reads it?
7. **Failure mode:** which enlargement, boundary or perturbation destroys it?

For example:

| Field | Rotation example |
|---|---|
| Object | state in an \(SU(2)\) representation |
| Transformation | spatial rotation |
| Invariant | \(J^2\) eigenvalue |
| Domain | unitary representation with declared generators |
| Conservation | only if the Hamiltonian commutes with \(J_i\) or at least \(J^2\) |
| Measurement | multiplet spectroscopy and angular correlations |
| Failure | anisotropic environment, spin-orbit exchange with an untracked subsystem, or enlarged symmetry structure |

This register prevents “invariant” from becoming an untyped synonym for an important number.

## Provisional answer

Yes: physics contains natural invariants associated with time, force, area, volume, material and rotation, but they are not six members of one uniform list. Time points to proper intervals, translation charges and reversal parity; force points back to invariant action/free-energy and stress-flux structures; area and volume point to metric, symplectic and measure invariants; materials point to spectral, tensor and topological response invariants; rotation points to angular-momentum Casimirs. Their common foundation is not their physical dimension. It is the existence of a declared class of admissible transformations under which some relational content survives.
