## Why the surrounding map matters

The Casimir effect is not an isolated force appearing only between ideal metal plates. It lies inside a broader landscape of fluctuation-induced electromagnetic interactions that includes molecular van der Waals forces, London dispersion, Casimir--Polder retardation and Lifshitz interactions between real materials. The names mark different objects, approximations, geometries and distance regimes within a connected theory; they are not freely interchangeable.

This landscape is a strong laboratory for admissibility foundations because a measurable force appears only after many structures are jointly admitted:

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\text{quantum fluctuations}
+\text{causal material response}
+\text{electromagnetic propagation}\\
&\quad+\text{geometry and boundary scattering}
+\text{state or temperature}
+\text{renormalized comparison}\\
&\qquad\longrightarrow
\text{dispersion free energy, stress and force}.
\end{aligned}
\]

No term in this chain can be replaced by the word “vacuum” alone.

## The vocabulary map

### Van der Waals

“Van der Waals” is used both broadly and narrowly. Broadly, it covers weak intermolecular interactions outside ordinary covalent or ionic bonding. In molecular physics it commonly includes three contributions:

1. **Keesom orientation:** thermally averaged interaction of permanent dipoles;
2. **Debye induction:** a permanent multipole induces a moment in a polarizable neighbor;
3. **London dispersion:** correlated quantum fluctuations of otherwise nonpolar charge distributions.

All three can produce an \(R^{-6}\) orientation-averaged energy in their simplest nonretarded dipolar regimes, so the power law alone does not identify the mechanism.

### London dispersion

London dispersion is the specifically quantum contribution generated by correlated fluctuating multipoles. For two neutral, isotropic and sufficiently separated ground-state atoms or molecules in the nonretarded dipole approximation,

\[
U_{AB}(R)=-\frac{C_6^{AB}}{R^6}.
\]

With SI polarizabilities and one common convention,

\[
C_6^{AB}
=\frac{3\hbar}{\pi(4\pi\varepsilon_0)^2}
\int_0^\infty d\xi\,
\alpha_A(i\xi)\alpha_B(i\xi).
\]

The imaginary-frequency polarizabilities carry the actual spectral information. The simple London single-oscillator formula is an approximation to this response integral, not a universal coefficient.

### Casimir--Polder

When separation is large enough that light-travel time is not negligible relative to the characteristic fluctuation time, retardation changes the interaction. For two isotropic atoms in vacuum at zero temperature, the far-zone behavior becomes

\[
U_{AB}(R)\sim-\frac{C_7^{AB}}{R^7},
\]

where the coefficient depends on static polarizabilities and electromagnetic constants. For an atom near a planar surface, the corresponding schematic change is

\[
-\frac{C_3}{z^3}
\quad\longrightarrow\quad
-\frac{C_4}{z^4}.
\]

The crossover is controlled by the material or atomic absorption spectrum, not by one universal length.

### Lifshitz

Lifshitz theory gives a macroscopic equilibrium framework for dispersion forces between bodies with causal frequency-dependent response. For parallel planar media separated by a gap \(d\), a standard nonmagnetic form of the free energy per area is

\[
\frac{\mathcal F}{A}
=\frac{k_BT}{2\pi}
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{}'
\int_0^\infty k_\perp\,dk_\perp
\sum_{p\in\{\mathrm{TE},\mathrm{TM}\}}
\log\!\left[1-r_p^{(1)}(i\xi_n,k_\perp)r_p^{(2)}(i\xi_n,k_\perp)e^{-2q_nd}\right],
\]

where

\[
\xi_n=\frac{2\pi n k_BT}{\hbar},
\qquad
q_n=\sqrt{k_\perp^2+\frac{\xi_n^2}{c^2}}.
\]

The prime assigns half weight to the zero-frequency term. Material information enters through reflection amplitudes derived from dielectric and magnetic response, while geometry enters through the scattering problem.

In dilute limits the same framework returns atomistic dispersion interactions. In ideal-reflector and zero-temperature limits it returns the familiar plate result

\[
\frac{E}{A}=-\frac{\pi^2\hbar c}{720d^3},
\qquad
P=-\frac{\pi^2\hbar c}{240d^4}.
\]

This is why London, Casimir--Polder and Casimir--Lifshitz belong on one map while retaining distinct regimes and assumptions.

## Distance and description ladder

There is no universal numerical boundary between the regimes. The comparisons below are parametric and system dependent.

| Regime | Dominant description | Typical scaling in simple geometries | Admission boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Electronic overlap | exchange, Pauli repulsion, chemical bonding and correlation | not a universal inverse power | separated-body multipole picture fails |
| Nonretarded molecular | Keesom, Debye and London | atom--atom often \(R^{-6}\) | \(R\) small relative to relevant electromagnetic wavelengths but large relative to overlap scale |
| Retarded molecular | Casimir--Polder | atom--atom often \(R^{-7}\) | finite propagation time matters |
| Nonretarded macroscopic | Hamaker/Lifshitz | planar energy often \(d^{-2}\), pressure \(d^{-3}\) | continuum response valid; retardation negligible |
| Retarded macroscopic | Casimir--Lifshitz | ideal planar energy \(d^{-3}\), pressure \(d^{-4}\) | full frequency-dependent propagation and reflection required |
| Thermal | Matsubara/zero-mode dominated Lifshitz behavior | geometry and material dependent | separation approaches the thermal electromagnetic scale up to factors of \(2\pi\) and response scales |

For orientation, define

\[
\ell_T=\frac{\hbar c}{k_BT}.
\]

The comprehensive thesis gives approximately \(7.6\,\mu\mathrm m\) at room temperature and \(0.84\,\mathrm{mm}\) at CMB temperature. Actual thermal crossovers depend on geometry, response and the Matsubara factor, so \(\ell_T\) is a scale marker rather than a universal switch point.

## From molecules to materials

A naive macroscopic construction would sum pairwise \(-C_6/R^6\) interactions over every molecule in two bodies. This produces the Hamaker picture and can be useful in dilute or approximate settings. It is not generally exact.

Dispersion forces are nonadditive because:

- the field is scattered repeatedly among bodies;
- the environment screens and reshapes polarizability;
- collective excitations replace isolated molecular modes;
- geometry couples multipoles;
- and genuine many-body terms survive.

The Axilrod--Teller--Muto triple-dipole interaction is the canonical early example: three-body geometry changes both magnitude and sign relative to a pairwise sum. Modern scattering and Lifshitz methods retain this collective structure rather than treating the force as an inventory of independent pairs.

For two half-spaces in a simple nonretarded Hamaker description,

\[
\frac{E}{A}\approx-\frac{A_H}{12\pi d^2},
\qquad
P\approx-\frac{A_H}{6\pi d^3},
\]

but the Hamaker constant \(A_H\) is a material/environment response quantity, not a universal constant. A full Lifshitz calculation replaces a fitted or pairwise Hamaker constant with frequency-dependent electromagnetic data.

## The admission gates made visible

The force landscape can be reconstructed as a sequence of physical admission gates.

### Gate D1 — separated subsystems

Atoms, molecules or bodies must be distinguishable enough for a multipole, response or scattering description. At electronic overlap, exchange and chemical structure enter and the long-range dispersion expansion loses authority.

### Gate D2 — stable quantum states

The constituents require a bounded physical state and well-defined correlation functions. A polarizability is not an arbitrary function; it arises from transitions and matrix elements of the admitted microscopic state space.

### Gate D3 — causality and passivity

The response functions must have the appropriate analytic structure. Causality relates their real and imaginary parts through dispersion relations, while passivity constrains absorption. Evaluation on the imaginary-frequency axis converts oscillatory real-frequency structure into the smooth response data used by London and Lifshitz formulas.

This is a direct contact with the corpus's Herglotz and positive-response programme: positivity and analyticity make a spectral response admissible, but do not choose the material spectrum.

### Gate D4 — propagation and retardation

Maxwell propagation supplies the finite speed \(c\) and Green tensor joining fluctuations at separated locations. Dropping retardation is an approximation with a spectral validity domain, not a different fundamental interaction.

### Gate D5 — geometry and scattering

Boundary shape, separation, polarization and intervening media determine how modes scatter and compose. Ideal plates are one solvable corner. Real surfaces require optical response, thickness, roughness and geometry.

### Gate D6 — state and temperature

At thermal equilibrium, the fluctuation--dissipation/KMS structure produces the Matsubara representation. Nonequilibrium bodies, excited atoms and moving systems require different state data and can exhibit effects not contained in the equilibrium Lifshitz formula.

### Gate D7 — reference subtraction and force

Only separation-dependent or otherwise operationally compared free energy and stress enter the measured interaction. Divergent absolute mode sums do not become observable merely by being assigned a formal value.

### Gate D8 — experimental separation

Electrostatic patches, residual potential, surface contamination, roughness, geometry calibration, capillary forces, drift and material uncertainty must be distinguished from the target dispersion interaction.

The result is an instructive form of the foundational thesis:

> The force is not one primitive object subsequently constrained by physics; it is what survives after response, propagation, composition, state, geometry and measurement are made jointly admissible.

## The nearby forces that must remain distinct

| Neighbor | Origin | Why it can be confused | Separation test |
|---|---|---|---|
| Electrostatic patch force | spatial work-function/contact-potential variation | strong at similar separations and often attractive | voltage dependence, surface mapping and distance law |
| Double-layer force | mobile ions and screening in a medium | common in colloidal and biological systems | salt concentration and Debye-length dependence |
| Capillary force | liquid meniscus and surface tension | dominates imperfect ambient nanoscale contacts | humidity and meniscus control |
| Exchange/Pauli repulsion | antisymmetry and wavefunction overlap | supplies the short-range wall paired with dispersion attraction | exponential overlap sensitivity |
| Chemical bonding | orbital rearrangement and charge sharing/transfer | can emerge before nominal contact | spectroscopy and electronic-structure dependence |
| Critical Casimir force | confined order-parameter fluctuations near a continuous phase transition | shares fluctuation and boundary language | temperature distance from the critical point and universality class |
| Acoustic or hydrodynamic fluctuation force | medium modes rather than vacuum electromagnetism | similar effective mode-sum mathematics | medium, frequency and dissipation dependence |

Formal similarity among fluctuation determinants is not physical identity. The fluctuating field, state, coupling and response dictionary must be named.

## Attraction is not the admission principle

The simplest atom--atom London interaction and ideal parallel-plate Casimir force are attractive, but “admissible” cannot be identified with “attractive.” Geometry, anisotropy, magnetic response, intervening media, nonequilibrium states and boundary conditions can alter magnitude and in controlled cases permit repulsive dispersion forces.

The more stable structural statement is that causal response and scattering data determine an allowed interaction free energy. Its sign is an output of the complete configuration, not a universal axiom.

## What is derived and what remains input

The surrounding map prevents a subtle circularity. Lifshitz theory powerfully unifies multiple regimes, but it begins with substantial physics:

- quantum or thermal fluctuation statistics;
- Maxwell propagation and the speed of light;
- causal constitutive response;
- equilibrium assumptions;
- a geometry and intervening medium;
- and an observable free-energy or stress prescription.

It therefore demonstrates how admissibility composes known physical structures; it does not yet derive electromagnetism, quantum probability or material response from the thin foundational packet.

The generative opportunity lies one level deeper: determine whether causality, passivity, composability and stable record formation restrict possible response theories strongly enough to recover the analytic/scattering architecture on which the Lifshitz formula rests.

## Connection back to the Casimir invariant

The representation Casimir and the dispersion-force landscape can meet, but only through typed intermediate objects:

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\text{admitted symmetry}
\to\text{representations and Casimir labels}
\to\text{allowed transitions/modes}\\
&\to\alpha(i\xi),\varepsilon(i\xi),\mu(i\xi)
\to\text{Green or scattering operator}
\to\mathcal F(d,T)
\to\text{force}.
\end{aligned}
\]

A Casimir invariant can constrain degeneracies, selection rules or mode spectra. It does not determine oscillator strengths, material filling, damping, boundary geometry or temperature. The bridge becomes physical only if the same microscopic representation data calculates the response functions used in the force law.

## Research packet D

### D1 — response-cone reconstruction

Start from a finite passive two-channel susceptibility with a positive spectral measure. Derive its imaginary-frequency response and insert it into the smallest planar or one-dimensional scattering problem.

**Question:** which features of the force are fixed by positivity and which require detailed spectral weights?

### D2 — London-to-retarded crossover

Use a declared oscillator model for \(\alpha(i\xi)\). Compute the atom--atom or atom--surface interaction without and with finite \(c\), locating the crossover from the response spectrum rather than choosing it graphically.

**Question:** does any dimensionless crossover invariant survive changes in oscillator content?

### D3 — pairwise-to-collective failure

Compare pairwise London/Hamaker summation with a three-body or scattering calculation in the smallest geometry where nonadditivity is unavoidable.

**Question:** can contextual admissibility be quantified as the correction required when pairwise-consistent relations fail global composition?

### D4 — material and nuisance separation

Construct a synthetic sphere--plate or atom--surface dataset with dielectric uncertainty, residual electrostatics, roughness and temperature. Fit the full model under preregistered nuisance treatment.

**Question:** which putative “universal” features remain after real response and calibration freedoms are admitted?

### D5 — Casimir-label descent

Choose a microscopic system whose transitions are organized by a known group Casimir. Calculate whether the Casimir label produces a protected feature in \(\alpha(i\xi)\), and whether that feature survives the Lifshitz/scattering descent into force.

**Question:** is there a genuine invariant-to-force prediction, or does unprotected spectral weight erase it?

## Promotion and failure criteria

The landscape supports a new admissibility claim only if it produces at least one of:

- a response restriction stronger than ordinary causality and passivity;
- a protected crossover relation shared across specified materials;
- a forbidden or required force contribution tied to a derived representation invariant;
- a nonadditive correction fixed by the contextual composition law;
- or a calibrated observable not obtainable by refitting conventional response data.

The branch fails as new fundamental physics if all results reduce to standard London, Casimir--Polder or Lifshitz theory after freely choosing polarizabilities, dielectric functions and boundaries. That outcome would still validate dispersion forces as a worked model of layered physical admissibility.

- I. E. Dzyaloshinskii, E. M. Lifshitz and L. P. Pitaevskii, [*General theory of van der Waals' forces*](https://doi.org/10.1070/PU1961v004n02ABEH003330), for the material, thermal and macroscopic response framework; and

These sources establish the conventional force landscape. They do not establish the corpus's stronger claim that admissibility is ontologically prior to physical structure.

## Provisional synthesis

The surrounding landscape sharpens the foundational proposal. London dispersion shows that a zero mean dipole does not make interaction inadmissible because correlations of fluctuations remain. Casimir--Polder shows that causal propagation changes the admissible long-distance scaling. Lifshitz theory shows that real material response, temperature and geometry are constitutive parts of the force rather than corrections to a universal ideal answer. Many-body dispersion shows that pairwise admissibility does not guarantee global additivity. The Casimir effect then appears not as an isolated extraction of energy from empty space, but as one macroscopic limit of a response-and-scattering architecture whose physical content is selected at every scale. This is precisely the kind of layered derivation the admissibility programme seeks—while the undeclared inputs in that architecture identify how far the programme still is from a fundamental reconstruction.
