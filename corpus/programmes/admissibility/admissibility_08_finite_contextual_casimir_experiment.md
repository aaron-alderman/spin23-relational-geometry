## Objective

Packet C1 asked for the smallest exact example in which a transformation group and a Casimir-like invariant are derived from contextual admission data rather than declared first.

The benchmark uses three initially unlabeled sites

\[
X=\{0,1,2\}
\]

and a relation matrix \(A\). A permutation \(p\in S_3\) is admitted as an automorphism only if

\[
A_{ij}=A_{p(i)p(j)}
\qquad
\text{for every }i,j.
\]

No symmetry group is supplied to the calculation. The verifier enumerates all six permutations and retains exactly those preserving the relation data.

This is a toy reconstruction, not a derivation of physical space, particles or quantum probability.

## Model U: uniform relational admission

Begin with one rule:

> Every distinct pair is related in the same way; no site carries an intrinsic mark.

Then

\[
A_U=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&1&1\\
1&0&1\\
1&1&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Exhaustive enumeration finds

\[
\operatorname{Aut}(A_U)=S_3,
\qquad
|\operatorname{Aut}(A_U)|=6.
\]

The symmetry has been derived from the indistinguishability of the admitted relations. It was not independently chosen, although the uniform relation that produces it remains an input.

## The finite Casimir analogue

Let \(P_{01},P_{12},P_{02}\) be the permutation matrices of the three transpositions. Their class sum is central in the represented group algebra:

\[
Z_{\rm tr}=P_{01}+P_{12}+P_{02}.
\]

In the three-site permutation representation,

\[
Z_{\rm tr}=J,
\]

where \(J\) is the all-ones matrix. Therefore

\[
C_U=3I-Z_{\rm tr}
=3I-J
=
\begin{pmatrix}
2&-1&-1\\
-1&2&-1\\
-1&-1&2
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

This is also the graph Laplacian of the admitted relation. It commutes with every derived automorphism:

\[
[C_U,P]=0
\qquad
\text{for all }P\in\operatorname{Aut}(A_U).
\]

The carrier decomposes as

\[
\mathbb R^3
=\operatorname{span}(1,1,1)
\oplus
\{x:x_0+x_1+x_2=0\},
\]

and the Casimir-like operator acts with eigenvalues

\[
C_U=0
\quad\text{on the common mode},
\qquad
C_U=3I
\quad\text{on the two-dimensional relational plane}.
\]

Thus a nontrivial sector invariant follows from the admitted equivalence of all three pair relations.

## Model O: orientation is added

Now replace the unoriented relation by the directed cycle

\[
0\to1\to2\to0,
\qquad
T=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&1&0\\
0&0&1\\
1&0&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The exhaustive automorphism group becomes

\[
\operatorname{Aut}(T)=C_3,
\qquad
|\operatorname{Aut}(T)|=3.
\]

Reflections are no longer admitted because they reverse the arrows.

The even quadratic operator remains

\[
(I-T)(I-T)^T=3I-J=C_U.
\]

It therefore cannot distinguish the two orientations of the relational plane. The missing information is carried by the odd operator

\[
K=T-T^T,
\]

which satisfies

\[
K(1,1,1)^T=0,
\qquad
K^2=J-3I.
\]

Every admitted cyclic rotation preserves \(K\), while every excluded reflection reverses it:

\[
PKP^T=K
\quad\text{for }P\in C_3,
\qquad
PKP^T=-K
\quad\text{for reflections}.
\]

This gives an exact finite result with direct significance for the corpus:

> The quadratic Casimir-like invariant sees the relational plane but forgets its orientation; an orientation-odd invariant exists only after oriented admission data reduce \(S_3\) to \(C_3\).

No isotropic unoriented rule can manufacture the sign of \(K\).

## Model M: one site is marked

Retain the uniform relation but add the color vector

\[
c=(1,0,0).
\]

Only permutations preserving both \(A_U\) and \(c\) remain:

\[
\operatorname{Aut}(A_U,c)\cong C_2,
\qquad
|\operatorname{Aut}(A_U,c)|=2.
\]

The surviving symmetry exchanges the two unmarked sites. A mark therefore creates a singlet-plus-parity organization relative to the selected site. The selection is real, but it came from new contextual data; it was not generated by the uniform relation alone.

## Model W: all pair weights differ

Finally choose the symmetric weighted relation

\[
A_W=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&1&3\\
1&0&2\\
3&2&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

All three edge weights are distinct, and exhaustive enumeration gives

\[
\operatorname{Aut}(A_W)=\{e\}.
\]

The weighted Laplacian is

\[
L_W=
\begin{pmatrix}
4&-1&-3\\
-1&3&-2\\
-3&-2&5
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

It has spectrum

\[
\operatorname{spec}(L_W)
=\{0,6-\sqrt3,6+\sqrt3\}.
\]

The relational degeneracy is completely split. The exact doublet in Model U was therefore protected by relational uniformity, not by the mere fact that three sites existed.

## What has been filled

This benchmark fills one small cell of the admissibility zone:

\[
\text{finite typed relations}
\to
\text{derived automorphism group}
\to
\text{central class sum}
\to
\text{sector invariant}.
\]

The computation establishes:

1. an automorphism group can be obtained by exhaustive preservation of admission data;
2. a central group-algebra element supplies a finite Casimir analogue;
3. its degeneracy is exactly tied to relational equivalence;
4. orientation requires an additional odd invariant and excludes reflections;
5. marking reduces symmetry by supplying a selector datum;
6. generic heterogeneity destroys the relational doublet.

## What remains unfilled

The model does not derive:

- why there are three sites;
- why the uniform relation is physically realized;
- a probability or positive-state interpretation;
- continuous time, causal propagation or a Hamiltonian;
- a physical inner product beyond the chosen finite Euclidean carrier;
- a scale or coupling;
- a material, force or detector map;
- or an empirical consequence.

The result is therefore a reconstruction of symmetry from a finite relation, not yet a reconstruction of physics from admissibility.

## Frontier update

The theory frontier has moved from

\[
\text{“can admission data generate any nontrivial symmetry?”}
\]

to

\[
\text{“which compositional or probabilistic principles select the admission data themselves?”}
\]

The next step is not to enlarge the group. It is to enrich the finite model with states, composition and positivity while retaining exhaustive classification:

1. replace a bare relation matrix with composable typed processes;
2. classify positive state cones preserved by the derived automorphisms;
3. test tensor or network composition of two copies;
4. determine whether the relational doublet is stable under composition;
5. identify whether an orientation can arise from path composition rather than being inserted as an arrow;
6. and derive the smallest detector map capable of distinguishing the even invariant \(C_U\) from the odd invariant \(K\).

## Conclusion

The first finite Casimir frontier is now crossed. A symmetry and a Casimir-like sector operator have been derived from explicitly declared admission relations without supplying the group in advance. The result also identifies the next obstruction exactly: uniform admissibility produces a degenerate relational plane but cannot orient it; orientation, marking and heterogeneous weights are new data that respectively reduce, select and split the symmetry. The open foundational problem is now the origin of those admission relations and their physical state, dynamical and detector structure.
