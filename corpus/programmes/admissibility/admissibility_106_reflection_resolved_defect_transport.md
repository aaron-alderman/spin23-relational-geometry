## Result

AZ-105 showed that a minimal chiral zero mode does not carry a fixed residue, localization length or raw phase beyond its signed index. AZ-106 adds one independently declared crystalline datum: the intrinsic mirror parity of the defect orbital relative to a separately calibrated mirror-even reference port.

The carrier is a thirteen-site chiral chain with seven A and six B sites. Its twelve bonds obey exact reflection symmetry about the central A site. Six smooth wall profiles retain one zero mode and a nonzero gap; the smallest gap is 0.546. For each profile, two carriers are compared. They have identical hopping spectra, zero-mode count and signed chiral index +1, but their defect orbitals have intrinsic mirror parity +1 or -1 relative to the reference.

The physical reflection eigenvalue of the zero mode follows that intrinsic parity. Coherent mirror-even and mirror-odd detector ports implement the projectors

\[
P_\pm=\frac{1\pm R}{2}.
\]

Across all twelve profile/parity cases, the matching port weight is one and the forbidden port weight is below \(1.2\times10^{-16}\). The reflection commutator and zero-mode parity residuals are below \(4.5\times10^{-16}\) and \(1.7\times10^{-16}\). The mirror label is therefore independent of the chiral index and detector-facing.

The protection mechanism is also explicit. A nondegenerate isolated mode cannot change a discrete mirror representation while the symmetry and gap remain intact. Deliberately changing one bond without changing its reflected partner preserves the chiral index but opens the forbidden port. For one to twenty percent bond asymmetry, the leakage rises from \(1.95\times10^{-7}\) to \(5.53\times10^{-5}\).

## Why this succeeds where AZ-105 failed

Residue and localization were continuous properties of the wall profile. Mirror character is a discrete representation label of an independently calibrated symmetry action. The two parity branches cannot be distinguished by their energy spectrum or chiral index, but they can be distinguished by which coherent detector port remains dark.

The reference is essential. Multiplying an abstract reflection operator by an overall sign would relabel even and odd. A physical orbital and calibrated reference port fix the relative action. The corpus therefore records the mirror label as supplied carrier structure, not something generated by the chiral index or inferred from a visually symmetric wavefunction.

## Open and closed

**Closed here**

- two carriers with the same spectrum and signed chiral index have opposite calibrated mirror representations;
- the label survives six reflection-preserving wall homotopies with the gap open;
- complementary coherent ports read it through an exact selection rule;
- controlled symmetry breaking opens the forbidden port while preserving the mode count;
- OT-015's declared finite existence criterion is satisfied.

**Still open**

- no natural material or fabricated device has been identified or measured;
- the intrinsic orbital parity and reference port are inputs, not derived from admissibility alone;
- OT-046's D4 quotient-singularity operator remains a separate construction;
- no non-Abelian transport, \(\operatorname{Spin}_0(2,3)\) dynamics or new physics follows.

## Implication

A defect can transport more than mode count, but only when the “more” belongs to an independently meaningful symmetry representation and the detector keeps that representation visible. The engineering target is direct: fabricate two otherwise isospectral domain-wall channels using orbitals or modes of opposite calibrated mirror parity, then measure bright/dark coherent-port exchange under symmetric and deliberately asymmetric bond sweeps. Agreement would validate symmetry-resolved defect routing; a residual forbidden-port signal beyond fabrication covariance would locate missing couplings, not automatically new physics.
