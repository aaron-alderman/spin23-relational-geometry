# Measurement creates branches, not unique unconditional selection

**Date:** 24 October 2026

## Result

Measurement can make individual records look selected while the unconditional physical state retains an entire neutral family:

\[
\boxed{
\text{trajectory collapse}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{unique unconditional fixed point}.
}
\]

This is the measurement analogue of the projector-only selection no-go. A record supplies a branch label. It does not, by itself, choose one branch for the ensemble.

## Exact weak-measurement witness

For a qubit weakly measured in the (sigma_z) basis, take

\[
M_s=
\begin{pmatrix}
\sqrt{(1+s\epsilon)/2}&0\\
0&\sqrt{(1-s\epsilon)/2}
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad s=\pm1.
\]

Conditioned on outcome (s), the state updates by

\[
\rho_s=\frac{M_s\rho M_s^\dagger}{p_s},
\qquad
p_s=\operatorname{tr}(M_s\rho M_s^\dagger).
\]

The two outcomes update the population in opposite directions. Repeated records therefore drive an individual trajectory toward one of the two poles.

If the record is not conditioned upon, however,

\[
\mathcal M(\rho)=\sum_sM_s\rho M_s^\dagger
=
\begin{pmatrix}
\rho_{00}&\sqrt{1-\epsilon^2}\,\rho_{01}\\
\sqrt{1-\epsilon^2}\,\rho_{10}&\rho_{11}
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Coherence contracts, but both populations are unchanged. Every (sigma_z)-diagonal density matrix is a fixed point. Monitoring creates a pointer basis and a record-indexed branch structure, not a unique unconditional selector.

The verifier evaluates this at (epsilon=0.6). Two conditioned states move toward opposite poles, their probability-weighted average exactly restores the initial populations, and the unconditional coherence multiplier is (0.8).

## The second datum is visible

Add a calibrated amplitude-damping channel with strength (gamma). Its energy-basis reservoir maps the excited-state population as

\[
p_1'=(1-\gamma)p_1,
\]

and repeated application has the unique ground-state fixed point. The uniqueness is real, but it comes from an additional datum: the reservoir's energy orientation and zero-temperature bias. It is not generated by (sigma_z) measurement alone.

This gives a sharp experimental comparison:

| Protocol | Conditioned records | Unconditional populations | Fixed set |
|---|---|---|---|
| weak measurement only | branch toward either pole | unchanged | all diagonal states |
| measurement plus outcome feedback | controller-dependent | can become biased | supplied by feedback law |
| measurement plus amplitude damping | branch record plus decay | excited population contracts | unique reservoir-selected ground state |

## Measurement and detector equivalence

The detector now has two roles that must not be conflated:

1. **compression:** finite resolution maps distinct latent states to the same observed record and loses Fisher information;
2. **backaction:** the measurement channel changes the state being inferred.

An experiment can suffer both simultaneously. The correct generative graph is

\[
\text{state}
\xrightarrow{\text{instrument outcome}}
(\text{conditioned state},\text{record})
\xrightarrow{\text{readout compression}}
\text{stored data}.
\]

The first arrow is a quantum instrument; the second is an ordinary detector/reconstruction channel. Treating both as one black box makes “selection” ambiguous.

## Executable experiment

Use a qubit, qutrit or coupled-mode analogue with tunable measurement strength:

1. prepare states with equal populations but different coherence;
2. prepare states with several different populations;
3. retain every weak-measurement outcome;
4. reconstruct both conditioned maps and the outcome-averaged channel;
5. repeat with feedback off and on;
6. add and remove an independently calibrated damping/selector channel;
7. account separately for system heat and record-memory reset work.

The signature of measurement-only branching is simultaneous trajectory purification and population neutrality in the ensemble. The signature of genuine added selection is contraction of the unconditional population map toward a unique state when the second datum is active.

## Current-research contact

Measurement-induced phase transitions extend this elementary distinction to many-body systems: varying measurement rate can change entanglement structure even when local linear observables do not reveal the transition. A 2026 trapped-ion experiment implemented a postselection-free protocol on tree circuits with universal gates, showing that the record-processing architecture is itself part of experimental reach.

This is a nearby research frontier, not evidence that the corpus selector explains those transitions. Its value is operational: it supplies platforms where channel dynamics, trajectory conditioning, decoding complexity and record formation can be varied separately.

## Consequence

The selector programme now has two independent no-go results:

- projector-only or self-generated memory does not choose an orientation;
- measurement-only branching does not choose a unique unconditional population.

In both cases a second physical datum produces uniqueness, and in both cases that datum must be independently identified rather than hidden inside the update rule.
