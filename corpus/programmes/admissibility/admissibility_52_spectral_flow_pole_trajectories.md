## Result

The useful primitive is not a label such as “free” or “bound,” but an **admissible deformation path** through which a pole can cross a spectral edge while its information is redistributed among a bound-state count, a continued-sheet resonance, scattering phase, delay, localization and environmental energy.  AZ-052 makes that proposal executable in the smallest model currently warranted by the corpus.

The run succeeds as a bridge and fails as a universal identification.  A tunable local branch is a real bound pole outside the continuum, becomes a finite-width continued-sheet pole inside it, and returns to the physical sheet beyond the other edge.  The same Hamiltonian family changes scattering delay and a scalar Casimir proxy.  A dimerization reversal changes those responses.  But the raw phase winding is not yet the index: it does not equal the raw bound-state count without band-edge, branch and background corrections.  That failure is informative.  It prevents the proposed common ledger from being declared before the correct finite-band Levinson theorem has been derived.

## 1. Model

Take an infinite one-dimensional tight-binding continuum with hopping (t=1), hence Bloch band

\[
E(k)=-2\cos k,\qquad -2\le E\le2.
\]

Attach one end of a finite eight-site SSH rail to a continuum site with coupling (v_c), and attach a tunable local level of energy \(\epsilon_L\) to the rail's other end.  The cluster Hamiltonian is

\[
H_C(\epsilon_L)=H_{\mathrm{SSH}}(t_1,t_2)
+v_L(|8\rangle\langle L|+|L\rangle\langle8|)
+\epsilon_L|L\rangle\langle L|.
\]

The continuum-dressed inverse Green function is

\[
M(z)=z-H_C-v_c^2g_0(z)|1\rangle\langle1|,
\qquad
g_0(z)=\frac1{\sqrt{z^2-4}}.
\]

Zeros of \(\det M\) on the physical sheet outside the band are normalizable bound states.  Zeros on the continued sheet in the lower half-plane are resonance candidates with width \(\Gamma=-2\,\mathrm{Im},z\).  The decay length of an exterior pole is

\[
\xi(E)=\left[\operatorname{arcosh}(|E|/2)\right]^{-1}.
\]

For real energy in the band, the side cluster supplies the energy-dependent potential

\[
U(E)=v_c^2\langle1|(E-H_C+i0)^{-1}|1\rangle,
\]

and the nontrivial scattering eigenvalue is

\[
s(E)=\frac{2i\sin k+U(E)}{2i\sin k-U(E)}.
\]

Its unwrapped phase supplies the scalar Wigner delay \(d\arg s/dE\).  Thus bound poles, resonances, phase and delay are computed from one model rather than compared by analogy.

## 3. Why the first index conjecture remains open

A tempting candidate is

\[
\mathcal I
=N_{\mathrm b}
-\frac{1}{2\pi}\Delta\arg\det S
+I_{\mathrm{edge}}
+I_{\mathrm{background}},
\]

with a constant value under admissible deformation.  The present calculation establishes all the raw ingredients but not the correction terms.  Its uncorrected phase winding is neither integer-stable nor numerically equal to the exterior bound-state count.  A finite lattice band has two thresholds; half-bound states, phase-branch choices, the decoupled scattering channel and background rail poles all matter.

Accordingly:

- spectral flow is **computed**;
- pole loss and recovery at the band are **computed**;
- scattering phase and delay are **computed**;
- a universal combined index is **not admitted**.

The next mathematical task is a finite-band Levinson ledger stated for this exact side-coupled graph, followed by numerical closure at each threshold crossing.

## 5. Detection, memory and capture

The scattering phase is operationally accessible through interference, and its derivative supplies a dwell-time-sensitive detector channel.  The sharp delay peaks therefore mark temporarily retained amplitude even when no physical-sheet bound state exists.

Hamiltonian spectral flow alone has no irreversible hysteresis.  AZ-052 adds a separately typed Markov relaxation law,

\[
\dot p=\gamma[p_{\mathrm{eq}}(\lambda)-p],
\]

and cycles \(\lambda\) through the boundary.  The resulting finite-rate memory loop grows from area (1.216) in the slower run to (1.380) in the faster run.  This is a demonstration that lag can decorate the spectral path; it is not evidence that SSH topology or Spin(2,3) causes metastability.

Radiative capture is represented by a **unitary radiative-capture surrogate** in the one-excitation sector.  An excited local matter state \(|e,0\rangle\) is coupled to 121 states \(|b,1_j\rangle\), each containing the lower bound matter state and one emitted scalar mode:

\[
H_{\mathrm{rad}}
=E_e|e,0\rangle\langle e,0|
+\sum_j(E_b+\omega_j)|b,1_j\rangle\langle b,1_j|
+\sum_jg_j\bigl(|e,0\rangle\langle b,1_j|+\mathrm{h.c.}\bigr).
\]

Starting in \(|e,0\rangle\) at \(E_e=0.65\), with \(E_b=-0.55\), the evolved population in the bound-plus-radiation sector reaches (0.99955) at the declared final time.  The conditional emitted-mode energy is (1.20000=E_e-E_b).  Norm and total energy close to (4.6\times10^{-13}) and (4.0\times10^{-13}), respectively.  This distinguishes a reversible one-channel resonance from transfer into a lower matter sector plus an explicitly retained radiation field.

The bath remains finite and scalar and its spectral density is prescribed.  The result therefore does not derive microscopic capture, electromagnetic selection rules, photon polarizations or QED rates.  Finite-bath recurrences will also occur beyond the reported time window.

## 6. Casimir placement

The toy Casimir quantity is calculated from the same cluster Green function by a scalar imaginary-frequency scattering integral,

\[
\mathcal E_C(L)\sim\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^\infty d\xi\,
\log\left|1-r_1(i\xi)r_2(i\xi)e^{-2\kappa(\xi)L}\right|.
\]

It is therefore an integrated readout of how the boundary rail reorganizes the spectrum.  It is not the electromagnetic Casimir energy of a proposed apparatus, and it remains categorically distinct from a Lie-algebra Casimir invariant.

## 7. Spin(2,3) implication

This computation sharpens the appropriate question.  Spin(2,3) need not be asked to “cause” a bound electron.  It may instead be asked whether a derived carrier Hamiltonian restricts:

1. which sheet-changing pole paths are admissible;
2. which total winding or spectral-flow quantity is conserved;
3. which subsystem invariant may change only through an explicitly represented boundary or bath;
4. which flat-space and weak-coupling limits recover the ordinary graph ledger.

Nothing here answers those questions.  No Spin(2,3) generator, representation or interaction appears in the executable Hamiltonian.  The result supplies a target that a future derivation must reproduce.

- Rahi, Emig, Graham, Jaffe and Kardar, [*Scattering Theory Approach to Electrodynamic Casimir Forces*](https://arxiv.org/abs/0908.2649), controls the use of imaginary-frequency reflection data in Casimir calculations.  AZ-052's scalar integral is structurally analogous but is not an electromagnetic prediction.
