## Exact closure

Place the quarter-turn and the two-rail mirror on one real plane:

\[
I=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
J=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]

They obey

\[
I^2=-1,
\qquad
J^2=+1,
\qquad
IJ=-JI.
\]

Their product

\[
K=IJ=\begin{pmatrix}-1&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix}
\]

also satisfies \(K^2=+1\). The four matrices

\[
\{1,I,J,K\}
\]

are linearly independent and span all of \(M_2(\mathbb R)\). Hence

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{alg}_{\mathbb R}(I,J)
=Cl(1,1)\cong M_2(\mathbb R),}
\]

also called the split-quaternion algebra. The local matrix closure sought in
the surrounding discussion is therefore already generated by the Wick-like
quarter-turn and the mirror-face exchange, provided they are placed on the
same typed plane and their anticommutation is justified.

## The dual seam is derived

Define the two null combinations

\[
N_+=\frac{J+I}{2}
=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\1&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
N_-=\frac{J-I}{2}
=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Anticommutation makes their squares vanish:

\[
N_\pm^2
=\frac14\left(J^2+I^2\pm(JI+IJ)\right)=0.
\]

Each span \(\mathbb R[1,N_\pm]\) is therefore a copy of the real dual
numbers. Moreover,

\[
N_+N_-+N_-N_+=1,
\qquad
[N_+,N_-]=K.
\]

The two nilpotent directions are not independent decorations. Together they
reconstruct the diagonal grading and the identity. They are the raising and
lowering rails of the same full matrix algebra.

This is the central new result:

\[
\boxed{\text{complex quarter-turn}+\text{split mirror}
\Longrightarrow\text{two dual-number seams}.}
\]

## Why the three-branch family appeared

Every generator in the earlier normalized family can be written

\[
X_\kappa
=\frac{1+\kappa}{2}J
+\frac{1-\kappa}{2}I
=\begin{pmatrix}0&\kappa\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Therefore

\[
X_\kappa^2=\kappa\mathbf 1.
\]

At \(\kappa=-1\), only the complex direction \(I\) remains. At
\(\kappa=+1\), only the split direction \(J\) remains. At \(\kappa=0\),
their equal null combination \(N_+\) remains. The elliptic--parabolic--
hyperbolic passage is literally a path through the two-generator Clifford
plane.

The determinant supplies its quadratic classifier. For every traceless
matrix in this plane,

\[
X_\kappa^2=-\det(X_\kappa)\mathbf 1,
\qquad
-\det(X_\kappa)=\kappa.
\]

Negative, zero and positive values give elliptic, parabolic and hyperbolic
generators. The parabolic direction is the null cone of this quadratic form.

## Relation to the two rails

The split generator \(J\) has idempotent eigenrails

\[
e_\pm=\frac{1\pm J}{2}.
\]

The nilpotents \(N_\pm\) instead move between the two eigenspaces. In the
\(K\)-graded basis they are off-diagonal matrix units. Thus two different
“rail” descriptions must be kept apart:

- \(e_\pm\) are persistent idempotent sectors of the split-complex algebra;
- \(N_\pm\) are directed square-zero passages between graded sectors.

This makes the ladder intuition precise. The two side rails are the
idempotent decomposition; the rungs are nilpotent maps. The quarter-turn joins
the two directed rungs with opposite sign, while the mirror joins them with
the same sign:

\[
J=N_++N_-,
\qquad
I=N_+-N_-.
\]

The resemblance to a two-rail ladder or paired strand is structural at the
matrix level. It does not identify the construction with DNA, a biological
polymer or a microscopic physical substrate.
