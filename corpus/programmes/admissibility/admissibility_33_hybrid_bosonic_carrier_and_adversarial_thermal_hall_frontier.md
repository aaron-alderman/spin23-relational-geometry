## Result

The phonon/magnon lane does not reduce to one experiment. It separates into two complementary programmes:

\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{ll}
\text{controlled-wave branch:}&\text{acoustic non-Abelian transport and non-Hermitian tomography},\\[1mm]
\text{material-response branch:}&\text{magnon--phonon hybridization and adversarial thermal Hall inference}.
\end{array}}
\]

The controlled-wave branch is the faster emulator of projector, holonomy, order and loss. The material-response branch is the stronger test of whether geometry can be inferred from a detector record when a distinct interacting mechanism produces the same transverse response.

## Platform A — acoustic non-Abelian pumping

An acoustic waveguide-array experiment realizes two noncommuting \(Z_3\) pump cycles in a three-band system. The cycles traverse band degeneracies, and reversing the order of the two pump operations changes the final state: one order shifts the acoustic state while the reversed order traps it.

This supplies:

- a three-component carrier;
- explicitly ordered cyclic operations;
- noncommuting observable transport;
- direct pressure-field mapping along the propagation direction;
- and inexpensive geometric redesign.

It does not yet supply the AZ-026 mechanism. Propagation distance acts as synthetic time, the pump crosses band degeneracies rather than maintaining a protected dark doublet, and no repeated two-datum contraction has been measured.

Verdict: **best rapid emulator of order-sensitive non-Abelian transport**.

## Platform B — acoustic non-Abelian phase elements

A separate acoustic metamaterial uses braiding-type couplings between pairs of triple-component acoustic dipoles. Sequencing these elements produces the \(i\), \(j\) and \(k\) non-Abelian phases and their quotient relations in a compact, Hermitian, time-reversal-invariant system.

This is unusually close to the corpus's repeated quarter-turn language: the quaternionic labels are operational phase classes with different endpoint states. But the resemblance is not an intertwiner. The experiment classifies multigap acoustic topology, not the corpus's relative-frame carrier or selector dynamics.

Verdict: **best discrete quarter-turn and composition emulator; physical identification open**.

## Platform C — acoustic non-Hermitian braid tomography

A 2026 active acoustic lattice reconstructs complex eigenfrequencies and eigenstates over a Klein-bottle Brillouin zone while tuning unidirectional coupling amplitudes through approximately 20, 24, 32 and 40 Hz. It observes exceptional-point splitting, charge inversion across a nonorientable boundary and non-annihilating collision.

This supplies an unusually complete laboratory for separating:

- real-frequency geometry;
- imaginary-frequency loss or gain;
- eigenvector braiding;
- boundary topology;
- and Hamiltonian-reconstruction error.

Because amplifiers implement the unidirectional couplings, the system is active. Any contraction or amplification must be attributed to the reconstructed nonunitary transfer before a geometric-selection claim is considered.

Verdict: **best loss-versus-topology audit platform**.

## Platform D — photon--magnon--phonon triple strong coupling

A YIG sphere in a microwave cavity now realizes triple strong coupling: microwave photons hybridize with magnons, and the resulting magnon polariton couples to a long-lived mechanical phonon through magnetostriction.

Reported values include:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\omega_a/2\pi&=7.213\,\mathrm{GHz},
&\kappa_{\rm int}/2\pi&=1.98\,\mathrm{MHz},\\
g_{ma}/2\pi&=6.63\,\mathrm{MHz},
&\kappa_m/2\pi&=0.49\,\mathrm{MHz},\\
\omega_b/2\pi&=10.9565\,\mathrm{MHz},
&\kappa_b/2\pi&=155\,\mathrm{Hz}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Coherent-perfect-absorption tuning reduces one polariton decay rate to approximately \(0.78\) kHz, produces a Wigner delay near \(0.2\) ms and enables a reported polaromechanical cooperativity of \(9.4\times10^3\).

This is the closest literal hybrid bosonic carrier in the survey. It offers independently addressable field, spin and mechanical degrees of freedom, tunable magnetic detuning, two microwave ports, amplitude/phase control and vector-network-analyzer readout.

It does not automatically realize a degenerate doublet or the selector datum \(Y\). The phonon interaction is drive-linearized and sideband-mediated, and coherent perfect absorption is a nonunitary interference condition rather than unitary holonomy.

Verdict: **best physical three-species carrier; effective-matrix design gate open**.

## Platform E — magnon-polaron thermal Hall response

In Na2Co2TeO6, thermal Hall measurements show a negative response at low magnetic field, a sign change to positive response at higher field and thermal Hall angles up to approximately two percent. A spin-lattice model attributes the sign reversal and enhancement to Berry curvature of magnon polarons counteracting a purely magnonic contribution.

This gives the programme a genuine hybrid-material guidepost: an avoided-crossing carrier changes a transverse thermal observable in a field-dependent way.

However, a distinct 2025 theory demonstrates that many-body magnon skew scattering can generate intrinsic thermal Hall current without disorder and without Berry curvature. The same headline observable therefore belongs to at least two detector-equivalence classes.

Verdict: **best adversarial inference problem; thermal Hall alone cannot certify quantum geometry**.

## Two executable experiments

### Experiment 1 — acoustic repeated-return selector emulator

Start from the non-Abelian acoustic pump architecture and fabricate modular loop blocks with full complex pressure readout. Add a switchable asymmetric terminal block as the independently calibrated datum \(Y\).

For each of several input state vectors:

1. measure loop order \(L_2L_1\) and \(L_1L_2\);
2. reconstruct the complex transfer matrix of each block;
3. polar-decompose coherent transport and attenuation;
4. cascade repeated loop-plus-selector blocks;
5. estimate fixed points and tangent slopes with \(Y\) on and off;
6. reverse the propagation construction or fabricate mirror-related copies.

The pass condition is not noncommutativity alone. It is projector closure plus order-sensitive coherent transport and a statistically bounded slope below one only when the independent block is present.

### Experiment 2 — adversarial magnon thermal-Hall separation

Measure thermal Hall response together with a spectroscopic map of magnon--phonon avoided crossings over magnetic field, temperature and angle. Fit at least two preregistered models:

- band-geometric magnon-polaron Berry curvature;
- interaction-driven many-body skew scattering.

Add discriminators that the headline \(\kappa_{xy}\) lacks:

- drive-frequency and thermal-lag dependence;
- linewidth and lifetime dependence near hybridization;
- controlled disorder and thickness series;
- longitudinal thermal conductivity and specific heat;
- field-angle and reversal parity;
- inelastic neutron, Raman or microwave spectroscopy where available;
- and higher-order or nonlinear response sensitive to collision integrals.

The goal is to find a channel in which the two mechanisms cannot be refitted into one another.

## YIG design fork

The YIG platform merits a separate calculation before hardware modification. Its effective rotating-frame susceptibility should be reconstructed from the reported photon, magnon and phonon modes, drive phases, detunings and damping rates.

Two possibilities must be tested:

1. **three-mode route:** tune the effective matrix so one instantaneous line is separated from a near-degenerate doublet and evaluate the attainable projector loop;
2. **four-mode route:** use two cavity modes plus magnon and phonon to create a tripod-like coupling vector with a protected two-dimensional dark space.

The second route is structurally closer to integrated-photonic \(SO(2)\) holonomy. The phonon may serve as a controllable perturbation or memory channel, but it becomes candidate \(Y\) only if independently calibrated and shown to change the return map.

## Promotion and failure criteria

Promote the controlled-wave branch if it supplies the full tuple

\[
(d_P,\theta_H^\pm,\nu,\widehat m,\text{loop-order commutator})
\]

with a declared detector covariance.

Promote the material-response branch if one mechanism makes a preregistered out-of-sample prediction that the other cannot reproduce across field, temperature, angle, frequency and spectral hybridization.

Do not promote if:

- acoustic loss or gain alone accounts for apparent convergence;
- arbitrary universal control is used to synthesize the desired map;
- thermal Hall sign reversal is the only geometric evidence;
- the YIG effective doublet is not spectrally resolved from damping;
- or model selection depends entirely on unconstrained response tails.

## Re-ranking consequence

The phonon/magnon lane moves from fifth to third in the execute-now queue. It now has both a fast classical emulator and a material-response inference problem, plus a newly demonstrated triple-hybrid carrier. The two-current transport lane remains ahead for purely computational closure; the same-carrier Casimir experiment remains higher-risk because its nuisance floor is not yet calculated.
