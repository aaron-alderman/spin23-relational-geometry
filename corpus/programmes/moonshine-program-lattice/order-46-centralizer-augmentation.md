## Result

The cyclic orientation no-go does not return the programme to an unbounded
search.  The smallest data outside the cyclic subgroup are finite and tiny.
For an order-46 root \(h\), every element of \(C_{\mathbb M}(h)\) commutes
with \(h\), hence with

\[
g=h^2,\qquad z=h^{23}.
\]

Insertions differing by a power of \(h\) remain inside the cyclic modular
orbit already exhausted.  The genuinely new commuting directions are
therefore indexed by

\[
Q_h=C_{\mathbb M}(h)/\langle h\rangle.
\]

ATLAS centralizer orders give

\[
|Q_h|=
\begin{cases}
184/46=4,&h\in46A,46B,\\[2mm]
92/46=2,&h\in46C,46D.
\end{cases}
\]

Thus the 2B/Leech rail has three nonidentity auxiliary cosets and the
2A/Baby-Monster rail has one.  These are the complete minimal noncyclic
insertion spaces at the order-46 junction.

## 1. Resolution through the two involution centralizers

The quotients have a precise relation to the centralizer lifts already found.

On the 2A rail,

\[
C_{\mathbb M}(z)=2.B.
\]

The image \(\bar h\) has order 23 in \(B\), and its Baby-Monster centralizer
has order 46.  The cyclic subgroup generated by \(\bar h\) has order 23, so
the remaining quotient has order two:

\[
Q_{46CD}\cong C_B(\bar h)/\langle\bar h\rangle,
\qquad |Q_{46CD}|=2.
\]

On the 2B rail,

\[
C_{\mathbb M}(z)=2^{1+24}.Co_1.
\]

Projection sends \(h\) to an order-23 Conway element whose centralizer is
exactly its order-23 cyclic subgroup.  The kernel of the restricted
centralizer projection has order eight, because \(184/23=8\).  Since its
central involution \(z\) already lies in \(\langle h\rangle\),

\[
Q_{46AB}\cong
\ker\!\left(C_{\mathbb M}(h)\to C_{Co_1}(\bar h)\right)/\langle z\rangle,
\qquad |Q_{46AB}|=8/2=4.
\]

This identifies where the earlier order-eight lift residue goes: after the
cyclic parity generated by \(z\) is removed, it leaves the four-coset
augmentation space.  Moreover, the kernel lies in the extraspecial normal
subgroup \(E=2^{1+24}\).  Since \(E/\langle z\rangle\) is elementary abelian,
every subgroup of it is elementary abelian.  Therefore

\[
Q_{46AB}\cong C_2^2.
\]

The order-four ambiguity is consequently resolved without having to decide
whether the order-eight preimage is dihedral or quaternionic.  On the other
rail, the order-two quotient is necessarily \(Q_{46CD}\cong C_2\).

## 2. The next trace family

Choose a representative \(k\in C_{\mathbb M}(h)\) of a coset in \(Q_h\).
Then \(k\) commutes with \(g\) and \(z\), so the next admissible observables
are centralizer-resolved traces such as

\[
Z(g,zk;\tau)
=\operatorname{Tr}_{V^\natural_g}
\!\left(zk\,q^{L_0-1}\right).
\]

The identity coset recovers the cyclic trace already shown to be
orientation-blind.  Only the nonidentity cosets need new evaluation:

- one on the 46CD/2A rail;
- three on the 46AB/2B rail.

This is an upper bound on new trace types, not a promise that every coset
gives a distinct function.  Conjugacy inside the centralizer, projective
characters or further modular relations may identify them.

## 3. The exact orientation test

Fifth power exchanges each oriented class pair.  Precisely because \(46A\)
and \(46B\), and likewise \(46C\) and \(46D\), are distinct Monster
conjugacy classes, this exchange is **not** implemented by an element of the
Monster normalizer of \(\langle h\rangle\).  If such an \(n\) existed with
\(nhn^{-1}=h^5\), the two elements would be conjugate and the ATLAS classes
would coincide.  The transport to compare is instead the cyclotomic Galois
or power-map transport on character values and twisted modules.

Write \(\sigma_5\) for that transport and \(k^{\sigma_5}\) for the
corresponding insertion label after a compatible choice of representatives.
Orientation is recoverable at genus one only if at least one normalized
auxiliary trace fails the Galois covariance equality

\[
Z(h^2,h^{23}k;\tau)
=
\sigma_5^{-1}Z(h^{10},h^{23}k^{\sigma_5};\tau).
\]

The notation deliberately does not assert a canonical group isomorphism
between the two centralizers.  Such an isomorphism, or a common rational
form of their character data, is part of the computation rather than input.

The finite task is consequently:

1. enumerate the character orbits of \(Q_{46AB}\cong C_2^2\) and
   \(Q_{46CD}\cong C_2\) as they occur in the two actual centralizers;
2. compute the fifth-power Galois transport between the 46A/B and 46C/D
   character data, without replacing it by nonexistent Monster conjugation;
3. evaluate one plus three nonidentity-coset trace families;
4. quotient by all forced conjugacy and modular identifications;
5. declare orientation recovered only if a normalized function actually
   splits a power-five pair.

If no split occurs, genus-one orientation recovery is exhausted for the full
order-46 centralizers, not merely for their cyclic subgroups.

## 4. Relation to the seam

The cardinalities two and four are tempting because the corpus contains
binary seam and metaplectic data.  No identification follows from the counts.
The quotient \(Q_h\) is a finite group-theoretic insertion carrier.  The
genus-two seam is a characteristic orbit with analytic theta multipliers.
A bridge requires an explicit character/intertwiner whose degeneration and
modular action agree on both sides.

The centralizer quotient is nevertheless the right source to test.  It is the
smallest extension that:

- commutes with the established order-23 twist;
- is not generated by the already exhausted cyclic root;
- remembers the different 2A and 2B lift geometries; and
- has a finite complete enumeration.

## 5. Subsequent scalar exhaustion

The [full order-23 centralizer census](order-23-centralizer-exhaustion.md)
closes the scalar question left open here. For \(g=h^2\),

\[
C_{\mathbb M}(g)/\langle g\rangle\cong S_4.
\]

The quotients \(C_2^2\) and \(C_2\) found above sit inside centralizers of the
two involution types of that \(S_4\). Every individual auxiliary insertion
has order \(1,2,3\), or \(4\), coprime to 23, so together with \(g\) it
generates a cyclic subgroup. Its generalized trace is therefore a modular
branch of an ordinary series from one of

\[
23AB,\quad46AB,\quad46CD,\quad69AB,\quad92AB.
\]

All five A/B series pairs coincide. Thus the four nonidentity quotient cosets
do not recover orientation as normalized scalar genus-one traces. The
quotients remain useful carriers for a vector-valued/projective character
calculation, but no additional scalar evaluation is warranted.

## 6. Exhaustion boundary

The quotient types close the label search space. The scalar failure is now
established by the larger \(S_4\) census rather than left as a future
conditional. No claim is made that \(Q_{46AB}\) is the Rule-60 deck carrier,
that \(Q_{46CD}\) is fermion parity, or that either quotient acts on the ten
even-characteristic genus-two family. Those are exactly the next intertwiner
tests. The programme must move to a vector-valued centralizer representation,
relational multi-insertion, or genus-two sewing.
