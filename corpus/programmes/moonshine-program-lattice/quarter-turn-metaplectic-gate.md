## Result

The mixed-level CRT product does not generate a coupling merely by placing
the level-2 and level-23 carriers beside one another. Over characteristic
zero, finite-group representations are semisimple, and irreducible
representations of a direct product factor as tensor products. The smallest
possible scalar couplings are therefore controlled by common characters of
the two factors.

At projective level there is exactly one nontrivial common character:

\[
Sp(4,\mathbb F_2)\cong S_6
\longrightarrow C_2
\longleftarrow D_{11}.
\]

On the left this is the sign/determinant character of the nine-dimensional
oriented-seam carrier. On the right it is reflection parity. This gives a
unique binary coupling candidate between the Pfaffian orientation line and
the projective order-23 transition skeleton.

It cannot retain the dicyclic central sign.

## 1. Why the sign-retaining lift needs a quarter-turn phase

Use the corpus presentation

\[
\mathrm{Dic}_{11}
=\langle r,s\mid r^{22}=1,\ s^2=r^{11},\
srs^{-1}=r^{-1}\rangle,
\]

where \(r=D_5\), \(s=S\), and \(s^2=r^{11}=-I\). In the abelianization the
last relation gives \(r=r^{-1}\), hence \(r^2=1\). Since 11 is odd,
\(s^2=r^{11}=r\), so the abelianization is generated by \(s\) and

\[
\mathrm{Dic}_{11}^{ab}\cong C_4.
\]

By contrast,

\[
D_{11}^{ab}\cong C_2,\qquad
S_6^{ab}\cong C_2.
\]

Any ordinary character shared with the finite \(S_6\) permutation carrier
therefore factors \(C_4\to C_2\). It sends the quarter-turn generator to the
nontrivial binary sign but sends its square to the identity:

\[
s\mapsto-1,\qquad s^2=-I\mapsto+1.
\]

Thus the finite determinant line sees reflection orientation and necessarily
forgets the dicyclic central sign.

## 2. The precise role of \(i\)

A sign-retaining one-dimensional character needs an order-four phase:

\[
s\longmapsto i,\qquad
s^2=-I\longmapsto-1.
\]

This is an exact representation of the abelianized dicyclic relation. It is
also the rigorous sense in which the finite quarter-turn behaves like the
imaginary unit: the operator \(S(a,b)=(b,-a)\) satisfies \(S^2=-I\), and its
minimal faithful scalar character has value \(i\).

This is **not** yet a physical Wick rotation. Wick rotation analytically
continues a time coordinate or contour and must be justified by spectrum,
causality and boundary conditions. The result here is a finite/metaplectic
phase law. It becomes physically relevant only if the same phase is derived
from the sewn anomaly/Pfaffian line.

## 3. Why the metaplectic carrier is the right target

The ten-characteristic permutation action supplies only \(C_2\), but theta
transformation laws carry eighth roots of unity on a metaplectic cover.
Their phase group contains the required order-four subgroup

\[
\mu_4=\{1,i,-1,-i\}\subset\mu_8.
\]

Accordingly, the next compatibility test is no longer “does the sign
match?” It is:

> Does the restriction of the genus-two theta/Pfaffian multiplier line to
> the proposed level-23 defect lift realize the character
> \(s\mapsto i,\ -I\mapsto-1\)?

Three outcomes are distinct:

1. **only \(C_2\) matches:** the projective 240-label seam is admissible, but
   the central sign remains lost;
2. **a \(C_4\) lift matches:** the 5,280-label sign-retaining seam has the
   required phase channel;
3. **the cocycles disagree:** the mixed-level bridge is obstructed even
   though its label counts and finite groups coexist.

## 5. Exhaustion boundary

At the finite one-dimensional level the coupling classification is complete.
There is a unique nontrivial projective \(C_2\) channel, and no ordinary
finite characteristic character can retain the dicyclic center. The only
remaining scalar-phase route is the \(C_4\) metaplectic lift.

Evaluating that lift requires the actual theta and twisted-module cocycles.
It cannot be decided from group orders, carrier counts or the equality
\(S^2=-I\) alone. A successful calculation would identify the missing
anomaly line; failure would force the programme to a higher-dimensional
vector-valued intertwiner rather than a scalar phase coupling.
