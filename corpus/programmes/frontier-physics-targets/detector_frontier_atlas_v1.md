## Result

The corpus currently resolves twenty distinct frontier regions with thirty-five detector bundles. Eight bundles are exact computational detectors, eight have physical precedents, three are directly executable, ten are specified but not yet implemented as a complete packet, one is a formal emulator schedule and five are missing translation-level detectors.

The word **detector** is used operationally:

> a detector is a declared map from latent alternatives to records, together with the controls and nuisance model needed to interpret those records.

It may be a laboratory instrument, a computational observable, a mathematical certificate or—in the deepest unresolved cases—the missing action and observable dictionary itself.

## 2. Actual detector stack

### Computational structure detectors

- **DET-001 — kernel elaboration and canonicalization:** records elaboration, type, normalized expression and presentation mobility.
- **DET-002 — Monster constructor-shape address:** records capped constructor counts, prime address and overflow scars.
- **DET-003 — adaptive syntax-scar ladder:** adds labels, local roles, rooted depth and corpus-relative separation depth.

These detectors map FA-01 through FA-03. They are exact within the pinned computational policy and have no direct claim to physical observability.

### Geometry, holonomy and selection detectors

- **DET-004 — projector and mode tomography:** reconstructs (P), (Y) and (operatorname{Tr}(PY)).
- **DET-005 — coherent transfer and holonomy interferometry:** reconstructs complex transfer, loop-order commutator, forward/reverse lifted phase and the coherently referenced endpoint-frame matrix. A central sign uses one-loop/two-loop parity because it is reversal-even.
- **DET-006 — complex spectral and biorthogonal tomography:** records complex eigenvalues, left/right modes and singular values.
- **DET-007 — three-record selector bundle:** records even response, odd response and branch populations on the same runs; phase-referenced four-setting tomography reconstructs the Gram-disk coordinate and separates centre, nearest-origin and boundary selected points. Wave 7.59 adds a hard limit: two-column channel volume and nearest-origin entropy can select the same point, so their principles require an additional path, intervention or cost record.
- **DET-008 — return-map identification:** estimates fixed points, basins and tangent spectral radius.
- **DET-009 — instrument and trajectory tomography:** separates conditioned trajectories from the unconditional channel.
- **DET-010 — thermodynamic resource ledger:** records heat, work, entropy production, activity, mixing time and reset cost.
- **DET-011 — rare-event recorder:** records dwell times, first passage, switching paths and full hysteretic distributions.

Together these span FA-04 through FA-10. No single headline signal—endpoint return, phase, contraction or hysteresis—closes the bundle.

### Material, spectral and force detectors

- **DET-012 — full thermoelectric/Hall tensor:** jointly measures electrical, thermopower, thermoelectric, thermal and magnetic channels.
- **DET-013 — frequency, phase and lag spectroscopy:** measures complex response, poles, linewidths and relaxation.
- **DET-014 — hybrid avoided-crossing spectroscopy:** resolves modes, splittings, lifetimes and hybridization.
- **DET-015 — adversarial thermal-Hall bundle:** joins thermal Hall with spectra, disorder, angle and nonlinear response.
- **DET-016 — broadband optical tensor:** supplies causal diagonal/off-diagonal response and imaginary-frequency continuation.
- **DET-017 — Casimir force/torque metrology:** records force, torque, distance law, angular equilibria and switching.
- **DET-018 — surface/geometry nuisance metrology:** records Kelvin potential, roughness, domains, drift and ordinary magnetic backgrounds.

These span FA-05 and FA-11–FA-12. Their nearest complete target is a same-film causal tensor that predicts a differential Casimir observable without refitting.

### Transport and boundary detectors

- **DET-019 — particle-energy spectral block:** measures (C_{NN},C_{NE},C_{EE}), determinant and eigen-directions.
- **DET-020 — finite-(q) continuity/gauge audit:** derives currents from continuity and collides energy-density conventions.
- **DET-021 — low-frequency KPM/typicality window:** resolves where integrated rank lives in frequency.
- **DET-022 — scattering phase and Wigner–Smith delay:** measures phase, proper delays and threshold scaling.
- **DET-023 — pole, residue and localization atlas:** now executes bound/continued-sheet pole tracking, localization and a radiative toy ledger; the corrected finite-band Levinson identity remains open.
- **DET-024 — defect index and embedding response:** holds topology fixed while testing phase and response fibres.
- **DET-025 — causal spread and scrambling probes:** compares fronts, OTOCs and mixing with calibrated speed bounds.
- **DET-026 — three-scale coarse-graining audit:** measures semigroup residual and invariant survival.

These cover FA-11 and FA-13–FA-15. The immediate computational edge is the low-frequency two-current rank; the immediate model edge is the three-sector spectral-boundary Hamiltonian.

### Formal, arithmetic and statistical detectors

- **DET-027 — relation-emulator schedule:** nine path, fourteen triangle and one direct `QR-003` channel.
- **DET-028 — lattice/code invariants:** determinant, discriminant, minimum norm, weight enumerator, distance and radical.
- **DET-029 — efficient information:** nuisance-projected Fisher/Schur separation and information per calibrated cost.
- **DET-030 — complexity card:** preparation, sampling, detection, verification and error scaling.

These span FA-03 and FA-16–FA-18. DET-027 is sufficient for the declared emulator, not yet for a physical relation carrier.

### Missing translation-level detectors

- **DET-031 — flavour/neutrino joined likelihood.**
- **DET-032 — torsion/spin-force comparison bundle.**
- **DET-033 — cosmological joined likelihood.**
- **DET-034 — uniform continuum/mass-gap certificate.**
- **DET-035 — typed action and observable dictionary.**

These are called missing detectors because no corpus-specific model presently maps the proposed latent structure into their ordinary observables. They occupy FA-17–FA-20 and are the fundamental firewall.

## 4. Admission rule

A detector result advances a frontier only if:

1. the latent alternatives are named;
2. the forward detector map is frozen;
3. nuisance directions and control parity are declared;
4. at least one positive and one negative control are retained;
5. the detector bundle separates the leading conventional alternative;
6. the result survives held-out settings;
7. complexity and backaction are reported;
8. a residual equivalence class is retained when separation fails.

More repetitions of a detector that maps two mechanisms to the same record do not move the frontier.

## 5. Current exhaustion boundary

The inventory is exhausted at the level of detector *types* represented in the current corpus: structural, geometric, dynamical, thermodynamic, material, spectral, topological, arithmetic, statistical and fundamental-translation detectors are all present. Exhaustion of the science has not been reached. The open work is to instantiate, join, calibrate and adversarially test these bundles on shared carriers. That work is tracked in the open-topic register rather than inferred from this taxonomy.
