# Cavity–shield force scan with an antinode hypothesis

**Date:** 11 November 2026  
**Status:** conditional force comparison and identifiability failure; no measured force, device prediction or physical admission  
**Calculation:** [gap-scan model](../../../scripts/model-cavity-shield-antinode.mjs)  
**Verifier:** [force and degeneracy checks](../../../verification/verify/verify_cavity_shield_antinode.mjs)

## Two balances and a movable shield

The cavity and shield are independently suspended. Positive force points from the cavity toward the shield, which is to its right. At each stationary gap, the two balances first record the RF-off baseline. The driven run holds the cavity's stored energy fixed and measures the outgoing RF power, reflection phase and thermal state. The observed variables are the two signed forces and the momentum reaching the chamber or leaving the measured assembly. The [momentum closure note](../admissibility/admissibility_163_internal_stress_medium_locomotion_and_momentum_closure.md) gives the control-volume accounting.

## Minimal antinode model

[McCulloch's optical-loop proposal](https://physicsfromtheedge.blogspot.com/2016/07/lemdrive.html) suggests a force toward a shield and stronger coupling at wave antinodes, with unknown damping efficiency. The microwave [cavity model](https://pearl.plymouth.ac.uk/bms-research/1789/) uses internal geometry and supplies no external-shield response. For this comparison assume a node at zero gap, a single effective wavelength of 40 mm, a force proportional to the squared standing-wave amplitude, a peak force of 1 nN at the chosen stored energy, and steady exchange of the entire extra momentum between the cavity and shield. All are model choices. The effective wavelength and force scale have no apparatus calibration or QI derivation.

For gap \(d\), the additional force pair is \((+f,-f)\), with \(f(d)=1\,\mathrm{nN}\sin^2(2\pi d/40\,\mathrm{mm})\). The pair is \((+0.50,-0.50)\) nN at 5 mm, \((+1,-1)\) nN at 10 and 30 mm, and zero at 20 and 40 mm. The pair sum is zero by the assumed reaction. It provides no thrust on the combined assembly.

## Ordinary force comparisons

For infinite parallel Drude plates in the large-thermal-gap limit at 300 K, the attractive Casimir benchmark magnitude for a nominal overlap \(A=0.01\,\mathrm{m}^2\) is \(C=k_BT\zeta(3)A/(8\pi d^3)\). This gives \((+C,-C)\), with \(C=0.00198\) fN at 10 mm. This is an infinite-plane pressure times nominal area, with no finite-size correction or patch-potential estimate. A finite cavity and shield need their own scattering calculation or RF-off measurement. [Thermal Drude benchmark](https://www.mit.edu/~kardar/research/seminars/Casimir2010/papers/Bimonte2spheres.pdf) · [Casimir scattering theory](https://journals.aps.org/prd/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevD.80.085021)

An independent traveling-wave calibration with 0.30 W launched toward and fully absorbed by the shield gives the pair \((-P/c,+P/c)\), approximately \((-1.00,+1.00)\) nN, when back-reflection and other momentum paths are negligible. The benchmark assumes complete interception at every gap. It does not describe a reflective shield in the cavity's near field. [Radiation pressure calibration](https://tsapps.nist.gov/publication/get_pdf.cfm?pub_id=914101)

A periodic ordinary RF force can reproduce the entire antinode curve. For example, a gap-dependent driven interaction energy proportional to \(-\sin(4\pi d/\lambda)\) gives a cavity force proportional to \(-\cos(4\pi d/\lambda)\), since \(F_{\mathrm{cavity}}=\partial_d V\). Subtract its value at a node and choose its amplitude so the force becomes \(F_*[1-\cos(4\pi d/\lambda)]/2=F_*\sin^2(2\pi d/\lambda)\). The shield takes the opposite force in this two-body example. This is an algebraic counterexample to identifying the mechanism from nodes, peaks and opposite balance readings alone. Its amplitude and phase are freely chosen for the counterexample. Neither was inferred for a real cavity. [Driven-cavity force precedent](https://journals.aps.org/prl/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevLett.95.033901)

## Next discriminator

Measure both balances with RF off and on at each gap. Use a calibrated RF boundary perturbation that changes the reflected RF phase, and map the resulting ordinary force variation using the complex RF response, independent energy calibration and thermal controls. Freeze the candidate residual's wavelength, phase, amplitude and dependence on that perturbation before opening the force data. Test its nodes and peaks on held-out settings. A static Casimir model fails to account for a drive-correlated residual, but the exact periodic RF counterexample passes the gap-shape and reaction-sign tests. A QI-specific inference remains open until a model supplies an independently testable response to the RF perturbation. Balance precision and residual uncertainties are proposals until measured. The [external-shield fibre-loop null](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12567-021-00366-4) is an existing experimental constraint on its tested configuration, independent of this calculation.

Run `node verification/verify/verify_cavity_shield_antinode.mjs` from the repository root to check the nodes, peaks, both force signs, both benchmark scales and the periodic RF degeneracy.

## Fixed-gap reflection-phase sweep

Keep the shield face at 10 mm and place a voltage-tuned reactive reflector on the shield balance. Changing its reactance changes the phase of the reflection coefficient \(\Gamma=|\Gamma|e^{i\phi}\) at the same reference plane. Step the bias and wait for steady state. Measure both quadratures of the returned wave at the cavity reference plane with a calibrated directional pickup. Match \(|\Gamma|\) between settings or include its measured change in the force model. Lock the drive to the loaded resonance and servo its amplitude against a calibrated cavity-field pickup, or ringdown-calibrated stored energy. Equal forward power alone cannot hold energy fixed when the loaded linewidth changes. Recalibrate the propagation phase at the actual drive frequency if resonance tracking changes it appreciably. Record RF-off force at each bias, bound RF-on heating and monitor force transmitted by bias lines or any other external support. A separate phase shifter on a third support would add a momentum port. [Reflection-phase control precedent](https://oa.upm.es/46195/1/INVE_MEM_2016_251571.pdf) · [Cavity electromechanics precedent](https://arxiv.org/abs/1011.3067)

The declared model retains a single reactive first harmonic. With \(k=2\pi/\lambda\) and \(\psi=2kd+\phi\), the cavity's ordinary RF force is \(R=-a\cos\psi\), and the shield takes \(-R\). A mode frequency with a perturbation proportional to \(-\sin\psi\) gives this force in the weak-shift, low-loss limit. The cavity force is \(+N\hbar\,\partial_d\omega_c=(U/\omega_c)\partial_d\omega_c\), where the derivative is at fixed mode occupation \(N\) and fixed bias. Evaluate it at the servoed energy \(U\) for each setting. This derivation assumes a relative-coordinate interaction and neglects momentum in feed lines, escaping radiation and transient field storage. A real apparatus needs the RF force and its uncertainty from independent calibration, including phase-independent driven stress and heating. The chosen \(a=0.5\) nN is the amplitude of the harmonic in the preceding gap-scan counterexample. Both model wavelengths are set to 40 mm for this comparison, with no QI relation to the drive wavelength implied.

The gap-locked extra force has the same \(f(d)\) as before and is unchanged by \(\phi\) as an explicit hypothesis. A second hypothesis lets the extra force follow the reflected phase, giving \(F_*[1-\cos\psi]/2\) instead. The cavity reads \(R\) plus the chosen extra term, and the shield reads its negative. At 10 mm, the cavity values at \(\phi=0,\pi/2,\pi\) are respectively \((+0.5,0,-0.5)\) nN for RF alone, \((+1.5,+1,+0.5)\) nN with the gap-locked term, and \((+1.5,+0.5,-0.5)\) nN with the phase-following term. These are model outputs after accounting for ordinary phase-independent baselines, not achieved instrument resolution.

The mean of the readings at \(\phi\) and \(\phi+\pi\) cancels the RF first harmonic. It does not cancel ordinary phase-even forces. At 10 mm the cavity mean is 0, 1 or 0.5 nN in the three ideal cases. Across gaps, the gap-locked mean repeats the original nodes and peaks, while the phase-following mean is constant in this model. For the chosen parameters, the phase-following total is exactly \(0.5\,\mathrm{nN}+2R\), so an unknown ordinary offset and RF gain can reproduce the entire phase sweep. The next test is to measure the RF force independently of the force residual and validate it on held-out bias settings and gaps, with the candidate antinode phase law frozen. McCulloch's proposal supplies no choice between the two hypotheses. Both the sign-changing first harmonic and the failure of mechanism identification are checked by the same [verifier](../../../verification/verify/verify_cavity_shield_antinode.mjs).

## Motion at two speeds and the settled readings

The shield moves from 10 to 20 mm with the same quintic position profile in 1 s and 2 s. Let \(s=t/T\) during a run of duration \(T\), and set \(p(s)=10s^3-15s^4+6s^5\). The gap is \(10\,\mathrm{mm}+10\,\mathrm{mm}\,p(s)\) with stationary endpoints. Its acceleration is \(a_T=0.01\,\mathrm m\,p''(s)/T^2\). The first half accelerates the shield away from the cavity, and the second brakes it. At a matched gap, the 1 s acceleration is four times the 2 s acceleration.

Assume a quasistatic force \(G_i(d)\) and an instantaneous linear acceleration contribution on each balance \(i\). The balance model is \(B_i=G_i(d)+m_i^{\rm eff}a_T\). For the illustration choose signed effective pickup coefficients of +20 and −80 micrograms on the cavity and shield channels. These coefficients parameterize sensor or support coupling and are neither the moving masses nor measured transfer functions. Any resultant on the two readouts from mechanical pickup can involve the actuator and supports. The quasistatic part adds the thermal Casimir benchmark, the phase-zero RF harmonic at fixed energy and the illustrative gap-locked antinode force. The three terms use the preceding definitions and introduce no new fitted parameter.

At one quarter of each move, both runs are at 11.035 mm. The fast-minus-slow signals are +0.84375 nN for the cavity and −3.375 nN for the shield. They reverse sign at three quarters. At rest both runs give approximately (−0.50, +0.50) nN at 20 mm. Here the antinode term is at a node and the thermal Casimir benchmark is about 0.000248 fN in magnitude. The ideal acceleration term is zero as soon as the move ends. A measured balance can continue ringing or drifting after the shield stops, so compare the endpoint after instrument and thermal transients have settled.

At a matched gap, write \(B_f=G+4I\) and \(B_s=G+I\), with \(I\) the slow run's acceleration contribution. The difference gives three times that contribution, and \(G=(4B_s-B_f)/3\) recovers the rate-independent total on either balance. This calculation leaves Casimir, RF and antinode contributions combined in \(G\). The RF-off and reflection-phase controls still apply. Speed-dependent drag, delayed field response or ringing can break the two-term fit. Use a held-out duration to test the inverse-square law and a time-reversed run at the same gaps to test a velocity term. Under time reversal velocity changes sign and acceleration keeps its sign at the matched gap. These controls check the assumptions of the model. No achieved force sensitivity or QI-specific transient is established. The result belongs to the same phenomenological evidence family as the gap and phase scans.

## Bidirectional motion with a held-out duration

Add a 4 s move and run all three durations in both directions. At a forward fractional time \(s\), compare with the reverse at fractional time \(1-s\), using the measured gap as the match criterion in a physical run. This pairs equal gaps and accelerations with opposite velocities. Add an instantaneous linear velocity pickup of +0.04 nN per (mm/s) to the cavity and −0.10 nN per (mm/s) to the shield. These assumed coefficients illustrate the measurement and have no instrument calibration. The acceleration coefficients, phase setting, stored energy and gap force model are those above.

For either balance, let \(V\) and \(A\) be the velocity and acceleration contributions for the 1 s forward move at the matched gap. Use \(r=T/(1\,\mathrm{s})\), with \(T\) the run duration. Then the half-difference is \(O_T=V/r\) and the half-sum is \(E_T=G+A/r^2\). The 1 s and 2 s half-sums give \(A=4(E_1-E_2)/3\) and \(G=(4E_2-E_1)/3\). The 2 s half-difference should be half the 1 s value. The 4 s pair is predicted by \(G+A/16\pm V/4\) before using that pair in the calculation.

At the matched gap of 11.035 mm, the modeled readings are as follows. Entries are ordered (cavity, shield) in nN.

| Move time | Forward | Reverse |
|---|---:|---:|
| 1 s | (+2.994, −7.002) | (+2.151, −4.893) |
| 2 s | (+1.940, −3.100) | (+1.518, −2.045) |
| 4 s | (+1.623, −1.993) | (+1.412, −1.465) |

The 1 s decomposition there gives \(G=(+1.448,-1.448)\), \(A=(+1.125,-4.500)\) and \(V=(+0.422,-1.055)\) nN. At 15 mm the acceleration vanishes while the 1 s half-difference is \((+0.750,-1.875)\) nN. Both pickup terms vanish at rest. The forward run settles at 20 mm and \((-0.50,+0.50)\) nN, and the reverse settles at 10 mm and \((+1.50,-1.50)\) nN, rounded at fixed RF setting. The [force verifier](../../../verification/verify/verify_cavity_shield_antinode.mjs) recovers the three modeled terms and predicts both 4 s traces across 101 matched gaps.

Reversal identifies the direction-odd part, which can include a response lag or nonlinear drag. The inverse-square fit identifies the direction-even part with that speed scaling. For example, a term proportional to \(v^2\) is direction-even and scales as \(1/T^2\) at each gap on these time-scaled paths, so the 4 s check alone cannot attribute the entire fitted term to acceleration. An even speed-dependent force at the 15 mm zero-acceleration crossing would reject the pure acceleration model. Away from that crossing, change the trajectory shape to vary velocity and acceleration independently at a selected gap. Check the transfer of both channels against independent position and actuator measurements. Compare RF-off and phase-controlled runs to constrain the composition of \(G\). These are proposed controls and simulated readings within the same evidence family as the original gap scan.

## Same gap and velocity with different accelerations

Anchor both quintic profiles at the quarter-time crossing of the 10 to 20 mm move. The crossing is \(d_*=11.03515625\) mm because \(p(1/4)=53/512\). For a chosen travel \(L\), set the initial gap to \(d_*-Lp(1/4)\). This holds the crossing gap fixed as travel changes. The 10 mm, 1 s move goes from 10 to 20 mm. The 20 mm, 2 s move goes from 8.96484375 to 28.96484375 mm. At the matched forward crossing, \(v=Lp'(1/4)/T\) is +10.546875 mm/s in both cases. Since \(a=Lp''(1/4)/T^2\), the accelerations are +0.05625 and +0.028125 m/s². Reverse trajectories cross at three quarters of elapsed duration with the same accelerations and opposite velocities. The comparison assumes the same stored RF energy, reflection setting and thermal state at the crossing.

Keep the previous linear pickup terms and add an illustrative term \(q_i v^2\) to each balance. Choose \(q_i=(+0.005,-0.010)\) nN per (mm/s)² in cavity and shield order. These are chosen coefficients in a simulation. All entries below are in nN.

| Condition | Cavity | Shield |
|---|---:|---:|
| 10 mm in 1 s, forward crossing | +3.551 | −8.115 |
| 20 mm in 2 s, forward crossing | +2.988 | −5.865 |
| 10 mm in 1 s, reverse crossing | +2.707 | −6.005 |
| 20 mm in 2 s, reverse crossing | +2.144 | −3.755 |
| Parked at the crossing gap | +1.448 | −1.448 |

Write the direction averages as \(E_{10}=G+Q+A\) and \(E_{20}=G+Q+A/2\), where \(Q=q_i v^2\) and \(A\) is the acceleration contribution in the 10 mm run. The direction difference within each run still measures twice the linear velocity term. The difference between the direction averages gives \(A/2\), and \(2E_{20}-E_{10}=G+Q\) extrapolates to zero acceleration at the same speed. Subtract the independently parked reading \(G\) to recover \(Q=(+0.556,-1.112)\) nN. Doubling both durations a second time while holding both travels fixed gives a new matched speed of 5.2734375 mm/s. The recovered contribution falls to one quarter in the quadratic model. The [verifier](../../../verification/verify/verify_cavity_shield_antinode.mjs) checks both matched trajectories, the recovered acceleration and the half-speed ratio.

Matching a nonzero speed cancels any instantaneous term that depends only on speed in the acceleration difference. The extrapolation assumes the remaining acceleration response is linear and has no speed-dependent change between the paired runs. A single parked subtraction measures a direction-even motion residual at one speed. A second matched-speed pair tests quadratic scaling. In a physical trial, unequal RF energy, thermal history or balance response after different preceding trajectories can bias both estimates. The next discriminator is a matched crossing with those conditions measured independently, plus the parked and half-speed comparisons. The numbers are simulated within the same evidence family as the preceding gap scans. They establish no instrument sensitivity or new force.

## Physical force scales and rotation

An emitter on one balance sending a fully intercepted 0.30 W beam to an absorber on the other gives approximately (−1.00, +1.00) nN, assuming normal incidence, negligible reflection and accounted thermal recoil. At the original 10 mm gap, the thermal parallel-plate Casimir benchmark above is only 0.00198 fN. A separate small-gap reference with 1 mm² of ideal parallel reflecting plates at a 1 μm separation gives approximately (+1.30, −1.30) nN from \(\pi^2\hbar c A/(240d^4)\). The latter is the zero-temperature ideal-conductor benchmark. Actual materials, temperature, surface potentials and alignment affect the measured force. The source and receiver loads belong to the selected control volume. [Radiation-pressure calibration](https://tsapps.nist.gov/publication/get_pdf.cfm?pub_id=914101) · [Parallel-surface Casimir experiment](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0203002)

For a fixed-axis 10 kg thin ring of radius 0.10 m spinning at \(\omega=1000\,\mathrm{rad/s}\), the inertia is \(I=MR^2=0.10\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2}\), angular momentum is \(J=I\omega=100\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2/s}\), and rim centripetal acceleration is \(R\omega^2=10^5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}\). A perfectly balanced ring carries this radial load internally. A 1 μm mass-centre offset produces a rotating bearing-force amplitude \(Me\omega^2=10\,\mathrm N\). This is the bearing load before applying the transfer function to either balance. Ramping at \(\alpha=100\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}\) gives tangential acceleration \(R\alpha=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}\) and motor reaction torque of magnitude \(I\alpha=10\,\mathrm{N\,m}\). A stopped motor torque term and the ordinary \(\omega^2\) terms are distinguished by comparing steady coast with matched-speed spin-up and spin-down. A 1 kg test body 1 m along the axis also feels about 0.658 nN of Newtonian attraction from the ring. The on-axis ring formula is \(GMm z/(z^2+R^2)^{3/2}\). At fixed geometry that force is common to both spin directions.

The weak-field angular momentum contribution to the gyroscope precession on the rotor axis is \(\Omega_{\rm LT}\simeq 2GJ/(c^2 z^3)\), keeping the leading far-field term. At \(z=1\) m it is \(1.49\times10^{-25}\,\mathrm{rad/s}\) for this example. The observable is gyro orientation against an independent reference. It persists in steady rotation, flips with source spin reversal and falls as \(z^{-3}\) in the far field. The strong-field Kerr solution motivates the comparison, and the rotor estimate uses linearized general relativity. Gravity Probe B measured Earth's frame-dragging drift magnitude as \(37.2\pm7.2\) milliarcseconds per year. The 39.2 milliarcseconds per year GR prediction for its orbit is about \(6.0\times10^{-15}\,\mathrm{rad/s}\), roughly forty billion times the illustrative ring rate. A translation balance would require a separate force coupling and calibration to infer a gyro precession. [Weak-field gyroscope derivation](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9909054) · [Gravity Probe B final result](https://arxiv.org/abs/1105.3456)

The force calibration discriminator uses beam power, a parked gap, and measured bearing and actuator transfer to both balances. Steady ±\(\omega\) tests identify the spin-odd component, which can also contain ordinary magnetic or mechanical effects. Matched \(\omega\) with opposite \(\alpha\) checks ramp torque pickup. The GR benchmark would additionally require a gyro or equivalent angular readout and the predicted distance dependence. The illustrated rotor dimensions, imbalance and ramp are scale inputs. The calculation supplies no calibrated rotor-to-balance coupling or physical amplitude for the hypothetical antinode force. The [scale verifier](../../../verification/verify/verify_cavity_shield_antinode.mjs) checks the force levels and the reversal signs within the same comparison family.
