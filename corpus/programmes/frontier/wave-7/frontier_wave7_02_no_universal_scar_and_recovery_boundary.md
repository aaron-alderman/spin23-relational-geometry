## The no-universal-scar statement

Let \(\pi:X\to Y\) be a quotient or detector map. If the retained theory contains only functions that factor through \(Y\), then two states \(x_1,x_2\) in the same fiber

\[
\pi(x_1)=\pi(x_2)
\]

are exactly indistinguishable within that theory. No estimator using only the retained data can reconstruct which state occurred.

This sounds elementary, but it blocks the strongest possible reading of the scar idea:

\[
\boxed{
\text{not every physical quotient leaves an accessible remnant of everything it removes.}
}
\]

### Topology without orientation

An index can retain a protected mode count while forgetting phase, representation embedding or selector orientation. Holding the index fixed while changing boundary realization is therefore a mandatory control, not an optional refinement.

### Dynamics without thermodynamic identity

The same contractive reduced map can arise from equilibrium relaxation, driven steady currents or measurement conditioning. Consequently, contraction is not a scar that uniquely identifies heat, work or entropy production.

## Recovery hierarchy

For a distinction removed at the base quotient, recovery can occur only by enriching the retained theory:

1. **refine the detector** with a second face or label;
2. **retain nonlinear records** such as higher moments or waiting-time derivatives;
3. **retain integral/topological data** such as monodromy or a discriminant group;
4. **retain a twisted sector or stabilizer label** rather than the coarse orbit space alone;
5. **retain dynamics** so hidden modes return through a memory kernel;
6. **retain ultraviolet boundary memory** outside the universal low-energy quotient.

If every permitted enrichment remains constant on the same fibers, the distinction is physically absent from the declared theory.

## A precise admissibility question

The foundational question is therefore not “does information survive?” but:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Which distinctions are required to remain recoverable under the reductions admitted as physical?}
}
\]

Quantum theory, gauge theory, statistical mechanics and effective field theory answer this differently. Gauge-related descriptions should not remain physically distinguishable. Coarse-grained microstates may be deliberately forgotten. Charges, anomalies, causal response and reproducible detector statistics may be required to survive.

## Implication for the grand programme

Admissibility cannot mean maximal information preservation. It must distinguish:

- redundancy that should be quotiented;
- microscopic information that may be discarded;
- protected information that must survive;
- and observational information required to test the theory.

The shape sought by the corpus is therefore a hierarchy of permitted forgetful maps together with their protected invariants—not a rule forbidding forgetting.
