## 1. Why the six-cycle is exact

Decimal digital roots are reduction modulo \(9\), with the zero residue
usually printed as \(9\).  The nonzero residues split according to their
greatest common divisor with nine:

- the units \(\{1,2,4,5,7,8\}\);
- the nonzero elements of the ideal \(3\mathbb Z/9\mathbb Z\), namely
  \(\{3,6\}\);
- the zero class.

Multiplication by the unit \(2\) preserves these strata.  Moreover, \(2\)
has multiplicative order six modulo nine and generates

\[
(\mathbb Z/9\mathbb Z)^\times\simeq C_6.
\]

The visually striking six-cycle is therefore exactly the Cayley orbit of a
generator of the unit group.  The \(3\leftrightarrow6\) orbit is the induced
action on the nonzero part of the ideal \((3)\), and zero is fixed.

This is worthwhile finite mathematics.  No vortex hypothesis is required to
explain it.

## 3. Base dependence is the decisive universality test

The decimal pattern depends on notation.  In base \(b\), the corresponding
digital-root reduction is modulo \(b-1\).  The doubling orbit therefore
changes with the base:

- base 8: modulo 7, \(1\to2\to4\to1\), period 3;
- base 10: modulo 9, period 6;
- base 12: modulo 11, period 10.

A proposed physical law cannot depend on whether an observer writes the same
integer in decimal, octal or duodecimal notation.  Any universal promotion
must therefore extract an invariant independent of the chosen base, or derive
why the physical apparatus implements modulus nine.  The post supplies
neither map.

This base-change test is stronger than arguing over the visual pattern.  It
identifies the exact missing arrow:

\[

\not\Rightarrow
\text{electromagnetic dynamics}.
\]
