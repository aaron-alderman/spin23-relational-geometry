## Result

The apparent conflict among kenosis, selfhood and material security becomes
more tractable after a type correction. They need not be three competing
scalar objectives:

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\text{security}&=\text{viability boundary},\\
\text{selfhood}&=\text{invariant structure},\\
\text{kenosis}&=\text{outward flow}.
\end{aligned}}
\]

The question is then not “which one wins?” but “which trajectories preserve
the boundary and the invariant while allowing a meaningful outward flow?”

## 1. Security is a floor, not an infinite objective

Let \(r(t)\) denote a reserve of money, time, health or practical capacity,
with family-adjusted minimum \(r_{\min}\). The viability condition is

\[
r(t)\ge r_{\min}.
\]

If income or replenishment is \(y\), maintenance is \(m\), and outward giving
is \(k\), then

\[
\dot r=y-m-k.
\]

At the boundary, forward viability requires

\[
\dot r\ge0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
k\le y-m.
\]

Security therefore has a natural stopping rule: once a robust viability
margin exists, further accumulation is not demanded by the constraint itself.
Maximizing wealth without limit is a new objective, not the logical meaning of
security.

## 2. Selfhood is continuity through transformation

Selfhood is better modeled as an invariant packet than as a fixed coordinate.
A person can change work, beliefs, skills, social roles and possessions while
preserving recognizable commitments, relationships, memories and modes of
responsibility.

Thus “be oneself” need not mean remaining stationary. It asks whether the
trajectory remains inside an integrity set \(I\), or whether a transformation
faithfully transports what is essential. This mirrors the corpus's repeated
intertwiner problem: resemblance after change is not enough; some identity-bearing
structure must actually survive the passage.

The two pathologies are opposite:

- rigid self-preservation mistakes every change for betrayal;
- self-erasure calls a trajectory faithful after its defining invariants have
  disappeared.

## 3. Kenosis is a controlled transfer, not the zero state

In this model kenosis is \(k(t)\): capacity passing outward. Setting the source
state permanently to zero is not automatically more kenotic, because it may
eliminate future capacity, abandon dependants or transfer costs to others.

A simple viable feedback bound is

\[
k(t)\le
\max\{0,y-m+\alpha(r-r_{\min})\}.
\]

The reserve margin permits a temporary pulse above current surplus. As the
boundary approaches, the permitted pulse tightens. This is not a universal
moral formula; it merely demonstrates that genuine outward flow and continued
capacity are mathematically compatible.

## 4. The paradox is dynamic

The three roles interact across time:

- building security now can enlarge later capacity to give;
- giving can create trust, relationship and mutual aid, changing future
  security;
- work aligned with selfhood may improve durable earning capacity;
- work that destroys integrity may increase short-term reserve while shrinking
  the future feasible set;
- family makes the state coupled: one person's reserve, risk and sacrifice
  alter the admissibility of everyone attached to the decision.

This means a snapshot can be misleading. A period of accumulation may belong
to a kenotic trajectory; a dramatic sacrifice may belong to an unsustainable
or self-serving trajectory. The path and its effects matter more than the
instantaneous label.

## 5. When the three truly conflict

The type correction does not make tragedy disappear. Emergencies can make the
viability and integrity conditions mutually incompatible. Someone may choose
to cross a security boundary for another person, or refuse security purchased
by a violation of conscience.

Mathematics can expose the crossing and its consequences. It cannot determine
the ultimate ordering of values without importing a moral or theological
commitment. That boundary should remain explicit.

## 6. Connection to the selector atlas

Wave 7.55 showed that an admissible region does not determine its objective.
The same correction applies here:

\[
\text{life conditions define what remains possible};
\quad
\text{values determine which possible path is sought}.
\]

But the present three-role typing goes one step further. Some things called
“values” are actually constraints, invariants or flows. Treating them all as
numbers to maximize creates a paradox that partly comes from the model rather
than the life.

## Interpretive synthesis

\[
\boxed{
\text{security keeps the trajectory viable;}\quad
\text{selfhood keeps it faithful;}\quad
\text{kenosis keeps it open to others.}
}
\]

A mature trajectory is not a static compromise at equal weights. It is an
adaptive control process: preserve enough, remain someone, and let something
real pass outward.

## Boundary

This document uses mathematical language to clarify an existential structure.
It does not derive an ethical duty from linear algebra, identify personal life
with a physical system, or claim that every sacrifice must preserve material
reserve. Exceptional commitments can deliberately override viability; the
model's contribution is to make that override visible rather than automatic.
