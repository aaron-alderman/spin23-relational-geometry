## Result

Claimipedia and Sourcery do share something exact, but it is smaller than a
common scientific algebra. Both retain directed paths and distinguish the order
of their steps. The pinned Claimipedia capture supplies 43 relationship records
on 38 claim nodes. Sourcery composes admitted transformations as ordered words.
That is the genuine overlap: a directed path skeleton.

The overlap stops there. None of the 43 Claimipedia relationship records carries
the endpoint signatures, dimensions, declared changes, assumptions,
irreversible losses, evidence references, analogy status and bounded authority
required for admission as a Sourcery `ScientificTransformation`. The exact
direct-admission count is therefore

\[
\boxed{0/43}.
\]

This is not a complaint that the records are incomplete Claimipedia records.
They perform a different job. `Supports`, `Challenges`, `Qualifies` and
`DependsOn` are epistemic relations between claims. Sourcery edges are typed
scientific operations between objects. Treating one vocabulary as the other
would erase the reason for both systems.

## Why the resemblance is nevertheless useful

The graph still tells us where a translation might be sought. It retains source,
target, direction and relationship type, and its finite paths can be enumerated.
There are 39 length-two path instances in the capture, represented by 12
relationship-type words. The word `Qualifies > Qualifies` alone occurs across
12 distinct endpoint pairs. A relation word therefore does not determine what
scientific objects were transformed, what changed, or what was lost.

Sourcery's ALE ledger answers precisely those questions:

\[
\nu(p)=(A(p),L(p),E(p)),
\]

while preserving the ordered path beside it. No map from the four Claimipedia
relation labels to those three ledgers is authored in the capture. Many maps can
be programmed; none is extracted.

## The semantic mismatch that matters

A Claimipedia `Challenges` edge remains a first-class relation that readers can
inspect. A transformation rejected by Sourcery never enters the admitted path
category: it stays in a rejection receipt. It is not a negative edge that can be
composed or cancelled. Likewise:

- `Supports` does not specify a physical or mathematical operation;
- `Qualifies` does not say whether a projection, continuation, transport or
  update occurred;
- `DependsOn` does not distinguish an assumption from evidence, a required
  object, a causal input or a computational prerequisite.

That difference is productive. Claimipedia maps the ecology of reasons around a
claim. Sourcery audits whether a particular transformation may be carried from
one typed object to another. One is not a lossy serialization of the other.

## The tempting completion and why it fails

It is easy to wrap every claim in the same opaque signature, assign zero SI
dimensions, use capture positions as rule IDs and cite the capture itself as
evidence. That synthetic graph can be made to pass a schema. It would also make
the result arbitrary: every scientific distinction was supplied by the wrapper,
not found in the source.

This fires the existing translation kill test. Matrix units or generic graph
paths can reproduce incidence while generating an unrelated operator algebra.
Programmability is not provenance. The same observation prevents the graph from
yielding a Jordan product, exceptional cubic, physical action or detector map.

## What remains open

OT-026 and OT-027 are sharpened, not closed. A real bridge now has a finite
acceptance test:

1. expose stable authored edge identities rather than relying on capture order;
2. give the participating claims exact scientific signatures and dimensions;
3. decide which relations are scientific transformations and which remain
   epistemic context outside the admitted category;
4. attach changes, assumptions, losses, evidence, analogy status and authority;
5. pass at least one nontrivial path through the unchanged Sourcery admission
   protocol; and
6. predict a held-out composition, product identity or detector record.

The reason for the boundary is now clear. Claimipedia says *why one claim bears
on another*. Sourcery says *whether a specified operation can carry one typed
object into another without hiding its assumptions and losses*. A future
Jordan or physical translation must preserve both jobs. The present capture
provides the places to look, but not yet the bridge.

## Implication

This is good news in the practical sense. The search no longer needs to ask
whether a suggestive graph algebra secretly is the scientific algebra. It is
not. The next improvement is concrete metadata and one held-out compositional
prediction. If that succeeds, it is useful theoretical engineering. If the
typed product then predicts a physical record unavailable from a generic graph
encoding, the new-physics question can reopen. Until then, the scientific
firewall remains intact.
