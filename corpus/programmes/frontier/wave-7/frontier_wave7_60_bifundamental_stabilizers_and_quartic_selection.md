## Result

The isotropic and causal-centre channels lie on different symmetry strata.
The distinction survives both the real locking model and the complex
metric-preserving carrier.

Put a rank-two map in singular form

\[
E=\begin{pmatrix}
\sigma_1&0\\
0&\sigma_2\\
0&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

## 1. Real locking carrier

Under

\[
SO(3)\times SO(2),
\qquad E\mapsto RES^T,
\]

the connected stabilizers are:

| stratum | stabilizer dimension | orbit dimension |
|---|---:|---:|
| zero | 4 | 0 |
| rank one | 1 | 3 |
| rank two, \(\sigma_1=\sigma_2\) | 1, diagonal \(SO(2)\) | 3 |
| rank two, \(\sigma_1\ne\sigma_2\) | 0 | 4 |

Thus the earlier residual diagonal rotation exists only on the isotropic
stratum. The causal checkpoint is anisotropic and has no connected stabilizer
in this real model.

## 2. Complex metric carrier

Under

\[
SU(3)\times SU(2),
\qquad E\mapsto U_3EU_2^{-1},
\]

with singular values held fixed, the strata are:

| stratum | stabilizer dimension | orbit dimension |
|---|---:|---:|
| zero | 11 | 0 |
| rank one | 4 | 7 |
| rank two, equal singular values | 3, diagonal \(SU(2)\) | 8 |
| rank two, unequal singular values | 1, \(U(1)\) | 10 |

The complex carrier therefore retains one phase rotation at a generic
full-rank point, but the enhanced diagonal \(SU(2)\) still occurs only when
the two singular values agree.

For the partial-sewing checkpoint,

\[
\sigma_1^2=\frac{16}{13},
\qquad
\sigma_2^2=\frac{10}{13}.
\]

It lies on the generic unequal stratum.

## 3. What the minimal quartic can select

The invariant ring relevant at quartic order is generated by

\[
I_1=\operatorname{tr}(E^\dagger E),
\qquad
I_2=\det(E^\dagger E).
\]

Writing

\[
a=\sigma_1^2,
\qquad b=\sigma_2^2,
\]

a transparent positive witness is

\[
V_0(E)
=\alpha(a+b-v^2)^2+\beta(a-b)^2,
\qquad \alpha,\beta>0.
\]

Its full-rank minimum obeys

\[
a=b=v^2/2.
\]

More generally, any unspurioned quartic symmetric under exchange of the two
source singular axes either forces \(a=b\) at an isolated interior stationary
point or is flat in the anisotropy direction. It cannot select a particular
unequal pair such as \((16/13,10/13)\) as an isolated stable vacuum.

## 4. Minimal anisotropic repair

An unequal stationary point can be selected by a term such as

\[
-h(a-b),
\]

or by unequal quadratic masses for the two source directions. But either term
requires a declared axis or reflection spurion. It is not invariant under the
full source \(SU(2)\) or \(SO(2)\).

The corpus already contains a candidate kind of datum: the normal/tangent
face reflection that distinguishes the two response channels. The remaining
question is provenance—whether that reflection acts on this bifundamental
field with the required normalization, rather than merely having the same
two-label vocabulary.

## 5. Consequence

The selector fork is now a symmetry-breaking fork:

\[
s=0
\Longrightarrow
\text{isotropic lock and enhanced diagonal stabilizer},
\]

\[
s=c\ne0
\Longrightarrow
\text{causal residual sewing and generic anisotropic stabilizer}.
\]

The causal construction does not inherit the old locking model's residual
rotation for free. Conversely, the minimal symmetric locking potential does
not derive the causal checkpoint.

## Boundary

The potential classification is scoped to low-degree unspurioned invariants
of one bifundamental. Higher-degree potentials can be engineered to have
unequal minima, but their coefficients encode additional selection data and
do not by themselves solve provenance.
