# Other Randall Programmes

These branches reveal a common research style but are not consequences of one
another. They must not be collapsed into a single warped-universe theory.

## 1. Sequestering and anomaly mediation

In the Randall--Sundrum supersymmetry-breaking work, visible fields are
localized on one brane while supersymmetry breaks elsewhere. Geometric
separation suppresses direct flavor-sensitive contact terms, but the
super-Weyl anomaly survives and generates soft terms related to beta functions.

Corpus contribution:

- a selector can suppress local relations without removing anomaly-mediated
  global relations;
- the low-energy scar can encode renormalization-group data rather than the
  microscopic location of the source;
- “hidden” does not mean causally or quantum mechanically disconnected.

No supersymmetric particles have been established, and anomaly mediation does
not validate the warped hierarchy model.

## 2. Partially interacting and dissipative dark matter

The double-disk programme asks whether a minority dark component can have its
own radiation and cooling while the majority remains effectively collisionless.
This yields a sequence

\[
\text{dark microphysics}
\to
\text{dissipation}
\to
\text{rotationally supported morphology}
\to
\text{stellar/gas/lensing or annihilation record}.
\]

This is valuable to the corpus because it refuses a false binary between
ordinary collisionless dark matter and no dark matter. Different species can
occupy different interaction and relaxation classes while sharing gravity.

The comet-impact proposal is a long downstream chain and should be assigned a
lower authority than the underlying allowed dark-sector model. It requires the
dark disk, Solar vertical motion, Oort-cloud perturbation and crater chronology
to remain jointly adequate. Later gas analyses explicitly found the available
measurements insufficient to decide whether a thin disk exists.

## 3. Inflation, moduli and baryogenesis

Earlier Randall work treated late weak-scale inflation as a possible solution
to the cosmological moduli problem while preserving baryogenesis and density
perturbations. The durable corpus lesson is again conditional: solving one
abundance problem by dilution creates obligations to regenerate or preserve
every other required relic and perturbation.

This is a conservation-ledger principle, not evidence for the corpus's
particular cosmology.

## 4. Gravitating baths, islands and Gauss-law obstruction

Long-range gravity makes spatial subsystem factorization delicate. A dressed
bulk excitation has an asymptotic gravitational record. If an “island” is
defined as an entanglement wedge disconnected from that asymptotic region, the
Gauss-law record can contradict naive commutativity with the exterior.

In carrier language:

\[
\text{local excitation}
\xrightarrow{\text{gravitational dressing}}
\text{asymptotic relation}
\xrightarrow{\text{detector}}
\text{nonlocal energy record}.
\]

A quotient that discards the middle arrow is inadmissible. Known examples can
evade the simplest contradiction when the graviton is massive, which makes the
Karch--Randall light-massive-graviton branch relevant to the information
problem as well as localization.

## 5. Common method

Across the otherwise distinct programmes, Randall repeatedly uses the same
methodological structure:

1. retain a standard effective theory where it is successful;
2. modify the geometry, sector content or boundary condition minimally;
3. calculate what relation survives the separation;
4. identify a spectral, collider, gravitational or astrophysical record;
5. keep the proposal conditional when nuisance data or completion is missing.

That method, rather than any one speculative conclusion, is the strongest
contribution to the corpus.
