## Decision

All nine lanes will be attempted. They will not receive equal effort indefinitely. Each begins with a tractable calculation that can map new structure, build a bridge or change an existing closure verdict. A lane that reveals a generic universality class remains useful even if it does not immediately select a unique physical theory.

The nine lanes are:

1. measurement-first inverse reconstruction;
2. renormalization-group and OPE closure;
3. nonperturbative lattice or functional response;
4. boundary theory and anomaly inflow;
5. spectral triples and graded superconnections;
6. amplitudes and bootstrap constraints;
7. modular/operator-algebraic reconstruction;
8. defects and topological response;
9. emergent or analogue response.

## What the corpus already contains

The boundary/inflow and defect lanes are already advanced conditional programmes. The corpus has domain-wall anomaly checks, explicit resolvents, mirror-mode obstructions, Toeplitz indices, Pfaffian zero-mode tests and a proof that strictly passive upper-half-plane response has no interior determinant winding.

The measurement, lattice and emergence lanes have usable seeds but no decisive physical implementation. The RG/OPE lane has exact free-UV and spurion constraints but not the interacting mixing calculation.

The spectral-triple and modular lanes have nearby ingredients but have not actually been attempted at their defining type. Completely positive projections are not automatically modular conjugations, and a gauge-equivariant pairing table is not automatically a spectral triple. The amplitude lane likewise has passive scattering models but no genuine crossing/bootstrap analysis.

## Execution order

The first breadth pass is ordered by information value:

\[
L2\rightarrow L1\rightarrow L4\rightarrow L6\rightarrow L5\rightarrow L7\rightarrow L3\rightarrow L8\rightarrow L9.
\]

- **L2 first:** it can invalidate or dynamically select the source plane before more structure is built on it.
- **L1 second:** an operational definition can resolve ambiguities that algebra cannot.
- **L4 and L6 next:** they directly test chirality/orientation and observable predictivity.
- **L5 and L7:** these are possible action/state selection mechanisms, but are vulnerable to hiding free data in a Dirac operator or faithful state.
- **L3, L8 and L9:** these provide nonperturbative and experimental realizations once the operators and target observable are sharply typed.

## Two-ledger evaluation rule

A lane advances the **landscape ledger** when it supplies a new equivalence, classification, model, calculational technique, obstruction, analogue or plausible experimental bridge.

It advances the stronger **closure ledger** if it supplies at least one of:

- a unique physical source map;
- an RG-stable or dynamically selected real frame;
- a source-derived orientation;
- a finite normalization/contact prescription;
- a physical channel map;
- a non-generic dimensionally normalized observable.

Finding another copy of \(H_2(\mathbb R)\), \(Spin(2,3)\), a symplectic group or an exceptional representation therefore can map the landscape and identify a universality class. It does not, without further consequences, establish microscopic ontology or predictive closure. The detailed revised method is recorded in [the landscape-first continuation map](programme_lane_expansion_03_landscape_value_and_closure_value_are_separate_ledgers.md).
