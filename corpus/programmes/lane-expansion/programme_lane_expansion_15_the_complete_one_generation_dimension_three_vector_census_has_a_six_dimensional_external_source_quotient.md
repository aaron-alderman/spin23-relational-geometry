## Result in ordinary language

Before asking whether two currents mix into each other, one must count every current they are allowed to mix with. This is like listing every direction on a map before claiming that one road is closed.

For one Standard-Model generation completed by a right-handed neutrino, the complete set of local Hermitian, gauge-singlet Lorentz vectors of canonical dimension three contains nine elementary operator types. After removing a total gradient and treating the Abelian gauge equation of motion correctly, seven phase-current representatives remain. Hypercharge is gauged rather than a global experimental port, so the external-global-source quotient has dimension six.

Therefore the natural renormalization problem is at least six-dimensional:

\[
\boxed{
\text{the proposed incidence/}B-L\text{ plane sits inside a six-dimensional external-source quotient.}
}
\]

Yukawa and anomaly constraints identify protected directions inside this space. They do not reduce the complete operator-mixing module to two dimensions.

## 1. Assumptions and meaning of “complete”

The census assumes:

- one fermion generation;

“Complete” applies only under those assumptions. Three generations introduce flavor-matrix currents and enlarge the module substantially.

## 2. The nine raw operators

### Six fermion phase currents

For each irreducible chiral multiplet,

\[
j_f^\mu=f^\dagger\bar\sigma^\mu f,
\qquad
f\in\{Q,u^c,d^c,L,e^c,N^c\},
\]

with all gauge indices contracted within the multiplet.

### Two Higgs bilinears with one derivative

The complex vector (H^\dagger D^\mu H) decomposes into two Hermitian pieces:

\[
j_H^\mu=iH^\dagger\overleftrightarrow D^{\mu}H,
\]

and

\[
\partial^\mu(H^\dagger H).
\]

The second is a total gradient. When coupled to a conserved external source (s_\mu), integration by parts gives

\[
\int s_\mu\partial^\mu(H^\dagger H)
=
-\int(\partial\cdot s)(H^\dagger H)=0
\]

up to boundaries and contact terms. It is excluded from the conserved-source quotient, but must be restored if longitudinal sources or local contact terms are studied.

### One Abelian gauge-field vector

The hypercharge field strength supplies

\[
\partial_\nu B^{\nu\mu}.
\]

The non-Abelian analogues (D_\nu W^{a\nu\mu}) and (D_\nu G^{A\nu\mu}) carry adjoint indices and are not gauge singlets by themselves.

Gauge-variant Chern-Simons currents are not admitted to this local gauge-invariant basis.

No other elementary monomial has the required dimension and quantum numbers: a fermion bilinear already uses dimension three; a Higgs vector requires exactly two Higgs fields and one derivative; and a field-strength vector requires one derivative acting on the dimension-two Abelian strength.

## 3. Equation-of-motion and hypercharge quotients

The Abelian gauge equation has the schematic form

\[
\partial_\nu B^{\nu\mu}
=g'\left(
\sum_fY_fj_f^\mu+Y_Hj_H^\mu
\right).
\]

Thus one may eliminate (\partial B) in favor of the seven phase currents. This leaves seven representatives, not six.

For an **external global source** classification, the hypercharge combination is then quotient-equivalent to a gauge-field equation-of-motion/improvement direction. Removing that gauged direction leaves

\[
7-1=6
\]

independent global-source directions.

This distinction matters:

- an off-shell local-operator basis may retain (\partial B) or a chosen representative of the hypercharge current;
- an on-shell global-source quotient treats that direction as gauged/redundant;
- contact terms can remember which representative was used.

A loop calculation must declare which of these objects it renormalizes.

## 4. Protected subspaces are not the full module

The four Yukawa interactions impose the incidence matrix

\[
V_H=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&1&0&0&0&0&1\\
1&0&1&0&0&0&-1\\
0&0&0&1&1&0&-1\\
0&0&0&1&0&1&1
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

It has rank four and a three-dimensional nullspace generated by hypercharge, baryon number and lepton number. Quotienting gauged hypercharge leaves two classical global symmetries, (B) and (L). Electroweak anomalies preserve only (B-L) as a continuous quantum axis under the stated Dirac-neutrino assumptions.

This gives two different dimension counts:

\[
\underbrace{6}_{\text{complete external-source quotient}}
\supset
\underbrace{2}_{\text{classically Yukawa-preserving global symmetries}}
\supset
\underbrace{1}_{\text{anomaly-protected }B-L}.
\]

The other directions do not disappear. They are broken operators and must be present in the renormalization and form-factor problem.

## 5. Consequence for the incidence plane

The proposed incidence current is not a Yukawa symmetry and therefore lies in the broken sector. Pairing it with (B-L) selects one protected and one broken direction inside a six-dimensional quotient.

For that plane to be dynamically meaningful, the loop matrix must show more than closure by assumption. It must exhibit one of:

1. an invariant two-dimensional subspace containing (B-L);
2. a uniquely separated broken eigenvector beside (B-L);
3. an experimentally defined projection that consistently removes the other four directions.

Without one of these, the two-channel plane is a chosen slice rather than an RG object.

## 6. Flavor expansion

With three generations, each fermion current becomes a Hermitian flavor matrix

\[
(j_f^\mu)_{ij}=f_i^\dagger\bar\sigma^\mu f_j.
\]

There are nine real Hermitian components for each of the six species before gauge and Yukawa restrictions, rather than one. Yukawa matrices then act as noncommuting flavor spurions. The complete three-generation problem is therefore not obtained by merely repeating the one-generation (7\times7) matrix three times.

This makes the one-generation result a necessary checkpoint, not the final Standard-Model answer.
