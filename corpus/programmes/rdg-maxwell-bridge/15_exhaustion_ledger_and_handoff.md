by the RDG--Maxwell comparison has either:

## What would move the corpus next

Only three next tranches would materially change the conclusion.

### A. Numerical-analysis tranche

Define a genuine family of changing meshes and prove or measure:

- uniform boundedness of transport and restriction;
- conditioning versus refinement depth and vertex degree;
- approximation order for Maxwell fields;
- stability with nonconforming interfaces and moving boundaries.

Success would turn the finite certificates into a numerical method. Failure
would close the remeshing branch without affecting the detector discipline.

### B. Topological-state tranche

Implement fields as differential characters and cut/glue operations as maps
of relative differential-cohomology data. The retained record must include
curvature, integral class and holonomy. Success would generalize the finite
topology receipts; failure would show that the current record schema is too
small.

### C. Experimental tranche

Choose one coherent multi-mode device, freeze a calibration and predict a
held-out complex transfer matrix under loop-phase reversal. Carry all eight
nuisance channels with uncertainties. This is the only route that tests
whether the admissibility/defect grammar improves physical discovery rather
than merely organizing known mathematics.

## Stop rule

Do not reopen this finite arm merely to:

- add a larger complex with the same commuting identities;
- rediscover another standard compatible discretization;
- rename a port, cohomology class or passive transfer as an RDG effect;
- treat nonzero (Q)-power as anomalous before closing the nuisance ladder;
- infer fundamental discreteness, aether, gravity or new particles from the
  existence of a useful discrete carrier.

Reopen it when there is a new theorem, calibrated device dataset, materially
different topology-changing operation, or a failure that survives the current
controls.

## Final implication

The arm supports a restrained claim:

> Admissibility can be implemented as a sequence of commuting, energetic,
> topological and observational compatibility tests. In Maxwell-like systems,
> these tests do not replace known physics; they make the loss of a relation
> explicit and experimentally auditable.

That is enough to justify the programme as an interface between corpus
grammar and current physics. It is not evidence that the grammar is
fundamental.
