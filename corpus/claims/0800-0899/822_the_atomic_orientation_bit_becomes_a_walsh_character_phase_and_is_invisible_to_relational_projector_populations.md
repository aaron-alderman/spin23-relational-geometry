# The Atomic Orientation Bit Becomes a Walsh-Character Phase and Is Invisible to Relational Projector Populations

## Claim status

Exact Fourier-duality theorem for the two source-to-projector orientations. It constructs the appropriate phase-sensitive relational observable and proves that population-only character data cannot distinguish the orientations.

## 1. Atomic orientation as a translation

The two graded source-to-projector maps preserve the handle sign and differ by global reversal of the channel-to-side sign:

\[
(h,c)\longmapsto(h,+c)
\]

versus

\[
(h,c)\longmapsto(h,-c).
\]

On the four atomic cells of \(V_4\), their difference is translation by the channel-flip element

\[
T_C=(u\ e)(\nu\ d).
\]

Thus the final atomic \(\mathbb Z_2\) orientation torsor is literally one of the square translations.

## 2. Walsh transform turns translation into phase

Let \(F\) be the normalized Walsh–Hadamard matrix with character order

\[
(1,H,C,HC).
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
FT_CF^T
=\operatorname{diag}(+1,+1,-1,-1).
}
\]

The trivial and handle characters are even under channel translation. The channel and product characters are odd.

This is ordinary finite Fourier duality:

\[
\text{translation of atoms}
\longleftrightarrow
\text{multiplication by character phase}.
\]

## 3. Projector populations forget the orientation

Let \(P_\chi=|v_\chi\rangle\langle v_\chi|\) be a Walsh-character projector. Under the orientation translation,

\[
|v_\chi\rangle
\longmapsto
\chi(T_C)|v_\chi\rangle,
\]

but

\[
P_\chi
\longmapsto
\chi(T_C)^2P_\chi
=P_\chi.
\]

Therefore every observable diagonal in the character basis—including character populations, eigenvalues and positive diagonal residues—has identical values for the two orientations.

The two atomic Jordan isomorphisms collapse to the same map on the commutative algebra generated by the four relational projectors.

This is a new information-loss boundary:

\[
\boxed{
\text{the relational projector algebra cannot retain the final orientation bit}.}
\]

## 4. Phase-sensitive relational witness

Orientation survives in coherences between characters of opposite translation parity. For example,

\[
X_{1C}
=|v_1\rangle\langle v_C|
+|v_C\rangle\langle v_1|
\]

obeys

\[
T_CX_{1C}T_C^{-1}=-X_{1C}.
\]

Likewise, any interference between

\[
\{1,H\}
\quad\text{and}\quad
\{C,HC\}
\]

is orientation odd. Interference within the same parity class is orientation even.

A normalized relational orientation observable therefore requires:

1. coherent preparation or access to two character modes;
2. a phase reference between an even and an odd mode;
3. an off-diagonal response measurement.

Positive character populations alone are insufficient.

## 5. Jordan versus oriented ternary information

The result matches an earlier structural warning: symmetrized Jordan triples forget outer-order orientation that an oriented TRO product retains.

Here the loss is explicit. Passing from character amplitudes to rank-one projectors removes the signs

\[
(+,+,-,-).
\]

An oriented amplitude map or TRO intertwiner can retain them; a commutative order algebra of projectors cannot.

Thus the atomic and relational phases require different orientation observables:

- atomic phase: channel-sign times flux-bias population correlation;
- relational phase: even/odd character interference.

## 6. Implication for causal residues

The positive rank-one residue model built from spectral projectors is phase blind. Even if its supports coincide with the Walsh character lines, its diagonal residue weights cannot select the orientation torsor.

The response needs either:

- off-diagonal residue coherences;
- a noncommuting matrix-valued frequency dependence;
- an oriented boundary amplitude before squaring to positive residues;
- or an external phase reference.

This sharpens why retarded/advanced pole prescriptions and positive residues did not select the final bit.

## 7. Stop-everything tests

- **Interference success:** an even/odd character coherence is physically measurable and reverses under flux orientation.
- **TRO success:** a source-derived oriented ternary map retains the sign phases before projector reduction.
- **Population falsifier:** orientation is claimed from character eigenvalues or diagonal positive weights alone.
- **Decoherence failure:** environmental or measurement dephasing removes all even/odd character coherences.
- **Reference failure:** the relative phase can be redefined independently and no boundary datum fixes it.
- **Jordan-information warning:** the commutative projector algebra is asked to distinguish maps that differ only by eigenvector signs.
