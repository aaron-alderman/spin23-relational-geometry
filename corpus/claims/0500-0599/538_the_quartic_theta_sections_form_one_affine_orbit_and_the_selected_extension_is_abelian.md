# The Quartic Theta Sections Form One Affine Orbit, and the Selected Extension Is Abelian

**Date:** 17 October 2026  
**Branches:** B01, B03, B04, B07, B09, B10, B13 and B14  
**Claim status:** exact section-classification and stabilizer theorem; physical field-label selection remains conditional

## 1. Result

Write

\[
G=M/I\cong\mathbb Z_4\langle r\rangle
\oplus\mathbb F_2^3\langle w,s,t\rangle,
\]

with quartic character \(\chi_r\).

A labelled binary theta section is determined by two order-four lifts

\[
g_1=-r+u_1,\qquad
g_2=-r+u_2,
\]

where \(u_1,u_2\in\mathbb F_2^3\) are distinct. Every such pair has:

\[
\chi_r(g_1)=\chi_r(g_2)=3,
\qquad
2g_1=2g_2=2r,
\]

and therefore reproduces the phase table \(0,3,3,2\).

There are

\[
\boxed{8\cdot7=56}
\]

ordered phase-compatible sections.

## 2. Character-preserving automorphisms

Every automorphism preserving \(\chi_r\) has the form

\[
r\mapsto r+u_0,
\qquad
u\mapsto Au,
\]

where

\[
u_0\in\mathbb F_2^3,
\qquad
A\in GL(3,2).
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Aut}(G,\chi_r)
\cong AGL(3,2),
\qquad
|\operatorname{Aut}(G,\chi_r)|=8\cdot168=1344.}
\]

On section tails it acts by

\[
(u_1,u_2)\mapsto(Au_1+u_0,Au_2+u_0).
\]

The 56 ordered sections form one orbit. For the selected ordered pair

\[
(u_1,u_2)=(s,t),
\]

the stabilizer has order

\[
\boxed{24.}
\]

The unordered-pair stabilizer has order 48.

The repeated order 24 is structurally interesting—it equals the twice-punctured projective code group—but equality of orders does not identify the groups' actions.

## 3. What selects \(s-r,t-r\)

The phase function alone does not select a section; all 56 lie in one affine orbit.

Once the quotient tails are fixed by field labels as

\[
u_1=s,\qquad u_2=t,
\]

the odd character requirement fixes the \(r\)-coefficient uniquely:

\[
\boxed{
g_1=s-r,\qquad g_2=t-r.}
\]

Thus the section is unique relative to the labelled null-leg identification. The remaining physical question is whether the Standard-Model/Furey data genuinely select those null-leg labels.

## 4. The extension is not Heisenberg

The subgroup generated by \(g_1,g_2\) has relations

\[
g_1^2=g_2^2=2r,
\qquad
(2r)^2=0,
\qquad
[g_1,g_2]=0.
\]

It has eight elements and group type

\[
\boxed{
\langle g_1,g_2\rangle
\cong\mathbb Z_4\oplus\mathbb Z_2.}
\]

This is an abelian non-split quadratic extension

\[
0\to\mathbb Z_2\langle2r\rangle
\to\mathbb Z_4\oplus\mathbb Z_2
\to\mathbb F_2^2
\to0.
\]

It is not the noncommutative finite Heisenberg group. The cocycle is symmetric and records the quadratic dot product; it produces no commutator.

The actual Heisenberg group requires both logical \(X\) labels and their dual \(Z\) characters:

\[
L_X\oplus L_X^*.
\]

The quartic extension should therefore be understood as the phase/quadratic half of the eventual Heisenberg-metaplectic structure.

## 5. Consequences

### Strengthened

- the exact phase-compatible section family is classified;
- the selected section is unique after field-labelled tails are fixed;
- its stabilizer is computed;
- the central extension type is known exactly.

### Corrected

- the common-square cocycle alone is not a finite Heisenberg group;
- the phase match does not supply noncommutative \(X/Z\) commutation;
- order-24 stabilizer coincidence is not an action intertwiner.

## 6. Stop-everything and falsification gates

### Stop-everything

1. The code/Furey field map derives the tails \(s,t\) without a fitted basis.
2. Adjoining the dual characters extends \(\mathbb Z_4\oplus\mathbb Z_2\) to the canonical finite Heisenberg group.
3. The resulting Weil representation recovers both the quartic phase and the \(Spin(2,3)\) wedge action.

### Falsifiers and boundaries

- Phase compatibility alone leaves 56 sections.
- The extension generated by the lifts is abelian.
- A Heisenberg interpretation without a dual-character sector is false.
- Stabilizer-order equality alone supplies no canonical identification.
