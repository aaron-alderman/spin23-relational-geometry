# The source five sign bits are totally isotropic and do not equal the ambient quadratic shell

## Status

**Exact quadratic-form comparison and direct-identification no-go.**

Two independently derived objects have underlying group

\[
(\mathbb Z_2)^5:
\]

1. the affine sign-bit quotient of the sixteen-field \(SO(10)\) spinor code;
2. the discriminant group of the ambient Hadamard intersection
   \[
   J\cong U(2)\oplus\langle-2\rangle\oplus U(-2).
   \]

They are not isometric as quadratic modules. With the natural sixteen-field pairing, the source sign-bit quotient is totally isotropic. The ambient shell has a nondegenerate quarter-valued quadratic form.

\[
\boxed{
\text{same five-bit group}
\quad\not\Rightarrow\quad
\text{same quadratic object}.}
\]

## 1. The source charge and sign-bit lattices

Let the sixteen even-parity five-bit strings label one \(SO(10)\) spinor generation. Define:

- \(F\): the all-one fermion-number word;
- \(b_i\): the five occupation/sign-bit words;
- \(q_i=F-2b_i\): the five sign-charge words.

The fermion-number-plus-charge lattice is

\[
L_Q=\mathbb Z\langle F,q_1,\ldots,q_5\rangle.
\]

Using the ordinary coordinate pairing on the sixteen fields,

\[
(F,F)=16,
\qquad
(F,q_i)=0,
\qquad
(q_i,q_j)=16\delta_{ij}.
\]

Therefore

\[
\operatorname{Gram}(L_Q)=16I_6.
\]

The affine sign-bit lattice is

\[
B=\mathbb Z\langle F,b_1,\ldots,b_5\rangle.
\]

It contains \(L_Q\) with

\[
[B:L_Q]=2^5=32.
\]

## 2. The index-32 quotient

The quotient is generated by the five classes

\[
[b_i]\in B/L_Q.
\]

Because

\[
2b_i=F-q_i\in L_Q,
\]

each has order two, and the five are independent:

\[
\boxed{
B/L_Q\cong(\mathbb Z_2)^5.}
\]

This is the exact five-bit quotient appearing in the source qubit construction.

## 3. Its induced quadratic form is zero

The lattice \(B\) is even. Explicitly,

\[
(b_i,b_i)=8,
\qquad
(b_i,b_j)=4\quad(i\ne j),
\qquad
(F,b_i)=8.
\]

For any

\[
x=\sum_i\epsilon_i b_i,
\qquad
\epsilon_i\in\{0,1\},
\]

one has

\[
\frac{(x,x)}2\in\mathbb Z.
\]

Likewise,

\[
(x,y)\in\mathbb Z
\]

for every pair of sign-bit representatives. Hence the restriction of the discriminant form of \(L_Q\) to the gluing subgroup \(B/L_Q\) is

\[
\boxed{
q_{\rm sign}\equiv0.}
\]

The source quotient is a totally isotropic gluing subgroup inside \(A_{L_Q}\). It is not itself a nondegenerate discriminant module.

This is exactly what an even overlattice requires: an isotropic subgroup of the smaller lattice's discriminant form.

## 4. The ambient five-bit shell is different

For

\[
J=U(2)\oplus\langle-2\rangle\oplus U(-2),
\]

the discriminant group is

\[
A_J\cong(\mathbb Z_2)^5,
\]

but its quarter exponent is

\[
q_J(a,b,c,d,e)
=2ab+3c+2de\pmod4.
\]

Its distribution is

\[
0^{10},\quad1^6,\quad2^6,\quad3^{10}.
\]

In particular, \(q_J\) is nonzero and nondegenerate. Its normalized Gauss sum is an eighth root of unity, whereas the zero form on the source glue has Gauss sum \(32\) and is maximally degenerate.

Therefore no group isomorphism

\[
B/L_Q\longrightarrow A_J
\]

can preserve the natural quadratic forms.

## 5. Consequence for the five-field/Furey comparison

The new ambient shell cannot be identified directly with the five source sign-bit halvings while retaining the natural sixteen-field pairing.

Any valid bridge must add structure that changes the quadratic refinement. Credible possibilities are:

1. puncturing one or more field coordinates;
2. twisting by chirality or an orientation character;
3. passing to a relative/support quotient rather than the full sign-bit quotient;
4. coupling to the independent quartic line;
5. using a different, explicitly derived bilinear form from the Furey/Jordan carrier.

Without one of these operations, the direct identification is falsified.

## 6. Why puncturing remains credible

The unpunctured sixteen-field code is anomaly-complete and its transversal phase is trivial on the stabilizer lattice. Puncturing removes a cancellation partner and creates nontrivial logical phases.

The contrast

\[
q_{\rm sign}=0
\qquad\text{versus}\qquad
q_J\ne0
\]

is therefore compatible with the programme's broader pattern: nontrivial quarter phases appear only after selecting a face, puncture, orientation or boundary.

This is a reason to test punctured and relative sign-bit modules next, not a reason to discard the source code.

## 7. Consequences

### Established

- the source affine sign-bit quotient is \((\mathbb Z_2)^5\);
- it is a totally isotropic subgroup for the natural sixteen-field discriminant form;
- its quadratic form is identically zero;
- the ambient shell has a nondegenerate quarter-valued form;
- the two objects are not quadratically isometric.

### Falsified

- equality of order \(32\) does not identify the two five-bit structures;
- the five source bits cannot be assigned directly to the five ambient discriminant generators while preserving the natural form;
- the ambient Gauss phase is not already present in the unpunctured sign-bit quotient.

### Open

- whether puncturing produces the ambient quadratic isometry;
- whether a chirality twist changes the source form to the required Brown class;
- whether the five selected Furey support fields carry a different relative form;
- whether the independent quartic orientation line supplies the missing refinement.

## 8. Stop-everything and falsification gates

### Stop-everything

1. A source-derived puncture or orientation twist produces \(q_J\) exactly.
2. The five-field Furey support quotient has distribution \(0^{10},1^6,2^6,3^{10}\).
3. A Jordan determinant or stress-response pairing induces the ambient form on the sign-bit quotient.
4. The code group preserves the twisted form and maps canonically into \(O(q_J)\).

### Boundaries

- no quadratic map may be inferred from the shared group type;
- changing the pairing requires a physical or algebraic derivation;
- the zero source form and nondegenerate ambient form lie in different isometry classes;
- arbitrary basis fitting cannot repair degeneracy.
