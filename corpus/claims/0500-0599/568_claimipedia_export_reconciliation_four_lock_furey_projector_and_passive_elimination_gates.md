## 8. Independent passive-elimination comparison

Consider the microscopic passive two-mode system:

\[
\dot x=-g(k)y,
\qquad
\dot y=g(k)x-\gamma y,

\gamma>0.
\]

Its energy

\[
E=\frac12(x^2+y^2)
\]

obeys

\[
\boxed{\dot E=-\gamma y^2\le0.}
\]

Eliminating \(y\) in Laplace space gives:

\[
K_{\rm eff}(s,k)
=s+\frac{g(k)^2}{s+\gamma}.
\]

The memory term is positive real for \(\operatorname{Re}s>0\):

\[
\operatorname{Re}\frac{g(k)^2}{s+\gamma}>0.
\]

This places the controlled elimination inside the existing causal Herglotz/positive-real response architecture.

### Derivative coupling gives diffusion

If

\[

\]

then:

\[
K_{\rm eff}(s,k)
=s+\frac{v^2k^2}{s+\gamma}
\simeq
s+\frac{v^2}{\gamma}k^2.
\]

Hence:

\[
\boxed{D=\frac{v^2}{\gamma}.}
\]

### Constant mixing gives a gap

If instead

\[
g(k)=m

is a momentum-independent sector mixing, then:

\[
K_{\rm eff}(0,0)=\frac{m^2}{\gamma}.

This is a relaxation gap/rate, not a diffusion constant:

\[
\boxed{

\qquad
D\ne\frac{m^2}{\gamma}
\text{ without a derivative dictionary}.}
\]

Thus the algebraic scaling \(m^2/\gamma\) is generic second-order elimination, but its physical dimension and meaning depend on how the mixing enters.
