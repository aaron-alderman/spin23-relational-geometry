# The Weak, Pauli and Gaussian Quartic Centres Match, but the Match Cannot Split the Affine Arrow Pair

**Date:** 17 October 2026  
**Branches:** B01, B03, B04, B07, B09, B10, B11, B12, B13 and B14  
**Claim status:** exact central-presentation and factor-through-kernel no-go; a nonsplit action on the full deformation extension remains open

## Executive result

Three formerly separate quartic structures now have the same exact presentation:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\text{weak Weyl lift}:&\quad
\langle W\mid W^4=1,\ W^2=-I\rangle,\\
\text{Pauli centre}:&\quad
\langle iI\mid(iI)^4=1,\ (iI)^2=-I\rangle,\\
\text{Gaussian units}:&\quad
\left(\mathbb Z[i]/(1+i)^3\right)^\times
=\langle i\mid i^4=1,\ i^2=-1\rangle.
\end{aligned}
\]

All three are canonically **of type** \(C_4\), and their order-two central squares correspond. But a square-preserving comparison still has two choices:

\[
W\longmapsto+i
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
W\longmapsto-i.
\]

More importantly, the two Gaussian arrow candidates

\[
e_0,
\qquad
e_0+u+e_1

\]

differ by \(k=u+e_1\), the exact kernel of universal arrow evaluation. Any central comparison that sees only their common \(E_{12}\) image necessarily identifies them.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
C_4\text{ convergence is exact, but a central map alone cannot select the Gaussian arrow candidate}.}
\]

## 1. Gaussian unit group at the first quartic rung

Let

\[
R_3=\mathbb Z[i]/(1+i)^3.
\]

The ring has eight elements. Its maximal ideal contains four elements, leaving four units. Exact enumeration gives unit orders

\[
1,2,4,4.
\]

Therefore

\[
R_3^\times\cong C_4,
\]

generated by \(i\), with

\[
i^2=-1,
\qquad
i^4=1.
\]

This is multiplicative structure. It is distinct from the additive group

\[
(R_3,+)\cong C_4\oplus C_2

\]

identified earlier. Keeping those two groups separate is essential.

## 2. Generator-preserving maps are still twofold

The automorphism group of \(C_4\) has two elements:

\[
i\mapsto i,
\qquad
i\mapsto-i.
\]

Both fix the central square:

\[
(\pm i)^2=-1.
\]

Accordingly, the data

\[
W^2=-I
\longleftrightarrow
(iI)^2=-I
\longleftrightarrow
i^2=-1

\]

identify the order-two centre but not an oriented fourth root. A further orientation, positive-frequency convention, Pfaffian trivialization or global gauge quotient is needed to choose between the two lifts.

## 3. Why the affine Gaussian candidates remain indistinguishable

In the certified cohomology basis

\[
(u,e_1,e_0,e_4),

\]

the candidates have masks

\[
e_0:4,
\qquad
e_0+u+e_1:7.
\]

Their difference is

\[
k=u+e_1:3.
\]

Universal evaluation has kernel

\[
\ker\mathcal E=\langle k\rangle

\]

and sends both candidates to the same relationship corner:

\[
\mathcal E(e_0)
=\mathcal E(e_0+k)
=E_{12}.
\]

Suppose a proposed weak-to-Gaussian comparison factors as

\[
H^1
\xrightarrow{\mathcal E}
\langle E_{12}\rangle
\xrightarrow{\psi}
C_4.
\]

Then automatically

\[
\psi\mathcal E(e_0)
=\psi\mathcal E(e_0+k).
\]

No choice of \(+i\) versus \(-i\) changes this fact. The obstruction is not merely the automorphism of \(C_4\); it is loss of the kernel direction before the central comparison is made.

## 4. What a successful selector must do

A selector must be nontrivial on \(k=u+e_1\). There are only a few credible mechanisms:

1. **Nonsplit representation lift.** Construct generator matrices over \(R_3\) whose multiplication defect evaluates differently on the two affine candidates.
2. **Pfaffian orientation.** Show that the fermion determinant line couples to \(k\), fixing one fourth-root lift.
3. **Global gauge quotient.** Include hypercharge and the \(\mathbb Z_6\) identification and prove only one weak lift is compatible with the complete matter representation.
4. **Opposite-glue/ribbon coupling.** Produce a bilinear or quadratic refinement pairing the invisible cohomology class with the second glue face or genus-one cycle.

A map defined solely on the evaluated finite arrow cannot succeed.

The agreement of centres is genuine convergence. The invisible kernel prevents it from becoming a selection theorem prematurely.

## 6. Stop-everything and falsification gates

### Stop-everything

1. An \(R_3\)-valued representation lift exists for exactly one affine candidate.
2. The global Standard-Model gauge quotient fixes \(W\mapsto+i\) or \(-i\) and acts nontrivially on \(k\).
3. A Pfaffian or opposite-glue quadratic refinement evaluates differently on \(e_0\) and \(e_0+k\).

### Falsification boundaries

- Isomorphic \(C_4\) groups do not determine a canonical generator.
- Matching central squares does not distinguish inverse fourth roots.
- A construction factoring through universal arrow evaluation cannot see its kernel.
- The multiplicative group \(R_3^\times\cong C_4\) is not the additive group \(C_4\oplus C_2\).
- Naming the weak lift as the Gaussian selector without a full deformation action is insufficient.
