# The Global \(\mathbb Z_6\) Quotient Absorbs the Weak Fourth Root but Still Does Not Orient the Two Cycles

**Date:** 17 October 2026  
**Branches:** B01, B03, B04, B07, B09, B10, B11, B12, B13 and B14  
**Claim status:** exact faithful-quotient and one-generation representation calculation; no induced action on the shell-deformation kernel has been constructed

## Executive result

The weak Weyl reflection appeared in the simply connected weak factor as the order-four lift

\[
\pm iP_2\in SU(2),
\qquad
(\pm iP_2)^2=-I_2.
\]

After incorporating integer hypercharge and the faithful Standard-Model quotient

\[
G_{\rm SM}
=\frac{SU(3)_c\times SU(2)_L\times U(1)_{Y_6}}{\mathbb Z_6},
\]

the two inverse fourth-root representatives become the same quotient element:

\[
\boxed{
(+iP_2,e^{+i\pi/2})
\sim
(-iP_2,e^{-i\pi/2}).}
\]

Their ratio and their squares equal the cube of the diagonal \(\mathbb Z_6\) kernel generator. Consequently the common class has order two in the faithful group and acts identically on every one-generation multiplet.

This removes the \(\pm i\) ambiguity at the level of the faithful matter representation. It also corrects the provisional hope that the global quotient might choose one Gaussian affine arrow: the quotient action remains identical on the two completed Furey cycles and has no currently defined action on the invisible class \(u+e_1\).

## 1. Faithful kernel normalization

Use integer hypercharge \(Y_6=6Y\). The completed generation has

| species | colour triality | weak parity | \(Y_6\) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| \(Q\) | \(1\) | \(1\) | \(1\) |
| \(u^c\) | \(-1\) | \(0\) | \(-4\) |
| \(d^c\) | \(-1\) | \(0\) | \(2\) |
| \(L\) | \(0\) | \(1\) | \(-3\) |
| \(e^c\) | \(0\) | \(0\) | \(6\) |
| \(\nu^c\) | \(0\) | \(0\) | \(0\) |

The diagonal kernel is generated by

\[
z=(\omega_3,-I_2,e^{i\pi/3}).
\]

Its cube is

\[
z^3=(I_3,-I_2,-1).
\]

This is the relation that absorbs the weak central square into a hypercharge half-turn.

## 2. The quotient Weyl representative

Consider

\[
g_+=(I_3,+iP_2,e^{+i\pi/2}),
\qquad
g_-=(I_3,-iP_2,e^{-i\pi/2}).
\]

Their squares are

\[
g_+^2=g_-^2=(I_3,-I_2,-1)=z^3.
\]

Their ratio is also

\[
g_-g_+^{-1}=z^3.
\]

Therefore

\[
[g_+]=[g_-]

\]

in the quotient, and

\[
[g_+]^2=1.
\]

The fourth-root behaviour belonged to the chosen lift in \(SU(2)\); the faithfully acting combined gauge transformation is an involution.

## 3. Exact action on one generation

For a weak doublet of charge \(Y_6=y\), the representative contributes

\[
(iP_2)e^{iy\pi/2}.
\]

For a weak singlet it contributes only the hypercharge phase. Both representatives give the same action:

| species | faithful action |
|---|---|
| \(Q\) | \(-P_2\) on the weak doublet |
| \(u^c\) | \(+1\) |
| \(d^c\) | \(-1\) |
| \(L\) | \(-P_2\) on the weak doublet |
| \(e^c\) | \(-1\) |
| \(\nu^c\) | \(+1\) |

Every action squares to the identity. The quotient equivalence is therefore visible directly on the full completed matter representation, not only abstractly in the group.

## 4. The two matter cycles still receive the same sign

The completed cycles are

\[
\gamma_C=(Q,u^c,e^c,L),
\qquad
\gamma_D=(Q,d^c,\nu^c,L).
\]

Multiplying their scalar factors gives

\[
\chi(g;\gamma_C)=(-1)(+1)(-1)(-1)=-1,
\]

and

\[
\chi(g;\gamma_D)=(-1)(-1)(+1)(-1)=-1.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\chi(g;\gamma_C)\chi(g;\gamma_D)^{-1}=+1.}
\]

The global quotient removes the inverse-fourth-root ambiguity but still does not distinguish aligned from opposite ribbon orders or select a relative cycle phase.

## 5. Consequence for the Gaussian branch

Cycle 500 left two potential repairs:

1. use the global quotient to choose \(+i\) versus \(-i\);
2. construct an action nontrivial on \(k=u+e_1\).

The first is now closed in a subtler way: the two signs are quotient-equivalent rather than one being preferred. The physical quotient element is order two and has a unique completed-generation action.

But no map has been constructed from that gauge action to the four-dimensional deformation cohomology

\[
H^1=\langle u,e_1,e_0,e_4\rangle.
\]

In particular, the calculation does not make the quotient element act differently on

\[
e_0
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
e_0+u+e_1.
\]

The Gaussian selector therefore remains pinned at a nonsplit representation lift or a Pfaffian/glue refinement that sees \(k\) before universal evaluation.

## 6. Interpretation of the quartic convergence

The weak \(C_4\) remains useful, but its role is now clearer:

- on the simply connected \(SU(2)\) cover it records the spinorial lift of the Weyl reflection;
- in the faithful Standard-Model group its square is absorbed by the diagonal \(\mathbb Z_6\) kernel;
- on matter it becomes an involutive permutation/sign operation;
- it is not by itself a physical fourth-root observable;
- a surviving \(\mu_4\) phase must live in a projective, boundary, Pauli or Pfaffian representation rather than the faithful ordinary matter action alone.

This is exactly the kind of cover-versus-quotient distinction the programme must retain when comparing \(Spin\), Clifford and finite-code structures.

## 7. Stop-everything and falsification gates

### Stop-everything

1. A projective boundary or Pfaffian representation retains the cover-level \(C_4\) after the ordinary matter action descends to order two.
2. That representation acts nontrivially on \(k=u+e_1\) and selects one Gaussian candidate.
3. The retained fourth root equals the glue/Pauli \(\mu_4\) centre under a field-labelled map.

### Falsification boundaries

- \(+iP_2\) and \(-iP_2\) are not distinct faithful transformations once the accompanying inverse hypercharge quarter-turns are included.
- The cover-level order four must not be called an order-four matter symmetry of the quotient.
- Equal cycle characters cannot orient the two Furey cycles.
- Quotient equivalence on matter does not automatically construct an action on deformation cohomology.
- A projective fourth root may survive on boundary states even when ordinary bulk matter sees an involution; this requires proof, not assumption.
