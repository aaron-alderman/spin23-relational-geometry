## 1. The mod-two kernel criterion

On any representation, the residual order-two element acts by

\[
g^3=(-1)^{X+Z}.
\]

The diagonal spin–weak element acts by

\[
(-1)^Fz_{2L}=(-1)^{F+w_L}.
\]

The representation descends through the quotient precisely when

\[
X+Z-F-w_L\equiv0\pmod2.
\]

For the established matter table this reproduces:

- weak-doublet fermions (Q,L): even (X+Z);
- weak-singlet fermions (u^c,d^c,e^c,\nu^c): odd (X+Z);
- the Higgs doublet: odd (X+Z).

## 2. Bilinear closure theorem

Let two left-handed Weyl fields (psi_a,psi_b) obey

\[
X_a+Z_a\equiv1+w_a,
\qquad
X_b+Z_b\equiv1+w_b
\pmod2.
\]

A bosonic scalar appearing in a gauge-invariant bilinear coupling has charge equal, up to signs and conjugations, to a sum or difference of the two fermion charges. Signs are irrelevant modulo two. Hence

\[
X_\Phi+Z_\Phi
\equiv
(X_a+Z_a)+(X_b+Z_b)
\equiv
w_a+w_b
\pmod2.
\]

The centre of (SU(2)_L) acts multiplicatively on a tensor product, so every irreducible component of (psi_a\otimes\psi_b) has

\[
w_\Phi\equiv w_a+w_b\pmod2.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{X_\Phi+Z_\Phi\equiv w_\Phi\pmod2,}
\]

which is exactly the bosonic quotient condition.

The result is independent of detailed Clebsch–Gordan coefficients. It uses only the central character, so it applies equally to elementary scalars selected by fermion bilinears and to composite scalar operators.

## 4. The odd-\(Z\) field is a branch choice, not a repair inside the quotient

For a primitive weak-singlet field (S) with ((X,Z)=(0,1)),

\[
(-1)^{X+Z}=-1,
\qquad
z_{2L}=+1.
\]

So (g^3) does not act trivially after the proposed diagonal identification. There are two consistent ultraviolet routes:

### Quotient completion

Identify (g^3) with ((-1)^Fz_{2L}) in the global gauge group. The primitive odd-(Z) weak singlet is then not an allowed representation. The covering (mathbb Z_6) has faithful new quotient (mathbb Z_3).

### Covering completion

Do not take the diagonal quotient. Admit the primitive odd-(Z) singlet in the covering theory and let its VEV remove the order-two remnant, leaving (mathbb Z_3).

Both routes can have the same infrared discrete symmetry, but they are globally different theories with different bundles, line operators, admissible representations and possible bordism anomalies.

**Primitive-field trigger:** a weak-singlet scalar with odd (X+Z) falsifies the quotient completion unless it is removed or the global group is changed.

**Closure boundary:** the theorem covers bilinear-sourced and polynomial-composite scalars; it does not prove that the complete spectral action contains only such fields.

**Incidence stop:** kernel compatibility is necessary for the quotient branch but is not sufficient for texture protection.
