# Note to Eric Weinstein on a small Jordan–Shiab analogue

**Status: draft; not sent**

**Subject: A finite Jordan “ship in a bottle” that may be close to your language**

Eric—

Your Shiab idea helped me recognize the shape of a problem I have reached from a rather different direction. I have been studying a rank-one idempotent \(P\in\operatorname{Herm}_3(\mathbb C)\), its \(1\oplus2\) Peirce split, and the uniquely normalized relative generator

\[
X=I-3P\sim\operatorname{diag}(-2,1,1).
\]

The cross-Peirce spaces have relative weights \(\pm3\). If the projector is allowed to vary, then \(D_AP\) is automatically entirely cross-Peirce, so it behaves like an internal-valued one-form measuring transport between the selected line and its complementary plane. This made me wonder whether the missing operation should be viewed as a small, typed Jordan analogue of a Shiab contraction: not another algebra, but a gauge-covariant way of transporting the finite relational geometry into curvature, matter, and an equation of motion.

There is a pleasantly rigid piece of geometry behind it. The orbit of primitive rank-one projectors is

\[
\mathbb{CP}^2\cong SU(3)/S(U(1)\times U(2)),
\]

and its tangent bundle is exactly the cross-Peirce bundle,

\[
T\mathbb{CP}^2\cong\operatorname{Hom}(L,Q).
\]

Consequently

\[
c_1(T\mathbb{CP}^2)=3h,
\qquad
K^{-1}\cong\mathcal O(3),
\]

while locally the same factor appears as

\[
-i\operatorname{Tr}\!\left(X,D_AP\wedge D_AP\right)
=3i\operatorname{Tr}\!\left(P,D_AP\wedge D_AP\right).
\]

So the “three” is simultaneously the relative Peirce weight and the canonical curvature coefficient of the projector orbit.

There is also a useful lift subtlety. The element

\[
e^{2\pi iX/3}=\omega I_3
\]

acts trivially on the projector and on \(T\mathbb{CP}^2\), but faithfully as \(\omega\) on the universal fundamental module \(L\oplus Q\). Thus any Shiab-like operation defined only in adjoint/projective data necessarily loses the central \(\mathbb Z_3\); the operation has to be paired with a compatible fundamental-module lift.

The question I would be interested in asking in your language is: **does this look to you like a genuinely finite “ship in a bottle” problem, or more properly like a Cartan/Gauss–Codazzi reduction in which \(D_AP\) is the second fundamental form and the module lift supplies the information invisible to the adjoint geometry?**

I am not claiming that this establishes a connection to Geometric Unity, only that your emphasis on building the transformation law into the contraction made the missing mathematical role much easier to see.

—Aaron
