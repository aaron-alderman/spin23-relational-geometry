## Executive result

Consciousness is a legitimate candidate for the *kind of organization* missing from the current model, but it is not presently a derived missing physical ingredient.

The exact frontier reached in document 1143 is

\[
\text{fixed conservative memory}
\quad\longrightarrow\quad
\boxed{\text{backreaction on the realization data }(M,B)}.
\]

A conscious organism plainly exhibits a relevant architecture: it forms models, represents some of its own modelling, evaluates error and confidence, selects action, and thereby changes the physical conditions governing what it encounters next. This suggests the operational chain

\[
\text{world state}
\longrightarrow
\text{internal model}
\longrightarrow
\text{self-model}
\longrightarrow
\text{policy}
\longrightarrow
\text{changed world and changed future model}.
\]

That architecture can produce the state-dependent realization sought by the programme. It does **not**, however, require phenomenal consciousness. Metacognitive and confidence-guided adaptation occurs in systems for which conscious report is absent, unavailable or scientifically unsettled. The mathematical work is done by an internal reflexive state with causal access to control and learning.

The strongest current formulation is therefore

\[
\boxed{
\text{the missing dynamical type may be reflexive internal modelling with causal efficacy,}
}
\]

not

\[
\boxed{\text{consciousness is a new fundamental force or field.}}
\]

Consciousness remains live in two more limited senses:

1. it may be the biological realization in which this reflexive architecture becomes especially deep, globally available and self-authoring;
2. phenomenal character may eventually prove identical to, emergent from, or irreducible to some part of that architecture.

Neither statement currently closes the physical equation.

## 1. What question are we actually asking?

“Is consciousness missing?” separates into four non-equivalent questions.

| Question | Present answer |
|---|---|
| Does the model lack an internal state that models and alters its own future dynamics? | **Yes** |
| Do conscious organisms instantiate such states? | **Yes, at least functionally** |
| Is consciousness necessary for such reflexive adaptation? | **No evidence of necessity** |
| Is phenomenal consciousness an independent physical degree of freedom? | **Undetermined; no programme coupling or prediction yet** |

The first question is already mathematical. The second connects it to cognitive and biological systems. The third prevents us from silently replacing “reflexive control” with “consciousness.” The fourth is the genuinely radical hypothesis.

## 2. The compile-out trilemma

Let \(z_n\) contain all admitted physical and internal-model variables, and introduce a proposed consciousness variable \(c_n\).

### Case A — readout only

If

\[
c_n=h(z_n),
\qquad
z_{n+1}=F(z_n),
\]

then \(c_n\) records or interprets the physical trajectory but does not alter it. It may identify consciousness with a feature of the system, but it cannot supply the missing feedback.

### Case B — derived variable with feedback

If

\[
c_n=h(z_n),
\qquad
z_{n+1}=F(z_n,c_n),
\]

then substitution gives

\[
z_{n+1}=F(z_n,h(z_n))=:\widetilde F(z_n).
\]

The consciousness-labelled variable is dynamically eliminable. This does not prove that consciousness is unreal. It proves only that the proposed dynamics do not require it as an *independent* physical state. The coupling is an ordinary nonlinear physical law on \(z\).

### Case C — independently variable causal state

If

\[
z_{n+1}=F(z_n,c_n),
\qquad
c_{n+1}=G(c_n,z_n),
\]

and no function \(h\) on the admitted physical state eliminates \(c\), then the theory contains a genuinely additional state variable.

This is the only case in which consciousness itself could be the missing physical ingredient. It inherits a demanding proof obligation:

> There must be physically indistinguishable admitted states \(z\) whose different \(c\)-states cause different future observable distributions, together with a lawful preparation or inference procedure that makes the difference testable.

Without that separability, \(c\) is either a renaming of physical organization or an empirically idle surplus variable.

The accompanying certificate verifies the elementary dynamical content of this trilemma: a derived feedback variable compiles out exactly, whereas an independent state can change later trajectories. It also shows that positive adaptive realization data can be generated by a wholly non-conscious reflexive update.

## 3. The minimal reflexive model

Use three state layers:

\[
x_n=\text{carrier/world state},
\qquad
m_n=\text{model of the carrier/world},
\qquad
s_n=\text{model of modelling reliability and control}.
\]

A minimal closed update is

\[
\begin{aligned}
y_n &= \mathcal O(x_n),\\
m_{n+1} &= G(m_n,y_n,u_n),\\
s_{n+1} &= H(s_n,m_n,y_n,\varepsilon_n),\\
u_n &= \pi(m_n,s_n),\\
x_{n+1} &= F(x_n,u_n).
\end{aligned}
\]

Here \(\varepsilon_n\) may contain prediction error, confidence calibration or evidence that the current policy is failing. The reflexive step is not merely that \(m\) remembers \(x\). It is that \(s\) changes how the system learns, attends, inhibits or acts.

This supplies an exact candidate form for the programme's open adaptive realization:

\[
M_n=M(s_n),
\qquad
B_n=B(s_n),
\qquad
(M_{n+1},B_{n+1})
=\mathcal U(M_n,B_n;\varepsilon_n,x_n).
\]

The three-mode bath of document 1143 could then have state-dependent pole locations or residues. Positivity can be preserved by parametrization, for example

\[
m_i(s)=m_{i,0}+e^{\theta_i(s)},
\qquad
w_i(s)=e^{\omega_i(s)}.
\]

This is a valid adaptive mechanism class. Nothing in these equations yet makes \(s\) conscious.

## 4. Why the consciousness intuition is nevertheless important

The intuition points to a real omission. The current physical models contain:

- state;
- memory;
- response;
- return;
- fixed conservative realization.

They do not yet contain:

- a distinction between the represented world and the system's model of it;
- an estimate of the reliability or limits of that model;
- a policy chosen using that estimate;
- an update that changes later response rules;
- self-attribution across time, allowing the system to treat prior action as evidence about itself.

Consciousness is where humans encounter all five together. That makes it an excellent *discovery prompt*. The mistake would be to treat the word as the mechanism.

This also clarifies the earlier recognition/transformation result:

\[
\text{representation of failure}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{causal access to the update law}.
\]

A system may know that its model is wrong while remaining unable to change attention, policy, environment, coupling strength or retention. Conscious reflection is therefore neither necessary nor sufficient for transformation. The missing arrow is access from representation to control and learning.

## 5. What present consciousness science contributes

The empirical literature supports a cautious organizational reading, not a new-force reading.

### Global availability and intrinsic causal organization

Global Neuronal Workspace Theory associates conscious access with amplification and broad availability of selected content. Integrated Information Theory instead emphasizes irreducible intrinsic causal organization. A preregistered, multi-laboratory adversarial comparison with 256 participants found results consistent with parts of both accounts while challenging key predictions of both. This is evidence that consciousness research now has discriminating experiments; it is also evidence that no settled organizational invariant can simply be imported into the programme. See the [Cogitate Consortium adversarial test](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-08888-1).

### Metacognition is a concrete causal mechanism

Confidence and error monitoring can alter waiting, exploration, learning rates and later decisions. That is precisely the *shape* of the missing adaptive arrow. But it is not uniquely conscious:

- Orbitofrontal confidence signals in rats predict adaptive willingness to wait, and the authors explicitly show that relatively simple decision operations can compute confidence. See [Kepecs et al., 2008](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature07200).
- Humans can learn to use decoded latent brain states while reporting no awareness of the relevant state information; metacognitive ability predicts aspects of that learning. See [Cortese et al., 2020](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-020-17828-8).
- A reinforcement-learning model can update meta-parameters controlling motor-memory retention and learning rate, realizing a minimal monitor-and-control loop. See [Miyamoto et al., 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-023-39536-9).

These results do not settle whether rats or subliminally trained humans have relevant phenomenal experience. They do defeat the inference that adaptive confidence, self-monitoring or meta-learning by itself uniquely identifies consciousness.

### Current theories do not license a fundamental coupling

The active-inference, global-workspace, higher-order and integrated-information families provide candidate computational or causal organizations. Even where they use terms such as self-model, broadcasting or intrinsic causal power, importing them would still require an explicit map to the programme's carrier, observables and update law. The theories do not presently derive a Spin(2,3)-specific term, Standard-Model coupling or modification of quantum probabilities.

## 6. Relation to Spin(2,3)

There are three possible relations, and they must remain separate.

### Relation I — representational convenience

The \(Sp(4,\mathbb R)\cong Spin_0(2,3)\) carrier may provide useful geometry for phase space, positive graphs and response transformations used by a reflexive controller. Consciousness then uses or is implemented by structures describable with this geometry. This is not evidence that Spin(2,3) explains consciousness.

### Relation II — organizational realization

A self-model might act on a response carrier by changing its positive graph or realization data:

\[
(X_n,s_n)
\mapsto
\left(g(s_n)\cdot X_n,\ H(s_n,X_n,\varepsilon_n)\right),
\qquad
g(s_n)\in Sp(4,\mathbb R).
\]

This is mathematically coherent. It is a semidirect or coupled dynamical system on the carrier and controller state, not a derivation of consciousness from the group.

### Relation III — fundamental conscious degree of freedom

A new \(c\)-field could be coupled to the Standard Model or to the Jordan/Spin carrier. To be physics it would require at minimum:

- a state space and transformation law;
- an action, Hamiltonian or stochastic transition rule;
- a coupling operator with correct dimensions and symmetries;
- conservation and no-signalling analysis;
- a limit reproducing established physics;
- a quantitative observation unavailable to an ordinary hidden-state model.

No such coupling has been derived. The earlier corpus's Orch OR, electromagnetic and Schumann branches do not supply it; their own claim ledger correctly treats them as speculative or rejects the stronger inferences.

## 7. “Three” is meaningful here, but not yet fundamental

The reflexive architecture naturally has three typed positions:

\[
\boxed{\text{carrier/world}}
\quad
\boxed{\text{model/world relation}}
\quad
\boxed{\text{model of that relation}}.
\]

Equivalently:

\[
\text{state},\quad \text{representation},\quad \text{reflexive regulation}.
\]

This makes “three” operationally meaningful. Removing the first leaves nothing to regulate; removing the second leaves direct reaction without representation; removing the third leaves modelling without model-mediated revision of the modelling process.

It would be premature to identify this triad with three generations, the \(P,I,N\) triangle, a Jordan cubic, the three stress rays or a theological trinity. An intertwiner or shared universal property would be required. At present it is a useful arity of control architecture.

## 8. Decisive experiments and stop conditions

### Test A — non-conscious sufficiency

Construct an adaptive controller with internal model \(m\), metamodel \(s\), and state-dependent \(M(s),B(s)\). If it reproduces every proposed dynamical signature, consciousness is not necessary for that signature.

**Effect:** closes the claim “consciousness supplies the mathematical feedback,” while leaving phenomenal interpretation open.

### Test B — compile-out test

For every proposed conscious variable \(c\), determine whether \(c=h(z)\) on the admitted physical state. If so, substitute it out and compare predictions.

**Effect:** if predictions are unchanged, consciousness is not an independent dynamical variable in that model.

### Test C — causal-separability test

Specify two states equal in every admitted physical coordinate but differing in \(c\), and derive different observable future distributions.

**Effect:** passing would be a stop-everything result because the physical state space is incomplete. Failure leaves Case A or B.

### Test D — consciousness-specific perturbation

Hold sensory input, arousal, memory, confidence, report, motor preparation and global availability as fixed as experimentally possible while perturbing the candidate consciousness variable.

**Effect:** without such discrimination, ordinary cognitive control remains the simpler explanation.

### Test E — Spin(2,3) specificity

Derive a sign, invariant, spectrum or transition law that depends on the Spin(2,3) structure and differs from generic nonlinear control, recurrent networks and Bayesian/active-inference models.

**Effect:** failure prevents the consciousness spike from becoming evidence for the core programme.

### Immediate stop conditions

- Stop if “consciousness” merely renames memory, recurrence, attention, integration or feedback.
- Stop if quantum indeterminacy is used as a vacancy into which agency is inserted without a probability law.
- Stop if correlations with report are treated as proof of phenomenal causation.
- Stop if the proposed \(c\)-coupling can be absorbed into an ordinary state-dependent \(F\), \(M\) or \(B\) without loss of prediction.
- Stop the *fundamental-physics* branch, but not the organizational branch, if no causal-separability experiment can even be specified.

## 9. Highest-value next move

The useful spike is not to add a consciousness term. It is to construct the smallest **reflexive adaptive realization** and then try to prove consciousness unnecessary for it.

Start with

\[
R_n(z)=B(s_n)(M(s_n)-zI)^{-1}B(s_n)^T
\]

and let \(s_n\) update from a physically available mismatch, such as prediction error between expected and returned response:

\[
\varepsilon_n=y_n-R_n(z)q_n,
\qquad
s_{n+1}=s_n-\eta\nabla_s\mathcal L(\varepsilon_n,s_n).
\]

Then require:

1. positivity and energy boundedness;
2. covariance under the admitted carrier transformations;
3. a physical source for the loss or objective \(\mathcal L\);
4. nontrivial fixed points and a full stability calculation;
5. comparison with an otherwise identical model carrying a conscious-access label.

If the conscious label adds no prediction, the spike has still succeeded: it has extracted the real missing mathematics from the intuition. If it adds an empirically separable prediction, the project changes category.

\[

That question is mathematical, implementable and falsifiable. Consciousness may turn out to be the deepest realization of it. It is not yet what derives it.
