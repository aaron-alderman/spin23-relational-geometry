## Result

The proposed counts

\[
16,\qquad 27,\qquad 32,\qquad 54
\]

are all meaningful, but they do not initially count the same kind of object.
In complex internal one-particle dimensions and an all-left convention,

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{route}&\text{representation}&\text{reality status}\\ \hline
16&(4,2,1)\oplus(\overline4,1,2)&\text{left--right-twisted }J,\\
27&16_1\oplus10_{-2}\oplus1_4&\text{chiral }E_6\text{ matter; no ordinary internal }J,\\
32&16\oplus\overline{16}&\text{ordinary }J,\\
54&27\oplus\overline{27}&\text{ordinary }J.
\end{array}
\]

This produces a precise correction to claim 1559. Its two branches are
exhaustive **only after fixing the fermion spectrum to one Pati--Salam/
Spin(10) family**: retain 16 dimensions by twisting reality, or double to 32
for ordinary reality. Once enlargement to an exceptional parent is allowed,
the statement “exactly two honest branches” is incomplete. The chiral
\(E_6\) \(27\) and its ordinary-real \(54\)-dimensional completion are
additional routes.

The correction does not make 27 a third ordinary-real solution between 16
and 32. It changes the matter spectrum by adding a Spin(10) \(10+1\), and
the \(27\) is complex. It must be treated as a chiral fermion multiplet with
no ordinary internal \(J\), supplied with a separately audited
outer-automorphism-twisted reality, or completed to \(27+\overline{27}\).
After E6 and \(U(1)_\psi\) are broken, one can retain the real \(10+1\) and
add only \(\overline{16}\), giving a 43-dimensional ordinary-real
Pati--Salam/Spin(10) list. That intermediate count is not a complete E6
representation and hence is not a fifth ultraviolet route.

None of the four routes closes the programme. The 16 route bears a
disconnected-reality burden; the 27 bears exotic-decoupling and scalar-
completion burdens; the 32 and 54 routes must distinguish a kinematic
\(J\)-antiparticle copy from an independent vectorlike mirror. Every route
still lacks the complete scalar vacuum, protected portal intertwiner,
threshold calculation and physical observable.

## 1. The count convention is physically decisive

A **chiral matter count** enumerates independent left-handed fermion
components. A **real-carrier count** enumerates a representation and its
conjugate as separate complex summands so that an antiunitary \(J\) can
exchange them. These are not automatically equal to the number of
independent propagating chiral fields.

If \(\overline{16}\) in the 32 route, or \(\overline{27}\) in the 54 route,
is the constrained antiparticle image under \(J\), it is a kinematic part of
the real Hilbert carrier and is not an additional mirror family. If both
summands are instead quantized as independent all-left matter multiplets,
the spectrum is vectorlike. A gauge-invariant mass then tends to remove a
representation together with its conjugate, and selective mirror
decoupling becomes a nontrivial dynamical demand.

This distinction is the first kill test for the 32 and 54 routes. A proposal
that counts the conjugate summand as necessary for \(J\), then later treats
it as an independently adjustable mirror without specifying the fermionic
action and reality projection, changes categories mid-derivation.

## 2. Route 16: minimal matter, maximal reality burden

The fixed-family content is

\[
16=(4,2,1)\oplus(\overline4,1,2).
\]

It is anomaly-consistent: the two weak components give cancelling
\(SU(4)^3\) indices, while each weak factor sees four doublets and hence an
even Witten parity. It closes under the antiunitary relation only when

\[
Jg=\sigma_{LR}(g)J,
\]

where \(\sigma_{LR}\) exchanges the two weak factors. This is not ordinary
gauge-equivariant reality.

Its economy is real: it adds no fermionic exotics or conjugate carrier. Its
decisive test is correspondingly structural. One must construct the
disconnected/twisted action, verify \(J^2\) and the fermionic action, audit
the discrete/global anomaly, break the extension consistently, and recover
ordinary low-energy charge conjugation. It also retains claim 1556's scalar
failure: bidoublets are needed for cross-chiral edges, while Majorana and
high-scale breaking require different multiplets.

## 3. Route 27: the genuine exceptional-parent alternative

The standard branching is

\[
27=16_1\oplus10_{-2}\oplus1_4
\quad\text{under }Spin(10)\times U(1)_\psi.
\]

The count is exact and its elementary subgroup anomaly traces cancel:

\[
16(1)+10(-2)+1(4)=0,
\]

\[
16(1)^3+10(-2)^3+1(4)^3=0,
\]

and, using \(T(16)=2T(10)\),

\[
T(16)(1)+T(10)(-2)=0.
\]

Thus 27 is a serious chiral-parent candidate rather than decorative
numerology. The \(E_6\) cubic includes the component channels
\(16_F16_F10_H\) and \(10_F10_F1_H\). A singlet expectation can therefore
lift the vectorlike states residing in the Spin(10) \(10\), as used in
explicit \(E_6\) models.

The same facts delimit the route. The fermionic \(10+1\) are additional
matter, not Higgs fields hidden inside the fermion \(27\). Their complete
mass matrix must be written. The \(27_H\) cubic does not supply the
conventional Spin(10) \(126_H\) Majorana branch; that points to a
\(351'_H\), while realistic \(E_6\) breaking requires further declared
scalar content. Claim 1535's one-tensor \(351'_H\) near miss remains useful,
but it does not by itself solve family alignment, thresholds or unwanted
components.

The decisive 27 test is therefore a rank-and-index calculation: after all
declared \(27_H\), \(351'_H\) and breaking VEVs, prove that the unwanted
\(10+1\) modes are acceptably lifted, exactly the intended chiral family
survives, and the one-parent-invariant relation is not destroyed by the
vacuum and thresholds. Ordinary \(J\) still
does not close on one \(27\). The \(E_6\) diagram involution suggests a
separate outer-twisted route, but that is a disconnected-extension model and
inherits the same type of anomaly and symmetry-breaking audit as route 16.

## 4. Route 32: the minimum fixed-spectrum ordinary-real carrier

For an ordinary gauge-equivariant \(J\), the minimum closure of the family is

\[
16\oplus\overline{16}.
\]

At the Pati--Salam stage this is precisely the four-summand 32-dimensional
carrier derived in claim 1556. It is the conservative route if the conjugate
summand is explicitly the \(J\)-related antiparticle carrier. Then “mirror
decoupling” is the wrong question; the correct question is whether the
fermionic action and reality projection remove the usual finite-Hilbert
doubling while retaining the intended chiral physics.

If instead \(16\) and \(\overline{16}\) are independent left-handed fields,
the model is vectorlike. Their invariant mass can lift both, so retaining
one light chiral family is not furnished merely by writing 32 states. This
interpretive fork must be resolved before beta functions or thresholds can
be counted.

The carrier also supplies no scalar closure by itself. Dirac, Majorana and
breaking interactions still require the declared \(10_H\), \(126_H\) and
breaking representations, together with their complete potential.

## 5. Route 54: ordinary reality after exceptional enlargement

The ordinary-real completion of the exceptional route is

\[
27\oplus\overline{27},
\]

of complex dimension 54. This is the minimum if both an \(E_6\) parent and
ordinary \(E_6\)-equivariant \(J\) are mandatory. It is automatically
vectorlike at the representation level and contains the conjugates of all
\(10+1\) exotics.

That strength is also its minimality weakness. As a kinematic \(J\)-pair it
is consistent bookkeeping but still needs the full exotic, scalar and
threshold audit. As two independent matter multiplets it admits a direct
\(27\overline{27}\) mass and does not automatically leave one chiral 27,
let alone one light Spin(10) 16. The selective-decoupling problem is larger
than in route 32.

Route 54 is therefore a useful control and may be compulsory under an
ordinary-real \(E_6\) axiom, but it is dominated in economy by route 27 if a
chiral matter formulation without internal ordinary \(J\) is acceptable.

## 6. Comparative decision and minimal kill tests

The honest branch tree is

\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{ll}
\text{fixed Pati--Salam/Spin(10) spectrum:}
&16\text{ twisted}\quad\text{or}\quad32\text{ ordinary-real},\\
\text{enlarged }E_6\text{ spectrum:}
&27\text{ chiral (or separately outer-twisted)}
\quad\text{or}\quad54\text{ ordinary-real}.
\end{array}}
\]

The earliest decisive tests are different:

1. **16:** disconnected-extension anomaly, consistent breaking and recovery
   of ordinary low-energy reality;
2. **27:** complete exotic mass-matrix rank and surviving chiral index;
3. **32:** explicit declaration of kinematic antiparticles versus
   independent mirrors, followed by the corresponding fermionic-action or
   selective-decoupling proof; and
4. **54:** the same declaration plus the larger exceptional exotic and
   scalar-threshold audit.

Passing a count or anomaly test is not scalar closure. All four still need
the Ward-protected typed portal intertwiner. Consequently there is no winner
yet. The most economical structural experiment is 16, the most promising
simple-parent experiment is 27, the most conservative ordinary-reality
experiment is 32, and 54 is the ordinary-real exceptional control.

## Spin(2,3) status

Spin(2,3) can continue to organize grading, correspondence orientation and
reality data, but this comparison does not derive which reality category is
physical. In particular it does not turn the complex \(E_6\) \(27\) into an
ordinary real representation, select the \(E_6\) exotic mass matrix, or
solve the portal Ward-intertwiner obstruction. A genuine Spin(2,3) advance
would have to choose or constrain one of these branch-specific missing
maps, not merely reproduce one of the four dimensions.

## Verdict

Claim 1559 was right at fixed Pati--Salam matter content and too strong as an
unqualified statement. The \(27\) route must be admitted once parent
enlargement is allowed, and its ordinary-real completion is 54. The four
routes are now separated without conflating chiral fields, real-Hilbert
copies and independent mirrors. That removes a false state-count contest
and replaces it with four short, falsifiable construction tests.
