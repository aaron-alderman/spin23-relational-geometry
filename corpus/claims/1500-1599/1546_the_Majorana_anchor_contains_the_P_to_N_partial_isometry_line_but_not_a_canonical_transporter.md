## Result

The finite relational data already contain the **one-dimensional complex
operator line** required by claim 1544, but do not canonically choose a
normalized vector on that line or identify its coefficient with the Dirac
portal coefficient.

Write the marked family projectors as

\[
p_P=|P\rangle\langle P|,
\qquad
p_N=|N\rangle\langle N|.
\]

The Majorana endpoints

\[
M_P=(P,I),\qquad M_N=(N,I)
\]

share their opposite anchor.  Their allowed (P\)-to-(N) operator space is

\[
p_N\mathcal B(H_F)p_P
=\mathbb C E_{NP}.
\]

Every normalized partial isometry in it is

\[
\boxed{t_\phi=e^{i\phi}E_{NP}},
\qquad
t_\phi^\dagger t_\phi=p_P,
\qquad
t_\phi t_\phi^\dagger=p_N.
\]

Thus the normalized choices form a (U(1))-torsor.  The anchors determine
the line, not its phase.  On a CP-real slice this contracts only to the two
choices (\{+E_{NP},-E_{NP}\}); CP still does not choose the sign.

By contrast, the Dirac endpoints

\[
D_P=(P,P),\qquad D_N=(N,N)
\]

share neither endpoint.  The first-order anchor rule removes their (PN)
one-form.  Relation-sheet parity says the same thing in the real-completed
language: the Majorana matrix unit is even, whereas the hypothetical Dirac
(PN) matrix unit is odd.

If the finite Dirac operator already has a nonzero Majorana block

\[
D_M|_{P,N}
=yE_{PN}+\bar yE_{NP},
\qquad y\ne0,
\]

then the compressed commutator gives

\[
p_N[D_M,p_P]p_P=\bar yE_{NP}.
\]

Its polar part is

\[
v_M
=\frac{p_N[D_M,p_P]p_P}{|y|}
=e^{-i\arg y}E_{NP}.
\]

This is a canonical normalized partial isometry **relative to the chosen
nonzero Majorana block**.  It is not generated by the projectors, anchors,
grading, relation parity or (J) alone.  It inherits the block's phase, is
undefined at (y=0), and uses functional calculus to divide by its norm.
The analogous Dirac compression is identically zero.

Consequently claim 1544's transporter is present as support and can be
extracted conditionally from (D_M), but the declared geometry does not yet
derive the common portal ray.  The exact missing datum is a physical
phase-and-coefficient locking law between the selected Dirac (PP) line and
the polar Majorana (PN) line.  It must be branch conditioned and
relation-sheet even; it must not be implemented by a carrier map between the
Dirac and Majorana wheels.

## 1. Complete anchored classification

form

\[

\]

The partial-isometry equations are

\[
t^\dagger t=|z|^2p_P,
\qquad
tt^\dagger=|z|^2p_N.
\]

Normalization therefore imposes exactly (|z|=1), proving the (U(1))
classification.  There is one complex line, no preferred nonzero element,

The reverse orientation is (t_\phi^\dagger=e^{-i\phi}E_{PN}).  The real

## 2. What the commutator actually generates

On the ordered basis ((P,N)), let

\[
D_M=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&y\\
\bar y&0
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
p_P=
\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Then

\[
[D_M,p_P]
=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&-y\\
\bar y&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The (N\)-(P) compression is the raw allowed matrix unit with its physical
coefficient.  Polar decomposition removes (|y|), but not the relative phase
against any other coupling.  A family rephasing can set this one edge to
(+E_{NP}) locally, but that is a basis convention: it simultaneously
rephases every other edge incident on (N).  No marked projector or anchor
selects that convention.

If CP is imposed, (y\in\mathbb R), so the polar part is
(\operatorname{sgn}(y)E_{NP}).  Positivity of (|y|) does not determine
(\operatorname{sgn}(y)).

For the Dirac branch, the null mask imposes

\[
p_N[D_D,p_P]p_P=0.
\]

Thus the commutator calculus reproduces exactly the intended asymmetric
support: conditional generation in the Majorana branch and no Dirac leakage.

## 3. Relation-sheet parity and branch conditioning

In the typed lift, (M_P) and (M_N) have equal relation-sheet sign, so

\[
\Gamma_{\rm rel}t_\phi\Gamma_{\rm rel}=t_\phi.
\]

The dangerous Dirac endpoints have opposite signs, so a hypothetical
(E_{D_ND_P}) obeys

\[
\Gamma_{\rm rel}E_{D_ND_P}\Gamma_{\rm rel}
=-E_{D_ND_P}.
\]

Parity therefore selects the correct **support class**, but every scalar
multiple on a selected support has the same parity.  It cannot reduce the
(U(1)) torsor to one point.

The safe branch-conditioned use of the transporter is

\[
b_D\otimes E_{PP},
\qquad
b_M\otimes(t_\phi E_{PP}+E_{PP}t_\phi^T).
\]

This creates no Dirac (PN) entry.  It also introduces no carrier block from
the Dirac wheel to the Majorana wheel.  Such a cross-wheel carrier would be
relation-sheet odd and, by claim 1521, generate the forbidden enlarged
carrier algebra.

## 4. Why existing support is not yet a common coefficient

Using the raw commutator block transports a coefficient (a) as

\[
aE_{PP}
\longmapsto
a\bar y(E_{NP}+E_{PN})
\]

up to the appropriate symmetric conjugation convention.  This imports both
the magnitude and phase of the independent Majorana block (y).

Using the polar part removes the magnitude:

\[
aE_{PP}
\longmapsto
ae^{-i\arg y}E_{NP}
+ae^{-i\arg y}E_{PN}.
\]

It still leaves a relative phase.  More importantly, the finite geometry
does not itself state that this polar part must be used to transport the
Dirac coefficient.  Existence of an allowed Majorana matrix unit is not a
Ward identity equating two portal coefficients.

The distinction is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{canonical supported line}
\;\ne\;
\text{canonical normalized vector}
\;\ne\;
\text{canonical common-coupling law}.}
\]
