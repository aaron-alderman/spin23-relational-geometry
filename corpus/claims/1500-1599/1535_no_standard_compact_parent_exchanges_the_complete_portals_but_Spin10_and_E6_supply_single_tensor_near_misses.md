## Result

None of the bounded standard candidates puts the **complete** selected Dirac
and Majorana portal complexes in one involutive orbit while also leaving
exactly one selected-family contraction:

\[
G_{PS}\rtimes\mathbb Z_2^{LR},
\qquad
SU(4)\times Sp(4),
\qquad
Spin(10),
\qquad
E_6,
\qquad
(G_{PS}\times G_{PS})\rtimes\mathbb Z_2.
\]

The literal exchange programme therefore remains negative in this search.
The decisive whole-multiplet invariant is already visible at the
Pati--Salam stage.  In \((SU(4),SU(2)_L,SU(2)_R)\) order, the scalar
dimensions are

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
H_{D1}=(1,2,2)&4\\
H_{D15}=(15,2,2)&60\\
H_{MR}=(10,1,3)&30\\
H_{M6}=(6,1,1)&6.
\end{array}
\]

An involution normalizing the chosen Pati--Salam subgroup permutes complete
irreducible representations and preserves their dimensions.  No sourced
Dirac scalar multiplet can therefore be exchanged with a sourced Majorana
scalar multiplet.  Left--right parity and the relevant Dynkin-diagram
involutions confirm, rather than evade, this obstruction.

There is, however, a more promising mechanism that must be kept distinct
from exchange:

\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{l}
Spin(10):\quad 16_F16_F126_H
\text{ contains both }(15,2,2)_D\text{ and }(10,1,3)_M,\\
E_6:\quad 27_F27_F351'_H
\text{ contains the Spin(10) }10_H\text{ and }126_H\text{ branches}.
\end{array}}
\]

Each displayed parent representation channel has multiplicity one at the
gauge-tensor level.  It can therefore fix a relative Clebsch ray without any
operator exchange.

This is not yet the claim-1521 common coefficient line.  With three family
copies, a symmetric parent Yukawa tensor carries a symmetric \(3\times3\)
matrix, hence six independent entries.  The selected Dirac coefficient
\(Y_{PP}\) and selected-null Majorana coefficient \(Y_{PN}\) remain distinct
entries unless a family intertwiner relates them.  Vacuum projection,
threshold normalization and unwanted parent components also remain open.

The highest-value next route is consequently the shared-\(126_H\)
Spin(10) construction, not a larger exchange group.

## 1. Scope and exact test

The audit is deliberately bounded to standard connected compact/simple or
natural semidirect parents containing Pati--Salam.  For every candidate it
asks two separate questions:

1. does an order-two automorphism normalizing the chosen Pati--Salam stage
   exchange the complete typed Dirac and Majorana operator complexes? and
2. after gauge and actual family multiplicities are included, is there
   exactly one invariant contraction?

“Complete” includes the scalar representation, constituent fermion types,
marked family character, finite particle/conjugate grading and \(J\)-pair.
It is not enough for two neutral components to be weights of one large
representation.

The search does not cover noncompact parents, arbitrary exceptional groups
above \(E_6\), or nonlocal dualities.  A parent group element that does not
normalize the selected Pati--Salam subgroup may relate different embeddings
before symmetry breaking, but it is not a symmetry of the chosen
Pati--Salam-stage action and vacuum.

## 2. Left--right semidirect parity

Pati--Salam parity acts schematically by

\[
(R_4,R_L,R_R)\longmapsto
(R_4^{\sigma},R_R,R_L),
\]

where \(\sigma\) accounts for the relevant conjugation convention.  It can
exchange the matter branches

\[
F_L=(4,2,1)
\quad\leftrightarrow\quad
F_R=(\overline4,1,2).
\]

But the scalar orbits are

\[
(1,2,2)\mapsto(1,2,2),
\qquad
(15,2,2)\mapsto(15,2,2),
\]

and

\[
(10,1,3)_R\mapsto(\overline{10},3,1)_L.
\]

Thus parity relates right and left Majorana triplets; it does not turn a
Majorana triplet into a Dirac bidoublet.  The two portal types remain distinct
invariant contractions.

## 3. The smaller SU(4) x Sp(4) near miss

The connected compact semisimple group

\[
SU(4)\times Sp(4)

\]

is a useful smaller test because

\[
Sp(4)\supset SU(2)_L\times SU(2)_R.
\]

Its fundamental and adjoint branch as

\[
4\to(2,1)\oplus(1,2),
\]

\[
10\to(3,1)\oplus(1,3)\oplus(2,2).
\]

It can therefore place left/right matter types together and can place a weak
bidoublet and weak triplets in one parent weak representation.

The obstruction moves to \(SU(4)\).  A product-group irrep carries one fixed
\(SU(4)\) representation across all those weak branches, whereas

\[
H_D:\ 1\text{ or }15,
\qquad
H_M:\ 10\text{ or }6.
\]

No desired pair shares its \(SU(4)\) type.  This is the smallest clear
connected near miss in the audit, but it does not contain the full scalar
orbit or one desired invariant.

## 4. Spin(10): no exchange, but the leading one-tensor route

The standard branchings include

\[
16_F\to(4,2,1)\oplus(\overline4,1,2),
\]

\[
10_H\to(1,2,2)\oplus(6,1,1),
\]

and, up to conjugation convention,

\[
126_H\to
(15,2,2)
\oplus(10,1,3)
\oplus(\overline{10},3,1)
\oplus(6,1,1).
\]

The matter product is

\[
16\otimes16=10_s\oplus120_a\oplus126_s,
\]

with multiplicity one in each displayed channel.

### Minimal singlet-bidoublet choice

If the selected Dirac portal uses \((1,2,2)\subset10_H\) and the selected
right-Majorana portal uses \((10,1,3)\subset126_H\), two inequivalent parent
tensors are needed:

\[
Y_{10}\,16_F16_F10_H
+Y_{126}\,16_F16_F126_H.
\]

Spin(10) does not equate \(Y_{10}\) and \(Y_{126}\).

### Shared-126 choice

If instead the sourced adjoint bidoublet

\[
H_{D15}=(15,2,2)

\]

is used for the Dirac portal, both it and \(H_{MR}=(10,1,3)\) occur inside
one \(126_H\).  The single parent invariant

\[
Y_{126}\,16_F16_F126_H

\]

then supplies fixed gauge Clebsches to both components.  This is a genuine
positive result at the gauge-tensor level and the smallest audited parent
that achieves it.

It is not an involutive exchange.  D parity preserves the bidoublet type and
maps the right Majorana triplet to its left partner.  The \(D_5\) diagram
automorphism conjugates chiral/self-dual representations rather than mapping
the bidoublet subspace to the right triplet.  The unequal Pati--Salam
multiplet dimensions give the same conclusion immediately.

## 5. E6: a broader one-tensor route, still not an orbit

The relevant standard decompositions are

\[
27_F\to16_1\oplus10_{-2}\oplus1_4

\]

and, suppressing convention-dependent \(U(1)\) signs,

\[
351'_H\to1\oplus10\oplus16\oplus54\oplus126\oplus144.
\]

The multiplicity-one \(351'\) Yukawa channel in

\[
27_F\otimes27_F

\]

contains both the Spin(10) \(10_H\) and \(126_H\) branches.  Consequently

\[
Y_{351'}\,27_F27_F351'_H

\]

can relate a singlet-bidoublet Dirac coupling and a right-Majorana coupling
by fixed \(E_6\) Clebsches.  This is precisely why published \(E_6\)
constructions can motivate a relation between the Spin(10) 10 and 126
Yukawa sectors.

Again, one tensor is not an exchange orbit.  The nontrivial \(E_6\) diagram
automorphism sends

\[
27\leftrightarrow\overline{27},
\qquad
351'\leftrightarrow\overline{351'},

\]

not the internal Spin(10) 10 branch to the 126 branch.  Any involution
normalizing the selected Pati--Salam subgroup retains the whole-multiplet
dimension obstruction.

The price is also larger: each matter \(27\) adds a Spin(10) \(10+1\), and
the \(351'\) includes \(1,16,54,144\) branches beyond the desired
\(10+126\).  These are not automatically harmless spectators.

## 6. Doubled Pati--Salam with exchange

For

\[
(G_{PS}^{A}\times G_{PS}^{B})\rtimes\mathbb Z_2,

\]

copy exchange maps a representation in \(A\) to the identical
representation in \(B\).  Therefore

\[
D_A\leftrightarrow D_B,
\qquad
M_A\leftrightarrow M_B.

\]

The invariant interaction is still

\[
\lambda_D(D_A+D_B)+\lambda_M(M_A+M_B).

\]

Link fields and deconstructed doubled-GUT models provide a standard setting
for such copy exchange, but they do not change this orbit count.  This is the
group-theoretic version of claim 1534's safe mirror closure.

## 7. One parent tensor is not one selected-family number

The single-tensor Spin(10) and \(E_6\) results are important but incomplete.
For \(n\) family copies, a symmetric Yukawa channel carries

\[
\dim\operatorname{Sym}^2\mathbb C^n=\frac{n(n+1)}2

\]

independent family coefficients.  At \(n=3\), this is six.  The programme's
two selected coefficients occupy different entries,

\[
k_D\sim Y_{PP},
\qquad
k_M\sim Y_{PN},

\]

and gauge unification alone does not set them proportional.  This is the
family-multiplicity obstruction of claim 1518 in GUT language.

The marked low-energy family dressing sharpens the issue: the Dirac channel
is neutral, whereas the selected-null Majorana channel uses charge-minus-three
dressing.  Neither ordinary Spin(10) nor ordinary \(E_6\) contains that
family-nonuniversal relation automatically.

Thus “one gauge invariant tensor” means one Yukawa **matrix**, not one
numerical coefficient.

## 8. Ranking and programme consequence

The candidates rank as follows.

1. **Spin(10) shared \(126_H\):** smallest multiplicity-one gauge tensor
   containing the sourced \((15,2,2)_D\) and \((10,1,3)_M\) branches.
2. **E6 \(351'_H\):** one tensor can contain the Spin(10) 10 and 126 scalar
   branches, at the price of a substantially larger spectrum.
3. **\(SU(4)\times Sp(4)\):** smallest connected near miss; weak types unify,
   but the \(SU(4)\) scalar representations do not.
4. **Left--right and doubled-Pati--Salam semidirect products:** exact
   involutions exist, but they exchange chirality partners or identical
   copies and retain two portal orbits.

The literal exchange search is exhausted for this bounded standard list.
The single-parent-tensor route is not exhausted and is now the higher-value
direction.

## 9. Strongest conclusion and reopening datum

No audited parent realizes both:

\[
\text{one complete involutive D/M orbit}
\quad\text{and}\quad
\text{one selected-family contraction}.

\]

Spin(10) and \(E_6\) do realize the weaker but physically meaningful
statement that both gauge components can descend from one multiplicity-one
parent tensor.

The leading gate is now concrete.  For Spin(10):

1. choose one \(126_H\) tensor and explicitly project its
   \((15,2,2)_D\) and \((10,1,3)_M\) components;
2. supply a family symmetry/intertwiner placing the exact \(Y_{PP}\) and
   \(Y_{PN}\) entries on one line while preserving the null mask;
3. compute the Clebsch, VEV and wavefunction-normalized threshold ray; and
4. audit every other \(126_H\) component and radiative counterterm.

If a literal exchange is still required, the reopening datum must be a
structure beyond the audited ordinary embeddings: an enlarged parent and
involution normalizing the low-energy embedding while mapping unequal
Pati--Salam multiplets as complete typed complexes.
