## Result

Claim 1551's unit-weight selection is not an isolated tuned point.  In
squared-edge-weight coordinates it occupies an open polyhedral chamber.
However, neither the most general renormalizable link potential nor the
repeated-charge identifications of claim 1542 force the vacuum into that
chamber.

Write the positive squared norms on the quotient edges as

\[
\begin{array}{c|ccccccc}
\text{edge}&AB&BC&CE&DE&EF&DG&FH\\ \hline
\text{weight}&x&y&z&u&v&r&s.
\end{array}
\]

Thus (x,y,z,v,r,s) are the squared norms generated by the six charged link
fields (a,b,c,e,f,g), while (u) is the squared neutral (SP-TP)
coefficient.  For a two-orbit projection (Q),

\[
S_\rho(Q)=\frac14\operatorname{Tr}([D,Q]^*[D,Q])
=\sum_{e\in\partial Q}\rho_e.
\]

The target has cost (S_\rho(AB)=y).

On (H_{12}), (AB) is the unique minimizer if and only if

\[
\boxed{
y<x+z,quad y<x+u,quad y<x+v,quad
y<z+u,quad y<z+v,quad y<u+v.}
\]

Equivalently, (y) is smaller than the sum of the two smallest elements of
\(\{x,z,u,v\}\).  All six inequalities define genuine facets.  The all-unit
vacuum lies strictly inside this cone, with unit margin on every wall.

On (H_{16}), (AB) is the unique minimizer if and only if

\[
\boxed{
y<u,quad y<v,quad y<x+z,quad
y<x+r,quad y<x+s,quad y<r+s.}
\]

This is again a nonempty open cone.  For example

\[
(x,y,z,u,v,r,s)=(1,\tfrac12,1,1,1,1,1)
\]

is an interior point.  Hence unique selection does **not** require exact
equalities or fine tuning.

But the all-unit stable vacuum of claim 1542 obeys

\[
y=u=v=1.
\]

It sits on the codimension-two intersection of the (DG) and (FH)
degeneracy walls, reproducing the threefold (AB,DG,FH) minimum of claim
1551.  The exact conclusion is therefore

\[
\boxed{
\text{robust selection exists on an open chamber, but the sourced potential
does not predict that chamber.}}
\]

## 1. Exhaustion and degeneracy walls

For every competing two-vertex subset, subtract the target cost (y).  A
competitor whose cut also contains (BC) has cost (y) plus positive terms
and can never beat (AB).  For the remaining competitors, positivity makes
an inequality redundant whenever its cut support strictly contains another
competitor's cut support.  The inclusion-minimal supports give the complete
irredundant list above.

The six (H_{12}) facet walls and tied competitors are

\[
\begin{array}{c|cccccc}
\text{wall}&y=x+z&y=x+u&y=x+v&y=z+u&y=z+v&y=u+v\\
\text{competitor}&BC&AD&AF&EF&DE&DF.
\end{array}
\]

The six (H_{16}) walls are

\[
\begin{array}{c|cccccc}
\text{wall}&y=u&y=v&y=x+z&y=x+r&y=x+s&y=r+s\\
\text{competitor}&DG&FH&BC&AG&AH&GH.
\end{array}
\]

Intersections of these hyperplanes give the higher degeneracies.  Strict
inequalities are necessary and sufficient for uniqueness; replacing any one
by equality puts the corresponding competitor at the same action.

## 2. What the potential enforces

The stable-vacuum theorem of claim 1542 ensures that all six charged link
VEVs are nonzero and that the neutral coefficient can also be nonzero.  This
places the weights in the positive orthant.  It imposes no ordering among
their norms.

Indeed, the general renormalizable invariant potential contains independent
modulus masses and quartics.  Its symmetry constraints determine which
operators are allowed, not inequalities such as (y<u) or (y<v).  The
nondegenerate all-one witness persists under small coefficient changes, so
nearby stable vacua occur on both sides of the two (H_{16}) walls unless an
additional coefficient relation is imposed.

The repeated-charge scalar identification is no stronger.  Identifying

\[
a=b=X_+,\qquad e=f=X_-
\]

does not identify the corresponding edge norms when their Yukawa maps remain
independent.  If the Yukawas are tied as an extra assumption, it gives

\[
x=y,\qquad v=r.
\]

On (H_{12}) the chamber then reduces to

\[
y<z+u,\qquad y<z+v,\qquad y<u+v.
\]

On (H_{16}) all but two facets become automatic, leaving

\[
y<u,\qquad y<v.
\]

Neither ordering follows from equal charge.  At the all-one witness both are
saturated.  Repeated charges simplify the chamber but do not select it.

## 3. Threshold and one-loop robustness

Model threshold or one-loop effects abstractly by independent shifts

\[
\rho_e\longmapsto\rho_e+\delta_e,
\qquad \rho_e+\delta_e>0.
\]

The exact radiative stability test is simply the same six strict linear
inequalities with every weight replaced by its shifted value.  At every
interior point the minimum facet margin is positive, so a nonzero open ball
of perturbations preserves the (AB) minimum.

At the all-unit (H_{16}) point, however, the two active first-order tests
are

\[
\delta_y<\delta_u,\qquad \delta_y<\delta_v.
\]

The other four facets begin with unit margin.  Arbitrarily small independent
shifts can therefore select any terminal branch:

- lowering (y) selects (AB);
- lowering (u) selects (DG); and
- lowering (v) selects (FH).

Thus the symmetric vacuum is not radiatively protected as an anchor selector.
A beta-function or threshold theorem ordering (y) below both (u) and
(v) would suffice, but no such theorem occurs in the current corpus.

## Verdict

The weighted problem does not call for a new equality law.  Exact equality
is what creates the (H_{16}) degeneracy.  It calls for a new **ordering
law**: a sourced dynamical reason that the (BC) boundary norm lies in the
six-facet chamber, especially below the neutral (DE) and terminal (EF)
norms.  Once that ordering holds, selection is open and structurally stable.
Without it, positivity, vacuum stability and repeated charges are
insufficient to predict the anchor.
