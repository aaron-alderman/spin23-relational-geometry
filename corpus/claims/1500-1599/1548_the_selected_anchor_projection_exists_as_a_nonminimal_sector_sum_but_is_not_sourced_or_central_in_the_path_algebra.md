## Result

The source representation contains a lift of the desired target projection,
but only after both represented and opposite character actions are used.
At multiplicity one it is

\[
Q_{\rm anc}=
p_Pp_N^o+p_Np_P^o+p_Ip_N^o+p_Np_I^o,
\]

the rank-four projection onto

\[
PN,NP,IN,NI.
\]

It is \(J\)-invariant and commutes with the KO-6 grading and relation parity.
Under the correspondence

\[
PN\mapsto I,
\qquad IN\mapsto P
\]

and its \(J\)-completion, it descends to

\[
q=p_I+p_P.
\]

This does not make \(q\) sourced or canonical.

- \(Q_{\rm anc}\) is not in the represented \(C^6\) image or its opposite image;
- it is a sum of four minimal joint left/right sector projections;
- it is one of 15 same-rank \(J\)-invariant two-orbit sums on the twelve-state
  model and one of 28 on the sixteen-state sector repair;
- it is not central in the connected path or Jordan algebra; and
- its interpretation as the physical \(I/P\) corner uses the descent dictionary
  that it was supposed to derive.

The matrix-multiplicity repair is stricter. Its \(IN/NI\) sectors are doubled.
The exact physical lift must select one line inside each \(\mathbb C^2\)
multiplicity block. That rank-one projection lies in the joint commutant but
not in the algebra generated by the represented and opposite (C^6) actions.
Its choices form a \(\mathbb{CP}^1\) family and reproduce the unsolved common-ray
selection.

Adjoining \(Q_{\rm anc}\) as a new represented algebra element is not inert. It
refines the six left-character blocks to eight blocks on the twelve-state
model and nine on either sixteen-state repair. The represented commutant
dimensions fall

\[
30\longrightarrow22,
\qquad
46\longrightarrow32,
\qquad
54\longrightarrow38
\]

for the twelve-state, new-sector and multiplicity repairs, respectively.
Thus it changes the available gauge/commutant structure before unimodularity
or kernel constraints.

The projection alone does not create forbidden primitive support. Its only
Dirac boundary edges are

\[
IN-IP
\qquad\hbox{and}\qquad
NI-PI,
\]

the already allowed \(P-N\) chain orbit and its \(J\)-partner. Quadratic open-path
leakage remains a separate issue and still requires the selected-return
expectation of claim 1543.

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
Q_{\rm anc}\text{ is algebraically available but not sourced, unique, or
central in the dynamically generated algebra}.}
\]

## 1. Projection lattices of the represented actions

Let

\[
\mathcal A_6=\mathbb C_P\oplus\mathbb C_I\oplus\mathbb C_N
\oplus\mathbb C_S\oplus\mathbb C_T\oplus\mathbb C_U.
\]

The represented image \(\pi(\mathcal A_6)\) acts by the left character, while
the opposite image \(\pi^o(\mathcal A_6)\) acts by the right character. Each
image has six minimal central projections and hence \(2^6=64\) projections.

The joint algebra generated by the two images resolves an occupied sector
\(ab\) through

\[
p_{ab}=\pi(e_a)\pi^o(e_b).
\]

On the twelve-state model, every occupied joint sector is one-dimensional.
The joint algebra is therefore \(\mathbb C^{12}\) and has

\[
2^{12}=4096
\]

projections. Reality groups the sectors into six \(J\)-orbits, leaving
\(2^6=64\) \(J\)-invariant projections.

On the sixteen-state \(SI/IS+NU/UN\) repair, the same construction gives
\(\mathbb C^{16}\), with

\[
2^{16}=65536
\]

projections and (2^8=256) real-completed choices.

The anchor lift is present in both joint algebras. It is not minimal: it is a
sum of four minimal sector projections, or two \(J\)-orbits. There are

\[
\binom62=15
\]

two-orbit choices on the first model and

\[
\binom82=28
\]

on the repaired model. The declared grading and relation signs commute with
all these diagonal sums and do not distinguish the anchor choice.

## 2. Why it is not in the represented algebra alone

A projection in the represented (C^6) image must be constant on every left
character fibre. On the twelve-state model, the left-\(P\) fibre contains

\[
PN,PI,PS,PT.
\]

The desired lift contains \(PN\) but excludes the other three. The left-\(I\)
fibre contains \((IN,IP)\); the lift contains only \(IN\). Therefore \(Q_{\rm anc}\) is
not a sum of left central projections.

The same argument with right fibres excludes membership in the opposite
algebra. Adding the four new sectors in the sixteen-state repair does not
change this conclusion.

The formula for \(Q_{\rm anc}\) uses products of left and right projectors. It is
central in the abelian joint diagonal algebra, but this is weak: every one of
the thousands of sector sums is central there. Its membership records that
the sector labels are available, not that the physical anchor mask was
selected.

## 3. The multiplicity repair needs a genuinely new projector

For the minimum multiplicity pattern

\[
(m_{PN},m_{IN},m_{IP},m_{TP},m_{SP},m_{TU})
=(1,2,1,2,1,1),
\]

the joint represented/opposite algebra acts as a scalar identity on each
multiplicity block. Its central projection lattice still chooses whole sector
types; it cannot select one of the two \(IN\) copies.

An exact lift of the one-dimensional physical \(P\) role must instead contain

\[
\ell_{IN}\subset\mathbb C^2_{IN}
\]

and its \(J\)-related line in \(\mathbb C^2_{NI}\). Such rank-one projectors lie in
the joint commutant \(M_2(\mathbb C)\) blocks. They are not central and form

\[
\mathbb{CP}^1.
\]

No line is fixed by the full \(U(2)\) multiplicity-basis action. This is exactly
the common-ray obstruction in projection-lattice form. In this branch the
desired \(Q_{\rm anc}\) does not merely lack uniqueness; it is absent from the joint
represented/opposite algebra until a ray projector is adjoined.

## 4. Path and Jordan closure

On the twelve-state model, the allowed Dirac graph has two connected
six-state relation sheets. Minimal sector projectors isolate each edge matrix
unit, and path multiplication generates every matrix unit within a sheet.
The generated associative algebra is

\[
M_6(\mathbb C)\oplus M_6(\mathbb C).
\]

The verifier counts

\[
6^2+6^2=72
\]

matrix units. On the sixteen-state sector repair the corresponding algebra is

\[
M_8(\mathbb C)\oplus M_8(\mathbb C),
\]

with 128 matrix units.

The self-adjoint Jordan closure is the Hermitian part of the same two full
component algebras. In either associative or Jordan language, the centre is
only

\[
\mathbb C1_+\oplus\mathbb C1_-,
\]

with four central projections. The anchor projection selects two states
inside each sheet and is not central.

It certainly belongs to the full path algebra, as does every subspace
projection. That abundance supplies existence but no selection principle.
Neither associative path closure nor Jordan closure promotes it to a
distinguished idempotent.

## 5. Cost of adjoining the projection

Consider adjoining \(Q_{\rm anc}\) to the represented left algebra rather than only
using it as an external corner.

For an abelian represented image, a partial projection splits each left fibre
on which it is neither zero nor one. On the twelve-state model it splits the
\(P\) and \(I\) fibres, raising the block count

\[
6\longrightarrow8.
\]

On the sixteen-state sector repair it also splits the enlarged \(N\) fibre,
giving

\[
6\longrightarrow9.
\]

The commutant of a diagonal representation with fibre dimensions \(n_a\) has
dimension \(\sum_a n_a^2\). Direct counting gives

\[
30\to22
\]

and

\[
46\to32,
\]

respectively. In the multiplicity repair, choosing rank-one \(IN/NI\) lines
refines the multiplicity blocks and gives

\[
54\to38.
\]

If the enlarged abelian image is interpreted as gauge algebra before the
usual kernel and unimodularity quotients, the extra blocks supply two new
\(U(1)\) directions in the twelve-state model and three in either repaired
model. A physical promotion therefore requires a fresh gauge-kernel and
anomaly audit; the projector cannot be called a harmless observable and an
algebra generator simultaneously.

## 6. Support audit

As an operator, \(Q_{\rm anc}\) is diagonal. It commutes with \(J\), \(\gamma\) and
\(\Gamma_{\rm rel}\), and cannot itself add an off-diagonal finite-Dirac entry.

The commutator ([D,Q_anc]) is supported exactly on graph edges crossing the
anchor corner. Exhaustion gives

\[
IN-IP
\quad\hbox{and}\quad
NI-PI.
\]

These are the existing \(P-N\) edge and its real partner. In particular, no new
primitive forbidden \(L-N\) edge appears. Inner fluctuations built linearly
from \([D,Q_{\rm anc}]\) remain on existing primitive support.

Products of such commutators can still form open length-two paths. The
projection-lattice result therefore does not replace the conditional
expectation of claim 1543. It only shows that adjoining the projector is safe
at primitive support level while nontrivially changing the represented
algebra and its commutant.

## Verdict

The search closes all obvious internal locations for the desired projector:

\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{rcl}
\pi(\mathcal A_6)\text{ or }\pi^o(\mathcal A_6)&:&Q_{\rm anc}\text{ absent},\\
\pi(\mathcal A_6)\vee\pi^o(\mathcal A_6)&:&Q_{\rm anc}\text{ present but nonminimal},\\
\text{path/Jordan centre}&:&Q_{\rm anc}\text{ absent},\\
\text{multiplicity commutant}&:&Q_{\rm anc}\text{ present only after a ray choice}.
\end{array}}
\]

Thus the physical projection \(q=p_I+p_P\) appears only after the correspondence
identifies the two relevant sector orbits. Promoting its lift to sourced
algebra data changes the gauge/commutant structure; leaving it in the joint
diagonal algebra makes it one arbitrary sector sum among many. The desired
selection remains new correspondence input, not a latent central idempotent
of the existing finite geometry.
