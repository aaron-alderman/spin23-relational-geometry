## Result

Claim 1559's statement that the physical carrier has exactly two honest
branches was too narrow.  It assumed that the parent was not enlarged.  A
third branch exists:

\[
27_{E_6}=16_{1}\oplus10_{-2}\oplus1_{4}.
\]

After reduction to Pati--Salam,

\[
\begin{aligned}
16_1&\to(4,2,1)_1\oplus(\overline4,1,2)_1,\\
10_{-2}&\to(6,1,1)_{-2}\oplus(1,2,2)_{-2},\\
1_4&\to(1,1,1)_4.
\end{aligned}
\]

The repaired sixteen-state correspondence can therefore be identified
exactly with the (16_1) branch, while the eleven new complex states are

\[
(6,1,1)\oplus(1,2,2)\oplus(1,1,1),
\qquad 6+4+1=11.
\]

This is a real improvement over an arbitrary enlargement.  One chiral
(27) is perturbatively anomaly-free, its restriction to Pati--Salam has
even Witten parity for both weak factors, and its extra (10+1) is the
standard E6 exotic-plus-singlet completion rather than an ad hoc set of
labels.

It does **not**, however, make 27 an ordinary real carrier.  The full E6
representation is complex:

\[
27\not\cong\overline{27}.
\]

Ordinary E6-equivariant reality requires

\[
27\oplus\overline{27},\qquad \dim_{\mathbb C}=54.
\]

There is a useful but easily confused intermediate count.  If E6 and
(U(1)_\psi) have already been discarded and only Pati--Salam covariance is
required, the extra (10+1) branches are real while the family (16) still
needs its conjugate.  The smallest representation-theoretic ordinary-real
sum is then

\[
16\oplus\overline{16}\oplus10\oplus1,
\qquad \dim_{\mathbb C}=43.
\]

That 43-state object is not an E6 representation.  It cannot be used while
claiming unbroken E6 covariance.  A 27-state reality is possible only in the
same qualified sense as the earlier sixteen-state reopening: compose
antilinear conjugation with the E6 diagram involution that exchanges (27)
and (overline{27}).  This is a semilinear representation of a disconnected
(E_6\rtimes\mathbb Z_2) extension, not an ordinary E6-equivariant real
structure.

The E6 cubic gives a second positive result and a sharp limitation.  The
minimal invariant

\[
27_F,27_F,27_H
\]

contains the charge-neutral branch couplings

\[
16_1,16_1,10_{-2},
\qquad
10_{-2},10_{-2},1_4.
\]

It therefore supplies an ordinary family Dirac channel through the
((1,2,2)\subset10_H) and masses for the (10_F) exotics after a singlet
VEV.  It contains no Spin(10) (126_H), hence no renormalizable
((10,1,3)) right-Majorana carrier.  The four states transforming as a
Pati--Salam bidoublet inside the **matter** (27_F) are fermions and cannot
silently be reused as the scalar bidoublet needed by the seven H16 edges.

The larger scalar (351'_H) does contain both Spin(10) (10_H) and
(\overline{126}_H) branches in the displayed
\(27=16_1+10_{-2}+1_4\) charge convention.  Thus the multiplicity-one channel

\[
27_F,27_F,351'_H
\]

can place a Dirac bidoublet and a right-Majorana triplet in one E6 Yukawa
tensor.  This is the strongest surviving E6 result.  It unifies the parent
gauge tensor but does not supply the claim-1557 portal Ward law.  After
breaking, the two coefficients have the form

\[
k_D=Y_{351'}C_D Z_D v_D,
\qquad
k_M=Y_{351'}C_M Z_M v_M.
\]

E6 fixes the parent tensor identity and group-theoretic Clebsches.  It does
not by itself fix the VEV ratio, wavefunction factors, threshold transport,
selected family entries or H16 null mask.  Nor does its diagram involution
exchange the unequal Spin(10) (10) and (\overline{126}) branches.  The E6 route is
therefore a one-tensor construction, not the missing normalized typed
intertwiner.

The corrected carrier decision is

\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{carrier strategy}&\text{reality mechanism}&\text{main unresolved cost}\\ \hline
16&\text{left--right twist}&\text{disconnected PS lift and scalar closure}\\
32&16\oplus\overline{16}&\text{mirror decoupling and complete action}\\
27&\text{E6 chiral parent / outer twist}&\text{grading, Majorana scalar and Ward law}\\
54&27\oplus\overline{27}&\text{ordinary E6 reality at doubled spectrum cost}
\end{array}}
\]

The 27 is consequently the most economical **chiral E6 parent**, but it is
not a replacement for either 32 or 54 when ordinary reality is required.

## 1. Exact state map

The H16 identification is not just a dimension coincidence.  Under the
standard subgroup chain the two eight-dimensional pieces of the Spin(10)
spinor are exactly

\[
F_L=(4,2,1),
\qquad
F_R^c=(\overline4,1,2),
\]

which are the only dimension-compatible assignments of the H16
(8+8) grading from claim 1556.  The remaining eleven states are exhausted
by three complete Pati--Salam representations:

1. ((6,1,1)), a six-dimensional real colour representation;
2. ((1,2,2)), a four-dimensional real bidoublet representation; and
3. ((1,1,1)), a singlet.

Under Standard Model reduction the Spin(10) (10_F) supplies vectorlike
exotics, while the (1_F) is a gauge singlet.  Their possible masses and
mixing require an actual scalar vacuum and Yukawa calculation; anomaly
freedom does not make them harmless or heavy.

There is also a grading obstruction at the unbroken E6 stage.  The complex
(27) is irreducible, so Schur's lemma gives

\[
\dim\operatorname{End}_{E_6}(27)=1.
\]

Every E6-equivariant involutive grading is therefore scalar.  It cannot
restrict to the nontrivial (8+8) grading that distinguishes the two
Pati--Salam family halves.  The old grading can reappear only after the
Pati--Salam embedding and symmetry-breaking direction are selected, or it
must be declared external correspondence data on which full E6 does not
act equivariantly.  Enlarging 16 to 27 thus buys a parent representation at
the price of moving the grading problem into symmetry breaking.

## 2. Anomaly ledger

The (U(1)_\psi) charges give transparent checks of the complete chiral
multiplet.  The mixed gravitational sum is

\[
16(1)+10(-2)+1(4)=0,
\]

and the cubic sum is

\[
16(1)^3+10(-2)^3+1(4)^3=16-80+64=0.
\]

With Spin(10) quadratic indices normalized as
(T(16)=2), (T(10)=1), the mixed anomaly also vanishes:

\[
2(1)+1(-2)=0.
\]

At the Pati--Salam stage, the (16) already cancels its (SU(4)^3)
anomaly.  The six is real, and the weak bidoublet carries no (SU(4))
charge.  Each weak group saw four doublets in the family (16); the new
bidoublet adds two, giving six for (SU(2)_L) and six for (SU(2)_R).
Both mod-two Witten anomalies remain zero.

These checks establish the representation-theoretic anomaly advantage of
27.  They do not audit a disconnected (E_6\rtimes\mathbb Z_2) global
anomaly, nor any additional low-energy abelian factors introduced by the
label geometry.

## 3. Three distinct reality questions

The counts 27, 43 and 54 answer different questions.

### Full E6 covariance

Because the (27) is complex, ordinary conjugation lands in
(overline{27}).  The minimum ordinary E6 representation closed by (J)
is (27\oplus\overline{27}), of dimension 54.

### Broken Pati--Salam covariance only

After forgetting the broken E6 generators and (U(1)_\psi), the
(10+1) restriction is self-conjugate.  Only the (16) family branch needs
its conjugate, giving 43.  This asymmetric truncation is legal only as a
broken-phase Pati--Salam field list, not as a complete ultraviolet E6
multiplet.

### Outer-twisted covariance

The E6 Dynkin diagram has an order-two automorphism that conjugates the
complex fundamental.  Composing it with antilinear conjugation permits a
27-dimensional semilinear real structure.  Its covariance condition is
twisted,

\[
J\rho(g)J^{-1}=\rho(\sigma(g)),
\]

not ordinary commutation.  A physical use must specify the disconnected
extension, its anomaly status, its breaking, and the relation of its
restriction to the left--right twist of claim 1556.  Without those data,
“27 is real” is false; “27 admits an outer-twisted real form” is the precise
conditional statement.

## 4. What the cubic invariant actually buys

Charge conservation inside (27^3) exposes both allowed branch types.  The
(16,16,10) term gives the familiar Spin(10) Dirac Yukawa coupling.  The
(10,10,1) term can lift the vectorlike exotics when the singlet component
of a scalar (27_H) condenses.  Neither term contains a (126_H).

Consequently the minimal 27 scalar route does not close all of claim 1559's
required maps:

- it has a Dirac bidoublet;
- it can address exotic masses;
- it has no renormalizable right-Majorana triplet;
- it does not automatically supply the full staged breaking sector; and
- it does not turn the seven label-edge numbers into physical Clebsch maps.

A higher-dimensional effective operator using additional spinorial Higgs
fields could generate a Majorana mass, but that is a different, explicitly
nonminimal reopening and loses the claimed minimal cubic explanation.

The (351'_H) route is more powerful.  Its Spin(10) dimension ledger

\[
1+10+16+54+126+144=351
\]

contains both the (10_H) Dirac branch and the
(\overline{126}_H) Dirac/Majorana
branches.  It is therefore a credible parent for a one-Yukawa-tensor
relation of the kind used in E6-to-Spin(10) constructions.  But it is an
additional 351-dimensional scalar multiplet with many components whose
vacuum, masses and thresholds must be audited.  It is not supplied by the
eleven extra fermions of a matter (27).

## 5. Why the Ward problem survives

For one matter family, the symmetric (351') channel occurs with
multiplicity one.  At the unbroken gauge-tensor level that gives one Yukawa
number.  For three copies of (27_F), it gives a symmetric (3\times3)
matrix with

\[
\dim\operatorname{Sym}^2\mathbb C^3=6
\]

independent entries.  Gauge unification alone does not identify the selected
(Y_{PP}) and (Y_{PN}) entries or enforce the desired null mask.

Even in a one-family truncation, the low-energy coefficients are not merely
the parent (Y_{351'}).  They include distinct Clebsches, condensates,
normalizations and threshold maps.  The E6 Ward identities hold in the
unbroken theory; after choosing a vacuum they do not imply
(k_D=k_M), or even a scale-independent fixed ratio, unless the vacuum and
renormalization data preserve the corresponding one-dimensional line.

Finally, the relevant diagram automorphism conjugates full E6
representations.  It does not provide a Pati--Salam-equivariant isomorphism
between a bidoublet and a triplet, nor between the complete Spin(10) 10 and
\(\overline{126}\) branches.  Therefore it cannot be the typed exchange

\[
U:C_D\longrightarrow C_M
\]

required by claim 1557.  E6 supplies a common ultraviolet tensor, not a
normalized, threshold-parallel source bundle.

## 6. Programme consequence

The 27 branch deserves to remain open, but with a precise work order:

1. choose ordinary (27\oplus\overline{27}) reality or explicitly adopt
   the outer-twisted (27);
2. specify how the H16 grading emerges after E6 breaking;
3. add (351'_H), or declare and derive a controlled effective Majorana
   operator;
4. write the complete exotic-mass and scalar-vacuum system;
5. impose a family mechanism selecting the required tensor entries and
   null mask; and
6. compute whether VEV, wavefunction and threshold factors preserve one
   portal ray.

The early kill test remains item 6 together with the family selection in
item 5.  If those fail, the E6 parent is still an elegant unified spectrum,
but it does not explain the programme's common portal coefficient.

## Spin(2,3) status

Spin(2,3) can continue to organize the relation sheet, real/twisted-real
choice and the desired H16 grading after symmetry breaking.  This audit does
not find an embedding theorem by which Spin(2,3) selects the E6 diagram
twist, the (351'_H) vacuum direction, the family tensor entry, or the
parallel portal source.  Those are now the exact points at which a genuine
Spin(2,3)--E6 theorem would have to act.

## Verdict

The answer to “not 27?” is yes, but in the chiral-parent sense.  The E6
(27) is a third and promising carrier branch because it embeds H16 exactly,
adds eleven anomaly-compatible states, and exposes a standard cubic
structure.  It is not an ordinary real completion, does not preserve the
nontrivial H16 grading before symmetry breaking, and its minimal cubic lacks
the right-Majorana representation.  Adding (351'_H) unifies the two portal
types in one parent tensor but still fails to derive the selected-family,
VEV-normalized, RG- and threshold-parallel Ward line.
