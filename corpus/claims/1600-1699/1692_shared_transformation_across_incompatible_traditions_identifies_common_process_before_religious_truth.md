# Shared transformation across incompatible traditions identifies common process before religious truth

**Date:** 22 October 2026

**Gate:** C2 and P1

**Status:** comparative-identifiability theorem and current negative result

**Depends on:** [the rival matrix](1682_the_participatory_remainder_requires_active_religious_and_secular_rivals_not_a_belief_versus_unbelief_binary.md), [the empirical audit](1687_current_evidence_supports_small_heterogeneous_practice_effects_but_not_a_christian_specific_field_tilt.md), and [the participatory epistemology collision](1688_kenotic_hiddenness_preserves_freedom_only_by_accepting_nonresistant_nonbelief_as_adverse_evidence.md)

## Result

Suppose mutually incompatible traditions (T_1,ldots,T_n) produce the same outcome profile (O) through matched practices. Then (O) identifies neither tradition's incompatible truth claims. The first identified target is a common process (C): attention, ritual, expectation, costly commitment, social regulation, narrative reframing or some combination.

Formally, if

\[
P(O\mid T_i,M_i,Z)\approx P(O\mid T_j,M_j,Z)
\]

for rival (i,j), then the Bayes factor contributed by (O) between those traditions is approximately one unless a tradition independently predicts that equivalence.

## 1. Current collision

Experimental prayer effects on retaliation have been reported across Christian, Hindu and Muslim participants without detectable affiliation moderation ([Fincham et al. 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25640652/)). Meditation and secular mindfulness also produce overlapping prosocial outcomes in meta-analysis. These findings support practice-induced change but presently favour shared mechanisms over Christian uniqueness.

Religious experience faces the same discriminator: incompatible experiential contents and naturalistic explanations are prima-facie defeaters of automatic veridicality ([SEP, "Religious Experience"](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/sum2022/entries/religious-experience/)). Intensity, sincerity and transformation can be common while propositional interpretations conflict.

## 2. Component decomposition

A useful comparative design separates:

\[
M=(\text{attention},\text{petition},\text{narrative},\text{community},
\text{service},\text{ritual},\text{authority},\text{expectancy}).
\]

Factorial or micro-randomized assignments can estimate components that are ethically manipulable. Worldview identity and divine relation remain nonrandomized. Mediation analysis should be exploratory unless sequential ignorability is defensible.

## 3. Possible Christian specificity

Christian specificity would require a preregistered pattern tied to distinctive content rather than a larger generic wellbeing score—for example costly enemy-love, confession without coercion, status reversal and durable out-group service. The same instruments and moral standards must be applied to rivals. Failure to outperform an equivalence margin demotes specificity even if all programmes help.

## Verdict

The current evidence identifies overlapping human practice mechanisms before any exclusive theological interpretation. Comparative transformation is evidence that participation matters; it is not yet evidence that Christianity alone is true.
