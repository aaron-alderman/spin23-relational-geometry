# Spiritual safety and adverse outcomes are co-primary, not optional corrections to participatory research

**Date:** 22 October 2026

**Gate:** E1

**Status:** ethical admission protocol; human study requires independent review

**Depends on:** [the study design](1683_a_preregistered_longitudinal_active_control_design_can_test_participatory_consequences_but_not_divine_agency.md) and [the anti-circularity gate](1684_the_kenotic_field_tilt_fails_if_hiddenness_and_fruit_are_allowed_to_explain_every_outcome.md)

## Result

A participatory study is inadmissible if it counts benefit carefully and harm anecdotally. Spiritual coercion, manipulation, shame, unsafe reconciliation, institutional dependency and trauma must be preregistered co-primary outcomes with independent reporting routes.

The Belmont principles require respect, beneficence and justice, with consent free of coercion and undue influence, especially where authority relationships or vulnerable participants are involved ([US HHS, Belmont Report](https://www.hhs.gov/ohrp/regulations-and-policy/belmont-report/read-the-belmont-report/index.html)). Clergy, mentors and community leaders therefore cannot control recruitment, consent, withdrawal or adverse-event adjudication for their own programme.

## 1. Mandatory protections

- consent and withdrawal handled independently of religious authority;
- no loss of pastoral, social, medical or material support for refusal or withdrawal;
- explicit permission to doubt, decline practices, change affiliation or leave;
- no required trauma disclosure or confrontation with an offender;
- forgiveness never operationalized as reconciliation, restored access or waiver of justice;
- independent safeguarding, referral and complaint pathways;
- blinded adverse-event adjudication where feasible;
- predefined individual and programme stopping rules;
- full reporting by assigned arm, including attrition and nonadherence.

CONSORT Harms requires systematic definitions, instruments, metrics and time points for adverse outcomes, not generic statements that no serious event was noticed ([CONSORT Harms 2022](https://www.bmj.com/content/381/bmj-2022-073725)).

## 2. Harm measurement

The outcome battery should include religious/spiritual struggle, psychological safety, coercive leadership, identity suppression, shame, PTSD symptoms, delayed care and deterioration in close relationships. A new Spiritual Safety Scale reports a three-factor structure for psychological safety, leadership and culture, with strong internal reliability and associations with PTSD symptoms and negative religious coping; it is promising but currently validated mainly in North American Christian samples and requires independent transport tests ([Chong and Tortez 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42474978/)). The Religious and Spiritual Struggles Scale provides a broader multidomain measure ([Exline et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0036465)).

## 3. Theological non-exemption

Suffering during a practice cannot be classified as purification, conviction or spiritual warfare by default. Participant interpretation may be recorded, but clinical and safeguarding criteria remain independent. A programme that improves selected prosocial scores while increasing coercion or trauma has not demonstrated good fruit.

## Verdict

The ethics gate is locally specified but not self-authorizing. Any real study requires institutional ethics review, community-independent safeguarding and validated cross-cultural harm measures. Until then, the empirical protocol remains a design artifact.
