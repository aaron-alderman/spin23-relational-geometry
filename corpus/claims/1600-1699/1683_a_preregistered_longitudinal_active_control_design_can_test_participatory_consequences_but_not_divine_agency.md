# A preregistered longitudinal active-control design can test participatory consequences but not divine agency

**Date:** 22 October 2026

**Scope:** empirical protocol for consequences of participatory practices

**Status:** executable study design; no data collected

**Depends on:** [the rival-model matrix](1682_the_participatory_remainder_requires_active_religious_and_secular_rivals_not_a_belief_versus_unbelief_binary.md)

## Result

The most informative feasible test is a multisite, preregistered longitudinal study of people independently choosing intensive practice pathways, augmented where ethical by randomized encouragement and practice-component assignments. It estimates consequences of participation; it does not randomize God, faith or revelation.

## 1. Design

Recruit participants before entry into four matched programmes:

1. Christian communal prayer, scripture, service and reconciliation practice;
2. non-Christian religious practice matched for intensity and community;
3. secular contemplative plus service community;
4. low-intensity information or wait-list comparator.

Measure at baseline, during the programme, immediately after it and at delayed follow-ups. Publish the protocol, hypotheses, instruments, exclusion rules and analysis code before inspecting outcomes.

Where assignment to worldview is neither credible nor ethical, randomize narrower components: invitation timing, additional service opportunities, reflection prompts, mentorship access or programme intensity. Use these as instruments only if exclusion and monotonicity assumptions are stated and stress-tested.

## 2. Primary estimands

For outcome (O_k), estimate both intention-to-treat and treatment-on-the-treated effects where assignment permits:

\[
\Delta_k=E[O_k\mid A=1]-E[O_k\mid A=0].
\]

For self-selected pathways, use repeated measures, baseline adjustment, overlap diagnostics, negative controls and sensitivity bounds rather than presenting adjusted association as randomization.

Primary outcomes should combine:

- incentivized allocation to out-groups;
- behavioural willingness to repair a real but safe conflict;
- independently coded helping under cost;
- informant reports from people not recruited by the participant;
- retention of intellectual humility under identity threat;
- preregistered adverse-event measures.

Self-reported spiritual experience is secondary because expectancy directly affects it.

## 3. Measurement changes the outcome

Repeated questioning can itself prompt reflection and behaviour. Every arm therefore receives the same measurement schedule, and a measurement-frequency subrandomization estimates reactivity. Measurement is included in the causal graph rather than treated as a transparent window.

The target contrast becomes

\[
P(O\mid M,Q)-P(O\mid M_0,Q),
\]

with (Q) the shared observation protocol. A nested comparison of (Q_{\rm high}) and (Q_{\rm low}) estimates measurement-induced change.

## 4. Analysis and openness

- estimate heterogeneous effects rather than only a pooled mean;
- report attrition by arm and inverse-probability sensitivity;
- publish null, adverse and subgroup results;
- use blinded outcome coding where possible;
- reserve a held-out site for transport testing;
- require independent replication before theological interpretation;
- prohibit post-hoc replacement of failed outcomes with testimony excerpts.

## 5. Ethical boundary

No participant should be pressured to adopt or abandon a religion, forgive an abuser, cease treatment, disclose trauma publicly or interpret distress as spiritual failure. An independent safeguarding route and adverse-event stopping rule are mandatory.

## Verdict

A study can estimate whether practice packages tilt observable human outcomes. It cannot make divine agency a randomized variable or convert a residual into proof.
