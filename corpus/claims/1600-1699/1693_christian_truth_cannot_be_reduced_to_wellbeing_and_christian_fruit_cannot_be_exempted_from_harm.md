# Christian truth cannot be reduced to wellbeing, and Christian fruit cannot be exempted from harm

**Date:** 22 October 2026

**Gates:** T1, T2, C2 and E1

**Status:** internal theological red-team boundary

**Depends on:** [the biblical synthesis](1681_kenosis_election_incarnation_participation_and_remnant_form_a_coherent_biblical_chain_but_not_a_deduction.md), [the empirical demotion](1687_current_evidence_supports_small_heterogeneous_practice_effects_but_not_a_christian_specific_field_tilt.md), and [the ethics gate](1691_spiritual_safety_and_adverse_outcomes_are_coprimary_not_optional_corrections_to_participatory_research.md)

## Result

Christianity does not predict simple prosperity, comfort or symptom reduction. Its own texts include persecution, lament, costly discipleship and apparent abandonment. Therefore generic wellbeing cannot serve as the decisive Christian outcome.

But this correction cannot exempt Christianity from observable moral failure. The same canon gives public criteria concerning love, mercy, truthfulness, service, domination and treatment of vulnerable people. A participatory model must distinguish expected cost from preventable institutional harm without deciding the distinction in its own favour.

## 1. Three outcome layers

1. **Clinical welfare:** distress, health, safety and functioning. Christianity need not dominate every measure, but caused deterioration remains morally relevant.
2. **Moral fruit:** costly care, enemy-love, honesty, forgiveness without impunity, status reversal and out-group concern. These are closer to the internal claim.
3. **Truth:** incarnation and resurrection are historical and metaphysical claims. Moral fruit may affect their credibility but cannot make them true by definition.

The field-tilt programme primarily investigates layers one and two. It cannot close layer three.

## 2. Failure symmetry

If favourable Christian behaviour is admitted as evidence for the theological model, systematic Christian domination, abuse, exclusion or dishonesty must count as adverse evidence under the same bridge. Appeals to human freedom may explain variance but cannot make the aggregate pattern evidentially one-sided.

Likewise, good fruit outside Christianity must be counted using the same standards. Otherwise "fruit" becomes a protected label rather than an outcome.

## 3. The surviving theological claim

The strongest defensible proposal is not "Christians are better". It is:

> the Christ-pattern supplies a particular normative and participatory form that can transform people, can be betrayed by its carriers, and is judged partly by whether its enacted consequences converge on that form.

This formulation is meaningful but modest. It predicts both transformation and recognizable betrayal, so quantitative content must come from fixed rates, contrasts or component effects rather than from the narrative alone.

## Verdict

The participatory programme may test consequences and integrity of enactment. It cannot infer Christianity's truth from wellbeing, excuse harm as discipleship, or infer falsity from suffering alone.
