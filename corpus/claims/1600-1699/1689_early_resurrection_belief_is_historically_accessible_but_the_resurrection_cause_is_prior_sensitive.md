# Early resurrection belief is historically accessible but the resurrection cause is prior-sensitive

**Date:** 22 October 2026

**Gate:** H1

**Status:** historical-method boundary; neither apologetic proof nor naturalistic disproof

**Depends on:** [the theological chain](1681_kenosis_election_incarnation_participation_and_remnant_form_a_coherent_biblical_chain_but_not_a_deduction.md) and [the hiddenness collision](1688_kenotic_hiddenness_preserves_freedom_only_by_accepting_nonresistant_nonbelief_as_adverse_evidence.md)

## Result

Historical method can investigate Jesus' execution, the early transmission of resurrection claims, Paul's testimony and the emergence of communities organized around those claims. It cannot identify "God raised Jesus" merely by showing that resurrection belief was early, sincere or transformative.

First Corinthians 15:3--8 is the earliest extant documentary appearance list and preserves material Paul says he received; scholarship treats it as the natural starting point for resurrection investigation ([Carnley, *Resurrection in Retrospect*, chapter 4](https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctv173dzf3.8); [Bell 2019](https://nottingham-repository.worktribe.com/output/919412)). Early attestation shortens the development window. It does not by itself determine the experiences' cause, physical character or theological interpretation.

## 1. Separate the evidence levels

1. **Text:** Paul transmitted a death, burial, raising and appearance tradition and included his own experience.
2. **Historical reconstruction:** named early Christians and groups were reported as recipients of appearances, and resurrection belief became central early.
3. **Experience hypothesis:** some participants had experiences interpreted as appearances of the risen Jesus.
4. **Event hypothesis:** Jesus was bodily raised by God.
5. **Theological identity:** this event vindicates the Christian account of God and participation.

Each step requires premises not supplied by the previous step. Empty-tomb traditions, appearance narratives and Paul's language must be analysed rather than combined into one maximally strong "minimal fact."

## 2. Comparative hypotheses

The evidence must be evaluated under at least:

- (H_R): bodily resurrection by God;
- (H_V): bereavement, visionary or revelatory experiences interpreted within Jewish apocalyptic expectation;
- (H_L): narrative and liturgical development around an earlier proclamation;
- (H_S): social reinforcement and identity formation following traumatic leader loss;
- (H_M): mixed sequences combining experiences, interpretation and later narrative development.

These are model families, not single fully specified likelihoods. "Resurrection best explains the facts" is incomplete until priors, likelihood-bearing details and the complexity assigned to every family are declared.

## 3. Prior sensitivity is irreducible

For evidence (E), posterior odds obey

\[
\frac{P(H_R\mid E)}{P(H_M\mid E)}
=
\frac{P(H_R)}{P(H_M)}
\frac{P(E\mid H_R)}{P(E\mid H_M)}.
\]

The historical record may change odds substantially while leaving disagreement because the prior probability of divine action and the specification of the naturalistic mixture differ. Philosophical treatments of miracles emphasize that Bayesian machinery cannot decide the matter before the historical evidence and background assumptions are supplied ([SEP, "Miracles"](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/spr2014/entries/miracles/)).

## 4. Participatory evidence does not remove the boundary

Present transformation can be evidence about the consequences of resurrection belief and practice. It is not independent confirmation of the historical event when the practice package is constituted by that belief. Conversely, the inability of ordinary historical method to reproduce a miracle is not proof that no miracle occurred.

## Verdict

The historically secure target is early resurrection proclamation and experience-reporting, not unique causal identification of bodily resurrection. The H1 gate terminates at a prior-sensitive abductive boundary unless genuinely new historical evidence or a justified background model is supplied.
