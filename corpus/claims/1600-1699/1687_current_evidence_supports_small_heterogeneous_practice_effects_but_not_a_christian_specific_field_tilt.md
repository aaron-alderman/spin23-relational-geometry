# Current evidence supports small heterogeneous practice effects but not a Christian-specific field tilt

**Date:** 22 October 2026

**Gates:** C1, C2 and M1

**Status:** external-evidence audit; strong uniqueness claim unsupported

**Depends on:** [the rival matrix](1682_the_participatory_remainder_requires_active_religious_and_secular_rivals_not_a_belief_versus_unbelief_binary.md) and [the proposed study](1683_a_preregistered_longitudinal_active_control_design_can_test_participatory_consequences_but_not_divine_agency.md)

## Result

The available evidence makes a broad practice-effect hypothesis plausible but does not presently identify a Christian-specific field tilt.

A 2024 meta-analysis covering 701 effects, 237 samples and 811,663 participants found religiosity associated with prosociality at (r=.13), but the association was (r=.15) for self-report and only (r=.06) for directly measured behaviour, with substantial heterogeneity. This is evidence against using testimony alone as the main outcome ([Kelly, Kramer and Shariff 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38407059/)).

A 2026 target-trial emulation across six annual waves found that deterministic "make everyone attend" contrasts failed positivity because only about 2--3% of non-attenders initiated monthly attendance per year. Supported stochastic interventions produced selective small gains in meaning, purpose, forgiveness and sexual satisfaction, with little movement in distress, somatic health, belonging or perceived support ([Bulbulia et al. 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13122395/)). The result rejects both a global-benefit story and a simple social-contact reduction, but does not isolate Christianity or divine agency.

## 1. Active rivals already produce overlapping effects

Meditation and mindfulness meta-analyses report small-to-medium prosocial effects, including overt helping, but disagree about robustness. One review found effects across active and inactive controls ([Luberto et al. 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6081743/)); another found compassion effects concentrated in passive-control studies and where the intervention teacher was a study author ([Kreplin, Farias and Brazil 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5799363/)). A later meta-analysis of ethics-free mindfulness reported (g=.426) for overt prosocial acts ([Donald et al. 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31971095/)).

Brief prayer can reduce anger or retaliation in laboratory experiments, but one cross-cultural experiment found no moderation by Christian, Hindu or Muslim affiliation ([Fincham and colleagues 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25640652/)). A 16-day Christian-college experiment found both prayer and devotional-attention groups changed in state forgiveness, with an additional empathy change in the prayer arm ([Vasiliauskas and McMinn 2013](https://digitalcommons.georgefox.edu/gscp_fac/163/)). These results keep practice effects live while directly weakening easy claims of Christian specificity.

Intercessory prayer for another person's medical outcome tests a different causal claim from participatory transformation. A Cochrane review of ten trials found no clear differences in clinical state, complications or leaving the study early and high heterogeneity in the mortality result ([Roberts, Ahmed and Hall 2007](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17253449/)). It neither verifies nor cleanly falsifies the participatory model.

## 2. Measurement reactivity is real but small on average

An updated meta-analysis of 43 randomized studies and 104,096 participants found that merely asking behavioural questions produced a small effect, (SMD=.06), with heterogeneity and risk-of-bias concerns ([McCambridge et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7308800/)). Expert guidance therefore recommends explicitly designing trials around possible measurement reactions ([French et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7614249/)).

This supports the proposed measurement-frequency subrandomization. It does not support a quantum interpretation: the known mechanism class is psychological and procedural reactivity.

## 3. Updated empirical verdict

The current evidence ordering is:

\[
\text{generic practice effect: plausible}
>
\text{religious-package effect: plausible but selected}
>
\text{Christian-specific profile: unestablished}
>
\text{divine causal identification: not identified}.
\]

The next informative work is not another believer-versus-nonbeliever correlation. It is a preregistered active-rival design with behavioural outcomes, harms, positivity diagnostics and held-out replication.

## Verdict

The strong uniqueness version is demoted. The narrower participatory research programme remains live because current studies do not implement its full comparator and outcome design.
