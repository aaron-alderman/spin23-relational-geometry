# Participatory theology and Spin(2,3) share a selector--realization grammar but no cross-domain mechanism

**Date:** 22 October 2026

**Scope:** bounded theology-to-physics comparison

**Status:** structural analogy only; no physical promotion

**Depends on:** [claim 1680](1680_the_participatory_remainder_is_a_testable_relational_hypothesis_not_a_residual_proof_of_god.md), [the A5 selector theorem](../1200-1299/1221_symmetric_data_cannot_select_a_nonfixed_orientation_and_a5_requires_a_covariant_selector_input.md), and [the source-to-ansatz firewall](../1400-1499/1467_any_source_can_seed_an_ansatz_but_only_typed_translation_collision_and_kill_tests_can_promote_it.md)

## Result

Participatory theology and the Spin(2,3) programme exhibit the same abstract grammar:

\[
\text{possibility space}
\xrightarrow{\text{selector}}
\text{particular realization}
\xrightarrow{\text{participation or measurement}}
\text{observable consequences}.
\]

This common diagram is useful for detecting identity compression. It supplies no map between divine agency and a Spin(2,3) field, no Lagrangian term, no quantum measurement mechanism and no physical prediction.

## 1. Typed comparison

| Role | Theology | Spin(2,3) programme |
|---|---|---|
| possibility structure | creation and universal invitation | representation, orbit or vacuum candidate space |
| selector | election and concrete history | boundary datum, condensate, index, orientation or dynamical vacuum |
| realization | incarnation and community | selected phase, module and effective action |
| participation | discipleship, sacrament and service | state preparation, coupling and measurement protocol |
| remainder | freedom, nonresponse and epistemic exposure | residual orbit, unfixed modulus, unexplained data or model error |

The columns are analogical. Terms on a row are not identical and cannot be substituted into one another.

## 2. What transfers

Only methodological questions transfer safely:

- Has an identity (A=B=C) hidden unproved relational arrows?
- Does the proposed selector contain information absent from symmetric inputs?
- Is the particular carrier selected for a declared function?
- Does measurement alter the prepared state, and is that effect modelled?
- What remainder survives quotienting, fitting and observation?
- Which outcome would make the proposed bridge fail?

These questions sharpen both domains without making either evidence for the other.

## 3. What does not transfer

The following promotions are forbidden without new mathematics and evidence:

\[
\text{kenosis}=\text{spontaneous symmetry breaking},
\qquad
\text{election}=\text{a physical projector},
\qquad
\text{Christ}=\text{an order parameter},
\qquad
\text{grace}=\text{a quantum field},
\qquad
\text{faith}=\text{wavefunction collapse}.
\]

The resemblance may rank questions to investigate. It cannot choose coefficients, representations, vacua or experimental outcomes.

## 4. Programme consequence

The comparison nevertheless exposes a recurring architectural rule:

\[
\boxed{\text{identity compression erases the middle};\qquad
\text{typed relations expose the burden of proof}.}
\]

For Spin(2,3), the missing middle remains the actual selector, action, quantum closure and empirical map. For theology, it remains the historical and participatory bridge from the Christian claim to distinctive consequences. Failure in either row stays local to that row.

## Verdict

The common selector--realization grammar is retained as a source-blind research heuristic. The cross-domain mechanism claim is rejected.
