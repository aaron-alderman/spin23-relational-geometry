## Result

The metric completion of the trace-reduced axial action can be expanded
exactly far enough to identify every mixed metric--axial vertex on a flat,
constant axial background.  The result separates into two qualitatively
different pieces:

1. the divergence kinetic term couples to the metric only through the
   packet's harmonic gauge function; and
2. the algebraic axial mass term contains a mixing with transverse-traceless
   metric fluctuations that no harmonic gauge condition removes.

Thus the derivative mixing is gauge-sector mixing, but the remaining
constant-background graviton contribution to the axial mass response is a
genuine bosonic Hessian calculation.

## Linear divergence variation

Treat the reduced axial variable as a one-form and define

\[
X=Dp={1\over\sqrt g}\partial_\mu
\left(\sqrt g\,g^{\mu\nu}p_\nu\right).
\]

For \(\delta g_{\mu\nu}=h_{\mu\nu}\) at fixed \(p_\mu\),

\[
\delta_gX=-h^{\mu\nu}\nabla_\mu p_\nu
-p_\nu\left(\nabla_\mu h^{\mu\nu}-{1\over2}\nabla^\nu h\right).
\]

On a flat constant background \(\bar p\), write

\[
F^\nu(h)=\partial_\mu h^{\mu\nu}-{1\over2}\partial^\nu h.
\]

Then the complete first variation is

\[
\boxed{\delta X=\partial_\mu s^\mu-\bar p_\nu F^\nu(h).}
\]

Since \(\bar X=0\), the exact quadratic contribution of the rational kinetic
term is simply

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal L_{X}^{(2)}={F(\bar p^2)\over2}
\left[\partial\cdot s-\bar p\cdot F(h)\right]^2,
\qquad
F(u)={b_XC_t\over C_t+4b_Xu/9}.
}
\]

The derivative cross vertex therefore vanishes in strict harmonic gauge,
but must be retained at the packet point \(\alpha=1\), where \(F_\mu\) is a
Gaussian gauge mode rather than a delta-functional constraint.

## Exponential-coframe mass vertices

After the antisymmetric coframe fluctuation is gauge fixed, use the packet's
symmetric exponential coordinate

\[
g_{\mu\nu}=(e^h)_{\mu\nu},
\qquad
\sqrt g=e^{h/2},
\qquad h=h^\mu{}_{\mu}.
\]

For \(p=\bar p+s\), the quadratic part of
\(\sqrt g\,A g^{\mu\nu}p_\mu p_\nu/2\) is

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal L_A^{(2)}={A\over2}\bigg[&s^2
+h\,\bar p\cdot s-2h^{\mu\nu}\bar p_\mu s_\nu\\
&+{1\over2}\bar p_\mu(h^2)^{\mu\nu}\bar p_\nu
-{h\over2}h^{\mu\nu}\bar p_\mu\bar p_\nu
+{h^2\over8}\bar p^2\bigg].
\end{aligned}
\]

This expression uses the exponential split.  Replacing it with the familiar
linear-metric expansion would change the \(h^2\bar p^2\) seagull and would
not implement the frozen packet.

## The surviving physical mixing

Impose the linear harmonic condition \(F_\mu(h)=0\) and take a
transverse-traceless fluctuation, \(k^\mu h_{\mu\nu}=0\) and \(h=0\).  The
mixed mass vertex reduces to

\[
\boxed{\mathcal L_{A,hs}^{(2)}=-A h^{\mu\nu}\bar p_\mu s_\nu.}
\]

It is generically nonzero.  For example, with momentum along direction
zero, \(h_{12}=h_{21}\), \(\bar p\) along direction one and \(s\) along
direction two, the fluctuation is transverse and traceless while the vertex
is nonvanishing.

Therefore neither the harmonic gauge condition nor the source independence
of the ghosts can reduce the complete constant-background determinant to
the isolated axial scalar determinant of claim 1660.

## Closed and remaining source vertices

Equations above close the \((h,s)\) part of the constant-background
\(H_{B,1}\) and \(H_{B,2}\) vertices generated by the exact reduced axial
action.  At \(\bar X=0\), the tensor-torsion channel has no quadratic
background-source insertion, while trace torsion has already been eliminated
exactly.

The remaining operation was sharply delimited: combine these vertices with
the packet-normalized Einstein graviton and gauge-mode propagators and
evaluate

\[
{1\over2}\operatorname{Tr}(G_BH_{B,2})
-{1\over4}\operatorname{Tr}(G_BH_{B,1}G_BH_{B,1}).
\]

Claim 1662 performs that trace.  Opposite loop momenta make the mass and
harmonic-gauge vertices combine into \(A+b_Xk^2\), cancelling the
longitudinal axial propagator and leaving only scaleless integrals.  Hence
the constant-background metric contribution to \(\delta A\) is zero.
Nonconstant curved backgrounds remain necessary for the other four response
coefficients.
