## Result

The supersymmetry route is now bounded at both a general matter-coupled
extended example and the maximal endpoint.  (N=2) leaves target geometry,
gauging and vacuum data to be chosen.  (N=8) is much more rigid in field
content, yet the (omega)-deformed (SO(8)) family shows that maximal
supersymmetry and a common maximally symmetric quadratic spectrum still do not
select unique interactions.

This is stronger than observing that a coupling is absent from a Ward
identity.  It supplies inequivalent fully supersymmetric theories that agree
on the relevant quadratic spectrum.  Therefore neither pole counting nor
raising (N) can by itself derive the axial coefficient.

The maximal theory also changes the problem radically: its massless multiplet
has 128 bosonic and 128 fermionic on-shell degrees of freedom.  Calling one of
its pseudoscalars “torsion” does not establish that it is the propagating
longitudinal mode of an independent Lorentz connection, and it does not derive
its normalized coupling to the observed fermions.

The generic higher-(N) path is exhausted as a model-independent selector.
The route can reopen only with a concrete construction that supplies:

1. a selected gauging/deformation and electric--magnetic frame;
2. a controlled vacuum and supersymmetry-breaking or truncation trajectory;
3. a higher-dimensional or other microscopic origin where claimed;
4. an explicit map to the independent PGT connection and its (0^-) pole;
5. the full normalized mixing and mass matrices; and
6. threshold matching that computes the fermion axial coefficient.

The other live construction boundaries are unchanged: a complete
higher-spin/PGT dictionary or a stabilized torsional compactification.  Each
requires new model data rather than further inference from the current corpus.
