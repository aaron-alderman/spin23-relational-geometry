# The Zenodo Chern quadrature reproduces, but it is not the exact \(\sigma_z\)-Lindblad Hall response

**Date:** 20 October 2026  
**Status:** exact finite-dimensional Liouvillian/Kubo no-go plus reproduced phenomenological quadrature; no physical data and no Spin(2,3) evidence  
**Source audited:** [Zenodo 10.5281/zenodo.19874093](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.19874093)  
**Protocol:** ZENODO-19874093-EXACT-LIOUVILLIAN-KUBO-KILL-1

## Result in one sentence

The released code correctly evaluates its declared Berry-curvature quadrature and
reproduces the doubled cusp, but the finite softened plateau is not the exact
steady-state Hall response of the written Lindblad equation with \(L=\sigma_z\):
for every \(\gamma>0\), its generic momentum-sector steady state is \(I/2\), so
the exact Liouvillian Kubo forcing is zero and the DC Hall response vanishes.

## 1. What the preprint claims

The preprint studies the Qi-Wu-Zhang two-band Hamiltonian

\[
H(\mathbf k)=\mathbf d(\mathbf k)\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma,
\qquad
\mathbf d=(\sin k_x,\sin k_y,M+\cos k_x+\cos k_y).
\]

It writes the Markovian master equation

\[
\dot\rho=-i[H,\rho]+\gamma(\sigma_z\rho\sigma_z-\rho)
\tag{1}
\]

and then inserts a scalar imaginary self-energy \(i\gamma/2\) into a
closed-system Kubo-Bastin Green function. The resulting advertised formula is

\[
\sigma_{xy}^{\rm phen}(M,\gamma)=
{1\over2\pi}\int_{\rm BZ}d^2k\,
\Omega(\mathbf k)
{4|\mathbf d|^2\over4|\mathbf d|^2+\gamma^2}.
\tag{2}
\]

Equation (2) is a well-defined phenomenological quadrature. The question for A9
and A11 is whether it is the response generated by equation (1).

## 2. Reproduction of the released quadrature

The verifier independently implements the released periodic Riemann sum:

- an \(N\times N\) grid on \([-\pi,\pi)^2\);
- \(dk=2\pi/N\);
- the analytic QWZ Berry curvature;
- the released \(10^{-12}\) curvature-denominator regularizer;
- the factor \(4d^2/(4d^2+\gamma^2)\).

At \(M=-1,\gamma=10^{-4}\), it obtains:

| \(N\) | printed preprint value | independent result | absolute difference |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 100 | 0.999971 | 0.9999999977 | \(2.90\times10^{-5}\) |
| 200 | 0.999998 | 0.9999999977 | \(2.00\times10^{-6}\) |
| 300 | 1.000000 | 0.9999999977 | \(2.34\times10^{-9}\) |

Thus the near-quantized convergence statement reproduces, although the printed
\(N=100,200\) values are not the exact output of the released formula at the
displayed parameters. This small table mismatch does not affect the decisive
conceptual test.

At \(\gamma=0.35\) and displacement \(\delta=0.1\), the verifier also
reproduces the intended geometric diagnostic:

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{location}&1-|\sigma_{xy}^{\rm phen}|\\ \hline
M=-2+\delta&0.2560281\\
M=+2-\delta&0.2560281\\

\end{array}
\]

The doubled cusp is therefore a genuine property of equation (2), rather than a
plotting accident.

## 3. Exact dynamics of the stated jump operator

Write

\[
\rho={I+\mathbf r\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma\over2}.
\]

Equation (1) becomes the exact Bloch equation

\[
\dot{\mathbf r}=A\mathbf r,
\qquad
A=
\begin{pmatrix}
-2\gamma&-2d_z&2d_y\\
2d_z&-2\gamma&-2d_x\\
-2d_y&2d_x&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{3}
\]

Its determinant is

\[
\boxed{\det A=-8\gamma(d_x^2+d_y^2)
=-8\gamma(\sin^2k_x+\sin^2k_y).}
\tag{4}
\]

For every \(\gamma>0\) and generic momentum, \(A\) is invertible on the
traceless Bloch space. Hence

\[
\mathbf r_{\rm ss}=0,
\qquad
\boxed{\rho_{\rm ss}=I/2.}
\tag{5}
\]

This is also the fixed-algebra result: a stationary operator must commute with
both \(\sigma_z\) and \(H\). When \(d_x\) or \(d_y\) is nonzero, those two
operators generate the full two-by-two matrix algebra, whose commutant contains
only scalars.

The physics is simple. Orbital dephasing alone is unital: it does not cool the
system into the lower band. Since the Hamiltonian continually rotates orbital
population into orbital coherence, repeated \(\sigma_z\) dephasing drives a
generic two-level momentum sector to the infinite-temperature mixture.

## 4. Exact open-system Kubo test

Couple a weak vector potential to the \(y\)-current:

\[
\delta H(t)=-a_y(t)v_y,
\qquad
v_y=\partial_{k_y}H.
\]

The first-order Liouvillian source about a stationary state is

\[
\delta\mathcal L(t)\rho_{\rm ss}
=i a_y(t)[v_y,\rho_{\rm ss}].
\tag{6}
\]

Using equation (5),

\[
[v_y,I/2]=0.
\]

Therefore, for every frequency,

\[
\delta\rho(\omega)
=(i\omega-\mathcal L_0)^{-1}
\delta\mathcal L(\omega)\rho_{\rm ss}=0,
\]

and hence

\[
\boxed{\sigma_{xy}^{\rm exact}(\omega)=0}
\tag{7}
\]

for the stationary state of the stated Lindbladian. No inversion convention at
\(\omega=0\) changes this result because the forcing vector itself vanishes.

At the verifier's kill point \(M=-1,\gamma=0.35,N=400\),

\[
\sigma_{xy}^{\rm phen}=0.9720664,
\qquad
\sigma_{xy}^{\rm exact}=0.
\]

The disagreement is \(0.9720664\), over five orders of magnitude larger than the
registered \(10^{-6}\) kill threshold. The identification of equation (2) with
the exact response of equation (1) is decisively rejected.

## 5. Measure-zero exceptions do not restore the plateau

At \(k_x,k_y\in\{0,\pi\}\), \(d_x=d_y=0\), so \(H\) commutes with
\(\sigma_z\). Equation (4) vanishes and diagonal populations are stationary.
These four isolated momenta have zero measure in the Brillouin-zone integral.
At a phase boundary one or two of them may also be gapless, but this does not
create a finite stationary Hall plateau.

A distributional or finite-size claim concentrated at those points would require
a separate limiting prescription. It is not equation (2).

## 6. The noncommuting limits

The exact result has an important singular order of limits:

\[
\boxed{
\lim_{\gamma\to0^+}\lim_{t\to\infty}\sigma_{xy}(t,\gamma)=0,
\qquad
\lim_{t\to\infty}\lim_{\gamma\to0^+}
\sigma_{xy}(t,\gamma;\rho_-(0))=C.}
\tag{8}
\]

In the first expression, any nonzero Markovian \(\sigma_z\) dephasing is allowed
to reach its steady state and produces \(I/2\). In the second, the bath is
removed first and the prepared lower-band projector \(\rho_-\) remains a
closed-system stationary state with Chern response \(C\).

Equation (2) follows the second branch smoothly as \(\gamma\to0\), while being
presented as the steady response of the first model. That is the precise
order-of-limits error.

## 7. Why the scalar self-energy is not the Lindbladian

A retarded Green-function width does not by itself specify an open quantum
system. One must also specify the noise or lesser component that fixes the
stationary population, together with response-vertex corrections required by the
same bath.

The mismatch is especially visible here:

1. the scalar \(i\gamma/2\) treats all pseudospin directions alike;
2. \(L=\sigma_z\) singles out the orbital \(z\)-axis;
3. the exact jump changes the stationary distribution;
4. equation (2) retains the filled-lower-band occupation while damping only the
   retarded poles.

That hybrid can be used as a phenomenological broadening rule, but it is not a
derivation from equation (1).

## 8. What would define a different, viable model

A nonzero stationary Hall response requires additional physics, for example:

- a non-unital energy-relaxation jump
  \(L_-(\mathbf k)=|u_-(\mathbf k)\rangle\langle u_+(\mathbf k)|\);
- particle reservoirs fixing a Fermi distribution, with retarded and lesser
  self-energies treated consistently;
- explicit pumping and loss with a solved nonequilibrium stationary state; or
- a finite-time protocol that prepares the lower band and reads the response
  before \(\sigma_z\) dephasing heats it.

Each choice defines a different model. Its exact stationary state, Liouvillian
gap, current vertex and Kubo response must be recomputed. None licenses equation
(2) automatically.

## 9. A9/A11 consequence

The audit adds a useful gate to both programmes.

For A9, every dissipative transport claim must provide:

1. the complete Liouvillian or consistent Keldysh bath;
2. its stationary state;
3. the response superoperator about that state;
4. the order of the DC, thermodynamic, weak-bath and long-time limits.

For A11, a retarded linewidth multiplied into a closed-system spectral formula is
not yet a calibrated physical observable. The preparation, bath, physical ports,
noise correlations and contact/vertex terms remain part of the measurement
definition.

The doubled-cusp quadrature survives as a potentially useful phenomenological
shape template. It provides no evidence for a Spin(2,3) coupling, no protected
\(\mathbb Z_3\) sector, and no physical data.
