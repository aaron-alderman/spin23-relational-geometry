## Executive result

Let the source plane be

\[
S=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}\{E_2,I_2\},

\]

and the target anisotropy plane be

\[
A=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}\{Q_E,Q_I\},

\]

where (Q_E=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)) and (Q_I=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)).

CP acts on both planes as

\[
C=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1).

\]

The most general real CP-equivariant map is

\[
\boxed{
T_{\rm CP}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
g_E&0\\
0&g_I
\end{pmatrix}.
}

\]

Thus CP alone permits two independent real coefficients.

Both planes also carry a natural complex structure

\[
J=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&-1\\
1&0
\end{pmatrix}.

\]

On the source it is multiplication by (i); on the target it is the normalized rotation (Q_E\mapsto Q_I\), corresponding to the Furey identification (1\mapsto u).

Imposing

\[
TJ=JT

\]

forces

\[
\boxed{g_E=g_I=g.}

\]

The CP- and complex-linear Hom space is one-dimensional. This is exactly the common-coefficient ray required for

\[
2\theta=\arg Z_2.

\]

## 1. Real CP-equivariant classification

Write a general real map as

\[
T=
\begin{pmatrix}
a&b\\
c&d
\end{pmatrix}.

\]

CP equivariance requires

\[
CT=TC.

\]

This gives

\[
b=c=0,

\]

with (a) and (d) unconstrained. Hence

\[
\dim_{\mathbb R}\operatorname{Hom}_{CP}(S,A)=2.

\]

This proves that no argument using CP parity alone can identify the even and odd matching coefficients.

## 2. Complex-linear ray

The source complex structure is canonical because

\[
Z_2=E_2+iI_2.

\]

The target complex structure becomes canonical after the Furey unit (u) and normalized Clifford axes are selected:

\[
Q_E\longmapsto Q_I,
\qquad
Q_I\longmapsto-Q_E.

\]

The two Clifford axes have the same trace norm and square to the same identity, so no relative normalization is needed at the kinematic level.

For (T=\operatorname{diag}(g_E,g_I)), the equation

\[
TJ=JT

\]

is equivalent to

\[
g_E=g_I.

\]

The resulting map is

\[
\Phi_g(E_2+iI_2)
=g(E_2Q_E+I_2Q_I).

\]

This is the canonical normalized algebraic candidate.

## 3. Anti-complex-linear ray

There is a second distinguished possibility:

\[
TJ=-JT.

\]

Together with CP equivariance this gives

\[
T=g\operatorname{diag}(1,-1),

\]

or

\[
g_E=-g_I.

\]

This corresponds to mapping (Z_2^*), rather than (Z_2), into the target complex line. It reverses the predicted response angle.

Therefore a physical derivation must distinguish complex from anti-complex sewing; “preserves CP” is insufficient.

## 4. Why the candidate is natural but not forced

The ingredients now line up:

- the Nambu linking algebra supplies the complex scalar (Z_2);
- the Furey choice of (u) supplies a target complex line (operatorname{span}\{1,u\});
- the Clifford representation normalizes its even and odd axes equally;
- CP acts as conjugation on both.

These facts construct a preferred complex-linear isometry up to one real scale and an overall convention (u\mapsto-u).

They do not prove that the physical matter-to-stress response uses this isometry. A generic Hermitian effective action is only real linear on CP-even/odd coefficients and may generate (g_E\ne g_I).

## 5. Relation to Jordan-triple recursion

The Nambu TRO generates (G_{\ell\nu}), and the linking trace generates (Z_2). The response map then asks whether the complex scalar line generated by this recursion is transported as one object into the complexified anisotropy line.

If yes, the recursion preserves the selected complex structure before taking the Hermitian Jordan real form. If no, Hermitianization splits the real and imaginary components and destroys the half-phase prediction.

This is a precise formulation of the previously vague question “does the Jordan/Furey process remember the phase?”

## 6. Branch tree
