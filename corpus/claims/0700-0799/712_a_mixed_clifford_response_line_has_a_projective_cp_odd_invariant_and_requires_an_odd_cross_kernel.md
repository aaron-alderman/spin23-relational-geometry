## Executive result

After Furey chooses a complex unit \(u\), a color-compatible Clifford response line can be written

\[
q(\theta)
=\cos\theta\,1+\sin\theta\,u.
\]

Because \(q\) and \(-q\) generate the same \(Cl_2\) subalgebra, the response line is projective:

\[
[r_{\rm even}:r_{\rm odd}]
\in\mathbb RP^1.
\]

Furey conjugation acts as

\[
[r_{\rm even}:r_{\rm odd}]
\longmapsto
[r_{\rm even}:-r_{\rm odd}].
\]

The normalized quantity

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal I_{\rm CP}
=
\frac{2r_{\rm even}r_{\rm odd}}
{r_{\rm even}^2+r_{\rm odd}^2}
=\sin2\theta
}
\]

is:

- invariant under \(q\mapsto-q\);
- odd under Furey conjugation;
- zero precisely on the even and odd conjugation eigenlines.

Thus a generic mixed Clifford line carries a well-defined relative CP-odd order parameter once the Furey conjugation frame is fixed.

For a real symmetric quadratic response kernel

\[
K=
\begin{pmatrix}
\alpha&c\\
c&\beta
\end{pmatrix}
\]

in the \((1,u)\) basis, the eigenline satisfies

\[
\boxed{
\tan2\theta=\frac{2c}{\alpha-\beta}.
}
\]

The cross coefficient \(c\) changes sign under conjugation. A nonzero mixed angle therefore requires a conjugation-odd spurion or spontaneous breaking; it cannot arise from a strictly conjugation-even quadratic kernel.

## 1. Why the invariant must be projective

The physical object selected by the Clifford conditional expectation is the subalgebra generated by the line \(\mathbb Rq\), not an oriented vector \(q\). Therefore

\[
q\sim-q,
\qquad
\theta\sim\theta+\pi.
\]

The naive odd coordinate

\[
\sin\theta
\]

changes under \(q\mapsto-q\) and is not a line invariant.

The lowest nontrivial conjugation-odd projective combination is

\[
2\cos\theta\sin\theta=\sin2\theta.
\]

Its even partner is

\[
\cos^2\theta-\sin^2\theta=\cos2\theta.
\]

Together they obey

\[
\mathcal I_{\rm even}^2+\mathcal I_{\rm CP}^2=1.
\]

## 2. Fixed lines of conjugation

A projective line is conjugation invariant when

\[
[r_e:r_o]=[r_e:-r_o].
\]

This means the two vectors differ by an overall nonzero real scalar. The only solutions are:

\[
[1:0],
\qquad
[0:1].
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\mathcal I_{\rm CP}=0.
\]

These are precisely:

- the scalar-even selector of document 706;
- the imaginary-odd selector of document 710.

The odd axis is conjugation invariant **as a line** even though its generator changes sign. Therefore \(\mathcal I_{\rm CP}\) detects mixed-line CP breaking, not the odd parity of a chosen oriented generator.

## 3. General quadratic response kernel

Let the two response coefficients be

\[
r=
\begin{pmatrix}r_e\\r_o\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The most general real symmetric quadratic form is

\[
V_2(r)
=
\frac12
\begin{pmatrix}r_e&r_o\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}
\alpha&c\\
c&\beta
\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}r_e\\r_o\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Under conjugation,

\[
r_o\mapsto-r_o.
\]

The diagonal terms remain invariant, while

\[
c\,r_er_o\mapsto-c\,r_er_o.
\]

Hence exact conjugation invariance requires

\[
c=0
\]

unless \(c\) itself is an odd spurion.

## 4. Exact mixing angle

Diagonalizing \(K\) gives

\[
\tan2\theta
=\frac{2c}{\alpha-\beta}.
\]

The eigenvalues are

\[
\lambda_\pm
=\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}
\pm
\frac12\sqrt{(\alpha-\beta)^2+4c^2}.
\]

Changing \(c\to-c\) reflects the eigenline:

\[
\theta\to-\theta,
\]

while leaving the eigenvalues invariant.

The executable certificate verifies these relations and the transformation of both projective invariants.

## 5. Three distinct physical regimes

### Exact conjugation symmetry

If \(c=0\) and \(\alpha\ne\beta\), an eigenaxis is selected:

\[
q=1
\quad\text{or}\quad
q=u.
\]

Which axis is lighter depends on the sign of \(\alpha-\beta\). Symmetry alone still does not prefer the even axis.

### Accidental circle

If

\[
c=0,
\qquad
\alpha=\beta,
\]

the quadratic kernel is proportional to the identity and every \(\theta\) is degenerate. Higher-order terms or dynamics must choose a line.

### Explicit or spontaneous conjugation breaking

If \(c\ne0\), the eigenlines are mixed and

\[
\mathcal I_{\rm CP}\ne0.
\]

The origin of \(c\) must be identified as:

- a Standard-Model CP-odd invariant;
- a pseudoscalar background;
- a boundary orientation;
- a spontaneous order parameter;
- or a source-frame convention that is actually removable.
