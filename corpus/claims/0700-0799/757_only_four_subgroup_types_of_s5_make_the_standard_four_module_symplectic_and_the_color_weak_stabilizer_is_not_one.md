# Only Four Subgroup Types of \(S_5\) Make the Standard Four-Module Symplectic, and the Color/Weak Stabilizer Is Not One

**Date:** 17 October 2026  
**Branches:** B01, B02, B03, B04, B07, B09, B10, B11, B12, B13, B14 and the puncture-symmetry reduction branch  
**Claim status:** exhaustive finite subgroup classification with executable certificate; physical selection of any surviving subgroup remains open

## Executive result

Document 756 proved that the full puncture stabilizer \(S_5\) cannot preserve a symplectic form on its four-dimensional standard module

\[
V_4=\{x\in\mathbb R^5:\sum_i x_i=0\}.
\]

The natural next question is whether a physically motivated symmetry reduction can.

All 156 subgroups of \(S_5\), grouped into 19 conjugacy classes, have now been enumerated. For every class the certificate solves

\[
R(h)^TJR(h)=J,
\qquad
J^T=-J,
\qquad h\in H,
\]

and tests whether the invariant space contains a nondegenerate form.

Exactly four subgroup types succeed:

| subgroup type | action on five coordinates | \(\dim(\Lambda^2V_4^*)^H\) | nondegenerate form exists? |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| trivial | \(1+1+1+1+1\) | 6 | yes |
| \(C_2\), double transposition | \(2+2+1\) | 2 | yes |
| \(C_3\), three-cycle | \(3+1+1\) | 2 | yes |
| \(C_5\), five-cycle | \(5\) | 2 | yes |

Every other subgroup class fails, including

\[
S_3\times S_2,
\]

the stabilizer of the physically natural \(3+2\) color/weak partition of the five oscillator coordinates.

Thus ordinary electroweak/color splitting does **not** by itself repair the \(S_5\) symplectic obstruction. A successful reduction must reach one of the three nontrivial cyclic types above—or introduce structure not captured by a subgroup restriction.

## 1. Exhaustiveness

The certificate constructs \(S_5\) explicitly as permutations of five coordinates. Starting from the trivial subgroup, it closes under adjoining every group element. This yields

\[
156
\]

distinct subgroups. Conjugation by all elements of \(S_5\) reduces them to

\[
19
\]

conjugacy classes, matching the known subgroup count but obtained here directly rather than assumed.

For each representative, invariance equations are imposed on the six-dimensional space of alternating \(4\times4\) matrices. Nondegeneracy is tested using the four-dimensional Pfaffian.

## 2. Why the three nontrivial survivors work

### 2.1 Double-transposition \(C_2\)

A generator of cycle type \((ab)(cd)\) splits \(V_4\) into two two-dimensional real eigenspaces. Each plane admits an invariant area form. Their direct sum is symplectic when both coefficients are nonzero.

The two-dimensional invariant-form space means the subgroup does not choose their relative normalization.

### 2.2 Three-cycle \(C_3\)

The restriction consists of one real two-dimensional rotation plane plus a two-dimensional fixed sector. The rotation plane has its invariant area, and the fixed plane admits an arbitrary area. Again two independent coefficients remain.

### 2.3 Five-cycle \(C_5\)

The standard module decomposes into two inequivalent real rotation planes associated with the two conjugate pairs of nontrivial fifth roots. Each plane has an invariant area form. A nondegenerate sum requires nonzero coefficients on both planes.

This transitive case is especially interesting because it retains no preferred coordinate, but it still leaves a two-parameter invariant-form space.

## 3. Why common larger subgroups fail

The certificate finds no nondegenerate invariant form for any subgroup of order greater than five. In particular it excludes:

- \(S_3\) generated by a three-cycle and a transposition;
- \(C_6\) on \(3+2\) orbits;
- the order-six subgroup generated by a three-cycle and a double transposition;
- \(D_{10}\), because the reflection reverses the \(C_5\) rotation-plane areas;
- \(A_4\), \(S_4\), the order-twenty Frobenius subgroup, \(A_5\), and \(S_5\);
- \(S_3\times S_2\), the full color/weak partition stabilizer.

This pattern is informative: the rotations can preserve oriented areas, while adjoining the natural reflection/permutation extensions generally reverses or separates them so that no global symplectic form survives.

## 4. The Higgs/color/weak boundary

The decomposition of oscillator coordinates into

\[
3\text{ color}+2\text{ weak}

\]

has stabilizer \(S_3\times S_2\). Its action on \(V_4\) has

\[
(\Lambda^2V_4^*)^{S_3\times S_2}=0.
\]

Therefore the mere existence of the Standard-Model subgroup inside \(SO(10)\), or the separation of color and weak coordinate blocks, does not select the required alternating form.

The Higgs-selected crossed-octonion modules found in document 715 may reduce symmetry further than this coordinate-block stabilizer, but that reduced action must be computed explicitly. It cannot be inferred from the words “electroweak breaking.”

## 5. Existence is not selection

For every nontrivial successful subgroup, the invariant alternating-form space has dimension two. Hence the reduction supplies

\[
\text{permission for a symplectic form},
\]

not

\[
\text{a unique normalized symplectic form}.
\]

A further datum must choose the relative areas of the two invariant planes. Candidate data include:

- a complex structure or Hodge star;
- the logical Pauli commutator;
- a Higgs/Yukawa polar decomposition;
- Furey conjugation parity;
- a topology-exchange orientation;
- or an integral unimodularity condition.

Without one of these, the old complement/shear ambiguity has only been reduced, not removed.

## 6. Consequences for the programme

The finite-symmetry path now has a precise decision tree:

\[
S_5
\xrightarrow{\text{must break}}
\begin{cases}
C_2^{(22)},\\
C_3,\\
C_5,
\end{cases}
\xrightarrow{\text{choose relative area}}
(V_4,\omega)

\]

The first arrow must be physically derived. The second must fix a point in a two-dimensional invariant-form space. Only then may the established primitive-two-form construction be applied.

## 7. Stop-everything and falsification criteria

Stop claiming that the color/weak \(3+2\) partition selects the symplectic carrier; its full stabilizer has no invariant alternating form.

Stop treating any successful cyclic subgroup as sufficient by itself; each leaves two invariant area coefficients.

Stop retaining a reflection extension such as \(D_{10}\) while using the \(C_5\)-invariant area unless the reflection acts anti-symplectically and that enlarged covariance is explicitly intended.

Reopen a subgroup route only when the physical action identifies the retained subgroup and selects a nondegenerate point in its invariant-form space.
