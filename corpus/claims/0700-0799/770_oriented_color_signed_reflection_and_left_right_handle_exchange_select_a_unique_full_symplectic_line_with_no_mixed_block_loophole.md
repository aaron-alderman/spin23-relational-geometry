# Oriented Color, Signed Reflection and Left–Right Handle Exchange Select a Unique Full Symplectic Line with No Mixed-Block Loophole

## Claim

On the full six-dimensional space \(\Lambda^2V^*\) of alternating forms on the four-carrier, impose all three already motivated covariance conditions:

1. the oriented color cycle \(C_3\) is symplectic;
2. the paired color/weak reflection is anti-symplectic; and
3. the left–right candidate handle exchange is symplectic.

Their signed invariant intersection is one-dimensional and is generated by

\[
J_{\rm eq}=J_c+J_f.
\]

Its Pfaffian is \(3\), so it is nondegenerate. The symmetry package selects the complete equal-area alternating form, not merely the ratio of two assumed block coefficients. No mixed-block loophole survives.

## 1. Why this audit was necessary

The two-area family

\[
J(a,b)=aJ_c+bJ_f
\]

was previously derived from oriented color covariance. The handle-exchange theorem then selected \(a=b\). A logical concern remained: perhaps an enlarged left–right response restores one of the four other alternating directions in \(\Lambda^2V^*\), so equal diagonal areas would not uniquely determine the full symplectic structure.

The present calculation starts from all six alternating basis forms and imposes the transformations directly. It therefore does not assume block locality.

## 2. Exact linear classification

For a carrier transformation \(R\), symplectic covariance is the linear condition

\[
R^TJR=J,
\]

and anti-symplectic covariance is

\[
R^TJR=-J.
\]

Writing a general alternating \(4\times4\) matrix with six coefficients produces a homogeneous linear system. The combined system has rank five:

\[
\dim\ker \mathcal C=6-5=1.
\]

The surviving generator is precisely

\[
J_{\rm eq}=J_c+J_f,
\qquad
\operatorname{Pf}(J_{\rm eq})=3.
\]

Thus every mixed alternating coefficient is either killed or tied to the unique equal-area form.

## 3. Minimal abstract symmetry package

The carrier-level implication is now exact:

\[
\left.
\begin{array}{l}
C_3\text{ oriented color covariance}\\
(12)(45)\text{ signed reflection covariance}\\
H\text{ handle-exchange covariance}
\end{array}
\right\}
\Longrightarrow
J\in\mathbb R^\times(J_c+J_f).
\]

After fixing overall normalization and integral completion, this is the canonical \(Sp(4,\mathbb Z)\) carrier whose primitive two-forms produce the integral \((2,3)\) lattice.

This is the strongest abstract selector obtained on this branch: it classifies the whole alternating-form space and leaves only scale.

## 4. Physical antecedent still missing

The theorem is not yet a derivation from Standard Model matter. The first two transformations descend from oriented color and paired color/weak permutation data. The third has a plausible left–right gauge envelope, but the programme still lacks one map that realizes all three actions on the same carrier:

\[
\Phi:\mathcal R_{\rm Furey/LR}\to V,
\]

with

\[
\Phi g=R_g\Phi
\]

for the color cycle, signed reflection and simultaneous left–right Weyl element.

Earlier no-go theorems show why this must not be asserted casually: microscopic color acts trivially on the two-puncture logical quotient but nontrivially on the arithmetic carrier, and pure Weyl puncture motion cannot repair the mismatch. The map must operate at the response, sewing or encoded-duality level.

## 5. Stop-everything outcomes

### Positive

Construct a rank-four response quotient of the completed Furey/left–right theory on which the three transformations act exactly as the certified carrier matrices. Then the unique symplectic form, arithmetic completion and primitive \((2,3)\) module follow in one chain.

### Negative

Prove that the signed reflection and left–right Weyl action cannot coexist on any rank-four gauge-compatible response quotient, or that every candidate quotient violates positivity, locality or anomaly matching. Then this combined selector is algebraically exact but physically unavailable.
