## 1. Base Weyl groupoid

On trace-zero placement amplitudes, the real arrows form

\[
W(A_2)=\langle A,F\mid A^3=F^2=I,\ FAF=A^{-1}\rangle.
\]

This is the six-element (S_3) action. It records placement permutations and their integral (A_2) action but forgets the common coherent phase of a response face.

Each real reflection has two coherent lifts differing by (Z=-I). At the unit-reactance checkpoint those lifts naturally carry factors (pm i), so their squares are (Z), not (I).

## 2. Dicyclic lift

The coherent generator (X=iF) obeys

\[
X^4=I,
\qquad X^2=Z.
\]

Together with (A), it gives the presentation

\[
\widetilde W
=\langle A,X\mid A^3=I,\ X^4=I,\ X^2=Z,\ XAX^{-1}=A^{-1}\rangle.
\]

Modulo the central subgroup ({I,Z}), (X) becomes an ordinary reflection and

\[
\widetilde W/\langle Z\rangle\cong S_3.
\]

Thus the coherent response is naturally a projective representation of the placement groupoid, or equivalently an ordinary representation of its dicyclic central extension.

## 3. Why this is not the real lattice normalizer

Document 990 found another twelve-element group,

\[
O(G_{A_2},\mathbb Z)=W(A_2)\times\{\pm I\}.
\]

The two groups have the same cardinality and the same quotient by (Z), but they are not isomorphic:

- the real normalizer has seven nonidentity involutions;
- the coherent dicyclic lift has only the central involution (Z);
- its six lifted reflections have order four.

Multiplication of the reflection coset by (i) changes the extension class. This is precisely the extra coherent information invisible to the real placement lattice.
