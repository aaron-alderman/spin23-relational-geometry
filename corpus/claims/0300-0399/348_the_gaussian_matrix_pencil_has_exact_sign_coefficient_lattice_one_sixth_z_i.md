# The Gaussian Matrix Pencil Has Exact Sign-Coefficient Lattice \(\frac16\mathbb Z[i]\)

**Date:** 17 October 2026  
**Branches:** B06, B09, B10, B11, B12 and B13  
**Claim status:** exact coefficient-lattice theorem for unrestricted \(M_3(\mathbb Z[i])\) complement triples; the physically relevant exceptional order may impose a different sublattice

## 1. Fix an explicit integral model

Take

\[
Z_1,Z_2,Z_3\in M_3(\mathbb Z[i])
\]

and form

\[
F_Z(x_1,x_2,x_3)
=\det(x_1Z_1+x_2Z_2+x_3Z_3).
\]

Every coefficient of \(F_Z\) lies in \(\mathbb Z[i]\).

Apply the normalized sign projector

\[
\mathcal A_{\rm sign}F_Z
=\frac16
\sum_{\pi\in S_3}
\operatorname{sgn}(\pi)F_Z(\pi x).
\]

Since the alternating cubic subspace is generated by

\[
\Delta=(x_1-x_2)(x_2-x_3)(x_3-x_1),
\]

there is one coefficient \(c\) such that

\[
\mathcal A_{\rm sign}F_Z=c\Delta.
\]

## 2. Upper lattice bound

Each coefficient of the raw alternating sum is a Gaussian integer. Division by six gives

\[
\boxed{
c\in\frac16\mathbb Z[i].}
\]

This is the denominator expected from the order-six sign projector. The determinant polarization formula is consistent with the same denominator: the \(x_i^2x_j\) coefficient equals \(3D_{iij}\), and the final sign combination introduces the remaining factor \(1/2\).

## 3. The bound is sharp

Choose the diagonal pencil

\[
x_1Z_1+x_2Z_2
=\operatorname{diag}(x_1,x_1,qx_2),
\qquad
q\in\mathbb Z[i],
\]

with \(Z_3=0\). Then

\[
F_Z(x)=q\,x_1^2x_2.
\]

The raw alternant obeys

\[
\sum_{\pi\in S_3}
\operatorname{sgn}(\pi)
(\pi x)_1^2(\pi x)_2
=-\Delta(x).
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
c=-\frac q6.}
\]

As \(q\) ranges over \(\mathbb Z[i]\), every point of \(\frac16\mathbb Z[i]\) occurs.

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\{c(Z_1,Z_2,Z_3):Z_i\in M_3(\mathbb Z[i])\}
\supseteq\frac16\mathbb Z[i],}
\]

and together with the upper bound,

\[
\boxed{
\Lambda_c=\frac16\mathbb Z[i].}
\]

This equality is for unrestricted Gaussian integer matrix triples.

## 4. Shortest unrestricted shell is CP trivial

The squared Euclidean norm is

\[
|c|^2=\frac{a^2+b^2}{36},
\qquad
a,b\in\mathbb Z.
\]

The shortest nonzero shell has

\[
a^2+b^2=1.
\]

Thus

\[
c\in
\left\{
\pm\frac16,\,
\pm\frac{i}{6}
\right\}.
\]

Every shortest nonzero coefficient has

\[
\operatorname{Re}c\,\operatorname{Im}c=0.
\]

A shortest-vector principle alone would select a CP-trivial coefficient.

## 5. Shortest CP-capable shell has equal quadratures

Require both quadratures to be nonzero:

\[
a\ne0,
\qquad
b\ne0.
\]

The minimum is then

\[
a^2+b^2=2.
\]

The four shortest CP-capable coefficients are

\[
\boxed{
c\in
\left\{
\frac{1+i}{6},
\frac{1-i}{6},
\frac{-1+i}{6},
\frac{-1-i}{6}
\right\}.}
\]

They have

\[
|c|^2=\frac1{18},
\qquad
|\operatorname{Re}c|
=|\operatorname{Im}c|
=\frac16.
\]

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{shortest CP-capable coefficient}
\Longrightarrow
\arg c=\frac\pi4+\frac{n\pi}{2}.}
\]

This is an exact arithmetic statement in the Gaussian matrix model.

## 6. Relation to the previous checkpoint

The earlier example used

\[
F_Z(x)=(-6-6i)x_1^2x_2.
\]

The formula above gives

\[
c=-\frac{-6-6i}{6}=1+i.
\]

The previously engineered factor of six is now explained as cancellation of the normalized sign-projector denominator.

## 7. What this does not establish

The exceptional complement fields may not range over unrestricted \(M_3(\mathbb Z[i])\). A physical integral exceptional order could require:

- Hermitian or octonionic constraints;
- trace or determinant conditions;
- a non-Gaussian complex suborder;
- correlated sheet triples;
- quotienting by automorphisms;
- a different normalization of the cubic form.

Any of these can shrink or rescale \(\Lambda_c\).

The theorem establishes a complete benchmark model and an exact denominator calculation, not the final exceptional-order lattice.

## 8. Improved arithmetic target

The next calculation should compare the actual exceptional order with the benchmark:

\[
\Lambda_c^{\rm exceptional}
\subseteq
\frac16\mathbb Z[i]
\]

after choosing a compatible complex slice and normalization.

The decisive questions are:

1. Does the exceptional sublattice contain axis points?
2. What is its shortest nonzero shell?
3. What is its shortest CP-capable shell?
4. Does the automorphism group identify that shell as one orbit?
5. Is there a physical reason to exclude the shorter CP-trivial shell?

## 9. Stop-everything and falsification gates

**Stop-everything trigger:** the actual integral exceptional order projects onto a lattice whose shortest allowed nonzero orbit is the equal-quadrature shell.

**CP-capable trigger:** a physical constraint excludes axis coefficients and leaves \((\pm1\pm i)/6\) as the shortest allowed orbit.

**Denominator falsifier:** the normalized sign projector is claimed to preserve \(\mathbb Z[i]\) for arbitrary triples despite the explicit \(x_1^2x_2\) pencil.

**Shortest-vector falsifier:** the unrestricted shortest shell is claimed to be CP violating.

**Exceptional-order falsifier:** the unrestricted Gaussian matrix benchmark is identified with an octonionic exceptional order without deriving the embedding and constraints.
