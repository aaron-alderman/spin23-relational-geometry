# The Exceptional Cubic Can Source the Pin Orientation Only Through a Second (O/R) Relationship Spurion

**Date:** 17 October 2026  
**Branches:** B01, B03, B04, B05, B06, B08, B09, B11 and B13  
**Claim status:** exact relative-phase and rephasing theorem; the exceptional-to-(O/R) charged intertwiner remains underived

## 1. The tempting shortcut

The exceptional branch supplies a complex sign-projected determinant
coefficient

\[
c=\operatorname{Re}c+i\operatorname{Im}c.
\]

The Pin branch needs an oriented neutral relationship term proportional to

\[
\sigma_y=-i(E_{OR}-E_{RO}).
\]

It is tempting to set

\[
\delta\propto\operatorname{Im}c.
\]

That equation is not basis invariant. The imaginary part of one complex number
depends on the unresolved relative phase convention of the (O/R) lines.

## 2. Relative (O/R) rephasing

Under

\[
U_\theta=
\operatorname{diag}(e^{i\theta/2},e^{-i\theta/2}),
\]

the matrix units transform as

\[
E_{OR}\mapsto e^{i\theta}E_{OR},
\qquad
E_{RO}\mapsto e^{-i\theta}E_{RO}.
\]

A basis-independent Hermitian relationship built from a complex coefficient
(q) is

\[
X(q)=qE_{OR}+\bar qE_{RO}
\]

provided

\[
q\mapsto e^{-i\theta}q.
\]

Writing (q=a+ib),

\[
X(q)=a\sigma_x-b\sigma_y.
\]

One nonzero (q) can always be made real by choosing
\(\theta=\arg q\). Therefore one isolated complex \(O/R\) link has no physical
orientation phase.

## 3. Two relationship spurions produce an invariant area

Let (q) be the selector/background relationship amplitude and let (c_{OR})
be a second amplitude sourced by the exceptional sector. If both carry the
same relative charge,

\[
q\mapsto e^{-i\theta}q,
\qquad
c_{OR}\mapsto e^{-i\theta}c_{OR},
\]

then

\[
\boxed{
\Omega_{OR}=\operatorname{Im}(c_{OR}\bar q)}
\]

is rephasing invariant.

It is the oriented area of the two quadrature vectors. Under complex
conjugation,

\[
\Omega_{OR}\mapsto-\Omega_{OR},
\]

so it is the required antiunitary-odd pseudoscalar.

In the gauge (q=|q|>0),

\[
\Omega_{OR}=|q|\operatorname{Im}c_{OR}.
\]

Only after the first spurion fixes the (sigma_x) axis does the imaginary
quadrature of the second define a physical (sigma_y) coefficient.

## 4. Conditional Pin map

The invariant class-D perturbation may therefore take the form

\[
V_{\rm Pin}
=\kappa\,\frac{\Omega_{OR}}{|q|}
\,I_{\rm kin}\otimes\sigma_y\otimes I_\tau
\]

in the (q>0) gauge, or an equivalent covariant expression before gauge
fixing. Here (kappa) carries any required normalization and dimensions.

This gives a clean conditional chain:

\[
\boxed{
\text{selector relationship }q
+\text{ exceptional relationship }c_{OR}
\Longrightarrow
\Omega_{OR}
\Longrightarrow
\delta\sigma_y
\Longrightarrow
\text{Pin Pfaffian phase}.}
\]

The phase is relational, not the imaginary part of an isolated coefficient.

## 5. What the exceptional coefficient actually supplies

The established exceptional coefficient (c) obeys

\[
c\mapsto-c
\]

under a triality-sheet transposition and

\[
c\mapsto\bar c
\]

under exceptional conjugation. These laws do not specify how (c) transforms
under the independent (O/R) relative rephasing.

To turn (c) into (c_{OR}), one needs an intertwiner

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal L:
L_{\rm exceptional}^{\rm sign}
\longrightarrow
\operatorname{Hom}(R,O)}
\]

with the correct gauge, antiunitary and relative-(U(1)) covariance.

Because both sides are complex lines once other labels are fixed, a nonzero
linear map exists abstractly. Its phase and normalization are arbitrary unless
the Furey/exceptional action, a boundary incidence relation or a common source
fixes them. Existence of a one-dimensional intertwiner is therefore not yet a
derivation.

## 6. Relation to the selector mass matrices

The Pin lattice already uses

\[
\beta_1=\sigma_z\otimes\sigma_x,
\qquad
\beta_2=\sigma_z\otimes\sigma_z.
\]

The (sigma_x) relationship component supplies a natural reference
quadrature (q) in the displayed basis. But this identification is physical
only if the selector mass really arises from the same neutral (O/R) link.
Otherwise the common matrix notation hides two unrelated two-state spaces.

Thus two intertwiners must ultimately agree:

1. selector mass (leftrightarrow X(q)) in the neutral block;
2. exceptional sign line (leftrightarrow X(c_{OR})) in that same block.

Their relative phase, not either map separately, determines
(Omega_{OR}).

## 7. Closed-loop alternative

The exceptional/Majorana three-port branch already has a basis-independent
holonomy

\[
\operatorname{Im}(r_{12}r_{23}r_{31}).
\]

A closed loop avoids the one-link rephasing problem. It could source
(Omega_{OR}) if a physical map from the three-port loop to the two-face
boundary orientation is derived. Such a map is more constrained than assigning
(c) an (O/R) charge by declaration, but it remains absent.

## 8. Consequences for predictivity

The exceptional cubic improves the problem from an arbitrary real coefficient
to a possible relative phase source. It does not yet predict (delta):

- the exceptional-to-(O/R) intertwiner phase is open;
- the selector relationship amplitude (q) is open;
- the normalization (kappa) is open;
- the continuum-stable Pin phase interval is still being mapped.

A future derivation must land inside a nontrivial interval without fitting
these three freedoms independently.
