# Two Higgs Block Components: Counterterm and Relative-Phase Obstruction

**Date:** 17 October 2026  
**Branches:** B03, B05 and B11  
**Claim status:** exact block-trace invariant classification; physical action may contain additional noncyclic intertwiners

## 1. The one-versus-two Higgs question

Cyclic Yukawa closure requires two positive-hypercharge block maps:

\[
h_C:C\to H,
\qquad
h_D:H\to D,
\]

where \(D\) is the \(d^c\) target. Both have the electroweak quantum numbers of a Higgs doublet.

There are two possible interpretations:

1. \(h_C\) and \(h_D\) are independent doublet fields;
2. one physical doublet is constrained to the diagonal embedding
   \[
   h_{\rm phys}=\alpha h_C+\beta h_D.
   \]

The block-trace counterterm algebra determines whether the second interpretation is dynamically natural.

## 2. Exhaustive quadratic result

Let lower-case \(c,d\) denote \(h_C,h_D\), and capital \(C,D\) their daggers. Requiring a nonzero cyclic internal trace permits

\[
\operatorname{Tr}(cC),\qquad
\operatorname{Tr}(Cc),
\qquad
\operatorname{Tr}(dD),\qquad
\operatorname{Tr}(Dd).
\]

It forbids every cross quadratic trace:

\[
\operatorname{Tr}(cD)
=\operatorname{Tr}(Dc)
=\operatorname{Tr}(Cd)
=\operatorname{Tr}(dC)
=0.
\]

Thus the trace-local quadratic potential contains separate masses,

\[
V_2=m_C^2\,h_C^\dagger h_C
+m_D^2\,h_D^\dagger h_D,
\]

but no term

\[
m_{CD}^2\,h_C^\dagger h_D+\text{h.c.}
\]

that could lock their directions.

## 3. Exhaustive quartic result

Among all hypercharge-neutral degree-four words, the allowed field-count signatures are only

\[
c^2C^2,\qquad
d^2D^2,\qquad
cCdD.
\]

They yield separate self-interactions and one mixed norm-type contraction:

\[
V_4=
\lambda_C(h_C^\dagger h_C)^2
+\lambda_D(h_D^\dagger h_D)^2
+\lambda_{CD}\,
\operatorname{Tr}_H
\left(h_Ch_C^\dagger h_D^\dagger h_D\right).
\]

The certificate finds no cyclic trace for the phase-sensitive or odd-copy structures corresponding schematically to

\[
(h_C^\dagger h_D)^2,
\qquad
(h_C^\dagger h_C)(h_C^\dagger h_D),
\qquad
(h_D^\dagger h_D)(h_C^\dagger h_D),
\]

or their conjugates.

In conventional two-Higgs-doublet notation, strict block locality therefore removes the analogues of

\[
m_{12}^2,\qquad \lambda_5,\qquad\lambda_6,\qquad\lambda_7.
\]

## 4. The diagonal embedding is not selected

The allowed potential distinguishes \(h_C\) and \(h_D\) through independent coefficients. A fixed nonzero vector \((\alpha,\beta)\) in copy space is not preserved by generic renormalization unless another symmetry or constraint relates

\[
m_C^2,\quad m_D^2,\quad
\lambda_C,\quad\lambda_D,\quad\lambda_{CD}.
\]

The trace rule itself supplies no exchange symmetry: the two components serve different Yukawa cycles. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\text{strict cyclic block locality does not technically select one diagonal Higgs doublet.}
}
\]

Imposing \(h_C=\alpha h_{\rm phys}\) and \(h_D=\beta h_{\rm phys}\) is a consistent parametrization only after a dynamical or algebraic constraint is added.

## 5. Relative-phase mode

The trace-local scalar potential is invariant under independent rephasings

\[
h_C\mapsto e^{i\theta_C}h_C,
\qquad
h_D\mapsto e^{i\theta_D}h_D.
\]

Electroweak hypercharge gauges the common phase. If both fields acquire nonzero vacuum expectation values, the remaining relative phase is spontaneously broken. In the scalar potential alone this produces a physical CP-odd zero mode.

The Yukawa sector may combine the scalar rephasings with fermion rephasings, and gauge anomalies may break a resulting global symmetry. Those effects must be computed in the full field theory. They do not create the missing tree-level scalar mass automatically.

Hence a phenomenologically viable completion needs at least one of:

- a source-derived soft connector analogous to \(m_{12}^2\);
- a phase-sensitive quartic generated by a larger linking path;
- an extra gauge interaction eating the relative phase;
- a constraint identifying the two block fields before quantization;
- a vacuum with only one component nonzero, followed by a demonstration that all required fermions still become massive.

## 6. Interaction with the one-generation result

The matter and Higgs results now pull in opposite directions:

- block incidence elegantly selects one anomaly-free matter topology;
- the same strict incidence rule forces separate Higgs endpoints;
- the absence of endpoint mixing prevents the simple one-Higgs reduction.

This is a productive obstruction. It names exactly what an external \(H_2(\mathbb R)\) carrier or source field would have to do: supply a positive, gauge-compatible connector between the two Higgs copies without restoring the mirror matter sectors.

## 7. Stop-everything and falsification tests

**Positive:** the external two-face carrier or nested real structure generates one forced connector, fixes \(\alpha/\beta\), and gives a positive mass to the relative CP-odd mode while preserving the four anomaly and Yukawa results.

**Negative:** every permitted connector either violates hypercharge, reintroduces mirrors, produces flavor-changing neutral currents, or is an arbitrary generic two-Higgs-doublet parameter.

**Current status:** the one-diagonal-Higgs interpretation is not technically natural under strict cyclic block locality.

**Unsafe shortcut:** substitute \(h_C=\alpha h\), \(h_D=\beta h\) in the Yukawa table and call the result a derived single-Higgs model without checking the orthogonal field’s equation of motion and counterterms.
