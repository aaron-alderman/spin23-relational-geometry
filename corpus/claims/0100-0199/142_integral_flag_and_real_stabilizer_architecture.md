# Integral Flag Selection and Continuous Stabilizer after Scalar Extension

**Date:** 17 October 2026  
**Branches:** B03, B10, B11 and B12  
**Claim status:** exact toy model and general calculation template; exceptional-Jordan/Leech instance remains uncomputed

## 1. The architecture required by the no-go

Exact lattice symmetry is discrete, while the Standard-Model and \(Spin_0(2,3)\) symmetries are continuous. A viable arithmetic selector must therefore distinguish:

\[
\text{integral points}
\]

from

\[
\text{the real identity component after scalar extension}.
\]

The relevant pattern is

\[
(J_{\mathbb Z},F_{\mathbb Z})
\longrightarrow
(J_{\mathbb R},F_{\mathbb R}),
\]

where \(J_{\mathbb Z}\) is an integral Jordan order and \(F_{\mathbb Z}\) an integral nested flag.

## 2. Exact rank-two toy model

Take

\[
J_{\mathbb Z}
=
\operatorname{Sym}_2(\mathbb Z)
\]

with determinant norm, and extend scalars:

\[
J_{\mathbb R}
=
J_{\mathbb Z}\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathbb R
=
\operatorname{Sym}_2(\mathbb R).
\]

Select the primitive rank-one ray generated by

\[
E_{11}
=
\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The determinant-preserving congruence action is

\[
X\longmapsto gXg^T,
\qquad
g\in SL(2).
\]

## 3. Real stabilizer

The one-parameter subgroup

\[
g_b=
\begin{pmatrix}1&b\\0&1\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
b\in\mathbb R,
\]

fixes \(E_{11}\):

\[
g_bE_{11}g_b^T=E_{11}.
\]

It is a connected real unipotent stabilizer.

For

\[
X=
\begin{pmatrix}a&c\\c&d\end{pmatrix},
\]

the action is

\[
(a,c,d)
\longmapsto
(a+2bc+b^2d,\ c+bd,\ d).
\]

## 4. Integral stabilizer

The same formula preserves

\[
\operatorname{Sym}_2(\mathbb Z)
\]

for

\[
b\in\mathbb Z.
\]

Hence the exact integral stabilizer contains the discrete arithmetic subgroup

\[
\{g_b:b\in\mathbb Z\},
\]

while its real scalar extension contains the connected group

\[
\{g_b:b\in\mathbb R\}.
\]

This is not a continuous homomorphism into a discrete group. It is one group scheme viewed through different sets of scalar points.

## 5. What the toy model proves

It proves the logical consistency of:

\[
\boxed{
\text{discrete arithmetic flag selection}
\quad+\quad
\text{continuous real stabilizer}.
}
\]

The integral points can classify admissible flags or charges, while the real identity component acts as the local continuous symmetry.

It does not prove that the exceptional Jordan order has the desired flag or stabilizer.

## 6. Exceptional-Jordan calculation template

The actual branch must perform the following steps.

1. Choose a specific integral exceptional-Jordan order
   \[
   J_{\mathbb Z}\subset H_3(\mathbb O).
   \]
2. Define primitive integral suborders whose scalar extensions reproduce
   \[
   H_2(\mathbb C)
   \subset
   H_3(\mathbb C)
   \subset
   H_3(\mathbb O).
   \]
3. Compute the stabilizer group scheme
   \[
   \operatorname{Stab}(F)
   \]
   over \(\mathbb Z\).
4. Compute its integral points and arithmetic orbits.
5. Base-change to \(\mathbb R\) or \(\mathbb C\) and calculate the identity component.
6. Verify whether that component is the faithful Standard-Model group already obtained from the continuous nested flag.
7. Compare the integral stabilizer or orbit data with the Wilson/Leech congruence conditions.

## 7. Concrete discriminants

The arithmetic branch advances only if it answers:

- Does at least one primitive integral nested flag exist?
- Are there finitely many arithmetic orbits?
- Is one orbit distinguished by norm, determinant or cubic integrality?
- Does real extension recover the desired continuous stabilizer?
- Does the integral stabilizer constrain hypercharge, the \(\mathbb Z_6\) quotient, family multiplicity or Yukawa data?
- Is Leech compatibility forced rather than added afterward?

## 8. Stop-everything criteria

**Advance strongly:** exactly one natural arithmetic orbit has the required real stabilizer and reproduces an observed discrete datum.

**Structured negative:** integral flags exist, but none extend to the required continuous nested pair.

**Demote Leech:** the continuous flag extends integrally, but its integral stabilizer has no canonical relation to the Wilson/Leech order.

**Reject the architecture:** the chosen exceptional order admits no primitive integral realization of the required complex subalgebras.
