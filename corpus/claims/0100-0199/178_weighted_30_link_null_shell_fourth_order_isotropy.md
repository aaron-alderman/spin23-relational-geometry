# A Weighted 30-Link Null Shell with Fourth-Order Isotropy

**Date:** 17 October 2026  
**Branches:** B07, B08 and B13  
**Claim status:** exact lattice-shell and moment construction; no unitary Weyl coin yet

## 1. Why the 24-vector escape is not enough

The mixed-parity shell generated by permutations and signs of

\[
(1,2,2)
\]

has 24 vectors, all of Euclidean length three. It escapes the body-diagonal antiperiodicity theorem because each coordinate takes both odd and even values.

But every vector has coordinate magnitude at most two. With null timing

\[
\Delta t=3a/c,
\]

the dependency cone permits at most

\[
|v_x|\leq\frac{2a}{\Delta t}=\frac{2c}{3}
\]

along a lattice axis. Thus the 24-vector shell cannot support an isotropic emergent cone of speed \(c\), regardless of its coin amplitudes.

## 2. Complete the same-radius shell

The same integer sphere

\[
x^2+y^2+z^2=9
\]

also contains the six axis vectors

\[
(\pm3,0,0),\quad(0,\pm3,0),\quad(0,0,\pm3).
\]

Define two cubic orbits:

\[
\mathcal S_A=\{\text{six axis vectors}\},
\]

\[
\mathcal S_M=\{\text{24 signed permutations of }(1,2,2)\}.
\]

Their union \(\mathcal S_{30}\) has 30 links of identical length \(3a\). Every link is exactly null for the same clock interval \(3a/c\).

The axis orbit restores causal support at speed \(c\) in the coordinate directions. The mixed orbit breaks the common-parity relation that forced \(U(k+\pi e_j)=\pm U(k)\).

## 3. Second moments

Normalize every vector by its length and write \(n=e/3\). Each orbit separately satisfies

\[
\langle n_i n_j\rangle=\frac{\delta_{ij}}3.
\]

Therefore every convex combination of the two orbit measures has exact quadratic isotropy.

For a scalar average this still gives the familiar \(c/\sqrt3\) coefficient. The purpose of the enlarged shell is not to alter that scalar fact; it is to make a spinorial unitary with a larger dependency cone possible.

## 4. Fourth moments determine unique orbit weights

For the axis orbit,

\[
\langle n_x^4\rangle_A=\frac13,
\qquad
\langle n_x^2n_y^2\rangle_A=0.
\]

For the mixed orbit,

\[
\langle n_x^4\rangle_M=\frac{11}{81},
\qquad
\langle n_x^2n_y^2\rangle_M=\frac8{81}.
\]

Let the total weights of the two orbits be \(w_A\) and \(w_M=1-w_A\). Rotational isotropy through fourth order requires the spherical values

\[
\langle n_x^4\rangle=\frac15,
\qquad
\langle n_x^2n_y^2\rangle=\frac1{15}.
\]

The unique solution is

\[
\boxed{
w_A=\frac{13}{40},
\qquad
w_M=\frac{27}{40}.
}
\]

Equivalently, each axis direction has weight \(13/240\), and each mixed direction has weight \(9/320\).

The weighted shell then obeys the full tensor identity

\[
\boxed{
\langle n_i n_j n_k n_l\rangle
=\frac1{15}
(\delta_{ij}\delta_{kl}
+\delta_{ik}\delta_{jl}
+\delta_{il}\delta_{jk}).
}
\]

Because the shell is antipodal, all odd moments vanish. It is therefore a positive cubic cubature exact for spherical polynomials through degree four.

## 5. Isotropy is not exact at every order

The sixth axis moment of the weighted shell is

\[
\langle n_x^6\rangle=\frac4{27},
\]

whereas the rotationally invariant sphere gives \(1/7\). Thus anisotropy returns at sixth derivative order.

This is an improvement over the eight-body-diagonal shell, not an exact restoration of continuous rotations.

## 6. Why the weights are suggestive but not yet a quantum walk

The positive weights define an isotropic classical stencil or quadrature rule. A unitary quantum walk requires matrix amplitudes \(A_e\) satisfying the nonlinear autocorrelation equations

\[
\sum_e A_e^\dagger A_{e+r}=\delta_{r0}I
\]

for every lattice displacement difference \(r\).

The scalar weights do not solve these equations. Nor do they show that a two-component carrier is sufficient.

The next algebraic ansatz should require:

- covariance under the binary octahedral action on a spinor carrier;
- one Weyl cone at the chosen infrared point;
- independent zero and \(\pi\) gaps;
- the 30-link support or a proved minimal subset;
- exact unitarity for every momentum;
- a controlled sixth-order anisotropy coefficient.

## 7. Relation to “statistics net out the effects”

This construction gives that intuition a precise limited meaning. Weighted local relations can cancel directional errors at second and fourth moment order even though every microscopic link belongs to a finite lattice shell.

What statistics do **not** guarantee is:

- unitary amplitudes;
- chirality;
- anomaly cancellation;
- a universal emergent metric;
- exact Lorentz symmetry;
- the Jordan cubic norm.

The moment identities are a kinematic precondition, not the dynamics.

## 8. Jordan and arithmetic questions

The shell is defined by an integral norm equation and decomposes into two cubic-group orbits. This gives the arithmetic branch a legitimate local target:

> Can an integral Jordan, Clifford or code structure select the radius-three shell and the ratio \(13:27\), rather than those numbers being chosen solely to fit moments?

At present the weights come from the fourth-moment equations and have no known Leech, Golay, exceptional-Jordan or Furey derivation. Numerical resemblance must not be treated as provenance.

## 9. Stop-everything and falsification gates

**Unitary success:** solve the 30-link matrix autocorrelation equations with a single Weyl infrared cone and a gapped \(\pi\) sector.

**Minimality:** prove that no smaller equal-radius mixed-parity cubic shell satisfies the required causal support and fourth-order isotropy.

**Arithmetic selection:** derive the shell and orbit weights from an independent integral or Jordan construction.

**Carrier obstruction:** prove that no two-component unitary exists on this support, then determine the minimal internal dimension and its unwanted species.

**Continuum failure:** sixth- or higher-order anisotropy remains too large at any viable lattice scale, or different fields acquire inequivalent cones.
