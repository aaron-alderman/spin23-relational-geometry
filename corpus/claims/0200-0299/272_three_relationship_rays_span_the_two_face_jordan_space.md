# Three Relationship Rays Span the Two-Face Jordan Space

**Date:** 17 October 2026  
**Branches:** B01, B04, B06, B07, B08, B09 and B11  
**Claim status:** exact real projective/Veronese theorem; canonical physical selection of the rays remains open

## 1. One carrier, two faces, three relationships

The finite-remnant construction produces matrices

\[
Q(b)=bb^T,
\qquad b=\begin{pmatrix}x\\y\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbb R^2.
\]

This gives an exact mathematical reading of the proposed phrase:

- **one carrier:** one real two-dimensional face-amplitude space \(V\cong\mathbb R^2\);
- **two faces:** the two components of \(b\);
- **three relationships:** the three independent symmetric bilinears
  \[
  x^2,\qquad 2xy,\qquad y^2,
  \]
  spanning \(\operatorname{Sym}^2(V)\cong H_2(\mathbb R)\).

The “three” is not added numerology. It is

\[
\dim\operatorname{Sym}^2(\mathbb R^2)=\frac{2(2+1)}2=3.
\]

## 2. Each relationship remnant is a Jordan light ray

In coordinates

\[
Q(b)=
\begin{pmatrix}
x^2&xy\\xy&y^2
\end{pmatrix},
\]

its Jordan determinant is

\[
\det Q(b)=x^2y^2-(xy)^2=0.
\]

For \(b\neq0\), it is positive semidefinite of rank one. The projective face direction \([b]\in\mathbb RP^1\) therefore maps to an extreme boundary ray of the Lorentz/Jordan cone. This is the quadratic Veronese map

\[
\nu_2:\mathbb RP^1\longrightarrow\mathbb P(H_2(\mathbb R)),
\qquad [x:y]\longmapsto[x^2:2xy:y^2].
\]

## 3. Exact three-ray determinant

For three face vectors \(b_i=(x_i,y_i)^T\), write

\[
q_i=(x_i^2,2x_iy_i,y_i^2)^T
\]

and the oriented face areas

\[
\Delta_{ij}=\det(b_i,b_j).
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\det[q_1\ q_2\ q_3]
=2\Delta_{12}\Delta_{13}\Delta_{23}.}
\]

Consequently, three remnant rays span all of \(H_2(\mathbb R)\) exactly when their face directions are pairwise distinct in \(\mathbb RP^1\).

This is a particularly clean bridge between “relationships” and area/orientation. Collapse of any pairwise face area makes two projective directions coincide and destroys the three-dimensional span.

## 4. Minimality

One rank-one remnant spans one ray. Two remnants span at most a plane. Three pairwise distinct projective remnants span the full three-dimensional Jordan vector space.

Thus three is the minimum defect inventory capable of reconstructing an arbitrary symmetric two-face matrix by **signed linear combinations**.

An explicit basis is

\[
b_1=(1,0)^T,
\qquad
b_2=(0,1)^T,
\qquad
b_3=(1,1)^T.
\]

Their outer products independently recover the two populations and the off-diagonal coherence.

## 5. Critical distinction: linear span is not the full positive cone

Three rays spanning \(H_2(\mathbb R)\) does not mean their nonnegative combinations equal all of \(H_2(\mathbb R)_+\).

The positive semidefinite cone has a continuum of extreme rays, one for every \([b]\in\mathbb RP^1\). A cone generated by finitely many rays is polyhedral and cannot equal the round Lorentz cone.

More directly, if a positive sum of rank-one matrices has rank one, every nonzero summand must have the same image line. Therefore a new boundary ray \(Q(c)\) cannot be a positive combination of finitely many different \(Q(b_i)\) unless one \(b_i\) is parallel to \(c\).

So:

\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{l}
3\text{ generic relationship rays span the Jordan vector space};\\
\text{a continuum or group orbit of rays generates the entire positive cone}.
\end{array}}
\]

## 6. Four Yukawa channels form a possible overcomplete frame

If the four dressed Yukawa channels each supplied a defect vector

\[
b_u,\ b_d,\ b_e,\ b_\nu\in\mathbb R^2,
\]

their remnants would be four vectors in a three-dimensional Jordan space and hence obey at least one linear relation. For the symmetric example

\[
b_u=(1,0),\quad
b_d=(0,1),\quad
b_e=(1,1),\quad
b_\nu=(1,-1),
\]

the relation is

\[
-2Q_u-2Q_d+Q_e+Q_\nu=0.
\]

This is only an illustrative frame, not a Standard-Model derivation. The actual orientations must come from normalized source couplings, Furey intertwiners or physical spectral residues. Gauge representation matching does not determine them.

If three of the four physical projective directions are distinct, the four channels provide an overcomplete relational frame. If they all collapse to one or two directions, the full Jordan carrier is not reconstructed.

## 7. Relation to \(Spin(2,3)\)

The rank-one rays form the projectivized null boundary of the Lorentz cone associated with \(H_2(\mathbb R)\). Their linear span reconstructs the Jordan algebra on which

\[
Sp(4,\mathbb R)\cong Spin_0(2,3)
\]

acts conformally through its Siegel-domain realization.

But a spanning frame does not derive that group as a physical symmetry. For that, the dynamics or sewing operations must generate an open portion of the cone and close under the relevant Jordan/conformal transformations. A finite frame may merely choose preferred directions and reduce the symmetry.

## 8. Relation to three relationships and a twist

The exact determinant identity contains three pairwise oriented areas:

\[
\Delta_{12}\Delta_{13}\Delta_{23}.
\]

Changing the orientation of one face vector changes two area signs and leaves the product unchanged, as required because \(b\) and \(-b\) define the same rank-one relationship. The construction therefore naturally lives projectively.

A global twist can act on the face vectors while the relationship matrices return single-valuedly. This is the familiar passage from a vector or spinor-like carrier to a quadratic observable. It is structurally compatible with the earlier support-projector and helicity-bundle branches, though no identification among their winding numbers is yet proved.
