# The graph-to-geometry source packet supplies a typed toolchain, but no paper derives \(Spin(2,3)\)

**Date:** 20 October 2026  
**Status:** 32-source atlas completed; direct \(Spin(2,3)\) derivation count zero  
**Evidence ledger:** `evidence/data/graph-to-spin23-source-atlas.json`

## Result

The supplied papers form a useful research programme when sorted by function. They do not form a cumulative proof that spacetime, matter, or a graph model has \(Spin(2,3)\) symmetry. Their strongest joint contribution is a typed toolchain:

\[
\text{quantum relations}
\longrightarrow
\text{graph differential operators}
\longrightarrow
\text{spectral metric/action}
\longrightarrow
\text{causal or Hamiltonian evolution}
\longrightarrow
\text{selected boundary response}.
\]

The last selection step is absent from the packet. It is the step at which the corpus's real rank-two response cone and four-dimensional symplectic phase space must be earned.

## Six lanes

### 1. Causal and emergent geometry

Quantum causal graph dynamics proves a locality statement for unitary causal evolution on quantum-superposed graphs: the evolution decomposes into a finite-depth local circuit. Quantum Graphity explores a low-energy local phase of a dynamical graph. Spin networks provide a discrete basis for quantum three-geometry, while the causal-set d'Alembertian supplies a controlled continuum limit, including the curved-space \(\Box-R/2\) term.

These papers provide locality, discrete geometry and continuum tests. They do not select a two-mode real carrier or the \((2,3)\) quadratic form.

### 2. Spectral and action geometry

Finite spectral triples classify finite noncommutative geometries by matrix data and diagrams; the spectral action converts spectral data into an action; graph Dirac and first-order formalisms construct \(D=d+d^*\), Laplacians and indices; Connes distance recovers a metric from a graph spectral triple; weighted graph Laplacians can converge spectrally to the Laplace--Beltrami operator.

This is the strongest action-and-continuum lane. Its native setting is not automatically the real two-mode response geometry used by the Spin(2,3) programme.

### 3. Quantum relational structure

Quantum relations, quantum functions, noncommutative graphs, graph-state equivalence and quantum isomorphism provide rigorous replacements for classical relations and maps. They are valuable for the corpus's typed relational layer and for distinguishing graph presentation from invariant content.

They are operator-algebraic or categorical kinematics, not a spacetime signature or gravitational action.

### 4. Gauge and QFT combinatorics

This lane can carry holonomy and amplitudes. It still needs a real-form selector and a demonstrated stress/response map.

### 5. Hamiltonian, symplectic and transport systems

The nearest mathematical contact with \(Spin(2,3)\) is here. Discrete graph Lagrangians carry a closed two-form on solution space; reduced Schrödinger dynamics is Hamiltonian; Gaussian continuous-variable networks evolve through real symplectic transformations. For exactly two selected modes, the phase space is four-dimensional and its full linear canonical group is \(Sp(4,\mathbb R)\).

The remaining papers supply path-integral, supersymmetric, zero-mode, lattice, reservoir and state-transfer examples. They are useful controls but do not force the two-mode reduction or full generator closure.

## Admission decision

Admit the packet as a source atlas and as support for separate relational, spectral, continuum and symplectic gates. Do not cite the number of graph formalisms, the occurrence of Clifford algebras, or the word “spin” as evidence for \(Spin(2,3)\).

The bridge can advance only after a specific graph model supplies a covariant two-mode selector, a real positive boundary response, full \(\mathfrak{sp}(4,\mathbb R)\) generator closure, refinement stability and a physical action or stress interpretation.
