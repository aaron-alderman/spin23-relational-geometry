## The path

\[

Every arrow has a different burden. Collapsing them into “graphs produce spacetime” would erase the principal result.

## Step 1 — relations become operator data

Weaver's quantum relations replace subsets of \(X^2\) by weak-* closed operator bimodules, while quantum functions and discrete quantum structures provide compositional maps and predicates. This is a clean language for admissible adjacency, incidence and transport, but it contains no preferred metric or signature.

**Gate:** choose a concrete relation and representation; do not infer geometry from relationality alone.

## Step 2 — incidence becomes a first-order Dirac operator

For an oriented graph with incidence map \(d:C^0\to C^1\), define

\[
D_G=\begin{pmatrix}0&d^*\\d&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
D_G^2=\begin{pmatrix}d^*d&0\\0&dd^*\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The square separates vertex and edge Laplacians. This is an exact algebraic bridge from graph relations to first-order spectral geometry. Graph Dirac, supersymmetric first-order and Connes-metric papers establish variants of this step.

**Gate:** specify domains, edge weights, boundary conditions and refinement maps. Different Dirac choices can define different metrics.

## Step 3 — spectral data acquire metric and action

Connes distance uses \(D_G\) to define metric information, while the spectral action supplies a functional such as \(\operatorname{Tr}\chi(D_G/\Lambda)\). The graph Laplace--Beltrami and causal-set d'Alembertian results show what a genuine continuum gate looks like: eigenvalues/eigenvectors or mean operators must converge under controlled sampling and scale assumptions.

**Gate:** demonstrate a Lorentzian or response-space continuation. A Euclidean spectral action does not automatically produce the corpus's \((2,3)\) carrier.

## Step 4 — dynamics supply a symplectic solution space

Graph Lagrangian systems can carry a closed two-form on solution space. Reduced Schrödinger evolution is Hamiltonian. Gaussian continuous-variable dynamics acts on phase-space covariances by real symplectic transformations.

This earns a symplectic structure, but not its dimension. A graph with \(n\) effective modes naturally produces \(Sp(2n,\mathbb R)\), not specifically \(Sp(4,\mathbb R)\).

**Gate:** derive a covariant rank-two boundary projection \(\Pi_2\) that survives refinement and is not chosen because the target group has rank two.

## Step 5 — two real boundary channels produce the Jordan cone

Let two selected real boundary observables have positive response matrix

\[
R=\begin{pmatrix}r_{11}&r_{12}\\r_{12}&r_{22}\end{pmatrix}\succeq0.
\]

Writing

\[
R=\begin{pmatrix}t+z&x\\x&t-z\end{pmatrix}

\[
\det R=t^2-x^2-z^2\ge0.
\]

Thus the selected response belongs to \(H_2(\mathbb R)^+\). Rank-one responses lie on the cone boundary; incoherent or mixed responses lie in its interior.

**Gate:** positivity and reality must follow from the measurement or reciprocity class. A generic complex cross-response instead belongs to \(H_2(\mathbb C)^+\), returning the \(Spin_0(2,4)\) parent.

## Step 6 — four quadratures must generate all of \(\mathfrak{sp}(4,\mathbb R)\)

For \(\xi=(q_1,q_2,p_1,p_2)^T\), a linear Hamiltonian generator satisfies

\[
K^T J+JK=0,
\qquad
J=\begin{pmatrix}0&I_2\\-I_2&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Every such generator has block form

\[
K=\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\C&-A^T\end{pmatrix},
\qquad B=B^T,\quad C=C^T.
\]

There are four parameters in \(A\), three in \(B\) and three in \(C\): ten total. Only when the physically available generators close on this ten-dimensional algebra has the model reached

\[
\mathfrak{sp}(4,\mathbb R)\cong\mathfrak{so}(2,3).
\]

A single oscillator rotation, state-transfer Hamiltonian or symplectic Wronskian proves compatibility with a subgroup, not full \(Spin(2,3)\).

## Step 7 — distinguish response symmetry from spacetime symmetry

The conformal action on the response cone and the canonical action on four-dimensional phase space establish the corpus's response carrier. To promote it to physical spacetime symmetry still requires:

1. a Lorentzian action with controlled physical states;
2. a stress tensor or boundary observable derived from that action;
3. a map from graph refinement to continuum causal geometry;
4. pole, residue and positivity checks;
5. a reason the selected two-mode sector is universal rather than apparatus-specific.

Until those gates close, \(Spin(2,3)\) is an exact response-space completion, not a derived fundamental spacetime group.

## Falsifier

The bridge fails if refinement changes the selected two-plane, if the cross-response remains essentially complex, if the accessible generator algebra has dimension below ten, or if the derived continuum action has a different real form. Those failures leave the graph, spectral and causal results intact while retiring this route to \(Spin(2,3)\).
