## 2. What is exact

### 2.1 The primitive line twist

On

\[
E_R=(L\otimes R^m)\oplus(Q\otimes R^n),
\]

determinant neutrality requires

\[
m+2n=0.
\]

Primitivity gives

\[
(m,n)=\pm(-2,1).
\]

Thus the relative charge of a cross-Peirce map is

\[
n-m=3.
\]

This is not a fitted three. It is the smallest faithful determinant-neutral weighting of a rank-one/rank-two split.

### 2.2 The faithful central remnant

\[
e^{2\pi i(I-3P)/3}=\omega I_3,
\qquad \omega^3=1.
\]

The centre therefore disappears from conjugation on \(P\), \(TP\), and adjoint curvature, but remains visible on fundamental matter. This proves why adjoint-only Jordan geometry cannot carry the discrete remnant and why the faithful module must be retained.

The order-three subgroup already identified with the colour centre is redundant in the global Standard Model gauge group. The split physical remnant is therefore \(\mathbb Z_3\), not an independently assumed discrete symmetry.

### 2.4 The return map

\[

\[
\mathcal R[P]=\frac{\delta\Gamma}{\delta P}.

## 4. The three exact obstructions

### 4.1 One projector cannot orient itself

The group \(U(3)\) acts transitively on the rank-one projectors \(\mathbb{CP}^2\). Therefore any \(U(3)\)-invariant functional of \(P\) alone is constant. Equivariance and the stabilizer of \(P\) force any response made only from \(P\) to have the form

\[
\mathcal R[P]=\alpha P+\beta Q.
\]

It commutes with \(P\) identically but contains no orientation information. The fixed-point equation is then true for every projector and selects none.

### 4.2 The protected BF core forgets the projector

The map from the continuous family split to the residual central action is constant:

\[
P\longmapsto e^{2\pi i(I-3P)/3}=\omega I_3.
\]

Consequently, BF level, braiding data, and the nine-state \(T^2\) Hilbert space cannot reconstruct which point of \(\mathbb{CP}^2\) produced them. No left inverse exists. Any genuine return channel must retain non-topological memory: masses, integer charge lifts, heavy-current operators, relational maps, or other gapped response data.

### 4.3 The minimal dynamical Yukawa potential over-aligns

For

\[
H_e=Y_eY_e^\dagger,
\qquad H_\nu=Y_\nu Y_\nu^\dagger,
\]

the minimal relative-frame invariant is \(\operatorname{Tr}(H_eH_\nu)\). In an eigenbasis it is

\[
\sum_{ij}a_i b_j|U_{ij}|^2.
\]

The matrix \(|U_{ij}|^2\) is doubly stochastic, and this expression is linear on the Birkhoff polytope. Its extrema therefore occur at permutation matrices. The minimal potential either aligns the two frames, anti-aligns them by a permutation, or leaves flat directions when spectra are degenerate. It does not derive generic PMNS mixing.

## 6. The precise surviving branches

The following branches remain mathematically live, but none is selected by the current premises.

### A. Nonminimal Majorana–Yukawa dynamics

A dynamical complex symmetric Majorana tensor supplies genuinely additional family structure. Higher-order mixed invariants, phases, and hierarchical Takagi spectra can frustrate simple alignment. What is missing is a principle fixing the field content, potential, coefficient signs, and vacuum—not permission to write such terms.

### B. Intrinsically generation-sensitive Furey geometry

One may seek an extension in which generation is not a passive multiplicity \(I_3\), and in which a typed operator with simple family spectrum is forced by the algebra. No such operator has been constructed. Any candidate must survive the commutant/naturality tests and must not insert the desired projector under another name.

### C. A gapped boundary memory channel

The protected BF quotient may coexist with massive non-topological observables that remember \(P\). Integrating them out could yield a non-scalar \(\mathcal R[P]\). The necessary calculation requires an explicit boundary action and spectrum. Pure BF data alone cannot do it.

### D. Additional relational tensors or flavons

Several competing tensors can generate a noncommuting vacuum and realistic mixing. This is ordinary viable model building, but it expands the coefficient space. Closure would require a higher principle that fixes or sharply constrains those tensors.

### E. Empirical inverse selection

Observed masses, mixing, and possible \(B-3L_i\) phenomenology can be used to infer which response functional is realized. That can close an effective reconstruction problem, but it is not an a priori derivation.
