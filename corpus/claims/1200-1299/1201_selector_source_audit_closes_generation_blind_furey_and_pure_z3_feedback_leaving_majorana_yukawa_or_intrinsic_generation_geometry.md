# Selector-source audit closes generation-blind Furey and pure-\(\mathbb Z_3\) feedback, leaving Majorana/Yukawa or intrinsic-generation geometry

## Main result

The three proposed sources of the non-scalar family response have now been audited against existing corpus results and the new BF information-loss theorem.

1. **Generation-blind Furey data:** closed as a selector source. Simple replication acts as \(A_{\rm int}\otimes I_3\), leaving a full \(U(3)\) commutant. It can select internal representation type and normalize an allowed map, but cannot orient a rank-one family projector.
2. **Pure residual-\(\mathbb Z_3\) defect response:** closed as a selector source. The centre acts as \(\omega I_3\), so gauge loops and topological observables built only from the residual charge are family scalar.
3. **Majorana/Yukawa response:** live. It produces genuine Hermitian tensors on typed family bundles and can select \(P\), but it is only absolutely dynamical if the Yukawa/Majorana tensors and their relative normalization are derived rather than inserted.

The remaining alternatives are therefore:

\[
\boxed{
\text{dynamical Majorana/Yukawa geometry}
\quad\text{or}\quad
\text{a genuinely generation-sensitive extension of the Furey/exceptional algebra}.
}

## 1. Existing Furey commutant theorem

For the presently constructed one-generation Furey system replicated over a flavour space

\[
F\cong\mathbb C^3,

\]

all established internal operators act as

\[
A_{\rm int}\otimes I_F.

\]

Therefore

\[
I_{\rm int}\otimes\operatorname{End}(F)
\subseteq
(A_{\rm int}\otimes I_F)'.

\]

At the unitary level, the commutant contains

\[
U(3)_F.

\]

Since \(U(3)\) acts transitively on the rank-one projector orbit

\[
\mathbb{CP}^2,

\]

no replicated one-generation operator can distinguish a point of that orbit.

This is the same symmetry obstruction derived independently in document 1196, now supported by the earlier Furey-specific certificates.

## 2. Furey results that remain valid

The selector no-go does not erase the established Furey contributions. They still provide:

- Standard Model representation content;
- one-generation ternary and linking structure;
- anomaly-free matter TRO selection;
- cyclic Yukawa support;
- particle–antiparticle reality and grading;
- punctured-torus/topological carrier geometry;
- normalization of certain admissible ternary maps.

What they do not presently provide is a non-scalar operator on an independently appended generation multiplicity space.

The correct statement is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Furey selects where and how an interaction may live, not which family tensor occupies it.}
}

## 3. Hom-space normalization does not orient the family line

The existing bosonic Furey fluctuation with the appropriate Standard Model type admits an intertwiner into three target copies:

\[
\operatorname{Hom}_{G_{\rm SM}}(S_1,S_1\otimes\mathbb C^3)
\cong\mathbb C^3.

\]

TRO compatibility normalizes a nonzero coefficient vector \(y\):

\[
y^\dagger y=1.

\]

But modulo its source phase the remaining choices are

\[
S^5/U(1)\cong\mathbb{CP}^2.

\]

The positive Gram

\[
K=yy^\dagger

\]

is rank one only after \(y\) is chosen. Naturality under the full surviving \(U(3)\) forces either a scalar Gram or the zero vector. Thus ternary structure fixes scale but not direction.

This directly parallels the present charge-three factorization

\[
\Phi=sK_{PQ}:

\]

the scalar/ternary rule can normalize the carrier while the neutral relation map remains free.

## 4. Furey real structure does not fix flavour reality

The replicated internal conjugation has the form

\[
J_{\rm int}\otimes J_F.

\]

But \(J_{\rm int}\) does not determine \(J_F\). Flavour conjugations form

\[
U(3)/O(3),

\]

and the full flavour group acts transitively on that space. No conjugation is fixed by all of \(U(3)\).

Therefore the one-generation Furey/CAR reality operation cannot supply the non-scalar Hermitian response, the flavour real structure, or the eigenline phase convention required by the closure action.

## 5. What would reopen the Furey branch

The no-go applies to simple tensor replication, not to every possible exceptional/Furey completion. The branch reopens if the theory derives one of the following:

- generations as intrinsic slots of one irreducible ternary representation rather than external copies;
- a nontrivial Furey arrow acting between generation slots;
- a source-defined Hermitian metric or incidence operator on generation space with simple spectrum;
- one unique symmetric flavour spurion shared by Majorana and exceptional sectors;
- a functorial map from the Furey carrier to the family multiplicity that is not proportional to the identity.

Without such a construction, citing Furey’s three-generation multiplicities is not enough to produce the projector response.

## 6. Pure residual defect loops are family scalar

After Higgsing,

\[
e^{2\pi iX(P)/3}=\omega I_3.

\]

Every lepton-family state in the fundamental therefore has the same residual central charge. A defect interaction depending only on this \(\mathbb Z_3\) representation is proportional to the identity in family space.

For example, a self-energy generated solely by pure residual gauge exchange has the form

\[
\Sigma_{\mathbb Z_3}(p)
=\sigma(p)I_3

\]

when masses and other couplings are family degenerate. It cannot supply

\[
[P,\Sigma]\ne0.

\]

Thus neither the BF core nor residual-charge loops produce the missing second tensor.

## 7. What massive boundary matter can remember

Massive matter can retain projector information only through data beyond its residual charge class:

- a family-dependent mass matrix;
- a neutral cross-Peirce Yukawa map \(K\);
- higher-dimensional operators left by the heavy \(U(1)_X\) boson;
- the integer lift of charges before reduction modulo three;
- projector-texture couplings involving \(DP\).

Each item is an ultraviolet memory channel. None belongs to pure BF theory.

Integrating out the heavy continuous gauge boson, for example, produces current-current terms

\[
\frac{g_X^2}{M_{Z'}^2}
J_X^\mu(P)J_{X\mu}(P),

\]

where

\[
J_X^\mu(P)
=\sum_r\overline\Psi_r\gamma^\mu q_X^{(r)}(P)\Psi_r.

\]

These operators retain the integer lift \(q_X(P)\), even though the pure topological quotient does not. But if all other family tensors are absent, their response is still equivariant in \(P\) and has the form \(\alpha P+\beta Q\); it reinforces a chosen split without orienting it.

## 8. Majorana/Yukawa response is genuinely non-scalar

The lepton sector supplies typed Hermitian tensors on \(V_L\):

\[
H_e=Y_eY_e^\dagger,

\]

and, after seesaw reduction,

\[
H_\nu=m_\nu m_\nu^\dagger.

\]

Alternatively, above the seesaw scale one may form

\[
H_{\nu,f}
=Y_\nu f(M_R^\dagger M_R)Y_\nu^\dagger

\]

for functions \(f\) determined by matching or loops.

These matrices transform covariantly on the left-lepton family bundle and are generically noncommuting:

\[
[H_e,H_\nu]\ne0.

\]

They therefore evade the one-projector no-go and can generate a cross-Peirce restoring force.

## 9. What Majorana reality does and does not supply

A real nondegenerate Majorana metric can reduce a sterile flavour symmetry

\[
U(3)\to O(3)

\]

and produce a real triplet of pair variables. But if the metric is proportional to the identity, it still selects no axis. A nondegenerate Majorana hierarchy supplies eigenaxes, but then the hierarchy itself is the second tensor requiring explanation.

Furthermore, the natural Majorana/Hodge metric is positive definite, whereas the \(H_2(\mathbb R)\) Jordan determinant has signature \((1,2)\). An additional selected axis

\[
n\in\mathbb R^3,
\qquad
\eta_n=2nn^T-I

\]

is required to create the split signature. Thus Majorana reality helps type the carrier but does not by itself complete the selector.

## 10. A minimal two-spurion selector

Normalize the Hermitian responses by a declared ultraviolet prescription, producing \(\widehat H_e\) and \(\widehat H_\nu\). Then the positive functional

\[
V(P)
=\kappa_e\|[P,\widehat H_e]\|^2
+\kappa_\nu\|[P,\widehat H_\nu]\|^2

\]

is basis covariant and can possess an isolated minimum.

A second canonical-looking operator is the Hermitian commutator

\[
C_\ell=i[\widehat H_e,\widehat H_\nu].

\]

It contains relative-frame information absent from either spectrum alone. Its square

\[
C_\ell^2=-[\widehat H_e,\widehat H_\nu]^2

\]

is positive and CP even. A term

\[
\operatorname{Tr}(PC_\ell^2)

\]

can select an eigenline without introducing a third family matrix.

However:

- the normalization of \(H_e\) relative to \(H_\nu\) remains underived;
- no current microscopic action fixes the coefficient or its sign;
- a CP-odd linear coupling to \(C_\ell\) requires an orientation pseudoscalar;
- the selected line need not coincide with a charged-lepton mass eigenstate and may induce flavour-changing gauge couplings.

Thus the commutator supplies a canonical algebraic candidate, not yet a physical selector.

## 11. Boundary-orientation portal

Let \(\chi\) be a defect-orientation pseudoscalar. The term

\[
V_{\chi P}
=g_\chi\chi\operatorname{Tr}(P C_\ell)

\]

is a possible combined-CP-even portal when \(\chi\) and \(C_\ell\) are both odd under the relevant involution. For fixed nonzero \(\chi\), it selects an extremal eigenline of \(C_\ell\); reversing the defect orientation reverses the spectral preference.

This is the smallest explicit operation tying:

- defect orientation;
- noncommuting lepton-family data;
- and projector selection.

But ordinary effective field theory permits independent portal coefficients for every allowed CP-odd invariant. Neither Standard Model gauge symmetry nor the current Jordan/Furey construction fixes \(g_\chi\) or excludes competitors. The portal is live but non-predictive until a source action derives it.
