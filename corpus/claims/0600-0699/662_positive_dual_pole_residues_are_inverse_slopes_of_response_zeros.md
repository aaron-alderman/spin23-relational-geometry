## Executive result

Let \(F(z)\) be a real-symmetric matrix Herglotz response on a real interval between its original poles. Suppose

\[
F(r)v=0,
\qquad \|v\|=1,
\]

is a simple response zero. If

\[
\mu'(r)=v^TF'(r)v>0,
\]

then the dual response

\[
H(z)=-F(z)^{-1}
\]

has a pole at \(r\) with Herglotz residue

\[
\boxed{
R_r=\frac{vv^T}{v^TF'(r)v}\succeq0.
}
\]

Thus positivity of the spectrum generated by inversion is not an accident of the examples. It follows from monotonicity of the vanishing response eigenvalue.

## 1. Local proof

Near a simple zero, analytic perturbation theory gives one eigenvalue

\[
\mu(z)=\mu'(r)(z-r)+O((z-r)^2)
\]

with normalized eigenvector \(v+O(z-r)\). Therefore

\[
F(z)^{-1}
=\frac{vv^T}{\mu'(r)(z-r)}+O(1).
\]

Changing to the positive Herglotz pole convention \(R/(r-z)\),

\[
-F(z)^{-1}
=\frac{vv^T/\mu'(r)}{r-z}+O(1).
\]

If \(\mu'(r)>0\), the residue is positive rank one.

## 2. Why the slope is positive

For a rational matrix Herglotz response

\[
F(z)=Q+Mz+\sum_j\frac{R_j}{s_j-z},
\qquad
M\succeq0,
\quad R_j\succeq0,
\]

the real derivative away from the poles is

\[
F'(x)=M+\sum_j\frac{R_j}{(s_j-x)^2}\succeq0.
\]

When the derivative is positive on the zero vector,

\[
v^TF'(r)v>0,
\]

the crossing is simple and the dual residue is positive.

This is the matrix form of Herglotz eigenvalue interlacing.

## 4. Physical meaning of inverse slope

The dual spectral weight is controlled by how rapidly the original response eigenvalue crosses zero:

\[
\operatorname{tr}R_r
=\frac1{v^TF'(r)v}.
\]

- a steep zero produces a weak dual pole;
- a shallow zero produces a strong dual pole;
- a zero whose slope vanishes requires higher-order analysis and can signal a merger or critical point.

This provides a quantitative bridge between response zeros and collective-mode strength.

## 5. Eisenbud–Wigner boundary

The slope relation is adjacent to time-delay language but does not yet equal it. Eisenbud–Wigner delay is derived from a unitary scattering matrix or phase shift,

\[
\tau\sim-iS^\dagger\frac{dS}{dE}.
\]

To identify \(v^TF'(r)v\) with a delay, the programme still needs:

- an on-shell scattering matrix;
- a response-to-phase-shift map;
- physical-sheet continuation;
- a channel normalization;
- treatment of widths and thresholds.

Until then, “inverse response slope” is the exact statement.

## 6. Stop-everything criteria

Stop asserting a positive dual particle pole if:

1. the zero is not real or lies on an unphysical sheet;
2. \(v^TF'(r)v\le0\) in the physical norm;
3. the zero is multiple and the simple-pole expansion fails;
4. gauge null vectors contaminate the kernel;
5. a time delay is claimed without a unitary scattering dictionary.
