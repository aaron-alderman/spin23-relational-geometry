## Executive result

The full response

\[
F(E)=EI+\frac{R_-}{-1-E}+\frac{R_+}{1-E}
\]

has an exact four-state Hermitian realization. Introduce a two-dimensional carrier sector, a two-dimensional internal sector, and

\[
\mathcal H=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&W^T\\
W&H_0
\end{pmatrix},
\quad
H_0=\begin{pmatrix}-1&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix},
\quad
W=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\1&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The Schur complement of the internal block of (EI-\mathcal H) is

\[
\boxed{
F(E)=EI-W^T(EI-H_0)^{-1}W.
}
\]

Thus the previously problematic (EI) term is simply the free inverse propagator of the carrier. The rational terms are the self-energy generated by coupling the carrier to two internal levels.

Even more strongly,

\[
\boxed{
P(EI-\mathcal H)^{-1}P^T=F(E)^{-1},
}
\]

where (P) projects onto the carrier. Therefore the dual response

\[
-F(E)^{-1}
\]

is the negative carrier-projected resolvent of an ordinary finite Hermitian Hamiltonian.

## 2. Why inverse slope equals carrier spectral weight

Suppose (F(r)v=0), with (v^Tv=1). The corresponding full eigenvector is

\[
\Psi_r=
\begin{pmatrix}
v\\
(rI-H_0)^{-1}Wv
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Its norm is

\[
\begin{aligned}
\|\Psi_r\|^2
&=v^Tv+v^TW^T(rI-H_0)^{-2}Wv\\
&=v^TF'(r)v.
\end{aligned}
\]

After normalizing the full eigenstate, its carrier-sector projector is therefore

\[
\boxed{
R_r^{\rm carrier}
=\frac{vv^T}{v^TF'(r)v}.
}
\]

This is exactly the positive dual-pole residue derived abstractly in document 662. The inverse-slope formula now has a direct Hilbert-space interpretation: it measures how much of the normalized eigenstate lives in the carrier sector.

## 3. Completeness explains the residue sum rule

The four normalized eigenstates of (mathcal H) form a complete basis. Projecting completeness onto the two-dimensional carrier gives

\[
\sum_{r}R_r^{\rm carrier}=I_2.
\]

Taking the trace yields

\[
\sum_r\operatorname{tr}R_r^{\rm carrier}=2.
\]

This explains the ultraviolet residue sum found computationally in document 661. It is not a numerical accident; it is projected spectral completeness.
