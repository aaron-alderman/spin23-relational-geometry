## Executive result

The symmetric trilinear sewing tensor defines a canonical recursion on all \(121\) points of

\[
\mathbb P(S)=PG(4,3).
\]

For every nonzero \(s\in S\), the middle-action form

\[
B_s(x,z)=\tau_\mu(s,x,z)
\]

has rank four. It therefore has a unique projective kernel point. Define

\[
\kappa([s])=[\ker B_s].
\]

Exact enumeration proves

\[
\boxed{\kappa^2=\mathrm{id}.}
\]

Its cycle structure is

\[
\boxed{1^{11}2^{55}.}
\]

The eleven fixed points form the complete Coxeter 11-cap in \(PG(4,3)\). Its 55 secants partition the other 110 projective points into pairs, and \(\kappa\) swaps the two exterior points on every secant.

This gives the programme its first exact, nontrivial “middle action and return” operation:

\[
[s]
\longmapsto
\tau_\mu(s,-,-)
\longmapsto
[\ker\tau_\mu(s,-,-)].
\]

It is an authentic recursion generated by the relational tensor. It is not yet a Jordan adjoint or a route to \(Sp(4,\mathbb R)\).

## 1. Constant rank four

There are \(121=(3^5-1)/2\) projective points in \(PG(4,3)\). For every one of them, the certificate forms the symmetric \(5\times5\) matrix

\[
(B_s)_{ij}=\tau_\mu(s,e_i,e_j).
\]

Every matrix has rank four:

\[
\operatorname{rank}B_s=4
\qquad
(s\ne0).
\]

Thus the kernel is always exactly one-dimensional and \(\kappa\) has no exceptional or undefined point over \(\mathbb F_3\).

This constant-corank-one property is stronger than the earlier zero-radical theorem. The zero-radical theorem says \(B_s\ne0\) for \(s\ne0\); the new result fixes its rank exactly.

## 2. The involution

Computing the kernel line for all 121 points gives:

- eleven fixed points;
- 110 nonfixed points;
- 55 two-cycles;
- no longer cycles.

Hence

\[
\kappa(\kappa([s]))=[s]
\]

for every projective point.

The involution is intrinsic to \(\tau_\mu\). Any tensor automorphism \(g\) obeys

\[
\kappa(g[s])=g\kappa([s]),
\]

because \(g\) transports both the middle-action form and its kernel. The fixed locus is consequently an intrinsic \(M_{11}\)-orbit.

## 3. The complete Coxeter 11-cap

Let \(\mathcal C\) be the eleven fixed points. Exact finite-geometric checks show:

1. no third point of \(\mathcal C\) lies on a secant through two points of \(\mathcal C\);
2. the \(\binom{11}{2}=55\) secants contain \(110\) distinct points outside \(\mathcal C\);
3. those are all \(121-11=110\) exterior projective points;
4. on every secant, \(\kappa\) swaps its two exterior points.

Thus every exterior point lies on a unique secant and \(\mathcal C\) is complete.

The standard literature identifies the complete 11-cap in \(PG(4,3)\) with the ternary Golay geometry; it is commonly called the Coxeter 11-cap. See the [ATLAS of finite group representations](https://brauer.maths.qmul.ac.uk/Atlas/spor/M11/) and the finite-geometry discussion in [Designs, Codes and Cryptography](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10623-023-01318-1).

The tensor does more than recover the cap: it canonically supplies the secant-pair exchange on its complement.

## 5. Primal fixed cap versus dual coordinate cap

The eleven original coordinate evaluations are the columns

\[
\ell_j=(S_{1j},\ldots,S_{5j})\in S^*.
\]

They form an eleven-point cap naturally belonging to the dual of the monomial \(5b\)-module.

The tensor fixed cap lies in the primal space \(S\). In the displayed basis, the two sets are not equal. This is important: there is no hidden identity map \(S=S^*\).

Nevertheless, the certificate finds an explicit projective equivalence. One matrix carrying the tensor fixed cap to the coordinate-evaluation cap is

\[
P=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&2&2&0&0\\
2&1&2&2&1\\
0&1&0&2&1\\
1&1&1&0&1\\
0&2&0&1&1
\end{pmatrix}
\in GL(5,3).
\]

Thus the configurations are projectively equivalent but not canonically identified by the displayed basis.

The absence of an invariant symmetric bilinear form now has a precise representation-theoretic meaning: the code and cocode modules are dual, not canonically self-dual.

## 6. A genuine relational-carrier recursion

The operation

\[
s\mapsto B_s=\tau_\mu(s,-,-)
\]

turns a carrier into a bilinear relationship object. Taking its null direction returns a carrier:

\[
B_s\mapsto\ker B_s.
\]

Projectively, doing this twice returns the starting carrier.

This is very close in shape to the programme's proposed nested operator:

\[
A\;X\;B,
\]

where the middle object is not merely multiplied but determines which relation survives. Here the exact operation is:

\[
\text{carrier}
\to
\text{middle-action form}
\to
\text{null carrier}.
\]

The fixed cap consists of self-null carriers:

\[
[s]=[\ker B_s]
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\tau_\mu(s,s,-)=0.
\]

That is a mathematically precise “carrier/relationship fixed point.”

## 7. Comparison with a Jordan adjoint

Jordan algebras often carry an adjoint or inversion operation derived from their norm. The formal analogy is:

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{Golay tensor}&\text{Jordan algebra}\\ \hline
s\mapsto\tau_\mu(s,-,-)&x\mapsto P(x)\text{ or a norm Hessian}\\
\text{take the kernel line}&\text{take adjoint or inverse}\\
\kappa^2=1\text{ projectively}&(x^\#)^\#\propto x
\end{array}
\]

But the analogy has strict limits:

- \(\tau_\mu\) is characteristic-three divided-power data, not an ordinary cubic norm;
- \(B_s\) is singular for every nonzero \(s\);
- the kernel map is obtained from \(4\times4\) minors and is not yet shown to obey a Jordan identity over a general field;
- its automorphism group is \(M_{11}\), not \(Sp(4,3)\);
- no invariant metric exists on the full module.

The correct status is therefore:

\[
\boxed{\text{adjoint-like finite recursion, not yet a Jordan adjoint}.}
\]

## 8. Consequence for the \(Spin(2,3)\) bridge

The code/cocode duality supplies a missing type distinction:

\[
\text{code carrier }5b

\text{cocode relation carrier }5a.

The trilinear tensor lives naturally on the \(5a\) relation side and reconstructs its cap. The characteristic-zero \(Spin(2,3)\) vector, by contrast, is a metric five-space.

A credible bridge can no longer be a direct identification of five-dimensional vector spaces. It must explain:

The new involution is a concrete candidate input to that construction, not its completion.
