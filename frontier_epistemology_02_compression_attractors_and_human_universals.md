# Compression, attraction basins and human universals

**Date:** 18 October 2026  
**Status:** speculative epistemic framework with explicit falsification gates  
**Parent:** [Legible and illegible knowledge](frontier_epistemology_01_legible_and_illegible_knowledge.md)

## The stronger proposal

Illegible knowledge often becomes legible through compression.

A high-dimensional history of sensation, action, correction and social interaction is compressed into a concept, skill, metaphor, rule, symbol or invariant. The result does not reproduce the complete trajectory. It preserves what repeatedly mattered across many trajectories.

This suggests a shift in question:

> Do not ask only whether a proposition is universally true. Ask which representations act as attractors, which initial conditions flow into them, and what remains invariant across changes of observer and compression scheme.

Human universals then become possible evidence about the shape of the epistemic landscape. They are not automatically truths. They are recurrent attractors in the coupled dynamics of world, body, culture and representation.

The speculative step is that sufficiently robust attractors may reveal something about reality itself.

## A dynamical model

Let (x) denote a high-dimensional state containing experience, embodiment, environment and social context. Let

\[
x_{t+1}=F(x_t;W,B,C)
\]

describe learning or adaptation under world structure (W), biological constraints (B) and cultural conditions (C).

A compression map

\[
\pi:x\mapsto z
\]

produces a lower-dimensional legible representation (z): a word, concept, gesture, equation, norm or practical rule.

An attractor (A) is a stable region for the compressed dynamics:

\[
z_t\longrightarrow A
\]

from a basin

\[
\mathcal B(A)=\{x_0: \pi(F^t(x_0))\to A\}.
\]

The epistemically interesting object is not merely the attractor label. It is the full package:

\[
\left(A,\mathcal B(A),\text{stability},\text{transition costs},
\text{observer dependence}\right).
\]

Two cultures may use different symbols but occupy equivalent basins. Conversely, identical words may conceal different underlying attractors.

## Why compression can reveal structure

Compression discards information, but it cannot be arbitrary if the compressed agent must continue to predict and act successfully.

Suppose many agents with different histories independently learn a compact variable (z) because retaining it improves prediction or control. That recurrence suggests that (z) tracks a stable regularity of their interaction with the world.

This is similar to several familiar ideas:

- macroscopic variables survive coarse-graining because microscopic details become irrelevant at the chosen scale;
- sufficient statistics preserve the information relevant to a specified inference task;
- invariants survive changes of coordinate;
- effective theories retain the degrees of freedom that govern accessible behaviour;
- learned skills compress many corrections into a small number of actionable discriminations.

The speculative epistemic principle is therefore:

\[
\boxed{
\text{convergence under diverse, success-constrained compressions is evidence for latent structure.}
}
\]

It is evidence, not entailment.

## Human universals as attractors

A human universal can be interpreted at several levels.

### Biological attractor

Shared sensory systems, motor constraints, affective regulation and developmental architecture make some distinctions easier to learn than others.

### Ecological attractor

Humans repeatedly confront objects, agents, scarcity, injury, cooperation, birth, death, day/night cycles and spatial navigation. Similar worlds can induce similar compressions.

### Coordination attractor

Some norms or categories recur because they make collective action easier, not because they mirror a mind-independent taxonomy.

### Representational attractor

Some concepts are stable because language and finite memory favour them. Binary contrasts, narratives, agents and causal chains may be cognitively cheap compressions.

### Truth-tracking attractor

Some structures may recur because agents who fail to represent them systematically predict or act worse. Arithmetic regularities, object permanence and many causal constraints are candidates of this stronger kind.

One observed universal may be produced by several of these mechanisms simultaneously.

## Attractor is not truth

Convergence alone is insufficient. False beliefs can be powerful attractors.

They may arise from:

- common perceptual illusions;
- shared biological heuristics;
- coordination equilibria with multiple equally viable conventions;
- prestige, coercion or institutional replication;
- path dependence and frozen accidents;
- compression pressure favouring a simple but biased model;
- selection for social cohesion rather than prediction;
- training data that repeatedly reproduce their own categories.

Thus

\[
\text{large basin}\not\Rightarrow\text{truth}.
\]

An attractor tells us that something stabilizes the representation. The scientific problem is to identify the stabilizer.

## When an attractor becomes evidence for truth

The truth-tracking interpretation becomes stronger when convergence survives independent perturbations.

### Observer diversity

Does the attractor recur across cultures, developmental histories, sensory modalities, institutions and conceptual vocabularies?

### Intervention stability

Does it survive when incentives, teaching methods, labels and social authorities are changed?

### Predictive necessity

Do agents who omit the structure make identifiable errors that cannot be repaired by a mere change of convention?

### Instrumental extension

Does the same structure appear when biological senses are replaced or augmented by instruments?

### Compression-scheme invariance

Does it recur under different model classes, languages, loss functions and levels of coarse-graining?

### Novel-domain transfer

Does the representation continue to work outside the environment in which it was acquired?

### Counterfactual resistance

Can the attractor be escaped while preserving equal predictive and practical performance? If not, it is more likely to reflect a genuine constraint.

### Cross-species or artificial convergence

Do differently embodied animals or artificial agents discover an equivalent structure? Convergence beyond the human phenotype helps separate world structure from human-specific structure.

No single test is decisive. Together they turn universality into graded evidence.

## Basin geometry matters more than popularity

Counting how many people state a belief measures occupancy, not basin geometry.

More informative questions include:

- How many distinct starting states converge to it?
- How rapidly do they converge?
- How much perturbation can it withstand?
- What is the cost of leaving it?
- Does it reappear after being deliberately removed from teaching?
- Are there rival attractors with equal predictive power?
- Is the boundary between basins sharp, fractal or historically contingent?
- Which dimensions of experience determine the transition?

A socially dominant belief can have high occupancy but a shallow basin maintained by enforcement. A difficult scientific insight can have low occupancy but a deep basin: once the relevant evidence and tools are available, independent investigators repeatedly rediscover it.

## Legibility as renormalization

Illegible-to-legible transition can be understood as a coarse-graining process:

\[
x_{\rm lived}
\xrightarrow{\text{compression}}
z_{\rm practical}
\xrightarrow{\text{compression}}
c_{\rm concept}
\xrightarrow{\text{formalization}}
I_{\rm invariant}.
\]

At each stage, details are discarded. A good compression preserves the variables needed for prediction, action or explanation at the next scale.

This means illegible knowledge does not simply become legible by being described more fully. It often becomes legible when someone finds the correct lower-dimensional coordinates.

The breakthrough is frequently not additional data but the right quotient.

## Truth as an inverse problem

Observed attractors are generated jointly by reality and the observer:

\[
\text{observed basin structure}
=
\Phi(W,B,C,\pi,\mathcal L),
\]

where (W) is the world, (B) embodiment, (C) culture, (\pi) compression and (\mathcal L) the learning dynamics.

Inferring truth from universals is therefore an inverse problem. The same attractor pattern may be compatible with several combinations of world and observer constraints.

The proper target is not “prove the universal is true,” but:

1. vary (B,C,\pi,\mathcal L) as independently as possible;
2. observe which attractor features persist;
3. assign persistent structure to (W) only after observer-relative explanations have been tested;
4. retain equivalence classes when the data cannot identify a unique ontology.

This resembles tomography. Each population, embodiment or model architecture provides a different projection of the latent landscape.

## How LLMs can map attraction basins

LLMs are especially suited to basin mapping because they model large fields of legible traces rather than isolated propositions.

### Discovering semantic convergence

An LLM can search for structurally equivalent ideas expressed through different metaphors, languages and disciplines. This can reveal an attractor whose surface labels differ.

### Reconstructing basin boundaries

By generating near cases and asking where judgments reverse, an LLM can approximate the decision surface surrounding a concept or intuition.

### Measuring compression stability

The same material can be repeatedly summarized, translated, formalized and reconstructed. Features that survive many lossy transformations are candidate invariants; features that disappear immediately may be surface detail—or fragile information the compression is biased against.

### Comparing learning trajectories

LLMs can organize diaries, interviews, lab notebooks and historical records into recurring stages of conceptual change. This helps distinguish a genuinely recurrent discovery path from a retrospectively imposed narrative.

### Generating perturbations

The model can systematically vary labels, framing, incentives, examples and background assumptions to test whether an apparent universal is robust or prompted.

### Locating unrepresented residues

When expert judgments remain predictable only from examples and not from the extracted rules, the residual error marks an illegible component. The LLM can help characterize where the formalization fails without pretending to eliminate the failure.

## LLM-specific danger: manufactured attractors

LLMs do not merely observe cultural basins. Once widely deployed, they reshape them.

A model may amplify a representation because it is common in training data, easy to verbalize, preferred by evaluators or repeatedly returned to users. The resulting convergence can be mistaken for independent discovery even though it is feedback from a shared model.

This creates an epistemic endogeneity problem:

\[
\text{corpus frequency}
\to
\text{model output}
\to
\text{human adoption}
\to
\text{future corpus frequency}.
\]

Therefore future claims about human universals must track whether participants were exposed to the same foundation models. Model-mediated agreement is not independent convergence.

## A research protocol for attractor epistemology

1. **Define the state space.** What counts as a trajectory, representation and successful outcome?
2. **Collect independent trajectories.** Preserve cultural, bodily and historical provenance.
3. **Cluster by structure, not vocabulary.** Look for transformations that map one representation into another.
4. **Estimate basin boundaries.** Use contrast cases and interventions rather than prevalence alone.
5. **Identify candidate stabilizers.** Separate biological, ecological, social, representational and truth-tracking mechanisms.
6. **Perturb the observer.** Change language, incentives, embodiment, instruments and learning architecture.
7. **Perturb the world.** Test whether the representation predicts novel outcomes and counterfactual interventions.
8. **Measure rediscovery.** Remove the representation and ask whether independent agents reconstruct an equivalent one.
9. **Audit model contamination.** Track common LLM, curriculum and institutional sources.
10. **Report an equivalence class.** Do not claim a unique truth when several latent structures generate the same basin geometry.

## Connection to the frontier programme

The recurring attraction toward (Spin(2,3)), Jordan triples, one carrier/two faces/three relationships, wheels within wheels and source/mediator intertwiners can be treated as an observed basin rather than immediately as a theorem.

The research question becomes:

> Why do many mathematical routes compress toward this interface, and which features of the convergence survive changes of representation?

Some convergence is explained by known equivalences:

\[
Sp(4,\mathbb R)\cong Spin_0(2,3),
\]

the conformal action on the (H_2(\mathbb R)) Jordan cone, symplectic phase-space structure and recurring rank-two response geometry. Those are genuine mathematical stabilizers.

Other convergence may reflect human compression preferences: binary faces, ternary relations, cycles and nested symmetries are cognitively efficient motifs. Still other apparent convergence has failed exact typing tests, such as the absent canonical action on the three exceptional mediator copies.

The basin itself is therefore scientifically interesting even when its strongest ontological interpretation remains unproved. Its internal geography tells us which connections are forced, which are conventional, and exactly where intuition outruns the available map.

## Final speculative principle

Human universals are not ground truth. They are measurements of a coupled human–world dynamical system.

But if an attractor:

- is rediscovered from diverse starting points;
- survives changes of language, body, culture and model;
- resists incentive and framing perturbations;
- supports novel prediction and intervention;
- and is difficult to replace without loss;

then its basin geometry is evidence that the compression has encountered a real constraint.

The strongest form of the proposal is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{truth is not identified with the attractor;}\
\text{truth is inferred from what remains invariant across independently generated basins.}
}
\]
