# The Hadamard transition is a degree-24 rational symplectic correspondence between index-six fibers

## Status

**Exact rational symplectic and lattice-commensurability result.**

The determinant-two Hadamard matrix is neither an integral symmetry nor merely a formal odd-prime basis change. It defines a rational symplectic correspondence between commensurable rank-four carriers. On the primitive \(Spin(2,3)\) wedge, the selected index-six lattice and its Hadamard transform meet with index 24 on each side.

\[
\boxed{
[M:M\cap W_HM]
=[W_HM:M\cap W_HM]
=24.}
\]

One finite defect quotient is

\[
\boxed{
(M+W_HM)/M
\cong C_2\oplus C_{12}
\cong C_4\oplus C_6.}
\]

This gives a precise arithmetic role for another occurrence of 24. The defect is abelian, so it is not the nonabelian order-24 qutrit code automorphism group.

## 1. Rank-four rational symplectic transition

Let

\[
H=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&-1\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\det H=-2,
\qquad
H^2=2I.
\]

On \(V=D_2\oplus D_2^*\), define

\[
C_H=
\begin{pmatrix}
H&0\\
0&H^{-T}
\end{pmatrix}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
H&0\\
0&\frac12H
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Then

\[
C_H^TJC_H=J,
\]

so

\[
C_H\in Sp(4,\mathbb Q).
\]

It is not in \(Sp(4,\mathbb Z)\). The two halves have complementary lattice behavior:

- \(H\mathbb Z^2\subset\mathbb Z^2\) has index two;
- \(\mathbb Z^2\subset\frac12H\mathbb Z^2\) has index two.

Therefore

\[
[V:V\cap C_HV]
=[C_HV:V\cap C_HV]
=2.
\]

The determinant-two map is an isogeny-like correspondence, not an automorphism of either integral presentation.

## 2. Primitive-wedge action

In the primitive basis

\[
(E_1,F_1,E_2,F_2,R),
\]

the induced rational orthogonal transformation is

\[
\begin{aligned}
E_1&\mapsto-2E_1,\\
F_1&\mapsto-\frac12F_1,\\
E_2&\mapsto\frac12(R-E_2-F_2),\\
F_2&\mapsto-\frac12(R+E_2+F_2),\\
R&\mapsto E_2-F_2.
\end{aligned}
\]

It preserves the form

\[
U\oplus U\oplus\langle-2\rangle
\]

over \(\mathbb Q\), so

\[
W_H\in O(2,3;\mathbb Q).
\]

This is the rational \(Spin(2,3)\)-side image of the symplectic Hadamard transition.

## 3. Action on the selected index-six basis

Recall

\[
v=E_1+F_1,\quad
w=E_1-F_1,\quad
r=R,\quad
s=E_2,\quad
t=-3F_2.
\]

In this basis,

\[
\begin{aligned}
W_Hv&=-\frac54v-\frac34w,\\
W_Hw&=-\frac34v-\frac54w,\\
W_Hr&=s+\frac13t,\\
W_Hs&=\frac12r-\frac12s+\frac16t,\\
W_Ht&=\frac32r+\frac32s-\frac12t.
\end{aligned}
\]

The denominators \(4,3,2,6\) show immediately that \(W_H\) is not an automorphism of \(M\). They also exhibit the simultaneous interaction of:

- the quartic orientation direction;
- the binary determinant-two defect;
- the level-three null-leg restriction.

The transition mixes exactly the arithmetic data accumulated by the separate branches.

## 4. Exact commensurability calculation

Let \(T\) be the preceding matrix in the \(M\)-basis. Clearing denominators gives

\[
12T=
\begin{pmatrix}
-15&-9&0&0&0\\
-9&-15&0&0&0\\
0&0&0&6&18\\
0&0&12&-6&18\\
0&0&4&2&-6
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Its determinant is

\[
\det(12T)=12^5,
\qquad
\det T=1.
\]

Thus \(M\) and \(W_HM\) have equal covolume.

The subgroup generated by the columns of \(12T\) modulo 12 has order 24. Its element-order distribution is

\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrrrr}
\text{order}&1&2&3&4&6&12\\
\hline
\text{count}&1&3&2&4&6&8.
\end{array}
\]

It has four elements killed by two and exponent twelve, hence

\[
(M+W_HM)/M
\cong C_2\oplus C_{12}.
\]

Equal covolumes then give index 24 on both sides of the intersection.

## 5. Why 24 appears without identifying the groups

The programme now contains several exact order-24 objects:

1. the twice-punctured qutrit projective automorphism group;
2. the ordered quartic-section stabilizer;
3. the Hadamard fiber-correspondence index.

They are not automatically the same structure:

- the code group acts nontrivially on fourteen projective columns;
- the section stabilizer fixes the selected order-eight phase subgroup pointwise;
- the Hadamard defect is an abelian quotient \(C_2\oplus C_{12}\).

The repeated integer is therefore evidence of a shared arithmetic scale, not evidence of a common group action.

\[
\boxed{
\text{same order}
\ne
\text{same object}
\ne
\text{canonical intertwiner}.}
\]

## 6. Interpretation as a transition rather than a symmetry

The result supports a more precise version of the “two faces” picture:

- each integral fiber carries its own admissible lattice data;
- \(H\) is not an internal symmetry of either fiber;
- it is a rational transition between commensurable fibers;
- the failure of integrality is measured by finite defect data.

This is structurally closer to a Hecke correspondence or isogeny than to an ordinary gauge transformation. “Hecke-style” is used descriptively here; no automorphic Hecke operator has yet been constructed.

## 7. What is established and what remains open

### Established

- \(C_H\in Sp(4,\mathbb Q)\setminus Sp(4,\mathbb Z)\);
- its carrier intersection indices are \(2\) and \(2\);
- its primitive-wedge image lies in \(O(2,3;\mathbb Q)\);
- the selected index-six fibers meet with indices \(24\) and \(24\);
- the computed defect quotient is \(C_2\oplus C_{12}\);
- this order-24 object cannot be identified with the nonabelian code group.

### Open

- whether \(W_HM\) has a direct code, Furey or Jordan interpretation;
- whether the intersection lattice carries the common physical observables of the two presentations;
- whether the defect quotient supports a canonical Weil or theta representation;
- whether a genuine Hecke operator on an arithmetic \(Sp(4)\) quotient realizes this correspondence;
- whether the two fibers are local charts of one relational carrier.

## 8. Stop-everything and falsification gates

### Stop-everything

1. The common intersection lattice reproduces the source field labels without a fitted basis.
2. A theta, anomaly or Pfaffian line realizes the degree-24 correspondence as a physical transport operator.
3. Jordan-triple recursion generates \(C_H\) as the canonical passage between its two faces.
4. The code's order-24 action is shown to act naturally on the 24 cosets without identifying the abelian quotient with the group itself.

### Boundaries

- \(H\) is not an integral symmetry;
- “degree 24” is an intersection index, not a group order identification;
- the finite defect is abelian;
- a Hecke interpretation remains a research direction, not an established automorphic theorem.
