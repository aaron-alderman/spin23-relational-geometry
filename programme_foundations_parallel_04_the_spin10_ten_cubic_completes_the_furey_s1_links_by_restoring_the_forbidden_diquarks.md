# Foundations Parallel 04: The \(Spin(10)\) \(10\) Cubic Completes the Furey \(S_1\) Links by Restoring the Forbidden Diquarks

**Date:** 17 October 2026  
**Status:** exact representation-theoretic comparison; no mass spectrum or proton lifetime is calculated

## Result

The exceptional cubic channel

\[
16_F\,16_F\,10_H
\]

does not select the four-link Standard Model branch. Its Standard Model decomposition contains:

\[
\boxed{
4\text{ ordinary Higgs Yukawa channels}
+3\text{ Furey }S_1\text{ leptoquark channels}
+2\text{ diquark channels}.}
\]

The first seven are exactly the seven links allowed by the minimal doubled Furey-arrow first-order condition. The last two are precisely the \(Q-Q\) and \(u^c-d^c\) links which that condition excludes.

Thus the two structures almost coincide, but their disagreement is physically decisive:

\[
\boxed{
\text{full }Spin(10)\text{ covariance restores the proton-dangerous completion that Furey first order removes}.}
\]

## 1. Branching chain

In the exceptional route, the \(E_6\) fundamental branches under \(Spin(10)\times U(1)_\psi\) as

\[
27\longrightarrow16_{+1}+10_{-2}+1_{+4},
\]

and the cubic contains the charge-neutral channel \(16_{+1}\,16_{+1}\,10_{-2}\). Under \(SU(5)\),

\[
16_F=10_F\oplus\bar5_F\oplus1_F,
\qquad
10_H=5_H\oplus\bar5_H.
\]

The vector Higgs contains

\[
\begin{aligned}
5_H&=H_u\oplus T
=(\mathbf1,\mathbf2)_{1/2}
\oplus(\mathbf3,\mathbf1)_{-1/3},\\
\bar5_H&=H_d\oplus\bar T
=(\mathbf1,\mathbf2)_{-1/2}
\oplus(\bar{\mathbf3},\mathbf1)_{1/3}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The color antitriplet \(\bar T\) has the gauge representation called \(S_1\) in leptoquark notation. The \(10_H\) contains no

\[
(\bar{\mathbf3},\mathbf3)_{1/3},
\]

so it cannot realize the \(S_3\) branch.

## 2. Exact component expansion

The three \(SU(5)\) pieces give nine Standard Model coupling types:

\[
\begin{array}{c|l}
SU(5)\text{ invariant}&SM\text{ components}\\ \hline
10_F10_F5_H
&Q u^c H_u,\quad QQT,\quad u^ce^cT\\
10_F\bar5_F\bar5_H
&Qd^cH_d,\quad Le^cH_d,\quad QL\bar T,\quad u^cd^c\bar T\\
\bar5_F1_F5_H
&L\nu^cH_u,\quad d^c\nu^cT.
\end{array}
\]

The matter product satisfies

\[
16\otimes16=10_s\oplus120_a\oplus126_s.
\]

Hence the single \(10_H\) channel carries a symmetric family coupling matrix. Its component terms are not independent arbitrary additions; they are projections of one \(Spin(10)\) invariant, up to normalization and Clebsch conventions.

## 3. Comparison with the doubled Furey geometry

The common seven-channel intersection is

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{ordinary}&Q-u^c, Q-d^c, L-e^c, L-\nu^c\\
\text{colored }S_1&Q-L, u^c-e^c, d^c-\nu^c.
\end{array}
\]

The full \(Spin(10)\) orbit adds

\[
QQT,\qquad u^cd^c\bar T.
\]

These are exactly the two basic diquark channels found to be noncomposable in the Furey endpoint geometry.

Therefore no lone \(16_F16_F10_H\) invariant realizes the proton-safer Furey \(S_1\) pattern. Keeping its three leptoquark components while deleting both diquark components is not \(Spin(10)\)-equivariant. It requires symmetry breaking, an additional selector, cancellation among several Higgs representations, or a model-specific texture.

## 4. Baryon and lepton number

The triplet \(T\) participates in both

\[
QQT
\]

and

\[
u^ce^cT,\qquad d^c\nu^cT.
\]

The first would assign it baryon number \(-2/3\) and lepton number zero. The latter assign it baryon number \(+1/3\) and lepton number \(+1\). No single separate \((B,L)\) assignment works. The same conflict occurs for \(\bar T\) between \(QL\bar T\) and \(u^cd^c\bar T\).

However, every vertex consistently assigns

\[
3(B-L)(T)=-2,
\qquad
3(B-L)(\bar T)=+2.
\]

Thus the cubic may preserve \(B-L\) while violating baryon and lepton number separately. Matter parity cannot remove the triplets because these integer charges are even.

Exchange of a triplet that has both leptoquark and diquark vertices permits proton-decay operators. The rate depends on whether the model is supersymmetric, on triplet masses and mixing, and on family textures; none is fixed by this representation audit. The literature therefore treats doublet–triplet splitting and colored-triplet suppression as additional model-building problems rather than automatic consequences of \(SO(10)\).

## 5. Sharp verdicts

### Does the exceptional \(10\) enforce the conventional four links?

**No.** An irreducible \(Spin(10)\) vector packages the Higgs doublets with colored triplets. Projecting to the doublets alone breaks \(Spin(10)\). A low-energy four-link theory is possible only after doublet–triplet splitting or decoupling.

### Can it realize the optional leptoquark branch?

**Partly.** It realizes \(S_1\) and all three singlet leptoquark links, but not \(S_3\).

### Does it preserve the Furey proton-safety feature?

**No.** Exact \(Spin(10)\) completion restores both forbidden diquark links. The proton-safer seven-link Furey intersection is not closed under \(Spin(10)\).

### Representation-theoretic decision

\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{l}
\textbf{Go: }10_H\text{ is an economical common origin for the four Dirac Yukawa channels.}\\
\textbf{Interesting: }its colored partner reproduces precisely the Furey }S_1\text{ links.}\\
\textbf{No-go: }a single exact }16_F16_F10_H\text{ cannot give the proton-safe }S_1\text{ subset.}
\end{array}}
\]

This supports the “larger symmetry as crystal” reading only in a qualified sense: the larger representation shows why the channels fit into one pattern, but it does not choose the phenomenologically acceptable face of that pattern.
