## Correction to the method

The initial rule was too strict. It treated a lane as valuable only when it immediately supplied a missing selector, normalization or prediction. That is appropriate for a final closure claim but counterproductive for exploration.

The revised programme keeps two independent ledgers.

### Landscape ledger

A result earns landscape value when it does any of the following:

- reveals a new equivalence or a sharp non-equivalence;
- classifies possible carriers, states, defects or source protocols;
- supplies a calculational tool or tractable model;
- identifies a new obstruction and the assumption responsible for it;
- gives a physical analogue that makes an abstract structure measurable;
- creates a plausible bridge to a bounded calculation or experiment.

Generic structures are allowed here. Learning that several theories share the same Jordan cone is useful: it identifies a universality class and tells us which observations cannot reveal the microscopic origin.

### Closure ledger

A separate ledger asks whether the result actually selects:

- the physical source pair;
- the real structure;
- normalization and contacts;
- orientation;
- a physical scattering channel;
- a non-generic observable.

Failure to advance this ledger does not erase the landscape result. It only limits the strength of the ontological or predictive claim.

## Continuation rather than forced verdict

Each of the nine lanes now has a three-step continuation ladder:

\[
\text{map the objects}
\longrightarrow
\text{construct the bridge}
\longrightarrow
\text{perform a discriminating calculation or experiment}.
\]

The final step is not required to confirm the programme. It must merely distinguish meaningful alternatives. A negative answer can be just as informative as a positive one.

Examples include:

- determining whether the RG-closed carrier is two-dimensional or larger;
- seeing whether a wall normal simultaneously fixes chirality and response orientation;
- testing whether modular conjugation is stable under physically equivalent state choices;
- comparing two analogue devices with the same static cone but different coupling graphs;
- asking whether an orientation-sensitive amplitude coefficient remains free in every unitary local EFT.

## Revised stopping rule

A lane is not stopped because its first result is generic or fails to close the theory. It is paused only when:

1. its next bridge cannot be typed;
2. it repeats a classified equivalence without adding a discriminant;
3. no bounded calculation or experiment can distinguish the remaining alternatives.

This preserves openness without allowing the programme to drift into unfalsifiable accumulation.
