# The Octonion Cross Product Fixes the Colour Phase, but the Weak Jordan Algebra Is Phase-Blind

**Date:** 17 October 2026  
**Branches:** B03, B04, B10, B11 and B14  
**Claim status:** exact product-scaling theorem; reduction to the faithful \(\mathbb Z_6\) is conditional on a common volume identification

## 1. Starting ambiguity

The common exceptional/oscillator \(3+2\) carrier has equivariant maps

\[
T=\alpha I_3\oplus\beta I_2.
\]

Group equivariance leaves \(\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb C^\times\). The next question is whether the products carried by the two constructions fix these scalars.

They treat the two factors differently.

## 2. Colour phase from octonion multiplication

In the complex-vector presentation of the octonions,

\[
\mathbb O\cong\mathbb C\oplus\mathbb C^3,
\]

the vector part of the product contains

\[
\overline{a\times b},
\]

where \(\times\) is the complex bilinear cross product on \(\mathbb C^3\).

Under

\[
a\mapsto\alpha a,
\qquad
b\mapsto\alpha b,
\]

this term transforms as

\[
\overline{(\alpha a)\times(\alpha b)}
=\bar\alpha^2\,\overline{a\times b}.
\]

For the output to transform by the same \(\alpha\),

\[
\bar\alpha^2=\alpha.
\]

For nonzero \(\alpha\), this equation forces

\[
|\alpha|=1,
\qquad
\boxed{\alpha^3=1.}
\]

Thus octonion-product compatibility reduces the colour scalar to

\[
\alpha\in\mathbb Z_3.
\]

The certificate verifies the identity on generic complex vectors for all three cube roots and rejects a generic phase.

## 3. Why the weak Jordan algebra cannot see \(\beta\)

The weak carrier is the fundamental two-plane underlying

\[
H_2(\mathbb C).
\]

Its rank-one observables are

\[
P_v=vv^\dagger.
\]

For any unit phase \(\beta\),

\[
P_{\beta v}
=(\beta v)(\beta v)^\dagger
=|\beta|^2vv^\dagger
=P_v.
\]

Therefore the common phase of the weak spinor is projectively invisible to the Jordan observable algebra.

Consequently:

\[
\boxed{
H_2(\mathbb C)\text{ product compatibility alone does not fix }\beta\in U(1).
}
\]

This is not a computational failure. It is the usual loss of central phase when passing from vectors to observables.

## 4. Minimal product-compatible ambiguity

Combining the two results gives

\[
\alpha\in\mathbb Z_3,
\qquad
\beta\in U(1).
\]

So the products explicitly present in the exceptional flag reduce the original

\[
U(1)\times U(1)
\]

metric ambiguity only to

\[
\boxed{\mathbb Z_3\times U(1).}
\]

The weak phase needs additional structure.

## 5. Combined volume closes the ambiguity conditionally

Suppose both carriers come with an identified normalized five-dimensional volume form on

\[
\mathbb C^3\oplus\mathbb C^2.
\]

Then

\[
\det T=\alpha^3\beta^2=1.
\]

Since octonion compatibility already gives \(\alpha^3=1\),

\[
\boxed{\beta^2=1.}
\]

The remaining maps are

\[
(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathbb Z_3\times\mathbb Z_2,
\]

six possibilities in total.

Because \(2\) and \(3\) are coprime,

\[
\mathbb Z_3\times\mathbb Z_2\cong\mathbb Z_6.
\]

## 6. Relation to the faithful gauge kernel

The faithful Standard Model kernel is generated by

\[
(\omega_3,-1,e^{i\pi/3}).
\]

The residual carrier transformations are generated on the \(3+2\) vector factors by

\[
(\omega_3,-1).
\]

There is therefore a natural bijection:

\[
(\alpha,\beta)=(\omega_3^k,(-1)^k)
\longleftrightarrow
(\omega_3^k,(-1)^k,e^{ik\pi/3}).
\]

If the combined volume form and hypercharge phase are part of the same identified structure, then the remaining six intertwiners differ exactly by elements acting trivially on Standard Model matter. In that conditional sense the map is canonical up to the faithful gauge kernel.

Without the common-volume identification, this conclusion does not follow.

## 7. Where a common volume could come from

The oscillator construction has a natural fermionic top form

\[
a_1^\dagger a_2^\dagger a_3^\dagger a_4^\dagger a_5^\dagger.
\]

The exceptional side has:

- the \(SU(3)\)-invariant complex volume encoded by the colour cross product;
- a determinant line for the selected weak two-plane;
- the exceptional cubic norm on \(H_3(\mathbb O)\).

The missing proof must show that these data canonically identify the tensor product

\[
\Lambda^3\mathbb C^3\otimes\Lambda^2\mathbb C^2
\]

with the oscillator top line, including phase.

## 8. Branch decision

- **Closed:** octonion multiplication fixes the colour phase to \(\mathbb Z_3\).
- **Closed:** the weak Jordan observable algebra cannot fix a common \(U(1)\) spinor phase.
- **Conditional:** a common five-volume reduces the ambiguity to \(\mathbb Z_6\).
- **Pinned:** no source-derived identification of the exceptional and oscillator top lines has yet been constructed.

This is a clean pin rather than a failure: it states exactly what extra datum is needed.

## 9. Stop-everything and falsification gates

**Stop-everything trigger:** derive the common five-volume from the exceptional cubic and Furey/Fock top form, making the carrier map canonical modulo the faithful \(\mathbb Z_6\).

**Weak-phase trigger:** a source-derived symplectic or determinant form on the weak two-plane fixes \(\beta^2=1\) independently.

**Phase-blindness falsifier:** claim that \(H_2(\mathbb C)\) observables distinguish \(v\) from \(e^{i\theta}v\).

**Volume falsifier:** impose \(\det T=1\) without identifying normalized volume lines on both carriers.

**Kernel falsifier:** equate the six residual frame maps with the gauge kernel without including the compensating hypercharge phase.
