# D-02 bounded real-axis boundary final report

## Outcome

The boundary extension is measure-first. For a bounded operator-valued Herglotz response, the positive operator-valued representing measure is canonical; pointwise values, densities, atoms and principal values are derived projections with explicit loss and topology.

The required boundary record is:

`measure × density class × atom topology × singular-continuous part × principal value × scaling exponent × approach arc × Schur topology × inverse bound × LAP scale`.

## Strongest positive results

Scalarized nontangential boundary values exist almost everywhere. Their imaginary parts identify the absolutely continuous density. Atoms are recovered by `y Im M(lambda+i y)`, but the convergence topology must be stated.

Schur elimination commutes with a boundary limit in norm under norm convergence and an invertible limiting denominator. It commutes strongly under strong-* factor convergence and a uniform inverse bound. At such points the dissipative density transports as

`W_eff = W_A + B D_0^(-*) W_D D_0^-1 B*`.

## Strongest negative results

- Scalarized a.e. densities need not assemble into a bounded operator density.
- Strong atom recovery need not be norm recovery.
- Boundary divergence need not be an atom.
- Density and atoms can miss all singular-continuous mass.
- WOT limits do not commute with Schur products.
- One-sided strong convergence is insufficient because the adjoint factor matters.
- Strong-* convergence without a uniform inverse bound is insufficient.
- Nontangential limits do not determine arbitrary tangential limits.
- A principal value does not replace the measure.
- A generic boundary theorem is not a uniform LAP.
