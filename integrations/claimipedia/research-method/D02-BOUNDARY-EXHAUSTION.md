# D-02 bounded boundary exhaustion

## Strongest reusable theorem

For a bounded operator-valued Herglotz response on a separable Hilbert space, the positive operator-valued representing measure is the canonical real-axis object. After scalarization, nontangential boundary values exist almost everywhere. On a common full-measure set obtained from a countable dense vector family and polarization, the imaginary part identifies `pi` times the absolutely continuous Radon–Nikodym density as a scalar form.

That is not automatically a bounded operator-valued density. A WOT, SOT or norm boundary operator requires uniform boundedness and convergence in precisely that topology.

Boundary limits commute with Schur elimination in operator norm when all factors converge in norm and the limiting denominator is invertible. They commute strongly when both a coupling and its adjoint converge strongly and the denominator inverses are uniformly bounded. Weak convergence, one-sided strong convergence and pointwise invertibility without a uniform inverse bound each fail independently.

## Lossless boundary record

The dispatcher retains the representing measure, absolutely continuous density, topology-labelled atoms, singular-continuous component, principal-value real part, threshold exponent, approach region, Schur convergence topology and inverse bound. If a limiting-absorption principle is requested, it also requires the chosen weighted space or operator scale.

## Exhaustion result

Every technically actionable discriminant in the declared bounded boundary scope now has a disposition. In particular:

- density does not replace the measure;
- scalarized limits do not manufacture a bounded operator;
- strong atom recovery does not imply norm recovery;
- divergence does not imply an atom;
- zero a.e. density and zero atoms do not erase singular-continuous mass;
- WOT does not pass through a Schur product;
- nontangential limits do not control arbitrary tangential paths;
- a generic a.e. boundary theorem is not a uniform limiting-absorption principle.

The remaining frontiers are structurally new programmes: unbounded domains, model-specific LAP estimates, frequency-dependent/nonreciprocal coupling, and nonlinear/interacting response.
