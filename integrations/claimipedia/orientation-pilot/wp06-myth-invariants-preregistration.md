# WP06 — Mythic transformation invariants preregistration candidate

Status: **design candidate; not registered; proposal-only**

## Research question

Do prespecified families of narrative transformation recur across bounded myth corpora more often and in more structured sequences than expected under generic narrative, corpus-composition, descent, borrowing and coder-template controls?

The study does not test whether recurring transformations are morally true, metaphysically authoritative or evidence for one religious tradition.

## Claim separation

### O6a — Detection

Preregistered transformation families recur above specified null models in independently coded and held-out myth corpora.

### O6b — Explanation

Any detected recurrence is apportioned among:

- common descent;
- horizontal borrowing and blending;
- convergence under shared developmental, ecological or coordination constraints;
- genre and narrative-production constraints;
- archive and translation selection;
- coder-induced abstraction.

Detection does not select an explanation.

## Prior methodological evidence

Tehrani's analysis of 58 variants of ATU 333 and ATU 123 shows that cladistic, Bayesian and network methods can recover coherent tale relationships while also exposing blending, conflicting signal and uncertain classification: https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0078871

This is evidence that cultural phylogenetic analysis is feasible for bounded tale families. It is not evidence for universal Petersonian or Jungian invariants.

## Design stages

### Stage 0 — Governance and corpus rights

Before coding:

- document provenance, collector, date, language, translation history and community status for every text;
- distinguish public-domain archive access from ethical permission to universalize or republish;
- invite reviewers from represented living traditions;
- record contested translations and participant interpretations beside analytic codes;
- prohibit deficit labels for traditions that do not fit the proposed transformations.

### Stage 1 — Exploratory training corpus

Use no more than 20% of eligible texts to refine a finite codebook. Freeze the codebook, hypotheses and analysis before inspecting confirmatory outcomes.

The training corpus cannot contribute to confirmatory effect estimates.

### Stage 2 — Confirmatory corpus

Stratify sampling by language family, geographic region, collection period, genre, transmission medium and degree of documented contact. Precommit inclusion and exclusion criteria.

Unit of analysis: a dated narrative version, not an abstract tale type. Tale-family membership and version-level variation remain separate variables.

### Stage 3 — Held-out transfer corpus

Reserve traditions and genres absent from training for the strongest out-of-sample test. A codebook that fits only the traditions from which it was developed does not establish an invariant.

## Candidate transformation codebook

The confirmatory codebook may contain no more than eight transformation families. The current candidates are deliberately provisional:

1. stable order or ordinary regime;
2. anomaly, violation or insufficiency;
3. boundary crossing, descent, exile or encounter with alterity;
4. costly commitment, sacrifice, relinquishment or irreversible loss;
5. disintegration, death, defeat or identity dissolution;
6. acquisition, discovery, reconciliation or reconfiguration;
7. return, transmission or institutionalization;
8. restored, replaced or newly differentiated order.

Each family requires positive indicators, negative indicators and near-miss examples. Absence is a valid code. Coders must not force every narrative into a complete cycle.

## Rival codebooks

To test whether the proposed scheme is distinctive, compare it with:

- a generic story-grammar codebook;
- an event-valence and agency codebook;
- an ATU or motif-index-derived descriptive codebook where applicable;
- a data-driven topic or event-cluster representation trained only on the exploratory corpus.

The orientation codebook succeeds only if it adds held-out discrimination or explanatory compression beyond these rivals.

## Blinding and coding

- At least three coders per narrative.
- At least one coder with relevant linguistic or tradition-specific competence where feasible.
- Coders see the operational manual but not the target cross-cultural hypotheses.
- Reliability thresholds are preregistered per transformation rather than averaged into one permissive score.
- Low-reliability transformations are narrowed or removed before confirmatory unblinding.
- Machine-assisted coding, if used, is evaluated separately and cannot replace human disagreement records.

## Null models

N0 — Base-rate null: recurrence expected from marginal transformation frequencies.

N1 — Within-narrative permutation: preserve event counts while disrupting transformation order.

N2 — Genre-matched narrative null: apply the codebook to non-myth narratives matched for length, period and genre structure.

N3 — Descent/borrowing null: explain similarity using language-family, geography, documented contact and tale phylogeny without convergence terms.

N4 — Generic story-grammar null: test whether the proposed sequence adds anything beyond complication and resolution.

N5 — Coder-template null: estimate recurrence generated when coders apply broad orientation categories to shuffled or synthetic texts.

N6 — Archive-selection sensitivity: reweight or resample by collection source, colonial archive, translation availability and survival period.

## Primary outcomes

- out-of-sample transformation prevalence;
- sequence enrichment relative to N0–N2;
- incremental predictive or compression value beyond rival codebooks;
- variance attributable to descent, contact, region, genre and coder;
- robustness to archive and translation sensitivity analyses;
- false-positive rate on synthetic and non-myth controls.

## Analysis

- multilevel models with narratives nested in tale families, traditions and corpora;
- phylogenetic or network correction where descent data are defensible;
- permutation tests for sequence enrichment;
- model comparison for diffusion, convergence and mixed explanations;
- held-out predictive evaluation rather than in-sample thematic fit;
- full uncertainty and researcher-degree-of-freedom ledger.

No single global “invariant score” will combine unreliable or heterogeneous transformations.

## Falsifiers and subtraction rules

Narrow or retire a transformation when:

- blinded reliability remains below its preregistered threshold;
- it fails held-out discrimination from generic narrative controls;
- recurrence disappears after descent, borrowing or archive controls;
- near-miss cases require repeated post hoc exceptions;
- represented-tradition reviewers identify systematic mistranslation or category imposition;
- a simpler rival codebook matches or exceeds its performance.

Retire the strong invariant thesis if the complete orientation codebook adds no reliable out-of-sample value beyond generic story grammar and transmission history.

## Forbidden inferences

- recurrence therefore collective unconscious;
- recurrence therefore biological adaptation;
- recurrence therefore moral truth;
- recurrence therefore Christian metaphysics;
- phylogenetic persistence therefore psychological universality;
- coding reliability therefore participant-valid interpretation.

Each is a separate claim requiring additional evidence.

## Third-order audit

What this protocol makes legible: recurrence, sequence, transmission, borrowing and competing causal explanations.

What it may suppress: ritual performance, oral context, sacred restriction, polysemy, humour, embodiment and meanings available only to participants.

Authority risk: an apparently neutral codebook can universalize categories selected from the programme's own preferred mythic map.

Subtraction condition: if the orientation scheme does not outperform generic alternatives under held-out testing, retain local interpretive usefulness at most and retire the cross-cultural invariant claim.

## Completion gate

WP06 is not complete until:

1. the eligible corpus and governance process are fixed;
2. the exploratory/confirmatory/held-out split is immutable;
3. the codebook includes positive, negative and near-miss cases;
4. reliability and exclusion thresholds are numerical;
5. null models N0–N6 are executable;
6. represented-tradition review is arranged;
7. the preregistration is time-stamped before confirmatory coding.
