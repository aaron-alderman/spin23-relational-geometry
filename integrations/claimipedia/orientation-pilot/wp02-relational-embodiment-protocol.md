# WP02 relational orientation, embodiment and tacit legibility

Status: protocol candidate

## Governing distinction

The programme now separates two claims:

1. **Methodological definition (O1v2):** model orientation as a temporally extended agent–world–action relation.
2. **Constitutive hypothesis (O1c):** some orientation cannot be exhaustively realized as an internal state-estimation and policy-selection architecture.

The first is an adopted unit of analysis. The second is a live philosophical and empirical-model-comparison hypothesis. Evidence that bodies, tools or other people causally matter does not by itself establish constitution: an enriched internalist can treat each as an input, state constraint or learned transition structure.

## Rival model family

### I0 — thin internalism

Behaviour is generated by internal representations and policies while body and environment supply inputs and consequences. Coupling effects are reducible to changes in those inputs.

### I1 — enriched internal predictive control

The agent maintains learned generative and control models sensitive to bodily state, action contingencies, tools, other agents and external representations. Online dependence on the environment is expected, but cognitive organization remains internally realized.

### R1 — coupled-dynamic relation

Some task-relevant state variables are properties of the evolving agent–environment loop. Breaking action-contingent coupling can destroy an organization that is not recoverable from matched marginal inputs alone.

### R2 — socially scaffolded relation

Some orientation variables are maintained through reciprocal coordination, recognition, role and shared artifacts. Individual measurements can omit a causally organized dyadic or institutional state.

I1 is the strongest live rival. Defeating I0 is not enough.

## Primary discriminant: contingency-preserving versus contingency-breaking perturbation

Participants learn an adaptive tool task in which successful control depends on a stable mapping among action, tool dynamics and environmental response. After asymptotic performance, compare:

1. intact closed-loop coupling;
2. yoked replay preserving the marginal sensory distribution but breaking the participant's action contingency;
3. latency/phase perturbation preserving components while altering temporal relation;
4. restored coupling;
5. a novel but structurally homologous tool.

Pre-register predictions from I1 and R1 with equal parameter budgets and fit them on training trials. Score held-out perturbation, restoration and transfer trials.

Evidence favouring R1 requires all of:

- a coupling-specific loss not explained by sensory novelty, motor difficulty or generic prediction error;
- better held-out prediction by relational state variables than by the best preregistered I1 model;
- rapid restoration when the relation returns, distinguishable from relearning;
- transfer governed by functional dynamics rather than surface similarity;
- robustness across at least two task families.

If I1 matches or outperforms R1 out of sample, O1c is narrowed or retired. Parameter proliferation after inspecting failures is forbidden.

Even a victory for R1 would support a task-bounded constitutive model, not the claim that all mind is extended.

## Legibility-loss model

Articulation can help, harm or leave skilled action unchanged. Define the effect of a representational demand as:

`L = performance(with demand) - performance(matched no-demand control)`.

Negative `L` is legibility loss; positive `L` is legibility gain. Decompose the mechanism rather than treating tacitness as a sacred residue:

- **selection:** the report samples only part of the operative discrimination;
- **temporal:** serial report cannot preserve real-time coordination;
- **context:** laboratory elicitation removes cues or stakes;
- **reactivity:** attending to mechanics changes control;
- **translation:** experiential distinctions lack a stable reporting vocabulary;
- **authority:** interviewer categories displace practitioner categories;
- **reconstruction:** retrospective explanation confabulates or compresses;
- **instructional gain:** explicit structure improves planning, transfer or novice learning.

The model predicts interactions among expertise, task type, prompt, timing and pressure—not a universal cost of explicitness.

### Legibility experiment

Cross expertise (novice/expert), report demand (none/outcome-and-plan/mechanics), timing (concurrent/immediate retrospective/delayed self-confrontation) and coupling condition (stable/perturbed).

Measure:

- task success and movement/process telemetry;
- recovery after perturbation;
- transfer;
- report completeness and calibration against observable events;
- category convergence between practitioner and investigator;
- whether the report later improves teaching or self-correction.

The result is not “tacit knowledge exists” merely because reports are incomplete. It must identify when a representational demand changes performance, what information is lost or gained, and whether the effect generalizes.

## Embodied-skill case set

| Case | Relational target | Strong rival or confound | Discriminating intervention |
|---|---|---|---|
| Adaptive tool control | Functional dynamics span hand, tool and target | Internal forward model | Yoked replay, latency perturbation, homologous transfer |
| Expert acoustic diagnosis | Recognition may precede articulable features | High-dimensional internal classifier | Cue-preserving transformations, forced feature report, novel-fault transfer |
| Musical ensemble timing | Joint timing may be dyad-level | Individual prediction of partners | Partner swap, phase perturbation, solo-derived dynamic similarity |
| Skilled navigation | Landmarks and movement form an online system | Stored cognitive map plus live inputs | Landmark relocation, matched visual replay, route invention |
| Laparoscopic or craft skill | Functional outcome tolerates many joint profiles | Internal task-level control policy | Tool-dynamics change versus posture constraint |
| Collaborative external representation | Work is distributed across memory and artifact | Artifact as ordinary perceptual input | Re-encoding that preserves information but disrupts coordination |

No single case decides O1c. The set exists to test whether one relational model transfers across tasks without erasing their mechanisms.

## Evidence anchors and limits

- Zhang and Wang's external-representation experiments found that coordination between internal and external information mattered more than simply placing more information outside the head; one external encoding even impaired performance. This supports a coordination problem, not mind-extension by stipulation: https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0006513
- Nonaka and colleagues found skilled tool use was associated with regulation of task-functional dynamics rather than a specific joint-contribution profile. This motivates functional perturbations but remains compatible with internal task-level control: https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2014.00306
- Słowiński and colleagues found solo-movement dynamic similarity predicted interpersonal coordination in a mirror-game paradigm. This motivates dyadic state variables but does not establish that the dyad is a cognitive subject: https://doi.org/10.1098/rsif.2015.1093
- Zhu and colleagues found explicit sequence knowledge could enhance motor vigour and performance. This is counterevidence to any simple “explicitness disrupts skill” thesis: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4507957/
- Whitehead and colleagues found think-aloud conditions did not clearly degrade skilled golf performance and could aid planning. This constrains claims about interrogation destroying tacit knowledge: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4516813/
- Zhu and colleagues' instruction-timing experiments show effects depend on instruction focus and learning stage. This motivates interactions rather than a universal legibility cost: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3153805/

## Affected-party and third-order audit

Calling knowledge “tacit” can protect practitioners from crude extraction, but it can also protect guild mystique, unsafe routines and unaccountable authority. Calling it “measurable” can enable teaching and correction, but can also let institutions replace competence with proxies.

The programme therefore grants neither side presumptive authority. Practitioners may contest investigator categories; investigators may test practitioner claims; performance and affected-party consequences remain independent checks.

Reflexively, the programme prefers R1 because it fits its person-in-a-field vocabulary. That preference is a reason to privilege I1 in model development, penalize relational parameter expansion, and precommit to retiring O1c if it adds no transferable prediction.

## Completion gate

WP02 design completion requires:

1. frozen I1 and R1 predictions for at least two task families;
2. an operational coupling manipulation with novelty and difficulty controls;
3. a legibility protocol permitting gain, loss or null effects;
4. an embodied-skill case set with mechanism-specific rivals;
5. affected-party and reflexive audits;
6. an explicit retirement rule for O1c.
