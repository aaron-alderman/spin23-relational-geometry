# WP04 — Culture, norms, power and institutional legibility

Status: **protocol candidate; proposal-only**

## Aim

Test the bounded thesis that culture supplies socially distributed orientation scaffolding while institutions shape which behaviours, categories, experiences and corrections become consequential. “Culture is load-bearing” is treated as a family of causal hypotheses, not an explanatory stopping point.

## Claim decomposition

### D1 — Distributed orientation scaffold

Epistemic class: empirical hypothesis.

Shared practices, expectations, sanctions, roles and artefacts can reduce the individual cognitive and coordination work required to identify situations and select action.

Strong rival: the relevant effects are fully explained by incentives, information and repeated interaction without a distinct cultural construct.

Discriminant: after incentives, information and network position are represented, measures of shared normative expectations and learned practice improve prediction of coordination, interpretation or behavioural persistence.

Disposition: retain for operationalization.

### D2 — Norms are conditional and plural

Epistemic class: definition plus empirical hypothesis.

A social norm is not mere behavioural frequency. The programme will separately measure empirical expectations, normative expectations, sanction expectations, personal endorsement and observed conformity.

Strong rival: reports of normative expectation merely rationalize behaviour produced by incentives or experimenter demand.

Discriminant: independently manipulating perceived empirical and normative expectations produces separable behavioural effects.

Disposition: retain as measurement guardrail.

### D3 — Norms and institutions co-evolve

Epistemic class: empirical hypothesis.

Formal rules can alter behaviour and cultural similarity, while participant values, migration and resistance can select or reshape institutional rules.

Strong rival: observed institution–culture association is almost entirely sorting: similar people select similar institutions.

Discriminant: rule changes followed by within-community cultural change, with migration and composition modelled separately; conversely, member-value change predicts later rule change.

Disposition: retain for longitudinal testing.

### D4 — Shared categories are produced, not merely inherited

Epistemic class: empirical hypothesis.

Communication-network structure and scale can filter diverse individual classifications into stable shared categories, including categories without uniquely determined natural boundaries.

Strong rival: convergence reflects universally salient stimulus structure or task demand rather than network dynamics.

Discriminant: randomized network structure or scale changes convergence while stimuli and incentives remain fixed.

Disposition: retain with domain restriction.

### D5 — Institutional legibility is power-laden

Epistemic class: empirical and normative claim split.

- D5a: changes to categories, reporting channels, standing rules and aggregation procedures alter which experiences enter institutional records and decisions.
- D5b: institutional arrangements are normatively defective when systematically affected parties lack proportionate standing to contest consequential classifications.

Strong rivals: apparent exclusion reflects signal quality, privacy, expertise or decision cost; equal standing may reduce rather than improve decision quality.

Discriminant: compare category or standing-rule changes on error detection, appeal outcomes, unreported harms, decision latency and distribution of correction costs.

Disposition: split; D5a retain for testing, D5b retain as contestable governance proposal.

## Evidence ledger

1. Andrighetto et al., “Evidence from a long-term experiment that collective risks change social norms and promote cooperation,” *Nature Communications* 12, 5452 (2021), https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-021-25734-w
   - Supports: norms can be measured through empirical and normative expectations; a 30-day randomized collective-risk environment changed norm strength and cooperation.
   - Does not support: a general claim that threat always produces beneficial cultural tightness, or that experimental groups model institutions at civilizational scale.

2. Zhong and Frey, “Institutional similarity drives cultural similarity among online communities,” *Scientific Reports* 12, 18982 (2022), https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-022-23223-8
   - Supports: longitudinal online-community data can separate some institutional, cultural and migration dynamics; institutional similarity predicted cultural similarity in that domain.
   - Does not support: universal top-down institutional determination; online migration costs and administrator powers differ from many offline institutions.

3. Guilbeault, Baronchelli and Centola, “Experimental evidence for scale-induced category convergence across populations,” *Nature Communications* 12, 327 (2021), https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-020-20037-y
   - Supports: randomized network size can change convergence of categories for novel ambiguous stimuli.
   - Does not support: accuracy, justice or naturalness of converged categories; convergence can stabilize arbitrary classifications.

These studies establish tractable mechanisms and designs, not the entire normative-exoskeleton thesis.

## Study portfolio

### Study 04.1 — Norm-component factorial experiment

Manipulate empirical expectation, normative expectation and sanction expectation independently in a repeated coordination task. Measure behaviour, personal endorsement, perceived legitimacy and persistence after the manipulation is removed.

Primary test: whether the three expectation components have separable effects and whether personal endorsement mediates retention.

No-go: collapse the components if they cannot be measured with acceptable discriminant validity.

### Study 04.2 — Rule–culture longitudinal panel

Use communities with time-stamped rule changes, membership flows and behavioural traces. Model rule change, cultural similarity, migration and administrator turnover as separate processes.

Primary test: within-community change following rules, plus reverse-path prediction from member values to later rules.

No-go: do not infer causation from cross-sectional similarity.

### Study 04.3 — Category convergence under asymmetric authority

Extend a grouping-game design with randomized population size, communication topology and unequal category-setting authority.

Outcomes: convergence, accuracy where ground truth exists, minority-category survival, appeal success, correction latency and confidence.

Primary test: whether authoritative convergence improves coordination while suppressing recoverable minority information.

### Study 04.4 — Institutional correction-channel audit

Select bounded institutions with available decision and appeal records. Trace one consequential category from intake through classification, aggregation, decision, appeal and retention.

Compare official records with participant accounts without assuming either is complete. Record what is lost at every translation.

Primary test: whether changes in standing and reporting rules alter discovery of independently corroborated error.

### Study 04.5 — Norm-removal or disruption cases

Identify natural experiments where a stable norm or supporting artefact rapidly disappears while incentives are partly stable. Test whether coordination costs, ambiguity, enforcement burden or explicit rule production increase.

Primary test: a load-bearing norm should generate measurable substitution costs when removed.

## Measures

- empirical, normative and sanction expectations;
- behavioural conformity and deviation;
- personal endorsement;
- coordination success and error rate;
- enforcement and explanation cost;
- migration, exit and participation;
- category convergence and minority-information retention;
- correction-channel availability and usage;
- appeal success and reversal rate;
- distribution of reporting and correction labour;
- decision latency;
- durable retention after personnel change.

## Required perspectives

- institutional administrator;
- ordinary participant;
- affected non-participant;
- dissenter or appellant;
- independent domain expert;
- analyst of material incentives and power.

## Third-order audit

What this protocol makes legible: distributed norm components, formal-rule effects, classification dynamics and correction standing.

What it may suppress: aesthetic, sacred, historical and embodied dimensions of culture that do not reduce to coordination measures.

Authority risk: researchers define both “error” and “affected party,” potentially recreating the official reality they intend to audit.

Subtraction condition: narrow “culture as normative exoskeleton” to specific mechanisms if the cultural variables add no prediction beyond incentives, information and networks.

## Completion gate

WP04 is complete only when:

1. D1–D5 have machine-readable claim packets;
2. at least one study is specified sufficiently for preregistration;
3. power and material-interest rivals are present in every institutional model;
4. affected-party standing is operationalized rather than merely praised;
5. at least one result could narrow or defeat the normative-exoskeleton thesis.
