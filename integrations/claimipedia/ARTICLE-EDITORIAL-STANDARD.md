# Claimipedia article editorial standard

Claimipedia articles are standalone syntheses. Each published revision must present one coherent argument in its current form, without requiring the reader to reconstruct the chronology of prompts, additions, objections, or earlier drafts.

## Integration rule

New material must be placed where it belongs in the argument's dependency structure. The revision should update the abstract, definitions, transitions, formal summary, conclusion, and limitations wherever the new claim changes them. Superseded or duplicated wording should be removed. An addition must not be appended merely because it arrived later.

The resulting article should read as if it were composed in one pass for a reader encountering the subject for the first time.

## Kenotic presentation rule

The presentation should embody the same non-dominating discipline that the content attributes to good inquiry:

- distinguish argument, evidence, analogy, speculation, and confession;
- expose adverse evidence and genuine alternatives;
- preserve room for rational disagreement;
- avoid using volume, repetition, process history, or unexplained authority to overwhelm the reader;
- state scope and uncertainty beside the claims they qualify.

Kenotic presentation does not weaken an argument. It makes the argument's authority proportional to what its reasons can support.

## Warning requirement

If a page intentionally preserves chronological layers, unresolved draft accretions, or a presentation that does not satisfy the standalone and kenotic rules, it must display a prominent warning before the abstract or opening argument:

> **Warning: This page preserves an accretive argument history and is not a standalone synthesis. Later sections may revise or supersede earlier claims.**

The warning must remain until the page is rewritten into an integrated standalone form. A changelog or revision history may accompany an article, but it must remain separate from the article's argument.
