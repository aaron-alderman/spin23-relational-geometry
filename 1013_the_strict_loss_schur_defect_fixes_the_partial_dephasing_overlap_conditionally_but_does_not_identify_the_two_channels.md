## Executive result

The partial-dephasing overlap \(s\) from document 1012 can be matched exactly to the scalar strict-loss response already derived in document 961, provided one makes a specific identification of environment defect norms.

For

\[
f(\omega)=
\left(\frac{\omega-i\alpha}{\omega+i\beta}\right)^2,
\qquad
0<\alpha\le\beta,
\]

the transfer magnitude is

\[
|f(\omega)|
=\frac{\omega^2+\alpha^2}{\omega^2+\beta^2}.
\]

If the distinguishability of the two face-conditioned environment states is identified with the Schur loss defect,

\[
1-s^2=1-|f|^2,
\]

then positivity selects

\[
\boxed{
s(\omega)=|f(\omega)|
=\frac{\omega^2+\alpha^2}{\omega^2+\beta^2}.}
\]

The sixth-harmonic locking scale becomes

\[
\boxed{
\kappa_{\rm eff}(\omega)
=\kappa\left(
\frac{\omega^2+\alpha^2}
{\omega^2+\beta^2}
\right)^6.}
\]

This is an exact parameter bridge, not yet an environment identification. Strict amplitude loss and population-preserving phase damping are different channels. Equality of their scalar defect norms is necessary for the proposed identification but not sufficient to prove a common microscopic dilation.

## 1. Exact defect identity

Document 961 proved

\[
1-|f|^2
=\frac{(\beta^2-\alpha^2)
\bigl(2\omega^2+\alpha^2+\beta^2\bigr)}
{(\omega^2+\beta^2)^2}.
\]

Directly,

\[
1-\left(
\frac{\omega^2+\alpha^2}
{\omega^2+\beta^2}
\right)^2
=\frac{(\beta^2-\alpha^2)
\bigl(2\omega^2+\alpha^2+\beta^2\bigr)}
{(\omega^2+\beta^2)^2}.
\]

Thus the partial-dephasing determinant factor \(1-s^2\) can equal the already established response defect exactly.

## 2. Hyperbolic loss parameter

Using

\[
\alpha=\gamma e^{-\ell},
\qquad
\beta=\gamma e^{\ell},
\qquad
\ell\ge0,
\]

gives at zero frequency

\[
s(0)=\frac{\alpha^2}{\beta^2}=e^{-4\ell}.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{s(0)^6=e^{-24\ell}.}
\]

Even moderate hyperbolic loss strongly suppresses the phase-locking term at low frequency.

For strict loss \(\ell>0\) and finite real \(\omega\),

\[
0<s(\omega)<1.
\]

Thus the complete real-frequency response lies inside the determinant/coherence compatibility window. As \(|\omega|\to\infty\), \(s\to1\) and the defect tends to zero.

## 3. Frequency-dependent Jordan state

Under the conditional identification, the mixed response matrix is

\[
\rho(\omega)=
\begin{pmatrix}
|a|^2&|f(\omega)|a\bar b\\
|f(\omega)|b\bar a&|b|^2
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Its determinant is

\[
\det\rho(\omega)
=\bigl(1-|f(\omega)|^2\bigr)|a|^2|b|^2.
\]

The same positive polynomial that controls the dissipative Cayley density therefore controls the Jordan interior depth, while the surviving transfer magnitude controls the cubic phase visibility.

This gives a precise version of a useful physical sentence:

> The environment defect creates Jordan volume, while the surviving coherent amplitude carries orientation.

## 4. Why the channels are not yet identified

The scalar strict-loss transfer sends observed amplitude into an outgoing port. Its observed norm is reduced.

The phase-damping channel of document 1012 preserves both face populations and total trace. It only reduces off-diagonal coherence.

These maps have different actions on diagonal states. They cannot be declared equal because they share the same number \(1-|f|^2\).

A common microscopic realization would require a larger typed scattering matrix in which:

- the observed transfer block has amplitude \(f(\omega)\);
- face-conditioned environment states have overlap \(|f(\omega)|\);
- the relevant normalized or postselected reduced state preserves the face populations;
- placement, deck and Real actions extend consistently to the ports.

No such joint dilation has yet been constructed.

## 5. Two interpretations that remain open

### A. Normalized conditional response

Condition on survival in the observed sector, normalize the two face populations, and interpret loss-induced environment distinguishability as dephasing. This can yield \(s=|f|\), but conditioning is nonlinear at the state level and must be justified operationally.

### B. Separate channels with a shared spectral density

Treat strict loss and phase damping as different reduced channels generated by one bath spectral density. Their defect magnitudes may coincide while their system coupling operators differ. This is more conservative but adds a coupling-structure burden.

## 6. Stop-everything and falsification tests

**Joint-dilation trigger:** construct one unitary input-output matrix whose two reductions give the established strict-loss transfer and the phase-damping overlap \(s=|f|\).

**Bath trigger:** derive both channels from one spectral density and typed pair of system operators, with the same positive defect polynomial.

**Frequency trigger:** independently measure or calculate coherence visibility and verify

\[
s(\omega)=\frac{\omega^2+\alpha^2}{\omega^2+\beta^2}.
\]

**Channel falsifier:** the microscopic loss process changes face populations asymmetrically or produces an overlap unrelated to \(|f|\).

**Phase falsifier:** the environment overlap is complex with a non-removable frequency-dependent phase; then a real scalar \(s\) is insufficient.

**Suppression trigger:** a physical phase gap follows the predicted sixth power of the independently determined visibility.

**Overclaim warning:** the equality \(1-s^2=1-|f|^2\) is a conditional norm matching, not proof that loss is dephasing.
