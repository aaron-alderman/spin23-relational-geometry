# Degree eight can select noncommutation, but CP orientation first appears in the degree-twelve Jarlskog commutator

## Main result

The degree-eight commutator potential reopens nontrivial mixing, but it does not by itself orient the sign of flavour CP violation.

For

\[
A=Y_eY_e^\dagger,
\qquad
B=Y_\nu Y_\nu^\dagger,
\]

the degree-eight invariant

\[
\|[A,B]\|_{\rm HS}^2
\]

is CP even. It has the same value for a mixing matrix \(U\) and its complex conjugate \(U^*\). It can favour noncommuting frames and nonzero mixing angles, but it cannot distinguish the two CP-conjugate orientations.

The first standard weak-basis pseudoscalar made from two Hermitian flavour tensors is

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal J_{AB}
=i\operatorname{Tr}([A,B]^3).
}
\]

It contains three copies of \(A\) and three copies of \(B\). Since each is quadratic in a Yukawa field, \(\mathcal J_{AB}\) has total Yukawa degree twelve.

## 1. CP conjugation

In a basis where \(A\) is diagonal and real, CP conjugation sends

\[
U\longmapsto U^*,
\qquad
B\longmapsto B^*.
\]

Therefore

\[
\|[A,B^*]\|_{\rm HS}^2
=\|[A,B]\|_{\rm HS}^2,
\]

while

\[
i\operatorname{Tr}([A,B^*]^3)
=-i\operatorname{Tr}([A,B]^3).
\]

Any CP-even potential assigns equal energy to the two orientations. It may admit spontaneous CP violation, but the vacua occur in conjugate pairs unless an additional CP-odd datum biases them.

## 2. Relation to the Jarlskog invariant

For nondegenerate spectra,

\[
i\operatorname{Tr}([A,B]^3)
\propto
J(U)
\prod_{i<j}(a_i-a_j)
\prod_{k<\ell}(b_k-b_\ell),
\]

where \(J(U)\) is the rephasing-invariant Jarlskog quartet. Consequently the pseudoscalar vanishes when:

- either spectrum is degenerate;
- any required mixing angle vanishes;
- the physical CP phase is \(0\) or \(\pi\).

This is exactly the behaviour required of an oriented three-family quantity. A two-family mixing plane can be noncommuting but cannot carry a Jarlskog volume.

## 3. Three distinct achievements

The flavour-selection problem therefore separates into three levels:

\[
\begin{array}{lll}
\text{degree }4\text{--}6 &:& \text{relative alignment or flat directions},\\
\text{degree }8 &:& \text{noncommuting mixing magnitude can be selected},\\
\text{degree }12 &:& \text{an oriented CP-odd family pseudoscalar first appears}.
\end{array}
\]

These are polynomial-degree thresholds, not predictions that nature uses one operator at each threshold.

## 4. Consequence for a CP-symmetric action

If the microscopic action is CP symmetric, a term linear in \(\mathcal J_{AB}\) is forbidden. The action may contain

\[
\mathcal J_{AB}^2,
\]

but that is degree twenty-four in Yukawa fields and selects only the magnitude of the CP orientation. The two signs remain degenerate.

A lower-order way to orient the sign is to introduce a CP-odd field or spurion \(\chi\):

\[
V_{\rm orient}=\chi\,\mathcal J_{AB}.
\]

If \(\chi\) is dynamical and the full action is CP even, its two conjugate vacua remain paired. If \(\chi\) is externally signed, the sign has been supplied rather than derived.

## 5. Connection to the projector response

A nonzero \(\mathcal J_{AB}\) proves that \(A\) and \(B\) define an oriented three-family frame. It can therefore supply information that a single projector, pure \(\mathbb Z_3\) BF sector, or one-plane commutator vacuum cannot retain.

But it still does not automatically choose which eigenline becomes \(P\). The response must include a CP-even Hermitian operator built from \(A\) and \(B\), while \(\mathcal J_{AB}\) distinguishes the orientation of the resulting frame. Selection of a line and orientation of the full frame are related but separate tasks.

## Revised frontier

The first viable two-Yukawa programme is now layered:

1. degree-eight invariants generate a stable noncommuting frame;
2. degree-twelve pseudoscalar data detect its CP orientation;
3. the projector response selects one eigenline of a derived Hermitian combination;
4. a CP-symmetric theory must explain spontaneous selection between the conjugate vacua.

The next exact task is to classify the degree-eight Hermitian response basis together with the degree-twelve pseudoscalar and determine whether one bounded potential can simultaneously produce three nonzero angles, nonzero \(|J|\), and an isolated projector fixed point.
