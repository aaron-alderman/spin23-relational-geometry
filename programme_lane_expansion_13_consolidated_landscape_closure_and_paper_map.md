## Executive verdict

The nine-lane expansion was worthwhile. It did not rescue the missing fundamental bridge by changing mathematical language. It did something more useful: it separated structures that are robust, structures that are generic, and structures that still require new physics.

The strongest common result is operational:

\[
\boxed{
\text{one response gives a cone; multiple controlled responses can give a frame, an orientation and falsifiable invariants.}
}
\]

That hierarchy now appears independently in measurement theory, finite-state geometry, defect response and a literal mechanical analogue.

The honest closure verdict remains negative. None of the nine lanes presently derives all of:

1. the physical Standard-Model source protocols;
2. their normalization;
3. their orientation relative to the topology-first construction;
4. a non-generic observable fixed without refitting.

The work therefore changes the programme from “search for another algebra that makes the answer inevitable” to “identify which operational families carry stable information, then ask whether the Standard Model supplies them.”

## What genuinely joined across lanes

### 1. A preparation hierarchy

L1 and L7 give the same hierarchy in different languages:

\[
\text{one preparation: scale/axis}
\longrightarrow
\text{two: relative frame/plane}
\longrightarrow
\text{three: handedness}.
\]

This is not yet a fundamental law. It is a reusable design principle for experiments and calculations.

### 2. Topology migrates when positivity is imposed

The complex selector can carry determinant winding. A strictly positive real response cannot carry that winding without a zero or singularity. What may survive is the sign holonomy of a gapped real eigenline. Thus the invariant changes category:

\[
\text{complex winding}
\not\equiv
\text{positive-response determinant winding},
\qquad
\text{but may descend to a real }\mathbb Z_2\text{ eigenline parity}.
\]

This is a clearer continuation than attempting to force the original invariant through the positive cone unchanged.

### 3. Chirality, orientation and face response are distinct

L3 and L4 show that an index-protected chiral wall state can coexist with:

- an orientation-even spin-summed face response;
- a profile-dependent face eigenvector;
- a remote mirror mode in a finite slab.

Boundary geometry therefore supplies a place where the structures meet, not an automatic identification of them.

### 4. Generic analogues are controls, not objections

The spring network demonstrates the value of the user's landscape-first rule. Genericity does not make an effect useless. It tells us how to build a control experiment and which observations cannot support an ontological claim.

The analogue can validate calibration, reversal and tomography protocols. It also prevents the programme from treating a generic two-port sign flip as evidence for (E_8), Furey matter or (Spin(2,3)).

## Paper-worthiness

### 1. Best present paper-shaped cluster

**Calibration, state frames and topology in passive two-channel response** combines L1, L7, L8 and L9.

Its coherent contribution would be:

- the calibration hierarchy for one, two and three responses;
- the ordered-state construction of a real plane and handedness;
- the distinction between determinant winding and real eigenline holonomy;
- a three-resonator experimental null model.

This is paper-shaped now, but it still needs a focused novelty audit against inverse problems, polarimetry, real-band topology and network synthesis, plus either a bench experiment or a less elementary theorem. The present individual ingredients are mostly standard; the value lies in their integration and falsification architecture.

### 2. Strongest physics paper trajectory

**The Standard-Model current plane under Yukawa breaking and on-shell calibration** combines L2 and L6.

It becomes a real physics paper if the full one-loop operator mixing or renormalized form factors are computed. At present the exact classical quotient, spurion obstruction and projective cross-ratios are good foundations, not a complete paper claim.

### 3. Boundary/lattice paper trajectory

**Boundary chirality does not automatically orient a two-face response** combines L3 and L4.

The core distinction is sharp. Publication strength requires a gauge-complete finite-slab model, an overlap/domain-wall numerical study or an explicit allowed interaction that transmits helicity to the face carrier.

### 4. Specialist research note

**Furey TROs versus finite spectral geometry** is not ready as a full claim. The naive obstruction is elementary. The publishable object would be the complete minimal bimodule/Krajewski classification and its moduli count.

## Priority order

## Concrete go/no-go programme

The next phase should contain four bounded projects rather than nine indefinitely open lanes.

### Project A: three-preparation response tomography

Define the equivalence group of the apparatus, measure the two-response generalized spectrum and the three-state handedness scalar, then reverse an independent physical orientation.

**Go:** the sign relation survives every allowed recalibration.  
**No-go:** it depends on ordering, labelling or a forbidden choice of basis.

### Project B: full Standard-Model vector-operator mixing

Enumerate the operator basis, choose a scheme and compute the one-loop matrix including Higgs and equation-of-motion directions.

**Go:** a distinguished quotient or eigenframe survives.  
**No-go:** the candidate plane is an arbitrary scale- and scheme-dependent slice.

### Project C: gauge-complete finite wall

Measure Ward residuals, mirror contamination, helicity and two-face response while refining the lattice and varying wall profiles.

**Go:** the same bulk datum fixes chirality and the physical face orientation.  
**No-go:** orientation continues to require an independent boundary coupling.

### Project D: Furey-arrow finite geometry

Enumerate the smallest real graded bimodule representations, solve the axioms and count all surviving coefficients.

**Go:** one Furey TRO is selected and its physical moduli are reduced.  
**No-go:** the construction only repackages arbitrary Standard-Model Dirac data.

## Final expansive reading

The most charitable expansive reading is not that one algebra has won. It is that the same physical system may admit several useful shadows:

- Lie algebras describe infinitesimal symmetry directions;
- Jordan cones describe positive observables and response;
- TROs and bimodules describe directed matter links;
- modular data describe algebra together with preparation;
- defects describe globally twisted families;
- networks describe how hidden degrees of freedom appear at ports.

An (E_8)-like object could still be a large organizing “crystal” whose different sections display these shadows. Nothing in the completed lane work establishes that ontology, and nothing here rules it out. The epistemic lesson is narrower: changing algebraic language does not remove the need to derive the preparation, action, normalization and observable map.

Spin(2,3) remains an unusually productive junction because it touches two-port symplectic geometry, (H_2(\mathbb R)), causal response and several topological constructions. The analogue result also shows why that junction is not, by itself, evidence of fundamentality.

## Post-completion novelty correction

The subsequent literature audit found a direct October 2026 overlap with the ordered-state orientation result: Tao, Wu, Xie and Zhang identify the imaginary third-order Bargmann invariant with qubit orientation and develop the corresponding conversion theory. The L1+L7+L8+L9 cluster is therefore not a standalone mathematical novelty in its present form.

The exact remaining gap is the mismatch between unitary qubit equivalence and (GL_2) response congruence. Under the latter, response noncommutativity has an invariant magnitude but no invariant sign unless a physical handedness reference excludes reflections. The revised audit and certificate are in [Lane Expansion 14](programme_lane_expansion_14_novelty_audit_the_bargmann_result_preempts_the_three_state_theorem_but_not_the_gl2_bridge.md).
