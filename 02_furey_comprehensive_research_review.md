# Cohl Furey's division-algebra programme: comprehensive research review

**Prepared:** 16 October 2026  
**Scope:** the particle-physics programme from ideals and division algebras through the July 2026 nested-embedding paper.  
**Terminology:** recent papers are published under N. Furey; earlier work often appears under C. Furey.

## 1. The question driving the programme

The Standard Model works extraordinarily well but takes much of its internal structure as input: the gauge group, chiral representations, hypercharges, three generations, Higgs representation and Yukawa parameters. Furey's programme asks whether a small algebraic seed can make some of those choices inevitable.

The seed is built from the four finite-dimensional normed division algebras over \(\mathbb R\):

\[
\mathbb R,\qquad \mathbb C,\qquad \mathbb H,\qquad \mathbb O.
\]

Their dimensions \(1,2,4,8\), their automorphism/triality structures and the Clifford algebras generated by their left actions repeatedly reproduce representation spaces used in particle physics. The long-running core object is some variant of

\[
\mathbb A=\mathbb R\otimes\mathbb C\otimes\mathbb H\otimes\mathbb O,
\]

or its complexified/octonionic components and multiplication algebras.

The programme's strongest results are exact algebraic decompositions: particular ideals or modules transform like Standard Model multiplets under particular symmetry subalgebras. Its central unfinished task is dynamics: a local Lorentzian quantum field theory with a kinetic operator, interactions, masses, mixing, quantisation and experimentally distinctive predictions.

## 2. Intellectual lineage

Furey's work does not begin from an empty field. Relevant predecessors include:

- the relation between Clifford algebras and spinors;
- Günaydin and Gürsey's split-octonion description of colour and quark structure;
- Dixon's use of \(\mathbb C\otimes\mathbb H\otimes\mathbb O\);
- octonionic descriptions of \(G_2\), \(SU(3)\), \(Spin(8)\) triality and exceptional groups;
- the use of minimal left ideals as algebraic spinor spaces;
- Pati–Salam and \(Spin(10)\) grand-unified representations.

Furey's distinctive contribution is to turn division-algebra multiplication into explicit ladder systems, ideals and stabiliser/intersection conditions that closely reproduce Standard Model representation data.

## 3. Phase I: unified ideals and Lorentz representations

### 3.1 *Towards a unified theory of ideals* (2010/2012)

The early project studies \(\mathbb C\otimes\mathbb H\) and \(\mathbb C\otimes\mathbb O\) acting on themselves. The conceptual move is that particle states should not be arbitrary vectors labelled after the fact; they should arise as invariant or minimally invariant subspaces—ideals—under algebraic action.

For complex quaternions,

\[
\mathbb C\otimes\mathbb H\cong M_2(\mathbb C),
\]

so their action naturally produces two-component spinorial structures. Furey distinguishes several invariant actions using complex, quaternionic and Hermitian conjugations. Generalised ideals under these actions reproduce Weyl, Dirac and Majorana transformation behaviour and familiar bilinears.

**Established content:** the matrix/Clifford identifications and representation transformations.  
**Interpretive step:** calling the resulting ideal elements physical fermions.  
**Missing:** a spacetime-dependent field action that selects these modules and supplies propagation.

## 4. Phase II: one generation from complex octonions

### 4.1 *Charge quantization from a number operator* and the 2016 thesis

The complex octonions \(\mathbb C\otimes\mathbb O\) are non-associative, so one works carefully with **octonionic chains**—compositions of left-multiplication maps. These maps form an associative algebra isomorphic to a complex Clifford algebra. A chosen maximally totally isotropic subspace supplies three anticommuting lowering operators \(\alpha_i\) and their adjoints:

\[
\{\alpha_i,\alpha_j\}=0,\qquad
\{\alpha_i^\dagger,\alpha_j^\dagger\}=0,\qquad
\{\alpha_i,\alpha_j^\dagger\}=\delta_{ij}.
\]

From

\[
\omega=\alpha_1\alpha_2\alpha_3,
\qquad P=\omega\omega^\dagger,
\]

one builds a minimal left ideal

\[
S^u=\mathrm{Cl}(6)P
=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb C}\{P,\alpha_i^\dagger P,
\alpha_i^\dagger\alpha_j^\dagger P,
\alpha_1^\dagger\alpha_2^\dagger\alpha_3^\dagger P\}.
\]

Its \(1+3+3+1\) decomposition is identified with a neutrino-like singlet, an anti-down-like colour triplet, an up-like colour triplet and a positron-like singlet. The conjugate ideal supplies the opposite charges. The number operator

\[
N=\sum_{i=1}^{3}\alpha_i^\dagger\alpha_i,
\qquad Q=\frac{N}{3}
\]

has eigenvalues \(0,1/3,2/3,1\) on the successive exterior degrees. This exactly mirrors the electric charges in one particle/antiparticle half-generation, subject to the convention used to name the states.

The transformations preserving the ladder system's maximally totally isotropic structure give

\[
\mathfrak{su}(3)_c\oplus\mathfrak u(1)_{em}.
\]

This is a substantive result: charge quantisation and colour representations arise from one fermionic Fock-like construction rather than being independently assigned.

### 4.2 What is and is not derived

Derived algebraically:

- an eight-complex-dimensional minimal ideal;
- the correct \(1\oplus3\oplus\bar3\oplus1\)-type pattern;
- a number operator with the relevant fractional charge spectrum;
- colour and electromagnetic stabiliser actions.

Not derived:

- why that particular complex structure/MTIS is the physical one;
- local gauge fields and their Yang–Mills action;
- chirality of the full quark sector;
- weak isospin for a complete generation;
- masses, CKM/PMNS mixing, coupling constants or scattering amplitudes.

The expression “neutrino as vacuum” is a Fock-space analogy inside the ideal. It does not say the physical neutrino is the quantum vacuum of the universe.

## 5. Phase III: electroweak chirality

In the leptonic construction, right multiplication by complex quaternionic elements enlarges the relevant associative operator algebra. A minimal-ideal/MTIS stabiliser argument yields an \(SU(2)\) action that acts nontrivially only on left-handed states, accompanied by a hypercharge-like \(U(1)\).

The important aim is to explain parity violation structurally rather than imposing “\(SU(2)_L\) acts only on the left” as a label. The construction demonstrates this for a leptonic model.

The limitation is equally important: a successful leptonic representation model is not yet the full electroweak Standard Model. One must obtain all quark representations, the correct hypercharges, Higgs doublet, gauge connection, anomaly cancellation and Yukawa interactions in one consistent construction.

## 6. Phase IV: three generations in \(\mathbb C\ell(6)\)

### 6.1 *Generations: Three Prints, in Colour*

The associative algebra generated by complex-octonionic left chains is \(64\)-complex-dimensional:

\[
\mathbb C\ell(6)\cong M_8(\mathbb C).
\]

Under a selected \(SU(3)_c\) action, it decomposes into generators plus multiple triplets, antitriplets and singlets. The multiplicities mirror three generations of colour representations. Complex conjugation relates particle and antiparticle assignments.

### 6.2 *Three generations, two unbroken gauge symmetries, and one eight-dimensional algebra*

The later refinement incorporates the electromagnetic \(U(1)\), identifying \(48\) states with the colour and electric-charge behaviour of three generations of quarks and leptons, alongside complexified \(SU(3)\) generators.

This is stronger than observing that an algebra happens to be dimension 64: the subspaces are explicitly transformed by chosen generators and carry the desired charges. Yet representation multiplicity alone does not explain flavour masses or why the weak interaction mixes those generations as observed. A basis of three copies is not automatically the physical electron/muon/tau basis.

## 7. Phase V: division-algebraic symmetry breaking

Furey and Hughes recast familiar grand-unified symmetry reductions as the selection of compatible complex structures sourced successively by \(\mathbb O\), \(\mathbb H\) and \(\mathbb C\). Their 2022 result organises the cascade

\[
\mathrm{Spin}(10)
\longrightarrow SU(4)\times SU(2)_L\times SU(2)_R
\longrightarrow SU(3)_c\times SU(2)_L\times SU(2)_R\times U(1)_{B-L}
\]

\[
\longrightarrow SU(3)_c\times SU(2)_L\times U(1)_Y
\longrightarrow SU(3)_c\times U(1)_Q,
\]

with global quotients suppressed. They also obtain left-right-symmetric Higgs representations from quaternionic triality that reduce to the familiar Higgs representation after \(SU(2)_R\) breaking.

This is best understood as an algebraic origin for a **pattern of symmetry reduction**. It is not spontaneous symmetry breaking until a dynamical potential has a vacuum, excitations and an energy hierarchy that realise the pattern.

## 8. Phase VI: the Algebraic Roadmap and intersections

The three-part *Algebraic Roadmap of Particle Theories* reorganises the model around multivector fermions and algebraic compatibility. Part III shows that requiring three \(\mathfrak{so}(10)\) actions to agree on a fixed space gives the intersection

\[
\mathfrak{so}(10)\longrightarrow
\mathfrak{su}(3)_c\oplus\mathfrak{su}(2)_L\oplus\mathfrak u(1)_Y.
\]

Adding quaternionic and complex compatibility leads to a five-way intersection selecting the post-Higgs algebra

\[
\mathfrak{su}(3)_c\oplus\mathfrak u(1)_Q.
\]

The conceptual theme is “symmetry by agreement”: a large candidate algebra is reduced not by an arbitrary subgroup choice but by simultaneous preservation of multiple algebraic structures. The analogy with

\[
\mathfrak{so}(8)\longrightarrow\mathfrak g_2
\]

through coincident triality actions motivates the construction.

This is potentially valuable for Aaron's sector-selection idea: observed symmetry may be an intersection of structures that remain mutually admissible. But an algebraic intersection is a kinematic constraint, not yet an interaction-selected statistical phase.

## 9. Phase VII: three generations and three triality slots

The 2024 Furey–Hughes paper works with

\[
\mathbb C\otimes\mathbb H\otimes\mathbb O
\]

and the triality triple

\[
(\Psi_+,\Psi_-,V).
\]

The internal Standard Model algebra is identified inside

\[
\mathfrak{tri}(\mathbb C)\oplus
\mathfrak{tri}(\mathbb H)\oplus
\mathfrak{tri}(\mathbb O).
\]

Two spinor slots \(\Psi_+\) and \(\Psi_-\) provide irreducible representations for two generations. Through a defined Cartan factorisation, the vector slot \(V\) supplies representations associated with a third generation in a scalar-boson organisation that includes a Standard Model Higgs representation.

This is a much more specific triality proposal than the slogan “three generations because triality has three slots.” The nondegenerate trilinear form relates the slots, and multiplication supplies a factorisation map. The surprising feature is that the third slot is not simply a third identical fermion copy; division-algebraic multiplication reorganises it into scalar representations.

Open physical questions include:

- how all three observed generations remain fermionic asymptotic states;
- whether the scalar reorganisation supplies Yukawa fields, composites or only representation equivalence;
- how observed mass hierarchy and mixing emerge;
- whether triality-related couplings remain compatible with Lorentz spin statistics.

## 10. Phase VIII: a \(\mathbb Z_2^5\)-graded Jordan superalgebra

The 2025 preprint *A Superalgebra Within* assembles representations resembling Standard Model gauge bosons and three fermion generations—except irreducible representations involving the top quark—into a graded algebra isomorphic to

\[
H_{16}(\mathbb C),
\]

the Euclidean Jordan algebra of \(16\times16\) Hermitian complex matrices. Division-algebraic substructure gives a proposed factorisation of internal and spatial symmetries and a \(\mathbb Z_2^5\) grading. The compatible internal algebra contains

\[
\mathfrak{su}(3)_c\oplus\mathfrak{su}(2)_L\oplus\mathfrak u(1)_Y
\]

plus four additional \(\mathfrak u(1)\) factors, while the spatial side contains multiple \(\mathfrak{so}(3)\) copies.

This marks a shift from “states are ideals” toward “particle representations collectively close into a larger product structure.” The paper is explicitly non-relativistic in character and suggests a possible quantum-information bridge. The absence of top-related irreps and a relativistic action are material incompletenesses, not cosmetic details.

## 11. Phase IX: nested embeddings (July 2026)

The latest paper considered here uses

\[
\mathbb O\oplus\mathbb H\oplus\mathbb C\oplus\mathbb R
\]

as a module for its multiplication algebra. It defines “endomorphic models of particle physics” and obtains pre- and post-Higgs internal symmetries by annihilating selected highest-grade/volume elements and imposing an equal-trace condition on anti-Hermitian operators. The hypercharge and electric charge operators acquire the schematic form

\[
Y,Q\sim\sum_n\frac1n I_{n\times n}.
\]

With auxiliary imaginary units, the 15-real-dimensional direct sum embeds into several 16-dimensional algebras \(\mathbb V\). For the sedenions, the nested sequence becomes a Cayley–Dickson tower,

\[
\mathbb R\subset\mathbb C\subset\mathbb H\subset\mathbb O\subset\mathbb S.
\]

The multiplication algebra is related to

\[
\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb R}(\mathbb V)\simeq Cl(0,8),
\]

connecting the work to Bott-periodic particle-physics ideas. The paper also raises, rather than establishes, a possible relation between multiple complex structures and baryon asymmetry.

The main conceptual advance is that the Standard Model symmetry algebra emerges as the endomorphisms compatible with a nested internal architecture, not solely as the automorphism group of one algebra.

## 12. What the programme has genuinely achieved

1. It supplies explicit algebraic homes for Lorentz spinors and internal particle multiplets.
2. It produces the colour/electric-charge pattern of a generation from a fermionic ladder system.
3. It explains fractional charge as the spectrum of a normalised number operator within that model.
4. It finds three-generation multiplicities under unbroken gauge symmetries in complex-octonionic chain algebras.
5. It gives algebraic mechanisms—complex-structure selection and intersections—for reducing larger symmetries to Standard Model symmetries.
6. It uses full triality machinery to make a specific three-slot generation proposal.
7. It searches for closure of particle representations inside a graded Jordan algebra.
8. It progressively reduces ad hoc labelling: the goal is not only to embed the Standard Model somewhere large, but to characterise why a small subalgebra survives.

## 13. What remains missing for a physical theory

### 13.1 Dynamics

There is no accepted single local action whose Euler–Lagrange equations realise the complete programme. One needs kinetic terms, covariant derivatives, a Higgs potential, vacuum selection, propagators and interactions.

### 13.2 Quantisation and unitarity

The algebraic Fock analogy is not canonical quantisation of spacetime fields. A physical Hilbert space, positive inner product, Hamiltonian and unitary \(S\)-matrix are required.

### 13.3 Full chirality and anomalies

The exact Standard Model chiral representation must appear in one construction. Gauge and mixed gravitational anomalies must cancel in the physical field theory, not only in a representation table.

### 13.4 Parameters

The Standard Model has gauge couplings, Higgs parameters, Yukawa eigenvalues, CKM angles and phase, and—with a neutrino extension—further masses and mixings. Their values are not derived by obtaining the correct multiplet names.

### 13.5 Relativistic locality

Internal division-algebra actions must be combined consistently with \(Spin(1,3)\) spacetime symmetry. Coleman–Mandula-type constraints matter if one attempts a nontrivial ordinary Lie-algebra mixing of internal and spacetime symmetries; graded/supersymmetric or product constructions must state precisely how they avoid the obstruction.

### 13.6 Experiment

The work primarily reorganises known representations. A mature theory needs a robust new prediction: a forbidden coupling, a mass or mixing relation, a new particle with constrained properties, or a measurable deviation.

## 14. Fair overall assessment

Furey's work is not “the Standard Model derived from octonions” in the full physical sense. It is also not mere pattern matching. The explicit ideals, ladder operators, stabilisers, intersections and triality actions are real representation theory that recovers nontrivial portions of Standard Model structure with unusual economy.

The right description is:

> a technically developed algebraic reconstruction programme that has obtained significant kinematic representation results and increasingly constrained symmetry-selection principles, but has not yet supplied the complete local quantum dynamics or empirical predictions needed to replace the Standard Model.

That distinction is central to integrating the work with Spin(2,3). Furey is strongest exactly where Aaron's programme is presently weakest—internal particle representations. Aaron's programme is asking a different question—how Lorentzian spacetime and its gravitational response might be selected. Their union is promising only if neither side's missing dynamics is hidden by the other side's elegant algebra.
