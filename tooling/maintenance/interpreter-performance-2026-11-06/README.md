## Measured improvement

`kernel-reference.json` pins predecessor native-realm fixtures and their exact import-specifier adaptations. `compare-kernels.mjs` runs seven interleaved samples per side, with fixed clock, no I/O, identical inputs and identity checks. `kernel-comparison.json` records median helper latency reductions of 3.14x, 4.37x, 5.00x and 5.67x for 0, 1, 10 and 100 sessions. For 100 sessions the median fell from 15.23 ms to 2.69 ms.

This is a helper microbenchmark under concurrent machine activity. It does not establish native parity, production/end-to-end latency, an SLO or complete performance closure. CPU profiles and intermediate benchmarks explain development observations; only the final pinned comparison supports the stated result. The older file-session audit remains frozen. `baseline-session-benchmark.json` here is a new observation of its predecessor, not a replacement for historical evidence.

`canonical-comparison.json` separately checks the ordinary public evaluator against the pinned predecessor on scalar and 20-field record fixtures, using the same graph/context and seven interleaved samples. During concurrent integration, the current median was about 5.3% slower on both fixtures (2.21 vs 2.10 microseconds and 29.63 vs 28.14 microseconds). This bounded observation is retained as a limitation; the prepared protocol speedup is not a claim that every evaluator path becomes faster. Further representative end-to-end measurement remains open.
