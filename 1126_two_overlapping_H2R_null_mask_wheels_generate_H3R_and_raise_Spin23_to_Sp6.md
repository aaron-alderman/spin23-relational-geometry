## Executive result

The nested-commutant branch contains the desired null-mask relations, but their unrestricted Jordan closure is larger than \(H_2(\mathbb R)\).

Let

\[
X_{PI}=E_{PI}+E_{IP}
\]

represent the safe Dirac image relation, and let

\[
X_{PN}=E_{PN}+E_{NP}
\]

represent the rank-restoring Majorana null relation.

Each relation separately belongs to a principal two-state Jordan corner:

\[
J_{PI}\cong H_2(\mathbb R),
\qquad
J_{PN}\cong H_2(\mathbb R).
\]

Each local wheel therefore has conformal algebra

\[
\mathfrak{conf}(H_2(\mathbb R))
\cong\mathfrak{sp}_4(\mathbb R)
\cong\mathfrak{spin}(2,3).
\]

But the two wheels overlap at \(P\), and their mixed Jordan product creates the third relation:

\[
2\bigl(X_{PI}\circ X_{PN}\bigr)=X_{IN}.
\]

Their squares generate independent diagonal populations. Together with the identity, the closure is all real symmetric \(3\times3\) matrices:

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Jord}\langle I,X_{PI},X_{PN}\rangle
=H_3(\mathbb R).
}
\]

Consequently the unrestricted combined conformal algebra is

\[
\mathfrak{conf}(H_3(\mathbb R))
\cong\mathfrak{sp}_6(\mathbb R),
\]

not \(\mathfrak{spin}(2,3)\).

This is the first exact answer to what “wheels within wheels” does algebraically: two local \(Spin(2,3)\) wheels sharing one carrier generate a larger \(Sp(6,\mathbb R)\) wheel unless a projection, sector separation or dynamical constraint suppresses the induced \(I\!-!N\) relation.

## 1. The two local wheels

Use the ordered family basis \((P,I,N)\). The \(P/I\) principal corner consists of matrices supported on the first two basis vectors. Its self-adjoint real part is

\[
J_{PI}
=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}
\left\{
E_{PP},E_{II},E_{PI}+E_{IP}
\right\}
\cong H_2(\mathbb R).
\]

Likewise,

\[
J_{PN}
=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}
\left\{
E_{PP},E_{NN},E_{PN}+E_{NP}
\right\}
\cong H_2(\mathbb R).
\]

They share the carrier idempotent \(E_{PP}\). In the project’s language:

- \(P\) is the common carrier;
- \(I\) and \(N\) are the two faces;
- \(PI\) and \(PN\) are the two primary relationships.

Each pair is exactly a two-state population/coherence Jordan system and therefore supports the previously established \(Spin_0(2,3)\) conformal action.

## 2. The third relation is forced by Jordan closure

The Jordan product is

\[
A\circ B=\frac12(AB+BA).
\]

Direct multiplication gives

\[
X_{PI}X_{PN}=E_{IN},
\qquad
X_{PN}X_{PI}=E_{NI}.
\]

Hence

\[
X_{PI}\circ X_{PN}
=\frac12(E_{IN}+E_{NI})
=\frac12X_{IN}.
\]

The relation between the two faces is not optional under unrestricted Jordan composition. It is generated by composing each face with the shared carrier.

This is the exact realization of

\[
\boxed{
\text{one carrier, two faces, three relationships}.
}
\]

The third relationship is not postulated. It is the closure of the first two.

## 3. The diagonal populations are also forced

Squaring the two primary relations gives

\[
X_{PI}\circ X_{PI}=E_{PP}+E_{II},
\]

\[
X_{PN}\circ X_{PN}=E_{PP}+E_{NN}.
\]

Together with the identity, these span all three diagonal matrices. The three symmetric off-diagonal relations

\[
X_{PI},\quad X_{PN},\quad X_{IN}
\]

then complete the six-dimensional space

\[
\operatorname{Sym}_3(\mathbb R)=H_3(\mathbb R).
\]

There is no smaller unital Jordan algebra containing both complete principal corners.

## 4. Local \(Spin(2,3)\), global \(Sp(6,\mathbb R)\)

For the Euclidean Jordan algebra \(H_n(\mathbb R)\), the conformal algebra is \(\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}(\mathbb R)\). Therefore

\[
H_2(\mathbb R)
\longrightarrow
\mathfrak{sp}_4(\mathbb R)
\cong\mathfrak{spin}(2,3),
\]

whereas

\[
H_3(\mathbb R)
\longrightarrow
\mathfrak{sp}_6(\mathbb R).
\]

This does not falsify the \(Spin(2,3)\) pole. It changes its status:

- \(Spin(2,3)\) governs each two-face relational wheel locally;
- \(Sp(6,\mathbb R)\) governs the unrestricted closure of both wheels together;
- recovering one global \(Spin(2,3)\) requires a mechanism that prevents the full closure from becoming physical.

## 5. Relation to the null mask

The null mask distinguishes sectors:

- the Dirac sector retains \(P\leftrightarrow I\);
- the Majorana sector retains \(P\leftrightarrow N\);
- the dangerous Dirac \(P\leftrightarrow N\) relation is absent.

The Jordan calculation shows why sector dependence is essential. If both allowed relations are placed in one undifferentiated Jordan algebra, their closure produces \(I\leftrightarrow N\) and all population directions. The distinction between Dirac and Majorana wheels must therefore survive at the level of composition, not merely at the level of the initial generators.

The correct object may be:

1. a pair of Jordan modules sharing a carrier but not closed together under the binary Jordan product;
2. a Jordan triple system whose admissible middle element remembers the relationship sector;
3. a conditional expectation that projects mixed products back onto the allowed sector;
4. a Peirce-graded or groupoid composition in which only type-compatible paths compose;
5. a junk quotient that removes \(X_{IN}\) and the unwanted population difference.

## 6. Binary Jordan versus sector-typed triple composition

The binary product forgets which physical sector supplied each relation. The ternary product

\[
\{x,y,z\}
=\frac12(xy^*z+zy^*x)
\]

can retain that information if the middle slot is typed by the carrier or relationship sector.

This suggests a concrete revision of “Jordan-triple recursion”:

\[
\text{Dirac module}
\times
\text{typed carrier}
\times
\text{Majorana module}
\longrightarrow
\text{admissible relation module},
\]

rather than closing all self-adjoint generators under an untyped binary product.

The programme should now test whether the sector-dependent Krajewski anchors define exactly such a typing. If they do, \(Spin(2,3)\) may remain the conformal algebra of each admissible local triple subsystem even though the untyped envelope is \(Sp(6,\mathbb R)\).
