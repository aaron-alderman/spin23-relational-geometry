of the same group.

## 2. Independent groups forbid the obvious contractions

Write the initial symmetry as

\[
G_0=SO(3)_F\times SO(3)_\nu\times SO(3)_R.
\]

A proposed bilinear such as

\[
Q_{ij}\Sigma_{0,ij}
\]

contains an unspoken identification of the \(F\) and \(\nu\) index spaces. An
explicit Lie-algebra calculation on \(\operatorname{Sym}^2_0(\mathbb R^3)\)
finds

\[
\boxed{
\dim\operatorname{Hom}_{SO(3)_A\times SO(3)_B}
(\mathbf5_A,\mathbf5_B)=0.}
\]

Equivalently, a constant \(5\times5\) contraction tensor invariant under
independent rotations must vanish. The same applies to every pair among the
three fiveplets.

Therefore no cross-alignment potential exists before a locking tensor, common
origin or explicit spurion identifies the groups.

## 3. Once the group is diagonal, the contraction is unique

Under one common diagonal \(SO(3)_D\), the real spin-two representation is
irreducible. Its invariant bilinear is unique up to scale:

\[
\boxed{
\dim\operatorname{End}_{SO(3)_D}(\mathbf5)=1.}
\]

The lowest cross-invariants are consequently

\[
Q:\Sigma_0,
\qquad
Q:R_0,
\qquad
\Sigma_0:R_0.
\]

A transparent aligned potential is

\[
V_{\rm align}
=\lambda_\nu\|\Sigma_0-aQ(x)\|^2
+\lambda_R\|R_0-bQ(x)\|^2,
\qquad \lambda_\nu,\lambda_R>0.
\]

At fixed \(x\), its minimum is exactly

\[
\Sigma_0=aQ(x),
\qquad
R_0=bQ(x).
\]

Tensor deviations are massive. Thus simultaneous alignment is dynamically
elementary **after** the common group has been established.

## 4. The potential does not derive the diagonal group

Writing \(V_{\rm align}\) with one repeated index alphabet presupposes the key
intertwiner. There are only three honest origins for it:

1. **Kinematic identity:** all three tensors are derived functorially from one
   underlying triplet space.
2. **Explicit locking:** a fixed bifundamental spurion identifies the spaces.
3. **Spontaneous locking:** dynamical bifundamental fields acquire full-rank
   expectation values and reduce the product group to its diagonal.

The first would be most explanatory. The second is controllable but adds the
desired answer. The third has a substantial spectrum cost.

## 5. Goldstone cost of spontaneous locking

For three global rotation groups,

\[

\[
9-3=6

If the common diagonal group then selects one director,

\[

Any proposed low-energy theory must say which mechanism applies.

## 6. Vector versus director subtlety

The relationship tensor obeys \(Q(-x)=Q(x)\), but a fundamental vector field
\(x\) does not identify its two signs merely because its potential depends on
\(Q\). If \(x\) is physical, its fixed-radius orbit is \(S^2\); the two lifts of
one director remain distinct configurations.

The orbit is genuinely \(\mathbb{RP}^2\) only when:

- \(Q\), rather than \(x\), is the physical order parameter;
- \(x\sim-x\) is a gauge redundancy;
- or the sign lift is quotiented by the bundle construction.

An ordinary global \(x\mapsto-x\) symmetry does not itself identify states.
