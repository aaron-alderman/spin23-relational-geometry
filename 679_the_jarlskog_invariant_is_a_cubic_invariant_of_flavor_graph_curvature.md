## Executive result

Let

\[
H_u=Y_uY_u^\dagger,
\qquad
H_d=Y_dY_d^\dagger
\]

be the two Hermitian quark Yukawa operators in one common left-handed flavor frame. Define the two-parameter unitary family

\[
V_{\rm graph}(x,y)
=e^{ixH_u}e^{iyH_d}.
\]

The associated positive graph bundle has curvature at the origin

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal F_{xy}
=\frac14[H_u,H_d].
}
\]

For three generations,

\[
\boxed{
\det\mathcal F_{xy}
=\frac1{64}\det[H_u,H_d].
}
\]

The right-hand side is the standard Jarlskog commutator invariant up to normalization. Consequently:

- it is purely imaginary;
- it reverses sign under CP;
- it vanishes when the CKM phase vanishes;
- it vanishes when either same-charge mass spectrum is degenerate;
- it is nonzero precisely when the usual quark-sector CP obstruction is present.

The flavor-graph curvature therefore carries a recognized physical invariant, not merely abstract noncommutativity.

## 1. Derivation

For the graph frame,

\[
\mathcal A=\frac12V\,dV^\dagger,
\qquad
\mathcal F=-\frac14\theta\wedge\theta,
\qquad
\theta=VdV^\dagger.
\]

At \(x=y=0\),

\[
\theta_x=-iH_u,
\qquad
\theta_y=-iH_d.
\]

Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal F_{xy}
&=-\frac14[\theta_x,\theta_y]\\
&=-\frac14[-iH_u,-iH_d]\\
&=\frac14[H_u,H_d].
\end{aligned}
\]

Because a commutator is traceless, the three-by-three identity

\[
\operatorname{tr}(\mathcal F_{xy}^3)
=3\det\mathcal F_{xy}
\]

holds. The CP-odd invariant can therefore be read either as the curvature determinant or its cubic trace.

## 2. Relation to the conventional Jarlskog formula

In a basis where

\[
H_u=\operatorname{diag}(m_u^2,m_c^2,m_t^2)
\]

and

\[
H_d=V_{\rm CKM}
\operatorname{diag}(m_d^2,m_s^2,m_b^2)
V_{\rm CKM}^\dagger,
\]

the commutator determinant is proportional to

\[
iJ_{\rm CKM}
\prod_{i<j}(m_{u_i}^2-m_{u_j}^2)
\prod_{k<l}(m_{d_k}^2-m_{d_l}^2).
\]

Thus nonzero curvature volume requires:

1. three generations;
2. a nonzero mixing phase;
3. nondegenerate up-type masses;
4. nondegenerate down-type masses.

These are exactly the familiar conditions for CKM CP violation.

## 3. What the result says about the twist

The earlier abstract statement was that two noncommuting flavor directions can produce graph curvature. The present result identifies a canonical pair of directions and a physical invariant:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Yukawa noncommutativity}
\longrightarrow
\text{flavor-graph curvature}
\longrightarrow
\text{Jarlskog cubic}.
}
\]

In this restricted sense, the three-generation flavor sector contains an exact “twist”: a curvature whose orientation reverses under CP.

This twist lives in complex flavor space. It is not yet a \(Spin(2,3)\) face curvature, braid phase or spacetime holonomy.

## 4. Compatibility with the real carrier

The exact embedding

\[
A\mapsto A\otimes P_V
\]

keeps the \(H_2(\mathbb R)\) face triple real while the graph projector carries \(V\). Under exact tensor factorization, the Jarlskog curvature remains purely in the flavor factor:

\[
\mathcal F_{\rm mixed}=0.
\]

To connect this CP twist dynamically to the \(Spin_0(2,3)\) response geometry, a nonfactorizable face--flavor connection must be derived. The Jarlskog curvature supplies a target invariant but not that missing intertwiner.

## 5. Physical-loop caveat

The parameters \(x,y\) are canonical spurion-flow coordinates generated by \(H_u,H_d\). They are not automatically laboratory controls, RG time coordinates or a closed physical cycle.

A genuine holonomy claim additionally requires:

- an operational parameter surface;
- a connection induced by the action or renormalization;
- a closed path on that surface;
- gauge-invariant comparison of transported states;
- control of thresholds and degeneracies.

The curvature theorem is exact even if no such physical loop is selected.

## 6. Stop-everything criteria

Stop identifying the curvature cubic with physical CP if:

1. the mass operators are not placed in one common left-handed frame;
2. the mixing phase can be removed by a flavor redefinition;
3. either mass spectrum is degenerate;
4. only two generations are retained;
5. the formal spurion surface is described as an observed loop without an operational protocol.
