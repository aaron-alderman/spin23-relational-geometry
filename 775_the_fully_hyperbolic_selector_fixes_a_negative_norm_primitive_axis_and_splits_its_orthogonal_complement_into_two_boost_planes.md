# The Fully Hyperbolic Selector Fixes a Negative-Norm Primitive Axis and Splits Its Orthogonal Complement into Two Boost Planes

## Claim

The fully hyperbolic carrier word \(N=(UPHR)^2\) induces an explicit isometry of the primitive five-dimensional \((2,3)\) module. Its unique fixed primitive direction has coordinates

\[
v_0=(1,1,2,-1,0)
\]

in an orthogonal primitive basis with Gram matrix

\[
G=2\,\operatorname{diag}(-1,-1,-1,+1,+1).
\]

Its norm is

\[
G(v_0,v_0)=-10.
\]

Thus the fixed axis is non-null and of negative type—timelike in the stated convention. Its orthogonal complement has signature \((2,2)\) and carries the two reciprocal boost pairs found in document 774.

## 1. Primitive basis

Use two-form coordinates in the order

\[
(e_{01},e_{02},e_{03},e_{12},e_{13},e_{23})
\]

and symplectic form

\[
\omega=e_{02}+e_{13}.
\]

A convenient primitive basis is

\[
\begin{aligned}
B_1&=e_{02}-e_{13},\\
B_2&=e_{01}+e_{23},\\
B_3&=e_{03}+e_{12},\\
B_4&=e_{01}-e_{23},\\
B_5&=e_{03}-e_{12}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Taking \(G\) to be minus the exterior-product pairing gives signature \((2,3)\) and the diagonal matrix above. The overall sign is conventional; what matters invariantly is that the fixed direction is non-null and that its complement has split signature.

## 2. Induced fixed form

Solving

\[
(\Lambda_0^2N-I)v=0
\]

gives the one-dimensional fixed space generated by \(v_0\). In ambient two-form coordinates, the corresponding primitive form is

\[
\alpha_0=(0,1,2,2,-1,2).
\]

It obeys

\[
\alpha_0\wedge\omega=0,
\qquad
N^*\alpha_0=\alpha_0,
\qquad
G(\alpha_0,\alpha_0)=-10.
\]

Because the norm is not zero, the word stabilizes an ordinary orthogonal axis rather than a parabolic null flag. This distinguishes it sharply from the Ward-compatible affine remnants studied earlier, which preserved null planes or null lines.

## 3. Orthogonal boost decomposition

Removing one negative direction from signature \((2,3)\) leaves

\[
v_0^\perp\cong\mathbb R^{2,2}.
\]

The remaining eigenvalues are

\[
\mu_1,\mu_1^{-1},\mu_2,\mu_2^{-1},
\]

with

\[
\mu_1\approx8.06865,
\qquad
\mu_2\approx2.38880.
\]

For an orthogonal transformation, an eigenvector with eigenvalue \(\mu\ne\pm1\) is null, and it pairs only with the reciprocal eigendirection. Each reciprocal pair therefore spans a Lorentzian \((1,1)\) plane. Hence

\[
v_0^\perp=L_1^{1,1}\oplus L_2^{1,1},
\]

and \(N\) acts as an independent boost on each plane.

The word is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\) on the four-carrier even though the real primitive representation has this spectral decomposition. Rational coupling and real eigenspace splitting are not contradictory.

## 4. Relation to the earlier Spin(2,3) section analysis

The earlier linear carrier-section audit split the five-module into a chosen \((1,2)\) section and a \((1,1)\) complement, leaving a six-parameter embedding orbit. The present word selects different data:

- one negative fixed axis;
- two dynamical Lorentzian boost planes; and
- no preferred three-dimensional \((1,2)\) carrier section by itself.

Therefore the hyperbolic word does not solve the earlier section-selector problem automatically. It supplies a spectral flag that a physical response may use to select such a section, but an additional rule must decide whether the fixed axis joins one boost plane or how observables project onto the decomposition.

## 5. Physical reading and boundary

The fixed negative axis is a plausible algebraic version of a conserved or normalized carrier quantity while two relational modes undergo reciprocal transfer. But the calculation does not identify that axis with energy, mass, charge, a Casimir or a vacuum direction. Such an identification requires the missing response map.

The strongest current statement is:

\[
\text{selected integral coupled word}
\Longrightarrow
\text{negative fixed primitive axis plus two }(1,1)\text{ boost planes}.
\]

This is a precise \(Spin(2,3)\) dynamical geometry, not yet a Standard Model observable prediction.
