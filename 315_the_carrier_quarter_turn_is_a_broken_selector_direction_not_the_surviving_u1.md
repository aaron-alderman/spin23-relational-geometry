# The Carrier Quarter-Turn Is a Broken Selector Direction, Not the Surviving \(U(1)\)

**Date:** 17 October 2026  
**Branches:** B01, B03, B04, B05, B06, B07, B08 and B11  
**Claim status:** exact stabilizer/orbit classification; the physical field or boundary operation implementing the orbit motion remains open

## 1. The new question

The domain-wall response and Furey survivor response use two different real forms inside \(H_2(\mathbb C)\):

\[
J_y=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}\{I,\sigma_x,\sigma_z\},
\qquad
J_z=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}\{I,\sigma_x,\sigma_y\}.
\]

The preceding calculation found the exact intertwiner

\[
U_x=\exp\!\left(-\frac{i\pi}{4}\sigma_x\right),
\qquad
U_xJ_yU_x^\dagger=J_z.
\]

Could this quarter-turn be generated by the continuous \(U(1)\) already retained by the population-normal Furey carrier? The answer is no. It is an orbit motion of the selector itself.

## 2. Real forms as selector normals

Write a Hermitian carrier as

\[
X=x_0I+x_x\sigma_x+x_y\sigma_y+x_z\sigma_z.
\]

A real form \(J_n\cong H_2(\mathbb R)\) removes the Bloch component normal to an unoriented line \([n]\in\mathbb{RP}^2\). Thus \(J_y\) and \(J_z\) correspond to the orthogonal directors

\[
[e_y],\qquad [e_z].
\]

Under \(SU(2)\) conjugation, \(U_x\) induces the \(SO(3)\) rotation

\[
R_x(\pi/2):e_y\longmapsto e_z,
\qquad e_z\longmapsto-e_y.
\]

The quarter-turn is therefore the shortest selector-orbit path between these two orthogonal director lines.

## 3. Stabilizer theorem

The connected subgroup preserving a fixed director \([n]\) consists of rotations about \(n\):

\[
\operatorname{Stab}_0(J_n)\cong SO(2)_n.
\]

At \(J_y\), its generator is \(\sigma_y\). At \(J_z\), its generator is \(\sigma_z\). But the quarter-turn is generated by \(\sigma_x\), and

\[
[\sigma_x,\sigma_y]=2i\sigma_z,
\qquad
[\sigma_x,\sigma_z]=-2i\sigma_y.
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{U_x\notin\operatorname{Stab}_0(J_y),
\qquad U_x\notin\operatorname{Stab}_0(J_z).}
\]

It changes which \(H_2(\mathbb R)\) subalgebra is selected. It is not an internal automorphism of either endpoint carrier.

## 4. Why the retained Furey complex structure is insufficient

The exchange-paired Furey carrier retains the complex structure

\[
\mathcal J=i\sigma_z
\]

on its two-face factor. Its exponential produces the relative-face subgroup

\[
V_z(\phi)=\exp\!\left(-\frac{i\phi}{2}\sigma_z\right).
\]

This subgroup fixes the population normal \(e_z\). It rotates \(e_x\) and \(e_y\) within their plane, and hence cannot carry \(e_y\) to \(e_z\). No choice of \(\phi\) gives the required \(U_x\).

So the earlier open question has a definite negative answer in the minimal two-face representation:

\[
\boxed{\text{the retained }U(1)\text{ does not generate the wall--Furey quarter-turn}.}
\]

Generating it requires the skew generator \(i\sigma_x\), which is transverse to the unbroken \(U(1)\) and moves the selector director.

## 5. The quarter-turn is one of the selector orientation modes

A continuous path is

\[
n(t)=(0,\cos t,\sin t),
\qquad 0\le t\le\frac\pi2.
\]

It begins at the wall real form and ends at the population-normal form. Its initial velocity

\[
\dot n(0)=e_z
\]

is tangent to the \(\mathbb{RP}^2\) selector orbit. In the isotropic selector theory this is a Goldstone direction; with anisotropy it is a pseudo-Goldstone direction; if the selector is gauged it is an eaten direction; and for a boundary-selected theory it is a change of boundary condition rather than a bulk excitation.

This makes the previously qualitative “next-rung Goldstone” language precise. The transformation that aligns the two response carriers is itself a motion on the next-rung selector manifold.

## 6. Four physically different realizations

The exact algebra permits four branches that must not be conflated:

1. **Global selector field.** A spatial profile \(n(x)\) can interpolate between \(J_y\) and \(J_z\). The quarter-turn is a physical texture and costs gradient plus anisotropy energy.
2. **Gauged selector.** A transverse gauge connection parallel-transports the real form. The quarter-turn is a Wilson-line holonomy, while the associated orientation mode is eaten.
3. **Boundary/source operation.** Phase-coherent probes implement \(U_x\) at the boundary. No new bulk particle follows.
4. **Abstract coordinate equivalence.** One merely rewrites the same complex carrier. This preserves the cone but supplies no reason that actual probes perform the rotation.

Only the first three can turn the mathematical intertwiner into a physical bridge.

## 7. Consequences for the synthesis

The result separates two roles that had been in danger of merging:

\[
\begin{array}{ccl}
\text{surviving }U(1)_z&:&\text{automorphisms within the selected population-normal carrier},\\
\text{quarter-turn }U_x&:&\text{motion between distinct selected real forms}.
\end{array}
\]

This is useful rather than disappointing. A single fixed \(U(1)\) no longer has to do two incompatible jobs. The Furey carrier can retain its charge-compatible complex structure while a separate selector field, connection or boundary quadrature supplies the wall-to-Furey map.

The price is also clear: a derivation of the bridge must now identify the transverse \(i\sigma_x\) generator and explain why the selector moves through a quarter-turn.
