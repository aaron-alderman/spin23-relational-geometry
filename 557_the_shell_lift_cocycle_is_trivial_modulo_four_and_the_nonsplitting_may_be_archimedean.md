# The shell lift cocycle is trivial modulo four, and the nonsplitting may be archimedean

## Executive result

The integral extension

\[
1\to K(2)\to O(J)\to O(q_J)\to1
\]

is nonsplit, but its first congruence-layer obstruction vanishes.

Choose normalized integral lifts \(Q_A\) for all 72 elements \(A\in O(q_J)\), and define

\[
c(A,B)=Q_AQ_BQ_{AB}^{-1}\in K(2).
\]

Linearizing by

\[
\ell(c)=\frac{c-I}{2}\pmod2
\]

produces a seven-dimensional \(O(q_J)\)-module \(W\). Its conjugation coinvariants have dimension two:

\[
\dim W=7,\qquad
\dim [O(q_J),W]=5,\qquad
\dim W_{O(q_J)}=2.
\]

However:

\[
\boxed{[\ell(c)]=0\in H^2(O(q_J),W).}
\]

The complete twisted cocycle is a coboundary. The lifts can therefore be changed so that they multiply correctly modulo four.

Consequently the two coinvariant bits are not intrinsic extension obstructions and cannot be identified directly with the quartic theta line. The proven integral nonsplitting is invisible in \(K(2)/K(4)\). It may first appear in a deeper congruence layer, or it may be an archimedean/global obstruction tied to the forbidden real signature.

## 1. A normalized lift system

For every

\[
A\in G:=O(q_J),
\qquad |G|=72,
\]

the certificate chooses a bounded integral lift

\[
Q_A\in O(J)
\]

with

\[
Q_A\bmod2=A.
\]

The identity lift is normalized:

\[
Q_1=I.
\]

The failure of multiplicativity is

\[
c(A,B)=Q_AQ_BQ_{AB}^{-1}.
\]

Because all three factors have the correct finite reductions,

\[
c(A,B)\equiv I\pmod2,
\]

so

\[
c(A,B)\in K(2).
\]

All

\[
72^2=5184
\]

corrections are computed exactly.

## 2. First congruence linearization

Every \(c\in K(2)\) can be written

\[
c=I+2D.
\]

Modulo \(K(4)\), only

\[
D\bmod2
\]

matters. Thus the first congruence layer is additive:

\[
K(2)/K(4)\subseteq M_5(\mathbb F_2).
\]

The 5,184 correction tangents span a seven-dimensional subspace

\[
W\subseteq K(2)/K(4).
\]

Conjugation by a lift \(Q_A\) reduces to

\[
D\longmapsto ADA^{-1}\pmod2,
\]

so \(W\) is a \(G\)-module.

The certificate verifies closure of \(W\) under all 72 conjugation actions.

## 3. The two-dimensional coinvariant quotient

The conjugation-difference subspace is

\[
[G,W]
=\langle w+A\cdot w:\ A\in G,\ w\in W\rangle.
\]

Its dimension is five. Hence

\[
W_G:=W/[G,W]\cong\mathbb F_2^2.
\]

For the chosen canonical lift system, projected cocycle values occur with counts

\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrr}
\text{label}&00&01&10&11\\ \hline
\text{count}&196&1886&3084&18.
\end{array}
\]

These counts show that both apparent bits occur nontrivially. They do **not** show that either bit is a cohomological obstruction: changing the lift section changes the cocycle by a coboundary.

## 4. Every scalar projection is a coboundary

There are three nonzero linear maps

\[
\mathbb F_2^2\to\mathbb F_2.
\]

For each projection \(\lambda\), the certificate solves the normalized cochain equation

\[
\lambda(c(A,B))
=f(A)+f(B)+f(AB).
\]

All three systems are consistent.

Thus no nonzero scalar projection of the two-bit coinvariant cocycle defines a nontrivial class:

\[
\boxed{
[\lambda\circ c]=0
\quad\text{for every }
\lambda\in(\mathbb F_2^2)^*.}
\]

In particular, neither visible bit, nor their sum, is the quartic orientation character.

## 5. The full twisted cocycle is also trivial

Scalar projections could miss an obstruction carried by the nontrivial \(G\)-action on \(W\). The stronger test solves

\[
z(A,B)
=f(A)+A\cdot f(B)+f(AB),
\]

where

\[
z(A,B)=\ell(c(A,B))\in W
\]

and

\[
f:G\to W.
\]

There are

\[
72\cdot7=504
\]

binary cochain variables. The full system contains

\[
72^2\cdot7=36\,288
\]

cocycle component equations, plus normalization \(f(1)=0\).

Exact binary elimination gives a consistent system of rank

\[
495.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{[z]=0\in H^2(G,W).}
\]

This is stronger than triviality of the two coinvariant projections.

## 6. A section exists modulo four

Changing lifts by a first-layer cochain,

\[
Q_A\longmapsto Q'_A=k_AQ_A,
\qquad
k_A\in K(2),
\]

changes \(z\) by its twisted coboundary. Since \(z=\delta f\), representatives can be chosen so that

\[
Q'_AQ'_B\equiv Q'_{AB}\pmod{K(4)}.
\]

Equivalently, the quotient extension

\[
1\to K(2)/K(4)
\to O(J)/K(4)
\to O(q_J)
\to1
\]

splits over the correction module generated by the chosen lifts.

The order-72 symmetry is therefore coherent through the first binary congruence layer even though no finite integral order-72 subgroup exists.

## 7. Why this does not contradict integral nonsplitting

Cycle 472 proves nonsplitting using real representation signature:

\[
O(q_J)\cong S_3\wr C_2
\]

has no faithful five-dimensional representation preserving signature \((2,3)\).

Modular quotients do not see real signature. Therefore at least two scenarios remain:

### Deeper two-adic obstruction

The lift may fail at some stage

\[
K(4)/K(8),\quad
K(8)/K(16),\quad\ldots
\]

even though the first layer splits.

### Purely global or archimedean obstruction

Compatible sections may exist through every finite two-power quotient, possibly even two-adically, while no integral finite subgroup exists because its real representation would have the wrong signature.

The present computation does not distinguish these possibilities.

## 8. Consequence for the quartic orientation line

The terminology “quartic” might have suggested that the old \(\mathbb Z_4\) theta direction should appear automatically at the mod-four lift stage. It does not.

The two first-layer coinvariants are:

- dependent on the initial lift section;
- scalar-cohomologically trivial;
- jointly trivial as a twisted \(W\)-valued cocycle.

Therefore the quartic theta line must enter through additional structure:

- a quadratic rather than linear refinement;
- a Clifford or Heisenberg central extension;
- a spin or metaplectic lift;
- a determinant/Pfaffian trivialization;
- a deeper congruence or global compatibility condition.

This closes the simplest proposed identification:

\[
\text{quartic orientation}
\ne
\text{raw first-layer congruence cocycle}.
\]

## 9. Claim status

### Established

- the chosen 5,184 kernel corrections span a seven-dimensional first-layer module;
- the conjugation-difference subspace has dimension five;
- the coinvariant quotient is two-dimensional;
- all three scalar projections of the coinvariant cocycle are coboundaries;
- the full twisted seven-dimensional cocycle is a coboundary;
- a multiplicative lift exists modulo four;
- integral nonsplitting is not detected by \(K(2)/K(4)\).

### Candidate bridge

- a deeper congruence layer may produce the quartic or Heisenberg obstruction;
- the integral obstruction may instead be archimedean and tied directly to signature;
- a quadratic refinement of the trivial linear cocycle may remain nontrivial.

### Not established

- splitting or nonsplitting modulo eight;
- existence of a compatible two-adic section;
- a canonical cochain trivialization;
- any equality with the quartic theta, Clifford or metaplectic class.

## 10. Stop-everything and falsification gates

### Stop-everything results reached

1. The first congruence cocycle is fully cohomologically trivial.
2. The apparent two-bit quotient does not supply a canonical orientation character.
3. Integral nonsplitting is invisible modulo four.

### Next stop-everything gates

1. The corrected mod-four section fails to lift modulo eight with a nontrivial scalar quotient.
2. Sections exist compatibly at every two-power level, isolating the obstruction as archimedean/global.
3. A quadratic refinement of the first-layer cochain reproduces the quartic theta phase.
4. The Clifford extension supplies the missing nonlinear refinement.

### Falsification boundaries

1. Raw coinvariant counts cannot be promoted to cohomology classes.
2. “Quartic” nomenclature is not evidence of a mod-four congruence origin.
3. The integral signature theorem does not prove a two-adic obstruction.
