For three identical Standard-Model families plus one \(\nu^c\) per family, with no extra chiral fermions, every family-universal anomaly-free continuous \(U(1)_X\) that permits the four Dirac Yukawa structures is a linear combination

\[
X=\alpha Y+\beta(B-L).

If the scalar charge is chosen to allow the three desired \(S_1\) vertices

This is stronger than the earlier \(B-L\) result. It excludes the entire two-parameter continuous class generated by hypercharge and \(B-L\), not merely one generator.

The live exits are now sharply identified: family-dependent charges, additional chiral anomaly-cancelling matter, an intrinsically discrete gauge symmetry, finite-geometry incidence selection, or multi-mediator interference.

## 1. Assumptions

Use left-handed Weyl fields

\[
Q,u^c,d^c,L,e^c,\nu^c
\]

and one Higgs doublet \(H\). The theorem assumes:

1. three generations have identical \(U(1)_X\) charges;
2. the only chiral fermions are the Standard Model plus three \(\nu^c\);
3. all four Dirac Yukawa structures are permitted;
4. the desired \(S_1\) source pattern is permitted;
5. anomalies cancel without a Green–Schwarz term or undisplayed heavy chiral sector.

Dropping any of these can reopen a branch. The theorem is deliberately not stated beyond them.

## 2. Solve the Yukawa constraints

Write

\[
x_Q=q,
\qquad
x_H=h.
\]

Neutrality of

\[
QHu^c,
\quad
QH^\dagger d^c,
\quad
LH^\dagger e^c,
\quad
LH\nu^c
\]

gives

\[
x_{u^c}=-q-h,
\qquad
x_{d^c}=-q+h,
\]

\[
x_{e^c}=-x_L+h,
\qquad
x_{\nu^c}=-x_L-h.
\]

The \(SU(2)_L^2U(1)_X\) anomaly imposes

\[
3q+x_L=0,
\]

so

\[
x_L=-3q,
\qquad
x_{e^c}=3q+h,
\qquad
x_{\nu^c}=3q-h.
\]

The \(SU(3)_c^2U(1)_X\) and mixed gravitational anomalies then vanish identically. The certificate also expands the cubic, \(Y^2X\), and \(YX^2\) anomalies as polynomials in \((q,h)\) and verifies that every coefficient vanishes.

## 3. Identify the complete continuous solution

Because \(H\) has \(Y=1/2\) and \(B-L=0\), set

\[
\alpha=2h.
\]

From the quark-doublet charge,

\[
q=\frac{\alpha}{6}+\frac{\beta}{3},
\]

so

\[
\beta=3q-h.
\]

Substitution into every fermion charge gives

\[
x_f=\alpha Y_f+\beta(B-L)_f.
\]

Thus the familiar \(Y\) and \(B-L\) span is not an assumption here; it is the solved minimal family-universal anomaly-free space.

## 5. Why classical baryon number looked promising

With the phenomenological assignment

\[
B(S)=\frac13,
\]

the desired vertices have total \(B=0\), while the diquark vertices have total \(B=\pm1\). That is a valid classical selection rule.

But family-universal baryon number has an \(SU(2)_L^2U(1)_B\) anomaly. The relation

\[
3x_Q+x_L=0
\]

required of the minimal gauged theory replaces separate baryon number by the anomaly-free \(Y\)–\(B-L\) plane, and that plane no longer distinguishes the vertices.

This precisely separates two claims:

- **classical global selection:** works;
- **minimal anomaly-free continuous gauge protection:** does not.

## 6. Discrete symmetries are not settled by this theorem

Any discrete symmetry obtained solely by breaking a member of this same minimal continuous \(Y\)–\(B-L\) family inherits the exact neutrality of all five vertices and therefore fails.

An intrinsically discrete gauge symmetry can evade that statement because its anomaly congruences can depend on heavy states and on the order of the group. Classifications such as baryon triality and proton hexality demonstrate that discrete proton-protection mechanisms can exist in enlarged settings, although their published MSSM assumptions are not the present non-supersymmetric \(S_1\) model. See [Dreiner, Luhn and Thormeier, *What is the Discrete Gauge Symmetry of the MSSM?*](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-ph/0512163).

Therefore no named discrete symmetry is imported by analogy. A valid candidate must be checked against this exact field content, every desired vertex, all Standard-Model Yukawas, mixed gauge anomalies, gravity, and the heavy completion.

## 7. The finite-geometry route becomes more important

The no-go leaves a mechanism that ordinary charge selection does not capture: the represented differential calculus may simply fail to contain the diquark source maps even though gauge quantum numbers permit them.

This is not speculative as a mechanism class. A noncommutative-geometric Pati–Salam model has been reported in which a scalar leptoquark inside a complex \((6,1,1)\) has no diquark coupling because of the underlying finite geometry, while a companion field carries the diquark coupling. The paper also emphasizes that suppressing mixing with that companion remains necessary. See [Aydemir, Minic, Sun and Takeuchi, *B-decay anomalies and scalar leptoquarks in unified Pati–Salam models from noncommutative geometry*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.05844).

That result does not prove the current Furey construction has the same calculus. It does prove that the desired category of mechanism exists and tells us exactly what to compare next.

## 8. Stop-everything and reopening criteria

**Continuous-charge stop:** do not propose another family-universal linear combination of Standard-Model charges in the minimal \(SM+3\nu^c\) spectrum. The complete anomaly-free space has already been exhausted.

**Remnant stop:** a discrete remnant of only \(Y\) and \(B-L\) cannot distinguish vertices that are exactly neutral under both parent generators.

**Extra-matter reopening trigger:** give the full additional chiral spectrum and verify every continuous anomaly explicitly.

**Discrete reopening trigger:** give integer charges modulo \(N\), the heavy-state assumptions, all mixed anomaly congruences, and the complete allowed/forbidden operator table.

**Finite-geometry reopening trigger:** construct the represented one-form module and demonstrate that the desired leptoquark maps exist while the two diquark maps are absent or live in a heavy, nonmixing companion sector.

The safe projector cannot be protected by the most economical new gauge boson. The anomaly equations fold every minimal family-universal continuous candidate back into the same \(Y\)–\(B-L\) plane that already fails.

That is a productive negative result. It pushes the mechanism away from “one more charge” and toward exactly the structures this programme is trying to understand: discrete global organization, enlarged matter, or a finite differential calculus that selects relationships rather than merely labeling fields.
