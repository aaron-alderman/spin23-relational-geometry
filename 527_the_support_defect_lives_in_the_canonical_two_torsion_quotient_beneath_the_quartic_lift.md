## 1. Executive result

Let

\[

\qquad I=K\cap M,

where \(K\) is the index-64 primitive source wedge and \(M\) is the index-six phase/selector lattice inside the standard rank-five \(Spin(2,3)\) lattice. The previous Smith calculation gave

That abstract decomposition is not canonical: choosing a particular copy of \((\mathbb Z_2)^3\) inside \(G\) requires a splitting. The intrinsic structure is instead the characteristic filtration

\[
\boxed{

\]

with

\[
\boxed{

\qquad
G[2]\cong(\mathbb Z_2)^4,
\qquad
G[2]/2G\cong(\mathbb Z_2)^3.}
\]

The three-bit support Smith defect therefore has a canonical **target type**:

\[
\boxed{D_{\rm supp}\longrightarrow G[2]/2G,}
\]

not a preferred subgroup \((\mathbb Z_2)^3\subset G\). The line \(2G\) is the square of every order-four lift and records precisely the obstruction to replacing the quartic direction by another binary bit.

This removes one arbitrary choice from the proposed Prakash–Ramya/Furey/\(Spin(2,3)\) interface. It does not yet construct the source-labelled map \(D_{\rm supp}\to G[2]/2G\), and it does not yet prove that the order-four generator carries the binary theta multiplier.

## 2. Why the filtration is canonical

For any abelian group \(G\), the subgroups

\[
2G=\{2g:g\in G\},
\qquad
G[2]=\{g\in G:2g=0\}
\]

are characteristic. Every automorphism of \(G\) preserves them. Hence the quotient

\[
G[2]/2G
\]

is defined without a Smith basis, a chosen complement or a physical labelling.

For

\[
G=\mathbb Z_4\oplus(\mathbb Z_2)^3,
\]

the order-four coordinate contributes its double to both \(2G\) and \(G[2]\). The remaining three order-two coordinates survive after quotienting by that common line. Thus

\[
|2G|=2,
\qquad
|G[2]|=16,
\qquad
|G[2]/2G|=8.
\]

The filtration, rather than a direct-sum presentation, is the invariant datum available before a source map chooses a splitting.

Those results explain why the binary shadow contains information finer than an ordinary \(\mathbb F_2\)-linear code. A transversal \(T\) phase is sensitive to weights modulo eight, and its projective/theta reductions naturally require fourth-root data.

The present filtration supplies an integral receiving structure with exactly that shape:

Conversely, the present lattice calculation does not re-prove their code theorem or identify its logical phase character automatically.

## 6. What this rules out

The calculation rejects four shortcuts.

### 6.1 Five independent binary bits

Although \(|G|=32\), its exponent is four. Therefore

\[
G\not\cong(\mathbb Z_2)^5.
\]

### 6.2 A canonical binary subgroup

There are splittings

\[
G\cong\mathbb Z_4\oplus(\mathbb Z_2)^3,
\]

but no splitting has yet been selected by the Standard-Model field labels. The canonical object is the quotient \(G[2]/2G\).

### 6.3 A theta character from group order alone

An order-four generator can carry a fourth-root character, but the group type does not specify which character, quadratic refinement or normalization is physically correct.

### 6.4 A derivation of \(Spin(2,3)\) from anomaly cancellation alone

The paper supplies a symmetric cubic tensor. The \(Spin(2,3)\) route additionally requires a rank-four symplectic carrier and its primitive wedge. The filtration shows compatibility between their arithmetic shadows; it does not erase that extra construction.

### Stop-everything triggers

1. A unique \(\phi\) satisfies all five compatibility conditions.

### Falsifiers

1. No intertwiner preserves both the binary quadratic form and the support stabilizer action.
2. Every possible order-four lift gives the wrong theta character.
3. Matching the qubit structure forces a qutrit map incompatible with \(K/I\cong\mathbb Z_3\).
