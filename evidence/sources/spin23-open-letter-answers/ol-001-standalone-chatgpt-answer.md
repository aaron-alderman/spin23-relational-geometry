**The sharp condition in the positive Jordan setting is rank \(2\) with real Peirce multiplicity \(1\).** This gives
\[
J\cong \operatorname{Sym}_2(\mathbb R),
\qquad
\mathfrak{tkk}(J)\cong\mathfrak{sp}(4,\mathbb R)
\cong\mathfrak{so}(2,3).
\]
Keeping positivity and rank two, the first enlargement of the Peirce multiplicity, from \(1\) to \(2\), gives
\[
J=\operatorname{Herm}_2(\mathbb C),
\qquad
\mathfrak{tkk}(J)\cong\mathfrak{su}(2,2).
\]
Here \(\mathfrak{sp}(4,\mathbb R)\) means the **ten-dimensional algebra of real \(4\times4\) symplectic matrices**; some Jordan references call it \(\mathfrak{sp}(2,\mathbb R)\). The rank-two, multiplicity-one identification is stated explicitly in Hilgert–Kobayashi–Möllers–Ørsted, Remark 1.13. [pure.au.dk](https://pure.au.dk/ws/files/69558107/bargmann_fock.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

There is a further minimality point: **positivity is stronger than necessary for the abstract Lie-algebra conclusion.** At fixed three-dimensional Peirce decomposition, nonzero coupling suffices, with either sign. Allowing that coupling to become zero produces a different, still ten-dimensional, nonsemisimple Lie algebra.

Thus “weakest” and “first weakening” need a specified category. The precise statements follow.

## 1. What “two-face” must mean

“Two-face” does not, by itself, specify a standard Jordan classification invariant. Three interpretations have different consequences:

- **The two components \(V^+\) and \(V^-\) of a Jordan pair:** every Jordan pair has these; this imposes no rank or dimension restriction.
- **Two chosen primitive faces or idempotents:** these may sit inside a larger Jordan system. They do not determine its full TKK algebra.
- **A complete Jordan frame consisting of two primitive idempotents:** this is the useful rank-two condition,
  \[
  e=c_1+c_2,\qquad c_i^2=c_i,\qquad c_1\circ c_2=0,
  \]
  with \(e\) the unit of the entire algebra.

The last condition should not be confused with a cone having exactly two extreme rays. The positive-semidefinite cone in \(\operatorname{Sym}_2(\mathbb R)\) has infinitely many extreme rays, represented by \(vv^{\mathsf T}\). A frame selects two complementary ones.

Likewise, “natural positive cone” should mean positivity compatible with the Jordan operations. In a Euclidean Jordan algebra,
\[
K=J_+=\{x^2:x\in J\},
\qquad
Q_x=2L_x^2-L_{x^2},
\qquad
L_x(y)=x\circ y.
\]
An arbitrary positive inner product, or a positive cone in a proper subspace, does not provide the same hypothesis.

Throughout, take “TKK closure” to mean the standard **effective inner TKK algebra**
\[
\mathfrak g=\mathfrak g_{-1}\oplus\mathfrak g_0\oplus\mathfrak g_1,
\qquad
\mathfrak g_{\pm1}\cong J,
\qquad
\mathfrak g_0=[\mathfrak g_1,\mathfrak g_{-1}],
\]
generated by its two outer graded pieces.

## 2. The sharp theorem in the positive category

Let \(J\) be a finite-dimensional real Euclidean Jordan algebra of rank two. For a complete frame \(c_1,c_2\), write its Peirce decomposition as
\[
J=\mathbb Rc_1\oplus J_{12}\oplus\mathbb Rc_2,
\]
where
\[
J_{12}
=
\left\{x\in J:
c_1\circ x=c_2\circ x=\frac12x
\right\}.
\]

Then
\[
\boxed{
\mathfrak{tkk}(J)\cong\mathfrak{sp}(4,\mathbb R)
\iff
\dim_{\mathbb R}J_{12}=1
\iff
\dim_{\mathbb R}J=3
\iff
J\cong\operatorname{Sym}_2(\mathbb R).
}
\]

**Simplicity need not be assumed separately.** At rank two, a nonzero off-diagonal Peirce space makes the Euclidean algebra simple; the reducible possibility is \(\mathbb R\oplus\mathbb R\), whose off-diagonal space is zero. Consequently, removing a separately stated simplicity assumption while retaining rank two and \(\dim J_{12}=1\) changes nothing.

### An elementary proof of the positive identification

Choose \(0\ne u\in J_{12}\). The Peirce rules give
\[
u^2=\alpha c_1+\beta c_2.
\]
The Jordan identity, in the form
\[
[L_u,L_{u^2}]=0,
\]
applied to \(c_1\), gives
\[
0=\frac{\alpha-\beta}{4}u.
\]
Hence
\[
u^2=\lambda(c_1+c_2)=\lambda e.
\]

Formal reality forces \(\lambda>0\). If \(\lambda=0\), a nonzero element has square zero. If \(\lambda<0\), then
\[
u^2+\bigl(\sqrt{-\lambda}\,e\bigr)^2=0
\]
contradicts formal reality.

Rescale \(u\) so that \(u^2=e\). The entire multiplication table is now
\[
c_i^2=c_i,\qquad c_1c_2=0,\qquad
c_i u=\frac12u,\qquad u^2=c_1+c_2.
\]
The map
\[
c_1\longmapsto
\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix},
\quad
c_2\longmapsto
\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix},
\quad
u\longmapsto
\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}
\]
identifies this with \(\operatorname{Sym}_2(\mathbb R)\), under
\[
X\circ Y=\frac12(XY+YX).
\]

Its quadratic operators and cone are therefore
\[
Q_X(Y)=XYX,
\qquad
K=\{X=X^{\mathsf T}:X\succeq0\}.
\]

In coordinates
\[
X=
\begin{pmatrix}
t+z&x\\
x&t-z
\end{pmatrix},
\]
one has
\[
\det X=t^2-x^2-z^2,
\qquad
X\succeq0\iff t\ge\sqrt{x^2+z^2}.
\]
Thus the relevant cone is the three-dimensional Lorentz cone.

### Direct verification of the TKK algebra

Define
\[
E(X)=
\begin{pmatrix}0&X\\0&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
F(Y)=
\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\Y&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
H(A)=
\begin{pmatrix}A&0\\0&-A^{\mathsf T}\end{pmatrix},
\]
with \(X,Y\) symmetric and \(A\in M_2(\mathbb R)\). Then
\[
[E(X),F(Y)]=H(XY)
\]
and
\[
[[E(X),F(Y)],E(Z)]
=
E(XYZ+ZYX).
\]
The last expression is exactly the polarized Jordan-pair product associated with \(Q_X(Y)=XYX\).

Products of symmetric \(2\times2\) matrices span \(M_2(\mathbb R)\), so these generators produce every matrix
\[
\begin{pmatrix}
A&B\\
C&-A^{\mathsf T}
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad B=B^{\mathsf T},\quad C=C^{\mathsf T}.
\]
These are precisely \(\mathfrak{sp}(4,\mathbb R)\). Its grading has dimensions
\[
3+4+3=10.
\]
This identifies the real Lie algebra directly, rather than relying only on its dimension.

## 3. A genuinely weaker algebraic hypothesis

If the objective is only the abstract TKK algebra, the Euclidean hypothesis can be weakened substantially.

> **Algebraic criterion.** Let \(J\) be a real unital Jordan algebra with the complete Peirce decomposition
> \[
> J=\mathbb Rc_1\oplus\mathbb Ru\oplus\mathbb Rc_2,
> \]
> where
> \[
> e=c_1+c_2,\quad c_i^2=c_i,\quad c_1c_2=0,\quad c_i u=\frac12u.
> \]
> Then
> \[
> \boxed{
> \mathfrak{tkk}(J)\cong\mathfrak{sp}(4,\mathbb R)
> \iff u^2\ne0.
> }
> \]

The displayed decomposition already supplies finite-dimensionality. The calculation above supplies
\[
u^2=\lambda e.
\]
Both \(\lambda>0\) and \(\lambda<0\) give the desired TKK algebra.

### Why negative coupling gives the same TKK algebra

For \(\lambda<0\), rescale to \(\lambda=-1\). Put
\[
H=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix}
\]
and define a new Jordan product on \(\operatorname{Sym}_2(\mathbb R)\) by
\[
A\circ_H B=\frac12(AHB+BHA).
\]
Its unit is \(H\). The elements
\[
c_1=E_{11},\qquad c_2=-E_{22},\qquad u=E_{12}+E_{21}
\]
satisfy the required relations, including
\[
u\circ_H u=-H=-e.
\]

Its quadratic representation is
\[
Q_A^H(B)=AHBHA.
\]
The associated doubled pair is isomorphic to the ordinary symmetric-matrix pair through
\[
f^+(A)=A,\qquad f^-(B)=HBH.
\]
For example,
\[
Q_{f^+(A)}\bigl(f^-(B)\bigr)
=AHBHA
=f^+\bigl(Q_A^H(B)\bigr),
\]
and the other quadratic identity follows in the same way.

So this indefinite Jordan algebra is an isotope with **the same Jordan pair and the same TKK algebra**. Its square cone, however, cannot be pointed: both \(e=e^2\) and \(-e=u^2\) are squares.

This is why “drop positivity and obtain a different real form” is not a valid general inference.

### How little cone positivity is actually needed

Within this three-dimensional Peirce setting, even the full Euclidean package is unnecessary for selecting \(\lambda>0\). It suffices that there be a **closed pointed cone containing all squares**.

Negative \(\lambda\) is excluded because the cone would contain both \(e\) and \(-e\). Zero \(\lambda\) is excluded because
\[
(e+tu)^2=e+2tu.
\]
For \(t>0\), scaling this square and passing to the limit gives \(u\) in the cone; using \(-t\) gives \(-u\). Closedness and pointedness then force \(u=0\), a contradiction.

Thus an economical ordered formulation is:

1. A real Jordan structure admitting a unital realization.
2. The complete three-dimensional Peirce decomposition displayed above.
3. A closed pointed cone containing its squares.
4. Its canonical quadratic operators and standard inner TKK construction.

Homogeneity, self-duality, simplicity, and a separate finite-dimensionality hypothesis do not need to be added to this particular formulation.

## 4. Which weakening produces a different Lie algebra?

There are two especially informative answers, depending on what is held fixed.

### A. Preserve positivity and rank two; enlarge the Peirce space

The rank-two Euclidean family is
\[
J_d=\mathbb R\oplus\mathbb R^{d+1},
\]
with product
\[
(a,v)\circ(b,w)
=
(ab+\langle v,w\rangle,\;aw+bv).
\]
Here
\[
\dim J_d=d+2,\qquad \dim(J_d)_{12}=d,
\]
and
\[
(J_d)_+=\{(a,v):a\ge\|v\|\}.
\]
For \(d\ge1\), these are simple spin factors, with
\[
\mathfrak{co}(J_d)\cong\mathfrak{so}(2,d+2).
\]
The spin-factor classification and conformal-algebra table give the following cases. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0911.2977?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

| Real Peirce dimension \(d\) | Jordan algebra | TKK/conformal algebra | Lie dimension |
|---:|---|---|---:|
| \(0\) | \(\mathbb R\oplus\mathbb R\) | \(\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb R)\oplus\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb R)\) | \(6\) |
| \(1\) | \(\operatorname{Sym}_2(\mathbb R)\) | \(\mathfrak{sp}(4,\mathbb R)\cong\mathfrak{so}(2,3)\) | \(10\) |
| \(2\) | \(\operatorname{Herm}_2(\mathbb C)\) | \(\mathfrak{su}(2,2)\cong\mathfrak{so}(2,4)\) | \(15\) |
| \(3\) | \(\mathbb R\oplus\mathbb R^4\) | \(\mathfrak{so}(2,5)\) | \(21\) |
| \(4\) | \(\operatorname{Herm}_2(\mathbb H)\) | \(\mathfrak{so}(2,6)\) | \(28\) |

The dimensions follow from
\[
\dim\mathfrak{co}(J_d)
=\frac{(d+4)(d+3)}2.
\]

Therefore, **ordered by increasing real dimension or Peirce multiplicity, the first different positive example is**
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Herm}_2(\mathbb C)
\quad\longmapsto\quad
\mathfrak{su}(2,2).
}
\]

This is a particularly strong counterexample to an insufficient hypothesis list. It retains a unit, simplicity, formal reality, rank two, the natural positive cone, and the standard quadratic identities. Only the one-dimensionality of the real off-diagonal Peirce space has been relaxed:
\[
\begin{pmatrix}a&t\\t&b\end{pmatrix},
\quad t\in\mathbb R,
\qquad\leadsto\qquad
\begin{pmatrix}a&z\\\bar z&b\end{pmatrix},
\quad z\in\mathbb C.
\]
The latter is still a **real** Jordan algebra; its real dimension is four.

In the other direction, allowing the Peirce space to vanish gives two uncoupled scalar factors and hence \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\oplus\mathfrak{sl}_2\).

### B. Preserve the three-dimensional Peirce decomposition; allow degenerate coupling

Now keep
\[
J=\mathbb Rc_1\oplus\mathbb Ru\oplus\mathbb Rc_2
\]
but weaken \(u^2\ne0\) to allow
\[
u^2=0.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
\mathfrak{tkk}(J_0)
\cong
\bigl(\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb R)\oplus\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb R)\bigr)
\ltimes(\mathbb R^2\otimes\mathbb R^2)
\cong\mathfrak{iso}(2,2).
}
\]

This algebra still has dimension ten, but has a four-dimensional abelian radical.

One can check this directly. Set
\[
D=[L_{c_1},L_u].
\]
The degree-zero structure algebra has basis
\[
L_{c_1},\ L_{c_2},\ L_u,\ D.
\]
Inside the TKK algebra,
\[
\mathcal R=\operatorname{span}\{u^+,u^-,L_u,D\}
\]
is an abelian ideal. The complementary algebra generated by \(c_i^\pm\) and \(L_{c_i}\) is
\[
\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb R)\oplus\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb R),
\]
and its action on \(\mathcal R\) is the tensor product of the two standard two-dimensional representations.

This degeneration also shows why **closedness of the positive cone matters**. Its exact square set is
\[
\left\{
ac_1+bc_2+tu:
a,b\ge0,\ a+b>0,\ t\in\mathbb R
\right\}\cup\{0\}.
\]
It is convex and pointed, but not closed; its closure contains the whole line \(\mathbb Ru\). Thus “a pointed cone of squares” without closedness is a plausible weakening that already admits a different closure at the same dimension.

### A qualification about other real forms

For a spin algebra
\[
J(B)=\mathbb R\oplus W,
\qquad
(a,v)\circ(b,w)=(ab+B(v,w),aw+bv),
\]
the determinant is
\[
\Delta(a,v)=a^2-B(v,v),
\]
and the conformal algebra is obtained by adding a hyperbolic plane to this determinant form. In dimension three, positive-definite and mixed-signature \(B\) both lead to \(\mathfrak{so}(2,3)\), whereas negative-definite \(B\) leads to \(\mathfrak{so}(4,1)\). [ms.u-tokyo.ac.jp](https://www.ms.u-tokyo.ac.jp/~toshi/texpdf/tk2011g-JMSJ66%282014%29349-414.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

The negative-definite case has no nontrivial real idempotents, so it loses the literal two-idempotent frame. It is a counterexample to retaining only generic degree two, not to retaining the full real Peirce assumptions above.

## 5. Translating the assumptions to pairs and triples

### Jordan pairs

For a pair, the ordered theorem applies when an invertible basepoint gives a unital realization \(J\) with the stated cone and Peirce data. The resulting pair must be isomorphic to
\[
\bigl(\operatorname{Sym}_2(\mathbb R),
      \operatorname{Sym}_2(\mathbb R)\bigr),
\qquad
Q_X(Y)=XYX.
\]

Changing the basepoint can change the Jordan algebra product by isotopy while preserving the pair. The negative-coupling example above makes this explicit. Consequently, properties of a particular unital presentation can be stronger than properties needed to identify its pair or TKK algebra.

### Real Jordan triple systems

Use the normalization
\[
Q(x)y=\{x,y,x\}.
\]
A clean compatibility condition that reconstructs a unital Jordan algebra on the **whole real triple** is
\[
\exists e\in T\quad Q(e)=\operatorname{id}_T.
\]
Then
\[
x\circ y=\{x,e,y\},
\qquad
x^2=Q(x)e.
\]
With a positive triple trace form, this recovered algebra is Euclidean. In the JB-triple formulation, the \(+1\) eigenspace of \(Q(e)\) is the unital JB-algebra; requiring \(Q(e)=\operatorname{id}\) makes that eigenspace the entire real triple. [math.u-szeged.hu](https://www.math.u-szeged.hu/~stacho/HTM2/CIT/kaup97.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

A complete or maximal tripotent is not, by itself, an adequate replacement for this condition.

For a concrete nearby example, take
\[
T=M_2(\mathbb R),
\qquad
\{X,Y,Z\}
=\frac12(XY^{\mathsf T}Z+ZY^{\mathsf T}X).
\]
This is a positive real triple of rank two. For \(e=I\),
\[
\{e,e,X\}=X,
\qquad
Q(e)X=X^{\mathsf T}.
\]
Its \(Q(e)\)-fixed part is precisely \(\operatorname{Sym}_2(\mathbb R)\), but the whole triple contains an additional skew-symmetric direction. The full pair is the square rectangular-matrix pair, and its TKK algebra is
\[
\mathfrak{sl}_4(\mathbb R),
\]
not \(\mathfrak{sp}(4,\mathbb R)\). More generally, the rectangular pair
\[
(M_{p,q}(\mathbb R),M_{q,p}(\mathbb R))
\]
has TKK algebra \(\mathfrak{sl}_{p+q}(\mathbb R)\). [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1011.3161?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

This pinpoints another plausible weakening:

> A positive cone in the self-adjoint part of a Peirce algebra does not determine the TKK algebra of the entire surrounding triple.

For a complex Hermitian triple, one must also distinguish its complex algebraic TKK from the real Lie algebra of its associated bounded domain. Complexifying the symmetric-matrix pair gives \(\mathfrak{sp}(4,\mathbb C)\); the corresponding tube-domain real form is \(\mathfrak{sp}(4,\mathbb R)\). The choice of real form is part of the statement. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1011.3161?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

## 6. “Closure of the quadratic operators” needs one final distinction

Even after fixing \(J=\operatorname{Sym}_2(\mathbb R)\), several natural closures differ.

| Construction | Result |
|---|---|
| Lie algebra of linear automorphisms of the positive cone | \(\mathfrak{gl}_2(\mathbb R)\), dimension \(4\) |
| Literal Lie closure of the linear maps \(Q_X\in\operatorname{End}(J)\) | \(\mathfrak{gl}_3(\mathbb R)\), dimension \(9\) |
| Full TKK/Jordan conformal closure | \(\mathfrak{sp}(4,\mathbb R)\), dimension \(10\) |

The TKK construction uses translations, structure operators, and the **quadratic vector fields**
\[
Z\longmapsto Q_Z(C).
\]
For symmetric matrices its infinitesimal conformal fields have the form
\[
Z\longmapsto B+AZ+ZA^{\mathsf T}-ZCZ,
\qquad B,C\in\operatorname{Sym}_2(\mathbb R).
\]
This is the constant–linear–quadratic realization of the Jordan conformal algebra. [heldermann-verlag.de](https://www.heldermann-verlag.de/jlt/jlt06/BERPL.PDF?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

The distinction from linear-operator commutators can be verified explicitly. In the trace-orthonormal basis
\[
E_{11},\quad E_{22},\quad
\frac{E_{12}+E_{21}}{\sqrt2},
\]
for
\[
X=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\b&c\end{pmatrix},
\]
the linear map \(Q_X:Y\mapsto XYX\) has matrix
\[
[Q_X]=
\begin{pmatrix}
a^2&b^2&\sqrt2\,ab\\
b^2&c^2&\sqrt2\,bc\\
\sqrt2\,ab&\sqrt2\,bc&ac+b^2
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Its six quadratic coefficient matrices span all symmetric \(3\times3\) matrices. Their commutators supply the skew-symmetric matrices, giving \(\mathfrak{gl}_3(\mathbb R)\). So merely saying “take the Lie closure of \(Q\)” would not specify the desired construction.

The precise boundary is therefore:

- **Within positive rank-two Jordan algebras:** real Peirce multiplicity \(1\) selects \(\mathfrak{sp}(4,\mathbb R)\); the first larger multiplicity gives \(\mathfrak{su}(2,2)\).
- **Within the complete three-dimensional real Peirce family:** nonzero coupling is necessary and sufficient; allowing zero coupling gives \(\mathfrak{iso}(2,2)\).
- **For general pairs or triples:** the unital realization, whole-space cone compatibility, real form, and meaning of closure must be specified before either statement applies.
