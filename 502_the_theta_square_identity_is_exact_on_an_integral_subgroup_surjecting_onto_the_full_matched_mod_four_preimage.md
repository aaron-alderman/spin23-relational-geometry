## 1. Theorem

Let \(P_4\subset Sp(4,\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z)\) be the full preimage of the matched binary handle group, let

\[
\chi:P_4\to\mathbb Z_2
\]

be the finite lift character, and let

\[
\eta:H_{\mathrm{match}}\to\mathbb Z_2
\]

be the common local permutation parity.

There is an explicit integral subgroup

\[
\widetilde P
\subset Sp(4,\mathbb Z)
\]

whose reduction modulo four is all of \(P_4\), and on \(\widetilde P\) the squared theta multiplier for the characteristic \(m_*=(1,1,1,1)\) obeys

\[
\boxed{
\varepsilon(M)^2
=(-1)^{\chi(M\bmod4)+\eta(M\bmod2)}.}
\]

The earlier numerical relation is therefore an exact character identity on the integral group generated by the transfer lifts and one forbidden kernel direction.

## 2. Why the squared multiplier is a character

For a symplectic transformation

\[
M=\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\C&D\end{pmatrix}
\]

stabilizing the characteristic, the theta transformation law has the form

\[
\theta_{m_*}(M\tau)
=
\varepsilon(M)\det(C\tau+D)^{1/2}\theta_{m_*}(\tau).
\]

After squaring,

\[
\theta_{m_*}(M\tau)^2
=
\varepsilon(M)^2\det(C\tau+D)\theta_{m_*}(\tau)^2.
\]

The determinant is an ordinary weight-one automorphy factor. Consequently \(\varepsilon^2\) is an ordinary group character on the characteristic stabilizer; the metaplectic square-root ambiguity has disappeared.

This standard conclusion also follows from Igusa’s characteristic transformation formula. See the [DLMF modular-transformation formulas](https://dlmf.nist.gov/draft1/21.5) and the [explicit characteristic formula](https://s3.cern.ch/inspire-prod-files-4/48202ffda1eca3b9f7ece732a610347d).

## 4. The two shear generators

For every admissible upper shear,

\[

\qquad
B\equiv kI_2\pmod2,

direct substitution in the theta series gives

\[
\varepsilon(T_B)^2

Since

\[
\chi(T_B)
=\frac{\operatorname{tr}B}{2}+k,
\qquad
\eta(T_B)=k,

For lower shears use the global Fourier matrix

\[
F=\begin{pmatrix}0&-I\\I&0\end{pmatrix}.

It preserves \(q_*\), and

\[
S_C
=F\,T_{-C}\,F^{-1}.

Because \(\varepsilon^2\) is a character, it is conjugation invariant. Hence

\[
\varepsilon(S_C)^2
=\varepsilon(T_{-C})^2
=(-1)^{\operatorname{tr}C/2}.

This proves the squared relation exactly for the entire lower-shear sector, including:

\[
\begin{aligned}

S_{2E_{11}}:&\quad \chi=1,\ \eta=0,\ \varepsilon^2=-1.
\end{aligned}
\]
